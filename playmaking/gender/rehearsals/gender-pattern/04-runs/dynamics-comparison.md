# Ten Minutes to Lock — dynamics comparison

The Playwright built the [authored handoff](../02-company/dynamics/play.md). Two fresh Directors received the same files and [brief](../01-setup/dynamics.md) at 0% staging latitude. The Directors worked separately and each performed a text read-through. All ten handoff files matched their pre-run SHA-256 hashes after both readings.

| | [Run A](dynamics-a/transcript.md) | [Run B](dynamics-b/transcript.md) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How the pressure appeared | Dev asked Nina to account for declining support, then turned to Owen for direction. | Mara pressed for an update, then Dev turned to Nina on support and to Owen on the decision. Nina was not asked to justify a refusal before the cut. |
| What appeared on the board | Owen entered a full proposed split with himself on review and Nina on support. | Dev proposed the same split verbally and entered only Nina’s name for support. |
| First consequential response | Nina withheld consent and asked Owen why he could not cover support. | Nina withheld consent and made her own bid to present on the merits of the work. |

The same deadline, competing work, and equal formal rights were recognizable in both runs. The ways the proposal acquired weight and Nina challenged it differed. Both runs converged on the same proposed allocation and ended with the decision open. This shows variation in how the pressure plays out within the fragment. It gives no evidence of different final allocations or a run where the room misses the Gender Projection.

Neither Director reported a blocking gap. Review surfaced a narrower ambiguity: “provisional nomination” can be read as presenter-only or as permission to fill either field. Run B used the second interpretation. The authored board could make that permission explicit if a later Plot depends on who can enter a support name.
