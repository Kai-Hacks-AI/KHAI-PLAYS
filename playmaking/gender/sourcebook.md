# Gender Sourcebook

## Line of Work

Model how a particular group interprets a cue as gendered and how that interpretation affects standing, obligations, or access. This is a research-grounded theatrical mechanism.

## Origin

- [West and Zimmerman, Doing Gender (1987), pp. 135–137](https://www.gla.ac.uk/0t4/crcees/files/summerschool/readings/WestZimmerman_1987_DoingGender.pdf) describes how people anticipate gender assessment and how others make actions accountable in interaction.
- [Risman, Gender as a Social Structure (2004), abstract](https://doi.org/10.1177/0891243204265349) distinguishes individual, interactional, and institutional dimensions.
- [Ridgeway and Correll, Unpacking the Gender System (2004), abstract](https://doi.org/10.1177/0891243204265269) connects gender salience in social settings with expectations and evaluation.
- [Moss-Racusin et al. (2012), abstract and experimental design](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1211286109) reports that science faculty evaluated otherwise identical laboratory-manager applications differently when names signaled different genders.
- [Crenshaw (1989), pp. 139–140](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/uclf/vol1989/iss1/8/) explains how single-axis analysis can obscure intersecting forms of disadvantage.

## Restrictions

Ground each reading in the setting and the people or institution doing the reading. The application study warrants a cue with consequences in a specific evaluation setting. It gives no universal treatment rule for other roles or institutions. Gender salience, disagreement between readers, and interactions with other forms of standing remain local authoring decisions. The legacy claims attributed to Manne, Connell, and Bourdieu need passage-level verification before they can warrant additional reusable material.
