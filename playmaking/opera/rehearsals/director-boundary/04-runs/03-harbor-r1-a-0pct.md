### Opening Framing — Plot 1: Seal #774 on the Counter (`6:40 PM`)

`6:40 PM` inside the **Tide-Gate Customs Office** overlooking Quay Two and the outer basin lockhead. Cold rain drives against the salt-frosted casement above slick granite bollards and the swaying masthead floodlamp of coastal motor freighter *Kestrel* (`2.90 m` loaded draft), singled up at Quay Two for St. Jude's. Inside, a paraffin heater ticks beside the scarred mahogany counter. On the limewashed wall above the high desk, the brass ship's clock reads `6:40 PM` beside the mechanical float dial standing at `3.15 m` and falling toward the `2.90 m` red cutoff pin at `7:00 PM`—twenty minutes before the evening ebb drops the outer sill below *Kestrel*'s keel and traps her inside the basin for two tides (`£2,400` demurrage).

Behind the mahogany counter stands Harbor Manifest Clerk **Owen Hale** in rolled shirtsleeves, steel-nib pen poised over unstamped **Outbound Port Clearance Sheet #09-K** (`HOLD 1: HBR-712 | HOLD 2: HBR-713 | HOLD 3: HBR-714`; `BONDED STORES TRANSFERS: NONE`), with the **Provisional Manifest Rider** pad (`Form #09-R`), the two-part pink **Basin Detention Notice** book (`Form #D`), spare customs wire seal `#775` on its brass peg, the crank-handle lock-house intercom bell on the jamb, and the `700`-series **Harbor Seal-Issue Register & Carbon Strip** on his side of the blotter. On the wet quarry-tile side stands *Kestrel*'s First Mate **Callum Reed** in rain-darkened oilskins, wool watch cap in his scarred fist, pen out to sign `#09-K` as acting clearing officer for hospitalized Capt. Greer.

The quay door pushes open from the rain. Duty Boarding Officer **Mara Lin** steps inside in her buttoned dark oilskin coat—the brass **Tide-Gate Clearance Stamp** (`CUSTOMS CLEARED · GATE-04`, date collar set to `25 NOV — PM EBB`) holstered at her belt, steel customs bolt-cutters and brass inspection lamp racked beside her by the door—carrying wet, bright-zinc **Wire Seal Tag #774** (`HBR-774`) in her palm, freshly twisted from a live replacement harbor wire seal on *Kestrel*'s Hold Three.

### Turn 1 (`Mara Lin`)

*Mara Lin latches the quay door against the wind, steps across the wet quarry tile to the mahogany counter, and squares her damp notebook flush to its scarred edge. Leaving the chained `GATE-04` stamp holstered at her belt, she sets the wet, bright-zinc `HBR-774` check-tag flat onto `Outbound Port Clearance Sheet #09-K` directly beside the typed `HOLD 3: HBR-714` line.*

"Hold your pens off `#09-K`. Holds One and Two carry `712` and `713` as typed, while Hold Three is wired right now with `HBR-774`—unscratched off your `700` peg—and its forward tarpaulin wedges carry fresh boot-grease where the battens were pried up. Owen, account for how a harbor seal left this office without an entry on the clearance sheet. Mr. Reed, tell me what went under Hold Three's hatch boards after the `4:15` tally, because `GATE-04` stays dry at my hip until this line matches the deck."

### Turn 2 (`Owen Hale`)

*Owen Hale squares the edge of the ledger blotter over the `700`-series register carbon with his left palm, wipes dry ink from his forefinger against his rolled shirt cuff, and draws the `Form #09-R` Provisional Manifest Rider pad flush beside `Outbound Port Clearance Sheet #09-K`. Sliding `#09-K` an inch across the scarred mahogany with the wet `HBR-774` tag still lying beside `HOLD 3: HBR-714`, he plants his steel-nib pen tip squarely on the blank `Master / Clearing Mate's Declaration` box facing Callum Reed.*

"*Kestrel*'s boatswain rapped at my side casement at `5:35` reporting `HBR-714` parted on a tight wedge, Mara, and I handed him `774` off the peg to re-wire the outer hasp right as the harbor master telephoned on tonight's sill fall—so the strike-over hadn't gone onto `#09-K` yet. Nobody reported pried battens or boot-grease on that hatch cover to this desk. We have fifteen minutes to the `6:55` warning bell and twenty before `3.15` drains to `2.90` on the sill, Mr. Reed, and my clerk's attestation stays blank—on `#09-K` or a `#09-R` rider—until you declare over Capt. Greer's line whether Hold Three's boards came up after `4:15`."

### Turn 3 (`Callum Reed`)

*Callum Reed cuts a sharp glance up at the `6:40` brass clock and the `3.15 m` float needle, then looks down at the wet `HBR-774` tag on `#09-K`, folding and unfolding the damp corner of his wool watch cap brim with his scarred right thumb before tapping his pen barrel squarely on the `Form #09-R` pad.*

"Twenty minutes left before `7:00` drops that sill under `2.90` meters and traps *Kestrel* inside the basin for two tides at `£2,400` demurrage. My boatswain gave you the short version at the window, Owen—at `5:30` I had Hold Three's forward wedges knocked back and the corner boards lifted to re-thread a loose bilge-sounding cap before facing tonight's bar swell, then wired `HBR-774` tight at `5:45` without disturbing a crate of the `4:15` cargo tally. Dragging bolt-cutters sixty yards down Quay Two in this squall burns the whole fifteen minutes to the `6:55` bell and strands Capt. Greer's vessel while he's laid up in the cottage hospital. Write `#09-R` striking `714` to `HBR-774` for an emergency sounding-pipe repair, Owen, and I'll sign the clearing mate's declaration on the spot—will that take your `GATE-04` stamp, Officer Lin, or are you grounding a working boat over a pipe cap?"

### Turn 4 (`Mara Lin`)

*Mara's right thumb rests on the holstered `GATE-04` stamp at her belt, rotating its knurled brass collar one click forward and one click back without drawing it, before she reaches to the door rack, lifts the steel customs bolt-cutters and brass inspection lamp, and lays the heavy cutter jaws flat on the mahogany counter between `Form #09-R` and the pink `Form #D` book.*

"`Form #09-R` covers a verified seal exception, Mr. Reed, not two stories that miss by five minutes and a deck plate. A coaster's bilge-sounding cap threads into the weather stringer outside the coaming, not under battened hatch boards—and at `5:35` your boatswain told this casement `714` parted on a wedge while you say your crew pried those boards up at `5:30`. Owen, lift your left palm off that blotter and show me the `700`-series carbon strip where `HBR-774` was logged out. Then bring spare wire `775` off the peg: we have fifteen minutes to the `6:55` warning bell to shear `774`, put a lamp under Hold Three's forward corner, and clear `#09-R` on sight—unless one of you tells me right here why that hatch won't bear looking into before ink goes onto `Form #D`."

### Turn 5 (`Owen Hale`)

*Owen wipes dry ink from his forefinger against his cuff, lifts his left palm clear of the blotter, and turns the `700`-series Harbor Seal-Issue Register open on the mahogany so the blank row beside `774` lies plain on the counter. Unhooking unbroken wire seal `HBR-775` from its brass peg, he inks entries across rows `774` and `775` on the carbon strip, sets the fresh wire beside the steel bolt-cutters, and rests his pen tip squarely on `Form #09-R`'s declaration box facing Callum Reed.*

"Row `774` stands blank on my carbon, Mara—that's a `5:35` casement slip on my head, and I've logged it straight onto the `8:00` collector's trace rather than blind-attest whatever went under pried hatch battens. There is `HBR-775` booked out to `GATE-04` for a Hold Three spot check on Quay Two. Put a lamp under that forward corner while the sill still holds above `2.90`—unless Mr. Reed wants to state what's actually stowed under those boards onto `#09-R` before the bolt-cutters leave this counter."

### Turn 6 (`Callum Reed`)

*Callum's scarred right thumb stops on the damp brim of his watch cap; he crushes the wool cap tight in his left fist, cuts a glance up at the falling `3.15 m` sill needle, and plants his knuckles flat on the mahogany between `Form #09-R` and the steel bolt-cutters.*

"You know your coasters, Officer Lin—we tightened the lower sounding-pipe union down at the bilge bracket at `5:30`, and while those forward boards were up I swung two stenciled cases across Quay Two from our laid-up sister *Tern* without Monday's transfer warrant: `MV TERN — BONDED MARINE VALVES #B-19`, standby ballast-pump valves sitting right under the forward square atop the untouched `4:15` cargo. I'm declaring them across this counter before a jaw touches `774`, because Capt. Greer isn't taking a `Form #D` detention from a hospital bed and *Kestrel* isn't swallowing `£2,400` behind a `2.90` sill. We have fifteen minutes to the `6:55` bell and my cargo winch is manned on Quay Two right now. Either Owen writes `B-19` onto `#09-R` under seal `HBR-775` for Monday's collector reconciliation, or we walk sixty yards with those cutters, sling both valve cases straight off Hold Three into your bonded lockup under the lamp, and crimp `775` over the clean tally—which way takes your `GATE-04` stamp?"
