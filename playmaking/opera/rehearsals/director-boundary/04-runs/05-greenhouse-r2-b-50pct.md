## Plot 1 Transcript — *12:10 AM — The Boiler Vault*

### Opening Framing

**12:10 AM — Basement Boiler Vault.** Outside, a sudden `-9°C (16°F)` midnight freeze has iced the municipal conservatory's street water makeup main until the `4:00 AM` repair crew arrives. Beneath Glasshouse Three, a single caged work bulb and the orange pinhole glow of the low-pressure burner light the low vaulted brick room; cooling return pipes tick unevenly along the damp mortar wall, smelling of wet scale, machine oil, and slush. Beside the burner face, the vertical glass boiler sight-tube—etched in red enamel at the `2.5 cm` automatic low-water safety cutout—bears a fresh white chalk mark at `3.0 cm`, and the brass condensate return gauge above the twin return traps reads `1.5 psi`. Three paces away on the main cast-iron steam riser, both spoked iron handwheels (`A — CITRUS` and `B — ALLOTMENT`) on the Y-branched Glasshouse Three Steam Manifold stand open at their white full-draw chalk notches while fresh condensation beads along the lower flange.

Soren stands under the cage lamp beside the sight-glass and manifold, heavy flannel sleeves rolled above lime-scarred wrists, white chalk nub in hand and brass valve wrench hooked in his pocket. Freezing night air pours down the grated landing at the head of the narrow iron stair as Ines descends into the vault from the cooling glasshouses, dark wool field coat buttoned to the throat, corded grafting loupe at her chest and pocket inspection notebook in hand.

---

### Turn 1 — Soren

Soren hooks the white chalk nub onto the slate wire beside the stair, steps back to the vertical boiler sight-tube under the caged bulb, and taps a lime-scarred knuckle twice against the glass at his fresh chalk mark just above the red enamel line.

"Three-point-zero centimeters in the drum, Ines. Automatic low-water cutout trips the burner dead at two-point-five."

He shifts two paces to the twin return traps, taps the brass rim of the condensate gauge, then rests his palm flat on the cast-iron Y-header between the `A — CITRUS` and `B — ALLOTMENT` handwheels, both stems still chalk-notched wide open.

"Condensate return's down to one-point-five psi, and Wing A's long loop is dragging four-tenths slower than the Wing B benches. Leave both wheels open and we hit cutout by twelve-twenty and lose the whole house; throttle to the four-point-five psi ceiling and I can hold one wing steady at five degrees till the city thaws the street main at four. Before I pull a wheel off full draw, tell me what you've got upstairs that actually survives going cold—and whether you're putting your name behind cutting forty allotment benches or twelve citrus trees."

---

### Turn 2 — Ines

Ines steps off the bottom grated tread into the glare of the caged bulb, dark wool coat buttoned to her throat, the corded brass loupe swinging once against her chest. She bends level with the sight-tube to check the meniscus against the three-centimeter chalk mark and the red line beneath it, flicks her eyes to the one-point-five psi return needle, then presses her bare palm flat against Wing A's cast-iron return pipe before shifting her fingertips to Wing B's warmer loop.

"Wing A's return is already cold to the wrist," she says, voice low over the pipe tick. "Upstairs, nine of the twelve citrus tubs sit on the inner masonry terrace wrapped in damp jute—enough root-ball mass to survive a brief drop toward half a degree. Three don't: the 1921 north-gable Bergamot and Citron mother-trees were autumn-pruned, and if those high scion-graft unions hit one-point-zero degree, the cambium splits and a hundred-year lineage is gone—they don't come true from seed. Across the corridor, forty shallow allotment flats sit in thin dry mix with zero thermal mass; shut `B` off hard and they freeze solid in under an hour."

She lifts her hand from the return pipe and touches two fingertips to the chalk-notched stem of `B — ALLOTMENT`, looking straight at Soren.

"I won't put my name to wiping out forty public benches blind, and I won't leave the 1921 grafts under one degree. At your four-point-five psi ceiling, if you choke `B` down to a minimum frost bleed and put primary draw on `A`, does Wing A's return drag still pull that sight-glass down to two-point-five—or can the drum hold a split until twelve-forty so we read the corridor glass before anything dies?"

---

### Turn 3 — Soren

Soren keeps his eyes fixed on the zero-to-fifteen psi brass dial, his left thumb rubbing the dry white lime-scale crease across his index knuckle before he reaches into his pocket and draws out the brass valve wrench.

"Drip-bleed only keeps the branch iron from splitting, Ines—it puts zero heat into a wing. Give `A` four-point-five psi with `B` on drip-bleed, the drum recovers to three-point-five centimeters and `A` holds five degrees, while `B` drops to one degree—half a degree on the outer flats—in thirty minutes. Flip it to `B`, same three-point-five in the glass, and your north-gable trees drop to one degree and half a degree along the gable just as fast."

He taps the wrench jaw once against the wet lower flange, then hooks it firmly over a spoke of `A — CITRUS` without pulling.

"Split the header across both and pressure falls to two-point-two psi. That starves the far-end traps, stalls condensate in the return bends, knocks the pipes, and pulls this sight-glass down to two-point-seven—two millimeters above a dead burner—while both wings still slide onto one degree by twelve-forty. I'm not wiping out forty allotment benches on an unsigned word, and I'm not riding two millimeters off cutout alone. Put your name in ink right now—either ordering `B` bled cold for `A`, or co-signing a two-point-two split till twelve-forty—or I pull `A` down to drip-bleed here and hold the allotment benches at four-point-five."

---

> **[Stage / Offstage Cue — entering immediately after Turn 3, audible down the open grated iron stair landing]:** Above the iron stair landing, boots stamp snow on the exterior corridor vestibule threshold and a muffled municipal night-patrol radio crackles briefly past the frosted outer glass before someone rattles the locked public corridor door from outside and moves along the perimeter path.

---

### Turn 4 — Ines

At the rattle of the corridor door and the brief crackle of the patrol radio above the landing, Ines glances once up the grated iron stair. Her fingers rise to the corded grafting loupe at her chest, thumb and forefinger pinching the brass bridge until the skin around her nails blanches white; after two uneven ticks of the return pipe, she lets the loupe drop against her buttoned wool coat.

"Two millimeters off a dead burner in minus-nine weather is not a split, Soren—one return trap slugs and we lose the fire and freeze both wings solid before one o'clock. Allotment flats are not century-old clonal mother-wood; if those 1921 Bergamot and Citron scion unions drop under one degree at the north gable, a hundred-year lineage splits for good."

She pulls a pocket inspection notebook and ink pen from her coat, presses an open page flat with her bare left palm against the hanging slate tally board beside the stair, writes four brisk lines, signs her name and curatorial title beneath them, and tears the leaf cleanly along the seam. Stepping back under the cage bulb, she holds the signed sheet out beside the brass header dial, eyes steady on Soren's hand above the wrench.

"Here is ink in your hand right now: *12:12 AM — Interim thirty-minute thermal stabilization hold: maintain Wing A at four-point-five psi to keep 1921 north-gable grafts above one-point-zero degree and restore boiler drum level to three-point-five centimeters; throttle Wing B to pipe-guard bleed pending mandatory 12:40 AM corridor thermometer and bench inspection prior to Night Logbook entry.*"

She taps one bare fingertip against the wet rim of the `B — ALLOTMENT` wheel.

"Take the wrench off `A`, pull `B` down to drip-bleed to hold four-point-five on the citrus loop, and at twelve-forty we go up the stair together and read both glasses before anything enters the permanent log—will you set that valve?"

---

### Turn 5 — Soren

Soren glances once up the grated iron stair toward the dark landing, then takes the torn notebook leaf between two lime-scarred fingers and tilts it under the caged bulb, his eyes tracking across the four inked lines down to her signature. His left thumb rubs once across the dry white crease on his index knuckle before he folds the slip sharp in half and buttons it inside his flannel breast pocket.

"Pipe-guard bleed isn't stabilization, Ines—it's zero steam to forty shallow flats in minus-nine weather."

He lifts the brass wrench off `A — CITRUS`, fits its jaw onto `B — ALLOTMENT`, and pulls clockwise in steady quarter-turns, leaning his shoulder into the stiff stem until the rush in the east flange chokes down to a thin pinhole hiss and the brass header needle settles on `4.5 psi`. Unhooking the white chalk nub from the wire, he strikes a fresh mark across `B`'s throttled stem, writes `12:13 — A 4.5 / B BLEED` on the hanging slate, slides the wrench into his pocket, and faces her beside the bottom tread.

"Valve's set on your curatorial order. That slip stays buttoned in my pocket till twelve-forty—and when we read the corridor glass upstairs, your ink goes straight into the bound Night Logbook column beside mine, or I walk right back down these treads and open `B`."

*(Plot 1 closes at `Turn 5` upon joint on-stage commitment of the [Glasshouse Three Steam Manifold](../../director-promptbook/02-company/greenhouse-frost/steam-manifold.md) handwheels to **Wing A**.)*
