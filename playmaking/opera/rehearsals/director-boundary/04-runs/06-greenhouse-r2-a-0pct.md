### Plot 1: 12:10 AM — The Boiler Vault

*12:10 AM. Beneath Glasshouse Three, a low vaulted brick room lit by a single caged work bulb and the orange pinhole glow of the low-pressure burner. Outside, a sudden -9°C (16°F) midnight freeze has iced the conservatory's street water feed main until the 4:00 AM municipal repair crew arrives; freezing air pours down the grated landing at the head of the narrow iron stair while cooling return pipes tick unevenly along the damp mortar wall, smelling of wet scale, machine oil, and slush. Beside the burner face and the Y-branched cast-iron Glasshouse Three Steam Manifold—both spoked handwheels, `A — CITRUS` and `B — ALLOTMENT`, still open at their white-chalked full winter draw with fresh condensation beading on the lower flange—Soren stands in rolled flannel sleeves with his brass valve wrench hooked in his pocket and a stub of boiler chalk at the vertical sight-glass. Iron steps ring on the stair as Ines descends from the glasshouses in her dark wool field coat buttoned to the throat, corded grafting loupe at her chest and pocket inspection notebook in hand.*

#### Turn 1

**Soren:** *Draws a flat white chalk tick beside the water meniscus in the vertical boiler sight-tube, taps his knuckle twice against the red enamel line half a centimeter below it, then steps three paces to the Y-branched steam manifold and taps the brass rim of the header dial at his fresh `4.5` chalk mark.* "Mind that bottom tread—slush off the grate's slick. Drum water's down to three-point-zero centimeters, five millimeters above the two-point-five automatic low-water cutout, and condensate return's sagging at one-point-five psi with Wing A's long iron loop lagging Wing B by four-tenths. If I leave `A — CITRUS` and `B — ALLOTMENT` wide at winter draw, the burner trips dead by twelve-twenty and the whole house goes under; if I throttle to this four-point-five psi ceiling, I can hold five degrees Celsius steady till the four a.m. water crew thaws the street main—on one wing, not both. You just came down from the glass: what upstairs can weather a drop, and which wheel are you telling me to close?"

#### Turn 2

**Ines:** *Steps off the slick bottom tread under the cage lamp, tucks her pocket notebook beneath her arm, bends level with the meniscus at the `3.0 cm` chalk tick and the `1.5 psi` condensate dial, then presses her bare palm flat against Wing A's cast-iron return pipe before shifting her fingertips to Wing B's return line.* "Wing A's return iron is already noticeably colder than B's, and five millimeters above the red line leaves no margin for a trip. Upstairs, nine of our twelve citrus tubs sit on the inner masonry terrace wrapped in damp jute—enough root-ball mass to weather a dip toward half a degree—but the three autumn-pruned 1921 Bergamot and Citron mother-trees under the north gable carry high exposed scion unions; drop below one-point-zero Celsius and that bark splits irreversibly, and century-old grafted stock cannot be regrown true from seed. Yet I walked past Wing B's forty community flats on the way down—shallow dry mix, zero thermal mass—and shutting `B — ALLOTMENT` to the seat at minus nine will freeze every tray solid before twelve-forty. Rather than spending your whole four-point-five psi ceiling holding five degrees in one wing, can this drum stay above the two-point-five cutout on a split throttle—choking both wheels to hold `A — CITRUS` safely clear of one degree on the north gable with a floor bleed into `B — ALLOTMENT` until a twelve-forty thermometer check upstairs, or does Wing A's lagging return drink that five-millimeter buffer dry?"

#### Turn 3

**Soren:** *Steps back to the sight-tube, ticks a faint chalk fleck two millimeters above the red `2.5 cm` enamel line, and keeps his eyes fixed on the meniscus while his left thumb rubs the white lime-scale crease across his index knuckle; after a beat, he crosses to the Y-header, taps `2.2` on the brass `0–15 psi` dial, and sets a palm on both spoked iron handwheels.* "This 1924 header won't hold a clean uneven bleed—not since they tapped `B — ALLOTMENT` onto the east flange in 'seventy-eight without upsizing the street feed. Drop a branch below four-point-five psi and the far-end steam traps starve. Off full draw I've got three settings: four-point-five psi into `A — CITRUS` with `B` choked to drip-bleed, four-point-five into `B — ALLOTMENT` with `A` on drip-bleed, or a two-point-two psi split across both—and on that split, cooling condensate stalls in the return bends, pulls this glass down to two-point-seven centimeters—two millimeters off hard cutout—and leaves both wings scraping right along your one-degree frost line. These wheels come off winter draw before twelve-twenty either way. I'm not killing forty neighborhood families' benches on my watch signature alone, and I'm not riding a two-millimeter boiler margin unbacked—so name which setting you're countersigning in the Night Logbook upstairs."

#### Turn 4

**Ines:** *Pinches the brass bridge of her corded grafting loupe tightly between thumb and forefinger until the skin blanches, lets it drop against her wool coat, then steps to the Y-header and presses her bare fingertips flat against the cold, condensation-beaded lower flange.* "Put `A — CITRUS` on drip-bleed and those far-end north-gable traps starve first—splitting 1921 Bergamot and Citron scions that no seed bank on earth can replace; choke `B — ALLOTMENT` to drip-bleed right now without a corridor reading and we wipe out forty neighborhood seed flats before we even measure the heat loss under the glass. Set both wheels to the two-point-two psi split now. I will countersign that two-point-two split in the Night Logbook upstairs as a joint thirty-minute triage hold—on one binding condition: if your drum drops below that two-point-seven fleck, or if Wing A's thermometer reads at or below one-point-two degrees Celsius at twelve-forty, `B — ALLOTMENT` goes to drip-bleed and I sign a formal Curatorial Priority Directive putting four-point-five psi into `A — CITRUS` on my own curatorial record. Do you accept those terms and set the two-point-two split?"

#### Turn 5

**Soren:** *Unhooks the brass valve wrench from his pocket, fits its jaw to the spoked rim of `A — CITRUS` to crack the packing, and turns the wheel clockwise off its white winter-draw notch; shifts to `B — ALLOTMENT` and turns it clockwise to match until the steam rush thins in the Y-header and the brass manifold needle settles on `2.2 psi`. He draws a fresh chalk mark across each throttled stem, chalks `2.2 SPLIT / 3.0 CM` onto the hanging slate tally board beside the iron stair, and hooks the wrench back into his pocket by the bottom tread.* "Both stems are off winter draw—two-point-two psi split is set, forty allotment benches still getting live steam. You've got your thirty-minute hold, with one thing straight between us now: if Wing A hits one-point-two Celsius at twelve-forty—or if that meniscus drops to my two-point-seven fleck—your Curatorial Priority Directive goes into the Night Logbook in ink before `B — ALLOTMENT` goes to drip-bleed, putting those forty neighborhood flats squarely on curatorial order, not boiler fault. And if drum water slips under two-point-seven toward the two-point-five red line before twelve-forty, my wrench moves immediately to save the burner. After you up the stair—let's get this twelve-ten split signed into the corridor book while the glass is still holding three centimeters."
