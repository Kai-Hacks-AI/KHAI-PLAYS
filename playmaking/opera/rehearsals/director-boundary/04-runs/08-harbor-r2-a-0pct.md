### Plot 1: Seal #774 on the Counter

**Opening Framing (`6:40 PM` — Tide-Gate Customs Office, Quay Two)**
Cold rain streaks the salt-frosted casement overlooking Quay Two, where coastal motor freighter *Kestrel* (`2.90 m` loaded draft, singled up for St. Jude's) lies under a swaying masthead floodlamp. Inside the cramped brick office, a paraffin heater ticks beside the scarred mahogany counter. Above the high desk, the brass ship's clock stands at `6:40 PM` beside the mechanical sill-depth dial: `3.15 m` over the granite outer basin sill and falling toward the `2.90 m` red cutoff pin at `7:00 PM` (`6:55 PM` warning bell five minutes prior).

Behind the counter, Harbor Manifest Clerk **Owen Hale** stands in rolled shirtsleeves over unstamped **Outbound Port Clearance Sheet #09-K** (`HOLD 1: HBR-712 | HOLD 2: HBR-713 | HOLD 3: HBR-714`), with the **Provisional Manifest Rider** pad (`Form #09-R`), the two-part pink **Basin Detention Notice** book (`Form #D`), spare wire seal `#775` on its brass peg, and the `700`-series **Harbor Seal-Issue Register & Carbon Strip** on his side of the blotter. On the wet quarry-tile side, First Mate **Callum Reed** stands in rain-darkened oilskins, wool watch cap in one fist, pen poised to sign ship's clearance in injured Capt. Greer's stead before two tides of berth demurrage (`£2,400`) lock in at `7:00 PM`.

The quay door pushes open against the rain. Duty Boarding Officer **Mara Lin** steps in wearing buttoned dark oilskins, damp notebook in hand, the **Tide-Gate Clearance Stamp** (`GATE-04`) holstered at her belt beside the racked **Customs Bolt-Cutters** at the door, carrying wet **Wire Seal Tag #774** (`HBR-774`) in her palm.

---

**Turn 1 — Mara Lin**
Mara shoulders the quay door shut against the rain, steps across the wet quarry tiles, and squares her damp notebook neatly against the scarred edge of the mahogany counter. She opens her gloved palm and sets wet tinplate check-tag `HBR-774` flat onto Outbound Port Clearance Sheet `#09-K`, directly beside the typed `HOLD 3: HBR-714` entry between Owen Hale and Callum Reed.

"Hold One and Hold Two check to `712` and `713`," she says, tapping the bright, unscratched zinc edge of the tag once with a forefinger while her other hand rests dry over the chained brass `GATE-04` stamp at her belt. "Hold Three's forward hasp is locked under live harbor wire `774`, and its forward tarpaulin wedges have fresh boot-grease smeared across the pried battens since four-fifteen." She looks from Reed's poised pen to the `700`-series Seal-Issue Register beside Hale's elbow. "`GATE-04` stays in the frog until one of you tells me how a `700`-series desk wire got onto Hold Three and who lifted that cover."

---

**Turn 2 — Owen Hale**
Owen's left palm settles flat against the leather edge of the desk blotter, squaring it neatly over the `700`-series register carbon strip while he wipes a fleck of dry ink from his forefinger against his rolled shirt cuff. "Your boatswain rapped at the side casement at five-thirty-five holding the parted twist of `714`, claiming the wire snapped while he drove a slack wedge tight in the squall—and the harbor master's telephone ring on tonight's sill fall caught my hand before I amended `#09-K`." With the tip of his steel-nib pen, Owen nudges wet tag `HBR-774` clear of the typed hold line, draws the carbon-backed Provisional Manifest Rider pad (`Form #09-R`) flush against `#09-K` on the mahogany counter, and plants his nib squarely on the Clearing Mate's Declaration box facing Callum Reed. "Tightening a rain-swollen wedge doesn't pry forward battens or leave fresh boot-grease across a tarpaulin, Mr. Reed, and the float's already dropping through `3.15` meters with fifteen minutes to the warning bell. Before I enter a `774` replacement trace or put my initials in the clerk's attestation box, state plainly for Officer Lin whether Hold Three's cover was lifted after four-fifteen."

---

**Turn 3 — Callum Reed**
Callum rests his pen beside Outbound Port Clearance Sheet `#09-K`, his scarred right thumb folding and unfolding the damp wool brim of his watch cap before he glances up at the brass wall clock and the `3.15`-meter float needle. "Twenty minutes to the seven-o'clock pin, Officer Lin, and with `2.90` meters under *Kestrel*'s keel I wasn't taking her into tonight's bar swell with a slack bilge-sounding cap weeping into Hold Three." He taps a scarred knuckle squarely on the blank `Form #09-R` pad, looking from the edge of Owen's blotter to the chained `GATE-04` stamp at Mara's belt. "I ordered that forward corner pried at five-thirty to wrench the sounding pipe tight clear of the baled twine, and my bosun carried sheared `714` straight to Mr. Hale's casement at five-thirty-five to draw `774` in its place. Enter `774` on `#09-R` as an alongside sounding-cap re-seal, Mr. Hale—I'll sign Captain Greer's box right now so Officer Lin can stamp `#09-K` while there's still water over the granite."

---

**Turn 4 — Mara Lin**
Mara unclips the brass `GATE-04` stamp to the length of its belt chain and sets its dry base beside `#09-R`, her thumb clicking the knurled date collar one notch forward and one notch back without touching the ink pad.

"A coaster's bilge-sounding pipe caps flush on the weather deck outside the coaming, Mr. Reed—you don't pry tarpaulin battens and lift sealed hatch boards to wrench a sounding plug, and your bosun told this window `714` snapped on an outside wedge." She shifts her gaze to Owen Hale's left palm resting over the edge of the blotter and points two gloved fingers at the `700`-series Seal-Issue Register beneath it. "And a replacement wire doesn't leave this counter through a side casement without a timed entry on that carbon strip and the sheared loop of `714` tagged beside it, Mr. Hale."

Mara hooks her left hand onto the steel handle of the racked customs bolt-cutters by the quay door while keeping `GATE-04` dry on the mahogany. "Put the `700`-series carbon strip and the parted twist of `714` in plain sight on this blotter right now, or in sixty seconds I walk sixty yards down Quay Two with the cutters and lamp to lift Hold Three's forward boards myself before the `6:55` bell."

---

**Turn 5 — Owen Hale**
Owen wipes his dry forefinger once against his rolled shirt cuff, lifts his left palm, and folds open the `700`-series Seal-Issue Register flat on the mahogany counter so the blank, uninitialed `774` line on the carbon strip lies in plain sight. "Line `774` is still blank and `714` never crossed the sill, Officer Lin—his bosun held a parted wire up against the wet glass at five-thirty-five, I passed `774` through the casement before the harbor master's telephone rang at five-forty, and that late desk entry goes down over my own initials." He unhooks spare wire `#775` from its brass peg, sets it beside the dry `GATE-04` stamp, and slides `Form #09-R` across the counter toward Callum Reed with his steel nib resting squarely on the Clearing Mate's Declaration box. "What I won't attest is a sounding-pipe story on a customs rider when your bosun gave me a slack-wedge story at the window, Mr. Reed. Either put in ink on `#09-R` what actually went under Hold Three's boards so Officer Lin can decide how to clear it, or let her take `#775` and those cutters down Quay Two right now with fourteen minutes left to the `6:55` bell—because if that hatch hides unmanifested stowage, my pen goes straight to pink `Form #D`."

---

**Turn 6 — Callum Reed**
Callum slaps his damp watch cap flat onto the mahogany beside `Form #09-R`, glances up at the brass clock and the falling sill needle, and leaves his pen untouched on the counter. "Nineteen minutes to the seven-o'clock pin, Officer Lin, and I won't put Captain Greer's ticket over a false rider." He braces both scarred knuckles on the counter edge between Mara's hand on the bolt-cutters and Owen's open `700`-series register. "What went under Hold Three's forward boards at five-thirty isn't freight—it's two stenciled cases of standby ballast-pump valves marked `MV TERN — BONDED MARINE VALVES #B-19`, swung across from our laid-up sister boat ahead of Monday's transfer warrant, sitting right under the forward coaming while my bosun still carries sheared `714` in his oilskin pocket. Either write `#B-19` onto `#09-R` as provisional sister-ship machinery stores for the eight-o'clock Collector's audit, or walk down Quay Two with `#775` and those cutters right now while my winch whips both cases back onto the stones into your custody in four minutes flat and hands over `714`—tell me yes or no while there's water over the sill."
