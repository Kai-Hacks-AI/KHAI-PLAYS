# Glasshouse Three Corridor

## Taxonomy

Central glazed threshold spine dividing Wing A and Wing B in [Frost Line at Glasshouse Three](play.md).

## Owner

[KHAI-PLAYS](https://github.com/Kai-Hacks-AI/KHAI-PLAYS)

## Shown

A narrow limestone-paved hall set between two frost-silvered glass wings at `12:40 AM`; the brass frost-drop contact bell chatters sharply above the wall thermometer board, sodium light from the frozen park road refracts through ice crystals forming on the high mullions, and the air smells of cold terracotta, damp peat moss, and bruised citrus oil.

## Holds

- Paired remote-bulb capillary wall thermometers (`WING A — CITRUS` on the left, `WING B — ALLOTMENT` on the right), each wired with a red frost-drop contact pin at `1.0°C (34°F)` linked to the ringing brass alarm bell and its manual silence latch.
- Full-height interior glass partition panes looking directly into Wing A's terraced citrus tubs (including the three tall `1921` mother-trees under the north gable) and Wing B's forty wooden allotment seedling benches.
- Heavy latched inner glass doors into Wing A and Wing B, sealing the corridor's `6.0°C (43°F)` masonry warmth between the two houses.
- The sloped oak watch lectern holding the chained [Conservatory Night Logbook](night-logbook.md) and indelible black pen, five paces from the iron stair head descending to the [Basement Boiler Vault](boiler-vault.md).

## Offers

Direct, simultaneous proof to both [Soren](soren.md) and [Ines](ines.md) of each wing's `12:40 AM` temperature and visible frost state; a manual brass latch to silence the alarm bell without clearing the thermometer needles; a brief bleed of `6.0°C (43°F)` corridor air into one wing if an inner door is unlatched (at the cost of dropping the corridor's thermal buffer); and permanent written entry in the [Conservatory Night Logbook](night-logbook.md).

## Withheld

The [Glasshouse Three Steam Manifold](steam-manifold.md) handwheels and boiler sight-glass down in the [Basement Boiler Vault](boiler-vault.md). Re-throttling steam flow requires walking away from the open logbook and partition doors to descend the basement stair.
