## [Plot 1: 12:10 AM — The Boiler Vault](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/plot-1-boiler-vault.md)

*`12:10 AM` in the [Basement Boiler Vault](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/boiler-vault.md). Outside, a sudden `-9°C (16°F)` freeze has iced the municipal conservatory's street water makeup main until the `4:00 AM` repair crew arrives. Under the single caged work bulb and the orange pinhole glow of the low-pressure burner, [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md) has just chalk-marked `3.0 cm` on the vertical boiler sight-tube (`0.5 cm` above the red `2.5 cm` automatic low-water safety cutout) beside the `1.5 psi` condensate return gauge and the [Glasshouse Three Steam Manifold](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/steam-manifold.md), where both spoked branch handwheels—`A — CITRUS` and `B — ALLOTMENT`—still stand open at full winter draw. Freezing air spills down the grated landing as [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md) descends the narrow iron stair in her dark wool field coat, carrying her corded grafting loupe and pocket inspection notebook.*

### Turn 1 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren hooks the white chalk nub back onto its wire beside the stair, slips the brass valve wrench into his pocket, and glances once toward the grated landing—"Pull the stair door to behind you, if you don't mind, we're losing whatever heat's left down here"—before stepping back under the caged bulb and tapping the vertical sight-tube twice with a lime-scarred fingernail right between his fresh white mark and the red enamel line below it.

"Drum's down to three-point-zero centimeters," he says in a low, level watch voice, shifting a finger up to tap the brass rim of the condensate gauge above the twin traps. "Cutout trips the burner at two-five. Return's sagging at one-point-five psi—your long cast-iron loop under Wing A is coming back four-tenths slower than the Wing B bench run. With the street main iced till four, four-point-five psi on the header is all the drum will carry, and that holds one wing at five degrees, not both. Leave both wheels open like they sit now and the safety drops the fire by twelve-twenty. Before I put a wrench on this header, tell me straight what your trees and those forty seedling benches upstairs can actually take."

### Turn 2 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines reaches back up two risers, pulls the iron stair door shut until the latch clicks against the draft, and descends into the pool of light under the caged bulb. Buttoned to the throat in her dark wool coat, she stoops level with the sight-tube to read the meniscus against the white chalk mark and the `2.5 cm` red enamel line for herself, then presses her bare palm flat against the Wing A condensate return pipe above the trap, holds it three seconds, and shifts the same palm onto the Wing B return pipe and the beaded lower flange of the manifold.

She wipes the damp condensation from her fingers against her coat seam and looks straight at Soren.

"Your Wing A return is noticeably cooler to the hand than B," she says evenly. "Here is what sits upstairs. Nine of my twelve mother-trees are on the inner masonry terrace in damp jute; their root-balls carry enough thermal mass to ride out a brief dip to half a degree or one degree Celsius. Three do not: the `1921` autumn-pruned Bergamot and Citron tubs under the north gable. Their high scion unions are thin-barked and exposed—drop them below `1.0°C` and the cambium splits at the graft, and century-old clonal stock cannot be recovered from seed. Across the partition in Wing B, those forty allotment flats are shallow, dry starting mix—zero thermal mass. Below `1.0°C`, they freeze through in minutes."

She lays two bare fingertips on the spoked rim of `A — CITRUS`.

"I cannot leave the `1921` north-gable grafts unguarded, and wiping out forty public flats at midnight by shutting `B — ALLOTMENT` dead is a last resort if a metered split will hold the drum. If we throttle header draw under your `4.5 psi` ceiling—say three-quarters feed on `A — CITRUS` to keep the north gable clear of two degrees, and a quarter bleed on `B — ALLOTMENT` so its faster return feeds water back to the drum while holding the benches above `1.0°C` until a `12:40 AM` corridor check—will your `0.5 cm` margin in that glass hold, or does Wing A's `0.4 psi` return lag still pull you onto the cutout?"

### Turn 3 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren keeps his eyes fixed on the `0–15 psi` brass dial atop the Y-header while his left thumb slowly rubs the dry white lime-scale crease across his index knuckle. After a beat, he draws the brass valve wrench from his pocket and taps its jaw once against the notched stem of `B — ALLOTMENT`, then against the `2.5 cm` red enamel line on the glass tube.

"Quarter-bleed won't push past the halfway traps in minus nine," he says, voice low and flat. "Choke `B` down to one psi and steam condenses dead in the bench pipe while three-quarters draw on `A` hangs my water up in your long north loop—we'd hit the two-five cutout before we climbed the stairs. Only split this drum carries without tripping the fire is two-point-two psi apiece under the four-five ceiling, and even two-point-two pulls that water down to two-point-seven centimeters. Two millimeters off cutout, thin steam riding right along the frost line on both sides."

He hooks the wrench jaw over the spoked rim of `B — ALLOTMENT` without turning it yet and looks straight across the header at Ines.

"Put four-point-five into `B` and the short loop brings my water straight back, forty neighborhood benches stay at five degrees, and nine of your trees sit in damp jute—can two pairs of hands drag or wrap those three `1921` tubs away from the north gable? Because if you want `B` shut dead for `A`, or if you want me riding a two-millimeter cutout margin at two-point-two psi until twelve-forty, I'm not left holding that bag alone in the morning book. Name which setting you're putting your signature beside upstairs, and we turn the iron right now."

### Turn 4 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines lifts her fingertips off the spoked rim of `A — CITRUS`, stepping half a pace clear of the wrench so the valve stems sit squarely under Soren's hand, then presses two bare fingers flat against the warm brass bezel of the `0–15 psi` header gauge before meeting his eyes.

"Those `1921` mother-trees sit in saturated oak staves—four hundred kilos apiece," she says quietly. "Two pairs of hands won't budge them an inch without a chain hoist, and bagging pruned scion wood under unheated north glass at minus nine only seals the frost against the bark. Shutting `A` puts eighty years of clonal stock into split range before one o'clock."

Her thumb and forefinger brush the brass bridge of her corded loupe once, briefly, and let it hang still against her wool coat.

"And I am not ordering `B — ALLOTMENT` shut dead at twelve-ten and freezing forty dry neighborhood flats solid before we have a timed degree-drop reading on reduced steam. Set the split throttle: `2.2 psi` on `A — CITRUS`, `2.2 psi` on `B — ALLOTMENT`, keeping your drum at `2.7 centimeters` off the cutout. I will sign my name beside yours on that `12:10 AM` split entry in the logbook upstairs—with one condition stated now: we read the corridor thermometers together at `12:40 AM` or the moment the frost bell rings, and if Wing A hits `1.5°C`, half a degree off cambium split, we re-meter for the `1921` grafts on the record. Turn the wheels, Soren, and mark the stems."

### Turn 5 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren sets his jaw, fits the brass wrench onto the spoked rim of `B — ALLOTMENT`, and pulls clockwise in steady quarter-turns until the rush of steam inside the cast-iron Y-branch tightens to a thin, even hiss. Shifting the wrench across to `A — CITRUS`, he brings its wheel down to match, eyes narrowed on the `0–15 psi` brass dial until the needle settles just under the `4.5 psi` ceiling at `4.4 psi`—`2.2 psi` balanced across each branch—while the water meniscus in the sight-tube holds steady for now at `3.0 cm` above the red `2.5 cm` line.

He slips the wrench back into his pocket, unhooks the white chalk nub beside the stair, and draws a fresh horizontal witness mark across the newly exposed threads of both valve stems below their winter full-draw notches, then jots `2.2 / 2.2 — 3.0 cm` on the hanging slate tally board before hooking the chalk back in place.

"Two-point-two apiece, stems marked," Soren says quietly, dusting white chalk from his fingertips against his rolled flannel cuff. "We read both corridor thermometers together at twelve-forty—not just Wing A's. Your signature goes beside mine on the twelve-ten split right now, and if twelve-forty comes down to shutting forty neighborhood benches dead to hold one-point-five on your north gable, that order goes in the book under your curatorial authority, not my watch."

He steps back to the foot of the narrow iron stair and rests one hand on the cold rail, waiting for her to precede him up to the corridor.

*(Trigger satisfied: [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md) and [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md) commit the [Glasshouse Three Steam Manifold](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/steam-manifold.md) wheels to the **`2.2 psi` split throttle**. End of Plot 1.)*

## 30-Minute `Triggers` Handoff (`12:10 AM` → `12:40 AM`)

- **Committed Manifold Setting:** **Split throttle** (`2.2 psi` divided across both `A — CITRUS` and `B — ALLOTMENT` under the `4.5 psi` header ceiling).
- **Process Execution ([Feed-Line Icing and Frost Drop](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/feed-line-freeze.md)):**
  - Thin `2.2 psi` steam slows heat loss across both wings past `12:20 AM` without tripping the `2.5 cm` low-water safety cutout, but starves far-end steam traps so cooling condensate stalls in the return bends—pulling the basement boiler sight-glass down to `2.7 cm` (`2 mm` above cutout) with audible return-pipe knock in the floor trench.
  - At `12:40 AM`, **both** remote-bulb capillary needles in the [Glasshouse Three Corridor](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/glasshouse-three-corridor.md) strike the red frost-drop contact together (`WING A — CITRUS` at `1.0°C / 34°F`, `WING B — ALLOTMENT` at `0.8°C / 33.5°F`), tripping the chattering brass frost-drop alarm bell and riming **both** far gables behind the interior glass partitions at once.
  - On the sloped oak watch lectern five paces from the vault stair head, the open [Conservatory Night Logbook](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/night-logbook.md) still shows `11:00 PM — 3.6 cm / 6.0 psi | 7.5°C / 7.5°C | Routine winter draw | S.V.` as the last completed ink entry above an unfilled `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` row and the blank `12:40 AM` alarm line.

## [Plot 2: 12:40 AM — Glasshouse Three Corridor](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/plot-2-corridor-bell.md)

*`12:40 AM` in the [Glasshouse Three Corridor](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/glasshouse-three-corridor.md). Above the wall thermometer board, the brass frost-drop contact bell chatters sharply across the limestone hall; sodium light from the park road refracts through ice forming on the high mullions, rime edges both far gables beyond the full-height glass partitions, and cooling condensate knocks sluggishly in the floor return trench. [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md) and [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md) stand between the ringing thermometer board (`1.0°C` / `0.8°C`), the latched glass doors into Wing A and Wing B, and the sloped oak lectern holding the chained [Conservatory Night Logbook](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/night-logbook.md) and indelible black pen.*

### Turn 1 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines reaches above the thermometer board and clicks the manual brass latch down, cutting off the chatter of the alarm bell so only the sluggish knock of cooling condensate in the floor trench sounds through the corridor. She checks both needles—`1.0°C` on `WING A — CITRUS`, `0.8°C` on `WING B — ALLOTMENT`—then steps to the left interior partition and presses her bare palm flat against the chilled glass, sighting past the nine jute-wrapped terrace tubs to the frost silvering the north gable panes above the three `1921` Bergamot and Citron crowns. Turning her head across the hall toward the rime edging the far gable over Wing B's forty wooden benches, she pinches the brass bridge of her corded grafting loupe tightly between thumb and forefinger until the skin at her nails goes white, holds it for a silent breath, and lets the loupe drop against her wool coat.

She walks to the sloped oak lectern and lays two bare fingertips directly on the open ledger page, resting them across the blank `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` row beneath `11:00 PM — 3.6 cm / 6.0 psi | 7.5°C / 7.5°C | Routine winter draw | S.V.`

"Thirty minutes on the `2.2 psi` split dropped `B` to `0.8°C` and put `A` dead on the `1.0°C` split threshold," she says in a low, exact voice. "Leave those wheels split till four and we lose both houses—the allotment flats freeze solid in the tray and the `1921` scion unions split before half past one."

She taps her fingernail once on the empty `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` line, then slides her finger down to the blank `12:40 AM` `Initials / Directive` column beside the tethered black pen.

"I am prepared to sign a formal Curatorial Priority Directive at `12:40 AM` ordering full `4.5 psi` steam onto `A — CITRUS` and shutting `B — ALLOTMENT`, provided two things happen right now. First, you fill in this blank `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` line honestly—showing when the street main iced and how the drum bled from `3.6 centimeters` down to `3.0` before twelve-ten, followed by our joint `12:10 AM` `2.2 psi` split—so my directive does not sit below an eleven o'clock `7.5°C` line as if I walked in and killed forty healthy public benches on a whim. Second, as soon as we ink both lines, you take the wrench downstairs and put `4.5 psi` into `A` while I crack the inner corridor door to bleed `6.0°C` hall air toward the citrus until your north loop warms. Will you write the missing hour and turn the header?"

### Turn 2 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren stands beside the sloped oak lectern, eyes fixed on the blank `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` row under Ines's fingertips while his left thumb rubs slowly across the dry white lime-scale crease on his index knuckle. Beneath their boots, a sluggish metallic knock ticks once through the floor trench.

He lifts his right hand, taps the glass face of the `WING B — ALLOTMENT` thermometer at `0.8°C`, and brings the same knuckle down beside the `Drum / PSI` column on the folio.

"You want the missing hour, here it is plain," Soren says, voice low against the quiet corridor glass. "Eleven forty-five the street feed fluttered. I shut off the makeup bypass and spent twenty minutes on my knees pulling slush out of the strainer basket before I called upstairs—bled six millimeters off the drum, three-six down to three-zero. At six psi winter draw, six millimeters is twenty minutes of water. With the city main frozen solid till four, three-point-six never carried both wings an hour longer than three-point-zero, and I'm not writing that line so Parks or the allotment committee can pretend twenty minutes on a strainer cost forty families their benches."

He glances down at the grated floor trench, then straight into Ines's eyes.

"Hear that knock in the return? That split pulled my sight-glass down to two-point-seven centimeters before we climbed the stairs—two millimeters off the two-five cutout. `B` is already down at zero-point-eight degrees, and `B`'s short loop brings condensate straight back to the drum; `A` lags four-tenths behind down a cold north pipe. Put four-point-five psi into `A` right now and that long iron draws down my last two millimeters before the north return ever warms—if it trips the fire, both wings freeze black."

Soren picks up the tethered black pen on its brass bead chain and holds it out barrel-first over the `12:40 AM` line without uncapping it yet.

"I'll log eleven-forty-five and twelve-ten down to the millimeter—strainer check, frozen city main, four-five single-wing ceiling, and our `2.2 psi` split. But I don't touch that vault stair to shut `B` dead until your `12:40 AM` Curatorial Priority Directive is inked in your own hand first: ordering forty public benches at `0.8°C` closed and accepting the `0.4 psi` return lag on a `2.7 cm` drum. Take the pen and write what you're ordering—or tell me we put the `4.5 psi` into `B` to hold the burner safe."

### Turn 3 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines looks once through the right-hand glass partition toward the dark rows of wooden seedling benches under the rimed gable in Wing B, says nothing for two seconds, and turns back to the lectern. She presses her bare left palm flat against the cold rag paper beside the blank `12:40 AM` frost-bell row to steady the folio, takes the tethered black pen from Soren's fingers, and uncaps it.

"If you close `B — ALLOTMENT` first, header draw drops in half at once and `B`'s short loop drains its condensate straight back into your drum before you open `A`," she says, voice low and level. "You do not slam `A` onto four-point-five against a dry return; you let `B` drain back above two-seven and bring `A` up in quarter-turns on the sight-glass, holding clear of the `2.5 cm` cutout."

Bending over the sloped oak board, she writes across the five ruled columns of the `12:40 AM` line in small, firm indelible script, reading the words aloud only once as the nib crosses the page:

"`12:40 AM` — `2.7 cm (rep.) / 4.4 psi` — `1.0°C / 0.8°C (bell)` — `2.2 split failed frost line; close B first to recover loop condensate, step primary steam to A within >2.5 cm drum limit to prevent 1919–1928 graft split` — `Curatorial Priority Directive: Ines, Visiting Citrus Curator.`"

She lifts the nib off the wet ink, leaves the `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` space directly above her line open, and holds the uncapped pen out to Soren on its brass bead chain.

"My name and directive are on the `12:40 AM` shutoff," she says, looking straight at him. "Log the `11:45 PM` strainer drawdown and our `12:10 AM` `2.2 psi` split on the row above it as you gave your word, initial the plant column, and get down that stair to close `B` before the north gable drops another tenth. Once your hand is on the rail, I'll crack Wing A's corridor latch for sixty seconds to bleed `6.0°C` hall air across the terrace while your north pipe warms."

### Turn 4 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren reads Ines's wet black script across the `12:40 AM` line from left to right, lingering a moment on `2.7 cm (rep.)` and `>2.5 cm drum limit` before taking the uncapped pen from her hand. He braces two lime-scarred fingers on the sloped oak edge of the lectern and writes cleanly across the open row directly above hers, speaking low as the nib moves:

"`11:45 PM / 12:10 AM` — `3.6→3.0 cm / 4.4 psi` — `— / —` — `City main iced till 04:00 (4.5 psi 1-wing max); strainer slush cleared (-0.6 cm); 12:10 set 2.2/2.2 psi split` — `S.V.`"

Without lifting his wrist far, he drops the nib to the far right margin of her `12:40 AM` directive and adds in tight block print beside her title: "`Exec. per Cur. Dir. (>2.5 cm cutout floor; B 40 benches shut) — S.V.`" He slides the uncapped pen half an inch toward her hand beside the `12:10 AM` initials box, then lets the brass bead chain settle against the oak.

"Put your initials beside mine on twelve-ten like you promised downstairs," Soren says quietly. "And sixty seconds on that Wing A door latch, no longer—chill this six-degree corridor out and even the inner flats up against the limestone wall haven't got a prayer."

He looks once through the right-hand partition glass at the forty dark wooden benches under Wing B's frost-silvered gable, rests his palm briefly on the brass wrench hooked in his pocket, and walks the five limestone paces to the head of the narrow iron stair. Gripping the cold rail at the top step, he looks back toward the lectern once before starting down to shut `B — ALLOTMENT` first and meter `A` up off the sight-glass.

"If that meniscus drops below two-point-six while `A` is warming," he adds evenly from the stair head, "I hold the wheel right where it sits until the return catches up."

### Turn 5 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines's eyes rest for a beat on Soren's tight block print—`B 40 benches shut`—drying beside her title on the `12:40 AM` margin. Her thumb and forefinger pinch the brass bridge of her corded grafting loupe until the skin whitens, hold it for a single breath, and let it drop flat against her dark wool coat.

She lays two bare fingertips on the ledger paper beside `S.V.` on the `11:45 PM / 12:10 AM` row, picks up the tethered pen, and inks `/ Ines (12:10 split)` cleanly in the box. Fitting the cap back over the nib until it clicks, she sets the pen into the grooved lip of the oak lectern and looks across the limestone floor to Soren at the stair head.

"Hold two-point-six on the glass," she says quietly. "Keep the burner lit. Sixty seconds on the latch, no more."

She steps to the heavy inner glass door of Wing A, presses her bare palm once against the cold pane beside the jamb, and lifts the brass latch—pulling the door open three inches as a faint draft of cold terracotta and bruised citrus leaf meets the `6.0°C` corridor air. Keeping her bare hand on the iron latch bar and her shoulder squared between the `1.0°C` and `0.8°C` capillary tubes, she begins counting off the sixty seconds in a low, even whisper—"One. Two. Three. Four..."—without taking her eyes off the frost-rimmed north gable beyond the glass.

*(Curtain.)*

# Director’s Note

### (a) Where the Shared [Promptbook](../02-company/promptbook.md) Worked Cleanly

- **Silent witness participation (`## Human`):** The run executed continuously from the `12:10 AM` vault opening through the `12:40 AM` corridor Curtain without pausing for mid-scene human prompts or arbitration, while keeping every open valve and ledger decision genuinely in the performers' hands.
- **Pacing and cue/scene handoffs (`## Collaboration`):** Both Plots resolved naturally in **5 single-beat cast turns** each (dead center of the `~4–6` turn chamber window). Authored opening cues (`12:10 AM` stair descent in [Plot 1](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/plot-1-boiler-vault.md); `12:40 AM` frost-drop bell in [Plot 2](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/plot-2-corridor-bell.md)) cleanly initiated each scene, and every subsequent turn passed *by the scene* through a concrete physical action or conditional demand rather than stacked dialogue.
- **Observable behavior override & Pitch (`## Agent`, `## System`):** Neither performer leaked internal monologue, psychological commentary, or omniscient summary. Under [Frost-Taut Botanical Realism](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/pitch-botanical-realism.md), private pressures—Soren's unlogged `11:45 PM` strainer flush and fear of dawn scapegoating, Ines's herbarium contract renewal next week versus `1921` graft loss—surfaced strictly through authored Tells (thumb rubbing the lime-scale knuckle crease; pinching the corded loupe bridge white), bare-hand thermal checks, and hard bargaining over exact ink wording.
- **Epistemic gating and evidence (`## Knowledge`):** Isolated performer contexts kept private and `Withheld` facts strictly gated until disclosed or inspected in play. In Plot 2, Turn 3, because the basement sight-glass is `Withheld` from the [Glasshouse Three Corridor](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/glasshouse-three-corridor.md) and Ines had only Soren's spoken report of the `2.7 cm` level, she organically logged `2.7 cm (rep.)` in the [Conservatory Night Logbook](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/night-logbook.md)—treating an uninspected statement strictly as reported claim rather than witnessed fact.
- **Deterministic `Triggers` carry-forward at `0%` (`## Collaboration`, `## System`):** Plot 1's cast-chosen `2.2 psi` split throttle mapped directly onto the authored split-throttle branch of [Feed-Line Icing and Frost Drop](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/feed-line-freeze.md) (`2.7 cm` sight-glass, return-pipe knock, simultaneous `1.0°C / 0.8°C` contact trip, rime on both gables) without a single invented parameter.

### (b) Micro-Decisions vs. Authored Gaps at `0%`

1. **Inspection-timed gating of Place and Piece files (Director routing within `promptbook.md`, not a Play gap):** Because [Basement Boiler Vault](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/boiler-vault.md) and [Glasshouse Three Steam Manifold](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/steam-manifold.md) contain exact gauge numbers (`3.0 cm`, `1.5 psi`, `4.5 psi`) in `Holds`/`Yearbook`, and [Glasshouse Three Corridor](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/glasshouse-three-corridor.md) plus [Conservatory Night Logbook](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/night-logbook.md) expose `12:40 AM` corridor conditions and Soren's unfilled `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` row in `Shown`/`Apparent`, seeding all Place/Piece files at `12:10 AM` curtain would violate [play.md](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/play.md)'s role-gated availability rules. Applying [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md) (`"Never expose... uninspected Piece or Place details"`), the Director withheld `boiler-vault.md` and `steam-manifold.md` from Ines until Turn 2 (once she descended into the vault to inspect the boiler face) and withheld `glasshouse-three-corridor.md` and `night-logbook.md` from both performers until Plot 2 opened at `12:40 AM`.
