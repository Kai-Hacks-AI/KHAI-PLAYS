## [Plot 1: 12:10 AM — The Boiler Vault](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/plot-1-boiler-vault.md)

**Place:** [Basement Boiler Vault](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/boiler-vault.md)  
**Piece in Play:** [Glasshouse Three Steam Manifold](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/steam-manifold.md)  
**Active Process:** [Feed-Line Icing and Frost Drop](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/feed-line-freeze.md)

*At curtain (`12:10 AM`), a `-9°C (16°F)` midnight freeze has iced the conservatory's street water makeup main until the `4:00 AM` municipal repair crew arrives. Under the single caged work bulb in the [Basement Boiler Vault](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/boiler-vault.md), [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md) stands beside the [Glasshouse Three Steam Manifold](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/steam-manifold.md)—both `A — CITRUS` and `B — ALLOTMENT` handwheels still open on their white full-draw notches—having just marked `3.0 cm` in fresh chalk on the boiler sight-tube (`0.5 cm` above the red `2.5 cm` automatic low-water safety cutout; condensate return at `1.5 psi`, safe continuous header ceiling chalked at `4.5 psi`). [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md) descends the narrow iron stair from the glasshouses in her dark wool field coat with her corded grafting loupe and pocket inspection notebook.*

### Turn 1 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines comes down the last iron treads with her dark wool coat buttoned to the throat, her pocket notebook tucked under her left arm and the corded brass loupe resting against her sternum. At the foot of the stair she stops, presses her bare right palm flat against the ticking cast-iron return pipe along the damp mortar wall for three full seconds, then lowers her hand and steps up to the edge of the cage lamp's light, her gaze moving from the two full-open manifold handwheels to the fresh white chalk mark on the sight-tube beside Soren.

"The fin-pipes under Wing A's north gable went tepid ten minutes before midnight, Soren, and Wing B's bench runs were slacking right behind them when I walked through." Her voice stays low and even under the uneven tick of the iron. "The city crew says four o'clock at the earliest on the street main. Where is your drum sitting on that glass right now—and how much header pressure can you actually hold without tripping the burner?"

### Turn 2 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren steps half a pace aside so the cage bulb throws light straight across the vertical sight-tube. He taps the glass with a blunt fingernail at the fresh white chalk mark, then drops his finger five millimeters to the red enamel line etched beneath it.

"Three-point-zero centimeters in the drum. Red line at two-point-five trips the automatic low-water cutout." He crosses three paces to the manifold, raps the brass rim of the `0–15 psi` header dial, and nods toward the condensate gauge sitting at `1.5 psi` above the return traps. "Return's down to one-five, and Wing A's long iron loop lags point-four behind Wing B's short bench run. Leave both wheels on full draw and we hit cutout by twelve-twenty—burner locks out, fire's dead, and both wings freeze solid before four."

He sets his right palm on the black cast-iron header between `A — CITRUS` and `B — ALLOTMENT`, leaving both stems untouched on their white chalk notches.

"Four-point-five psi is all I have on a blocked street feed. That holds one wing steady at five degrees through four o'clock. Split it to two-point-two apiece and sluggish return pulls my glass down to two-seven—two millimeters off a dead boiler." He looks across the lamp light at Ines. "Before I take iron off the mark, what's actually sitting on the benches and in the tubs upstairs, and which side can take a drop?"

### Turn 3 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines steps directly under the cage bulb, bends slightly to bring her eyes level with the meniscus sitting on the white `3.0 cm` chalk mark above the red `2.5 cm` line, then moves three paces to the twin return traps beneath the `1.5 psi` dial. She presses the bare fingertips of her right hand flat against Wing A's cast-iron return leg, shifts them across to Wing B's return leg for two seconds, and straightens beside Soren at the manifold.

"Nine of Wing A's twelve tubs sit back on the inner masonry terrace wrapped in damp jute—heavy root-balls, old bark, enough mass to ride a short dip down to one degree," she says evenly. "Three cannot: the 1921 Bergamot and Citron mother-trees right under the north gable, autumn-pruned with high exposed graft unions. Below one degree Celsius, ice shears the cambium at the union, and grafted 1921 clones do not come back from seed. Across the corridor in Wing B, you have forty shallow wooden flats in dry starting mix—almost zero thermal mass. Shut their wheel off at minus nine outside and every flat freezes hard through the peat in half an hour."

She rests one fingertip on the spoked rim of `A — CITRUS` without turning it. "If four-point-five is your ceiling, put two-point-eight on Wing A to push past that point-four return lag to the north gable, and leave one-point-seven on Wing B's shorter loop until a twelve-forty check on both your glass and the corridor thermometers. Will your drum hold above two-seven on that bias, or does the condensate hang up?"

### Turn 4 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren shakes his head once, unhooks the brass valve wrench from his pocket, and taps the cap of Wing B's return trap with its jaw.

"Condensate hangs up. Choke Wing B to one-seven and you lose the head to crack the far bench trap—water pools dead in the return bends and sucks my drum onto the two-five cutout, while two-eight still won't push past your north-gable lag with a second branch open."

He steps back to the header and fits the wrench over the stem nut of `B — ALLOTMENT` without turning it yet. Keeping his eyes fixed on the `0–15 psi` brass dial, his left thumb rubs slowly across the dry white lime-scale crease on his index knuckle.

"This manifold gives us three settings before twelve-twenty, not a bias. Four-point-five full on Wing B with Wing A on drip-bleed—forty flats stay thawed and nine of your wrapped tubs ride their root mass. Four-point-five full on Wing A with Wing B on drip-bleed—your three 1921 trees hold heat and forty neighborhood benches freeze solid in thirty minutes. Or two-point-two even across both wheels till twelve-forty, pulling that glass down to two-seven—two millimeters off a dead fire—right along the frost line."

He looks up from the dial at Ines, hand steady on the brass wrench. "I don't shut off forty public allotment benches on a night attendant's signature alone. Name the cut—full Wing A, full Wing B, or the two-point-two split—and tell me you're signing the directive column upstairs when we climb at twelve-forty."

### Turn 5 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines's right hand rises to the corded brass grafting loupe against her coat; she pinches its bridge tightly between thumb and forefinger until the skin across her knuckle blanches white, holds for two ticks of the cooling return pipe, and lets the loupe drop.

She steps closer to the header and taps the `0–15 psi` dial glass once at two-point-two with a bare fingernail, leaving the wrench to Soren's hand.

"I am not freezing forty living seed flats at twelve-fifteen without an upstairs thermometer reading, and I will not put the 1921 Bergamot and Citron grafts on drip-bleed," she says, voice low and clipped. "Bring both wheels down to the two-point-two split until twelve-forty, and chalk your glass at two-point-seven. We climb at twelve-forty—or the instant the one-degree corridor bell rings. If Wing A touches one-point-zero or your drum slips below two-seven, full four-point-five goes to Citrus, and I will sign the curatorial priority column directly beside your recorded boiler figures in the book. Set the iron before we lose more water."

### Turn 6 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren eases the stem nut on `B — ALLOTMENT` with the brass wrench, grips the cold iron spokes in both hands, and cranks the wheel three full turns clockwise off its white full-draw notch. He moves straight across to `A — CITRUS` and brings its spokes down to the exact matching thread height until the heavy rush inside the black Y-header drops to a thin, tight hiss and the brass needle steadies on `2.2 psi`.

He hooks the wrench back into his pocket, lifts the chalk nub from its wire beside the stair, and steps under the cage bulb to draw a sharp white tick across the sight-tube at `2.7 cm`—barely two millimeters clear of the etched red `2.5 cm` cutout line. For a single beat his left thumb rubs the dry white lime-scale crease across his index knuckle while his eyes stay fixed on the water meniscus.

"Two-point-two split across Citrus and Allotment. Glass marked at two-seven." He drops the chalk back onto its wire hook and meets Ines's gaze under the lamp. "I stay on these traps till twelve-forty—or the second that one-degree bell clatters upstairs. Then we read both wings at the board and put ink in the book together."

## [Plot 2: 12:40 AM — Glasshouse Three Corridor](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/plot-2-corridor-bell.md)

**Place:** [Glasshouse Three Corridor](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/glasshouse-three-corridor.md)  
**Piece in Play:** [Conservatory Night Logbook](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/night-logbook.md)  
**Active Process:** [Feed-Line Icing and Frost Drop](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/feed-line-freeze.md)

*At curtain (`12:40 AM`), [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md) and [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md) stand in the `6.0°C (43°F)` limestone [Glasshouse Three Corridor](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/glasshouse-three-corridor.md) between the continuously chattering brass frost-drop bell on the paired wall thermometers (`1.0°C` / `0.8°C`), the latched interior glass partition doors looking into Wing A and Wing B, and the sloped oak lectern five paces from the vault stair head. On the lectern, the chained [Conservatory Night Logbook](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/night-logbook.md) lies open beside the tethered indelible black pen, visibly showing `11:00 PM — 3.6 cm / 6.0 psi | 7.5°C / 7.5°C | Routine winter draw | S.V.` above an unfilled `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` row and the blank `12:40 AM` alarm line.*

### Turn 1 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines reaches up to the thermometer board and snaps the manual brass silence latch across, cutting off the chatter of the alarm bell so only the dull knock of condensate in the floor trench sounds beneath the flagstones. She leans in close to both capillary tubes—`1.0°C` on `WING A — CITRUS`, `0.8°C` on `WING B — ALLOTMENT`—then steps to the left partition, presses her bare palm flat against the chilled glass, and looks past the nine jute-wrapped terrace tubs toward the rime feathering the north-gable panes above the three tall 1921 trees. Turning once toward the opposite glass where ice webs the outer mullions beside Wing B's shallow flats, she crosses to the sloped oak lectern and reaches for the tethered black pen.

Her hand stops over the open folio. She presses her bare index fingertip flat onto the empty `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` row directly beneath `11:00 PM — 3.6 cm / 6.0 psi | 7.5°C / 7.5°C`, then looks up at Soren.

"Wing A is sitting right on one-point-zero with rime on the north gable over the 1921 grafts, Wing B is already down at point-eight, and your return line is knocking under the floor—the split is spent," she says quietly. "Under our terms, full four-point-five goes to Citrus now, and I came up to sign beside your watch figures. Why is the eleven-thirty and midnight row blank between three-point-six centimeters at eleven o'clock and three-point-zero when I came down the stair—what happened in that hour, Soren, and what are you putting on that line before my name goes below it?"

### Turn 2 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren steps up to the sloped oak lectern, boots planted over the dull knock of the condensate trench. Keeping his eyes fixed on Ines's fingertip on the empty `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` row, his left thumb rubs slowly across the dry white lime-scale crease on his index knuckle.

"Eleven-thirty held steady. At eleven-forty-five the makeup feed fluttered, and I spent twenty minutes on the vault floor pulling slush out of the street strainer basket by hand before logging midnight or calling upstairs." He taps the `Drum / PSI` column once with a fingernail. "That dropped the glass point-six centimeters—three-six to three-zero. Ten minutes of dual cushion. Once the street main froze hard, four-point-five psi was the ceiling whether that drum started at three-six or full top nut. It never held two houses till four."

He shifts a step right, raps the capillary tube on `WING B — ALLOTMENT` at `0.8°C`, and rests his hand on the brass latch of Wing A's inner glass door.

"That knocking under the flagstones means my drum's on two-seven—the split is finished, and one branch shuts now. Put four-point-five on Allotment so forty flats in thin peat don't freeze through by one o'clock, and crack this latch to bleed six-degree corridor air toward your Wing A tubs." He looks straight at the tethered black pen under her hand. "Or if you're ordering Wing B cut off at point-eight for those three north-gable grafts, I write my eleven-forty-five strainer flush plain on the midnight row—and before I go down that stair, you write and sign in ink on twelve-forty that an iced city main limited this plant to one wing and you ordered Allotment shut down."

### Turn 3 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

Ines looks from Soren's hand on Wing A's brass door latch toward the tall silhouettes of the three 1921 trees under the frost-rimmed north gable, then shakes her head once.

"Six-degree hall air from an inner door will spend itself across the front terrace in twenty minutes—it will never carry to high graft unions at the north gable through four o'clock, and 1921 Bergamot and Citron clones cannot be regrown from seed." She presses her bare left palm flat against the open folio page and lifts the tethered black pen. "And ten minutes of strainer water at eleven-forty-five did not freeze the city main till four."

Bending over the sloped oak lectern, she writes steadily in black ink across the five `12:40 AM` columns, speaking the words low as the nib moves: "`12:40 AM` — `2.7 cm / 2.2 to 4.5 psi` — `1.0°C / 0.8°C` — `Iced municipal main caps plant at 4.5 psi (single wing) to 04:00; 2.2 psi split exhausted at frost bell; full 4.5 psi ordered to Wing A (1919–1928 accessioned citrus), Wing B to drip-bleed` — `Curatorial Priority Directive per Municipal-Herbarium Agreement, Ines`."

She lifts the nib, leaves half an inch of blank space at the right edge of the directive column, and holds the chained pen out handle-first above the empty `11:30 PM / 12:00 AM` row.

"Write your eleven-forty-five strainer flush and the three-point-zero glass on the midnight line above mine, put your initials beside my directive, and keep Wing A's door latched—if this corridor's six-degree air can save the front row of seed flats against the stone wall, crack Wing B's latch and get down that stair to open Citrus before that gable drops another tenth."

### Turn 4 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren takes the tethered black pen from Ines's hand, bends over the sloped oak lectern, and writes steadily across the blank row above hers, reading the entry aloud in a level watch voice as the nib moves:

"`11:45 PM` — `3.6 to 3.0 cm / 4.5 psi cap` — `no hall read` — `Street main iced; manual intake strainer slush-flush (-0.6 cm drum; 1-wing ceiling unchanged)` — `S.V.`"

He shifts the nib into the half-inch gap at the right edge of Ines's `12:40 AM` directive column, writes `/ Mech. execution — S.V.`, taps the page margin once with a fingernail, and lays the pen back in the lectern groove.

Stepping across to Wing B's inner glass door, he lifts the heavy brass latch off its catch and pulls the leaf half an inch ajar; cold, damp-peat air slips low across the limestone sill as the corridor's warmer draft draws inward toward the front seed flats. He walks five paces to the head of the vault stair, unhooks the brass valve wrench into his right fist, and stops with one boot on the top iron tread, looking back at Ines.

"That ink clears my ticket with the Superintendent at four. It doesn't face forty allotment families at the gate when those outer flats turn black." He raps the wrench jaw once against the iron rail. "I'm going down now to choke Allotment to drip-bleed and put four-point-five on Citrus before that trench knock trips my burner. When the city crew restores water at four and the allotment steward walks down this hall at first light—are you still standing behind that book, or am I reading your name off dry paper to an empty corridor?"

### Turn 5 — [Ines](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/ines.md)

With Wing B's glass door standing half an inch off its catch, Ines's right hand rises to the corded brass loupe at her chest; she pinches its bridge tightly between thumb and forefinger until the skin blanches white, then lets it drop against her buttoned wool coat.

She takes her pocket notebook from under her left arm, sets it flat on the sloped oak lectern beside the open logbook, and steps across to Wing B's ajar door, pressing her bare fingertips against the chilled glass beside the front row of wooden seed flats before looking back down the hall at Soren on the top iron tread.

"I am logging both capillary tubes from this lectern every fifteen minutes until the four o'clock crew opens the street main," she says quietly. "And when the allotment steward comes through the gate at first light, I will still be standing here to walk those forty benches row by row with them myself and answer for my ink in person. Go set the manifold, Soren—call up the stair when Citrus takes full steam."

### Turn 6 — [Soren](../02-company/greenhouse-frost/soren.md)

Soren looks once from her pocket notebook on the oak lectern to her bare fingertips against Wing B's chilled glass, then gives a single short nod, his left hand resting still at his side.

"Listen to the trench pipe under the flagstones," he says evenly. "Three knocks and a steady hiss means the north-gable trap cracked and Citrus has the four-point-five. Call down the grate if that tube slips before one."

He turns, shifts the brass wrench into his palm, and goes steadily down the narrow iron treads into the damp orange glow of the vault, the ring of his boots fading beneath the corridor floor.

*Curtain.*
