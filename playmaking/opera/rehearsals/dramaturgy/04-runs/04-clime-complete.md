### Stage Voice — Opening (`4:40 p.m., Late-October Friday`)

Inside `Barrowgate Market Hall` (`play/place_barrowgate_hall.md`), veiled pewter dusk flattens the clerestory glass while forty-eight millimetres of October rain in seventy minutes hammer the Welsh slate and glazed roof overhead. Fresh north wind pushes spray through the gable louvers and bellies the loading-arch tarpaulin onto Sawyer's Lane; crisp autumn air sits cool on wet iron and stone. Built in 1908 for gentle temperate drizzles and stamped above the north sump `BOROUGH SURVEY 1908 — TWIN 12-IN. CULVERTS — MAX RATED SHED 22 MM/HR`, the hall's sunken central York-stone aisle stands under four and a half inches of brown rainwater—half an inch shy of the three-inch east and west perimeter plinths and the dry south clerk's dais, and half an inch above the two painted flood marks on the fluted north pier (`14 OCT 2022 — 3.5 IN.` and `25 OCT 2024 — 4.0 IN.`). Fifty minutes remain before the 5:30 p.m. wholesale carrier check at the north arch where `The Saturday Trading Slate` (`play/piece_saturday_board.md`) and padlock key hang.

On timber duckboards beside Stall 4 in the flooded central aisle, **Tomasz Kowalski** (`play/persona_tomasz_kowalski.md`) heaves dairy crates onto a raised trestle with a rubber squeegee propped nearby. On the dry south clerk's dais beside the cast-iron weigh-table, **Priya Nair** (`play/persona_priya_nair.md`) stands before the open `Hall Works & Gauge Ledger` (`play/piece_works_ledger.md`). From the lifted north sump grate, **Maren Holt** (`play/persona_maren_holt.md`) wades south toward the weigh-table carrying her iron sump hook and the dripping, leaf-free brass strainer basket.

---

### Round 1 — Opening Turns (Cued by `Stage`)

**Turn 1 — Maren Holt (`Hall Superintendent`)**
Maren wades south through the four and a half inches of brown water, oilskin buttoned to the throat and iron sump hook in her left hand, steps onto the dry edge of the south dais, and sets the dripping, bare brass strainer basket down on the cast-iron weigh-table beside the open Ledger.

"Both north throats are clean to the iron, and forty-eight millimetres in seventy minutes is more than double what twin twelve-inch pipes can shed," she says flatly, resting her right thumb against the notched cabinet key on her belt ring. "Before I pull the central floor breaker on the east pier, whoever wants that gate open at six tomorrow can point to the blockage they think is holding this back."

**Turn 2 — Tomasz Kowalski (`Traders' Convenor`)**
Tomasz heaves a crate of farm dairy from the timber duckboards beside Stall 4 onto his raised trestle, splashes toward the east pier in his knee-high rubber boots with his shirt-sleeves rolled above his forearms, and wipes his chalk-dusted right palm flat across the dry bib of his butcher's apron.

"Fifty minutes to the five-thirty carrier cutoff puts twenty-eight stalls on the hook for spoiled Saturday stock, Maren, and what backed this aisle up isn't in that basket—Highways sealed Sawyer's Lane in impermeable tarmac this August and shot the whole hill straight through the arch. Pull the floor breaker if the boxes are wet, drop chiller leads off the overhead festoon circuit, and we squeegee these flags clean for six."

**Turn 3 — Priya Nair (`Market Trust Treasurer`)**
Priya shifts the open Ledger an inch clear of Maren's dripping strainer basket on the cast-iron weigh-table, unclips the two loose September circulars from the back board, and spreads them flat beside the 1908 blue linen culvert drawing and the August tarmac entry.

"Sawyer's Lane tarmac is logged here in August, Tomasz, right ahead of the September bulletin moving a forty-five-millimetre autumn hour from one-in-fifty odds to one-in-twelve and our insurer doubling the excess if we trade after floor inundation without a dry-conduit sign-off. Locking the gate drains the maintenance reserve in rent rebates like twenty-two, so before five-thirty we name the exact terms the Trust can afford and co-sign them: closed hall, raised plinths on overhead leads only, or a dry-box sign-off Maren is willing to certify."

---

### Round 2 — Response Turns (Reacting *by the Scene*)

**Turn 4 — Maren Holt (`Hall Superintendent`)**
Maren steps from the south dais onto the dry three-inch east plinth, walks to the electrical distribution cabinet on the east pier, unlocks the steel door with her notched belt key, snaps the central-aisle floor socket breaker off, and locks the cabinet again, pressing her right thumb hard into the notches of the key.

"Central floor power is dead and staying locked out, and overhead festoon lamp wiring isn't rated to haul dairy chillers slung across wet flags," she says flatly to Tomasz and Priya. "Nobody gets a dry-conduit sign-off before five-thirty while twin twelve-inch pipes built for twenty-two millimetres an hour sit four and a half inches under brown water, whether Highways tarmac sent it through the arch or the cloud did."

**Turn 5 — Tomasz Kowalski (`Traders' Convenor`)**
Tomasz splashes south to the dais, snatches the carpenter's chalk off the weigh-table ledge beside Priya's circulars, wades north through the four and a half inches of brown water under the roaring clerestory downpour to the fluted iron pier by the north sump, and chalks three brisk lines on the dry iron just above the floating sawdust ring: `2022 ROAD PLATES`, `2024 WEIR SLUICE`, `2026 LANE TARMAC`. Pivoting back toward the dais and east pier, he wipes his chalk-dusted right palm flat across the dry bib of his butcher's apron.

"Three council street-and-weir botches don't make a September odds sheet gospel, Priya, and central-aisle meat and dairy aren't eating cancelled four-thirty van dispatches so your dry east plinth can trade alone. Leave the floor breaker locked out like October twenty-four, and name one physical reason to wave off wholesale carriers at five-thirty tonight when Maren has until six tomorrow morning to unbolt a drained box on squeegeed flags."

**Turn 6 — Priya Nair (`Market Trust Treasurer`)**
Priya aligns the loose edges of the two September circulars flush with the brass corner of the open Ledger under both thumbs while the downpour hammers the clerestory glass overhead, then taps the inked balance column beside the mutual insurer's endorsement.

"Stall nineteen didn't write this endorsement, Tomasz—unlike twenty-four, trading tomorrow without Maren's dry-conduit sign-off doubles our excess past what the Trust reserve holds, whether council tarmac or one-in-twelve rain drowned that pipe. With central sockets locked out and festoon wiring under-rated for chillers, letting vans roll at five-thirty either leaves only dry plinths and unpowered tables trading at six or forces a dawn closure that wipes out our culvert fund in rent rebates."

Overhead, the roar on the clerestory roof glass slackens enough for the open north sump throat to begin pulling an audible vortex through the brown water. Outside the north loading bay, the wholesale carrier's horn sounds twice—twenty minutes to the 5:30 p.m. dispatch lock.
