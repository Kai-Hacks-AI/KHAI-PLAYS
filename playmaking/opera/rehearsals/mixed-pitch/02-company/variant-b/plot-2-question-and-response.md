# Plot 2: Question and Response

## Taxonomy

Middle Plot of [The Giant’s Shoe](play.md), tuned for this scene by [Quiet Wonder](pitch-quiet-wonder.md).

## Owner

KHAI-PLAYS

## Cue

Continuing directly from [Plot 1](plot-1-strained-crossing.md)’s closing stride near the dry central fountain in [Market Square](place-square.md), carrying forward the exact grip, pace, and inward crease of the [Giant Papier-Mâché Shoe](piece-shoe.md)’s heel seam. Stepping down from the loose rope barrier onto the white chalk marshaling edge beside the flank of the eight-foot boot, a child looks up at the shoe and asks verbatim: “Is the giant coming?”

## Action

Caught mid-step between the child’s earnest question at the chalk line and the softening heel counter between their hands, [Maren](persona-toe-carrier.md) and [Tomas](persona-heel-carrier.md) must respond—whether by speaking, shifting their physical handling of the boot, or staying visibly silent—without dropping the load or inventing a second float that does not exist. Their instincts collide wherever answering or pausing for the child pulls against keeping the failing heel moving toward the east arch.

## Stage

Maren at the toe welt and Tomas under the buckling rear counter hold the [Giant Papier-Mâché Shoe](piece-shoe.md) off the uneven flagstones beside the fountain plinth (three paces off-line), with the child standing alongside the chalk edge within conversational earshot. Fixed opening event: the child delivers the single exact line—“Is the giant coming?”—and speaks no further lines. Maren and Tomas play one short open exchange (`2–3` carrier turns, hard cap `3`), each free to speak, act without words, or stay silent until both have committed their response and immediate physical handling of the boot.

## Tension

Both carriers know the Third Ward workshop ran short of timber and built only this single left boot—no giant is coming behind them—while Tomas feels the damp cardboard taking thumb-pressure over his knuckles and Maren hears the east-arch brass band tightening toward marshaling lock-in.
