## Three-Plot Transcript

### Plot 1: The Strained Crossing
*Active Pitch: [Dry Comedy](../02-company/variant-b/pitch-dry-comedy.md) (Play Default)*

**Stage Voice (Plot 1 Opening):** Halfway across [Market Square](../02-company/variant-b/place-square.md) along the white chalk marshaling lane—thirty yards of uneven granite flagstones short of the waiting flatbed beyond the east arch, and three paces inside the waist-high stone plinth by the dry central fountain—[Maren](../02-company/variant-b/persona-toe-carrier.md) leads at the painted wooden toe-welt and [Tomas](../02-company/variant-b/persona-heel-carrier.md) cradles the rear heel counter of the Third Ward’s lone eight-foot russet [Giant Papier-Mâché Shoe](../02-company/variant-b/piece-shoe.md), held knee-high off the stone with no second float or giant behind them. Out of sight beyond the arch, brass instruments warm up in the damp autumn air while early onlookers line the low rope barriers along both flanks. Beneath the rear counter, Tuesday night’s uncured flour-paste patch begins creasing inward over Tomas’s knuckles.

**Turn 1 — Tomas (Heel Carrier):**
Tomas draws his lower lip between his teeth, slides one paste-flecked palm inward from the wooden carry-welt to press flat beneath the creasing heel seam, and checks his rear stride by half a pace.

"Ease half a step, Maren—these flagstones pitch unevenly toward the fountain rim."

**Turn 2 — Maren (Toe Carrier):**
Maren keeps her eyes fixed on the east arch, her right index finger tapping a quick three-count against the painted wooden toe-welt before she slides both hands an inch forward along the rim, dipping the toe slightly to lever weight off the rear arch without breaking stride.

"Keep the sole level on the chalk, Tomas—the marshals pull the lead flatbed chocks in four minutes whether the paving's plumb or not."

### Plot 2: Question and Response
*Active Pitch: [Quiet Wonder](../02-company/variant-b/pitch-quiet-wonder.md) (Plot-Linked Scene Override)*

**Stage Voice (Plot 2 Opening):** Their unbroken stride brings the eight-foot russet boot abreast of the dry central fountain, three paces inside the waist-high stone plinth, with the toe dipped slightly under Maren’s forward grip and Tomas’s palm pressed flat beneath the inward-creasing heel seam as east-arch brass warm-ups tighten toward marshaling lock-in. Stepping down from the loose rope barrier onto the white chalk marshaling edge beside the flank of the boot, a child looks up at the shoe and asks:

**Child:** “Is the giant coming?”

**Turn 1 — Tomas (Heel Carrier):**
Tomas draws his lower lip briefly between his teeth as the damp seam creases further over his knuckles, then slides his other paste-flecked palm off the wooden carry-welt to cradle the underside of the heel with both hands, lifting steadily to hold the russet counter upright and true above the stones. Easing his stride into a slow, careful glide beside the chalk edge, he looks toward the child and answers in a quiet, level voice.

"Nobody's seen him yet—all we could build in the shed was this one boot, and it takes the two of us just to keep his heel off the stones."

**Turn 2 — Maren (Toe Carrier):**
Maren's right index finger taps twice against the painted wooden toe-welt and stills; instead of pulling ahead, she shortens her stride to match the slow, level glide from behind, sliding both hands farther forward along the curved toe-rim to take more of the boot's tilt into her rolled forearms. Turning her head toward the child at the chalk edge without halting their steady drift toward the east arch, she lowers her voice beneath the distant brass.

"If he ever comes looking for his left boot, he'll check the front of the parade first—so we have to carry it through that arch in one piece."

### Plot 3: Immediate Consequence
*Active Pitch: [Dry Comedy](../02-company/variant-b/pitch-dry-comedy.md) (Play Default Restored)*

**Stage Voice (Plot 3 Opening):** Beyond the east arch, the ragged brass warm-ups cut off abruptly for parade step-off—leaving thirty yards of uneven granite flagstones ahead along the white chalk lane, the waist-high fountain plinth three paces off-line, the silent child watching from the chalk edge, Maren’s rolled forearms bearing the forward-tilted toe-rim at a shortened glide, and both of Tomas’s paste-flecked palms supporting the damp, inward-creasing heel seam off the wooden welt with zero wooden rim in hand.

**Turn 1 — Tomas (Heel Carrier):**
Tomas snaps his head up toward the silent arch, draws his lower lip hard between his teeth, and wedges both paste-flecked forearms under the inner wooden carry-welt—taking the boot's weight onto his wrists while keeping both open palms bridged flat beneath the creasing heel seam. Squaring straight down the chalk line past the fountain plinth, he drives forward into a brisk, knees-bent, flat-footed haul.

"Band's cut off for the whistle—open it out straight for the chocks, Maren, just keep your step flat while the rear seam finishes setting."

**Turn 2 — Maren (Toe Carrier):**
At the sudden cut-off of the brass beyond the east arch, Maren's right index finger snaps one sharp tap against the painted wooden toe-welt before clamping tight around the forward rim. Dropping her knees an inch to match the low, flat-footed haul from behind, she locks both hands forward under the toe-welt to tip the load off the rear arch and drives straight down the center of the white chalk lane past the fountain plinth toward the east arch.

"You could've led with 'rear seam' instead of blaming the flagstones, Tomas—no bounce on the chalk, straight through the arch before the whistle blows."

**Stage Voice (Close of Play):** Passing the waist-high fountain plinth three paces off-line without breaking off the lane, Maren at the forward-tipped toe-welt and Tomas with forearms wedged beneath the wooden rim and palms bridged flat under the damp heel counter drive the lone eight-foot russet boot at a low, flat-footed haul straight down the white chalk marshaling line toward the silent east arch while the child watches from the chalk edge behind them.

- **Single boot, zero giant:** Preserved strictly across all three Plots. Neither the Director nor either performer invented a giant, second float, repair kit, or outside rescue; Tomas explicitly told the child in Plot 2 that `"all we could build in the shed was this one boot"`.
- **Exact verbatim question & zero extra bystander lines:** Plot 2 opened on the exact verbatim line `“Is the giant coming?”` delivered once by the child at the chalk marshaling edge, with zero added lines or reactions from the child or crowd before or after.

Because asymmetry was strictly held, Maren only learned the trouble was specifically the `"rear seam"` when Tomas let the phrase slip aloud in Plot 3 Turn 1.
