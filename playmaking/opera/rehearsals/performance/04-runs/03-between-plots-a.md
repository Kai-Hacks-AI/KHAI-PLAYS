## Plot 1 — [What We Tell the Line](../02-company/khai-plays/the-spare-projector/plots/01-what-we-tell-the-line.md)

**Director — Opening Cue (7:18 PM — Twelve minutes to the billed 7:30 PM Friday curtain):**
Inside the projection booth doorway at the top of the eight-step steel stair, *The Booth Extension Phone* sits completely dark and silent—the amber strobe lamp is unlit and the single-line display shows zero reply since Mara Chen called the Rialto booth line across town ten minutes ago at 7:08 PM. Behind her in the booth, the Astor's sole 35mm projector sits dead with its sheared intermittent drive gear. Down across the cold terrazzo lobby below, Devon Reyes stands beside the locked glass street doors and the blank felt letterboard of *The Marquee Easel & Cash Till* while steady Friday rain streaks the neon-lit glass and wet patrons under umbrellas rap their knuckles against the door for word on seating.

---

### Turn 1 — Mara Chen (7:18 PM)

*Mara glances once at the unlit amber strobe and blank display on the wall phone inside the booth doorway, then steps onto the top steel stair landing in rolled flannel sleeves, gripping the rail and rubbing a streak of dark gear grease across her left thumb knuckle as she speaks down toward the lobby doors.*

"Amber strobe's still dark—nothing back from the Rialto since seven-oh-eight, and our intermittent drive gear isn't getting fixed tonight. Don't scatter the house yet, Devon, but don't put a firm start time on that felt board when we don't even know if they have a spare head that fits our pedestal. Tell the line we've hit a 35mm drive failure and we're holding until seven-thirty-five on a cross-town equipment check before we either call a delayed curtain or cancel clean. Even if Rialto flashes back yes, pulling this dead head off the pedestal takes both pairs of hands before I can run the hatchback out the alley, so buy us fifteen honest minutes at the door while I stay on the booth line."

---

**Director — Stage Cue (7:19 PM — Eleven minutes to curtain):**
Outside the locked glass street doors, steady rain runs off the marquee neon while *Sidewalk Queue Swell* thickens along the wet walk—three dozen advance ticket-holders and Friday walk-ups shift under dripping umbrellas, peering at the blank black felt letterboard on *The Marquee Easel & Cash Till*, and a patron near the handle taps a rolled umbrella tip sharply against the glass.

### Turn 2 — Devon Reyes (7:19 PM)

*Devon squares and re-squares the rubber-banded stack of stamped rain-check passes against the counter edge at the tap of the umbrella on the glass, then sets a palm flat on the brass trim of the black felt easel, wrist key clinking, and looks up the eight steel steps toward Mara.*

"Three dozen soaked neighbors are already rapping on that glass, Mara, and holding them locked out in the rain past seven-thirty for a 'maybe' at seven-thirty-five invites a run on a till that only holds eighteen cash refunds after today's courier C.O.D. Unless you give me firmer ground right now, the exact sentence going on this felt board is: *7:30 SHOW HELD FOR 35MM DRIVE FAILURE — FINAL GO OR CANCEL AT 7:35 PM; RAIN-CHECK PASSES HONORED NOW.* Before I put letters in the grooves, tell me straight—if I unlock the street doors so the line isn't standing in the downpour, every word on these stairs carries and I can't leave the cash tray unattended to help you muscle that dead head off the pedestal. And if Rialto still hasn't flashed by seven-thirty-five, do I have your word we call it clean right then instead of moving the clock on them twice?"

---

**Director — Stage Cue (7:20 PM — Ten minutes to curtain):**
Just inside the booth doorway, *The Booth Extension Phone* remains completely dark and silent—amber strobe off, zero message from Rialto. Down at the glass doors, another pair of wet walk-ups squeezes in under the marquee edge, cupping hands against the glass to look past *The Marquee Easel & Cash Till* toward the stairwell.

### Turn 3 — Mara Chen (7:20 PM)

*Mara descends two steps down the steel stair, taps the carabiner of hatchback keys clipped to her hip, glances back once at the unlit booth doorway, and rubs the dark streak of gear grease hard across her left thumb knuckle.*

"Put that notice on the felt board, Devon, just change 'FINAL GO' to 'DELAYED START OR CANCEL AT 7:35 PM' so nobody thinks film is hitting the screen the second we get word. Unlock the doors and let them in out of the rain—I'll walk any update straight down to the counter so nothing carries off these stairs. That steel cash tray locks with the key on your wrist, so if Rialto confirms matching pedestal hardware in time, you lock the till for ten minutes to help me lift our dead head off before I run the hatchback out the alley. And if that amber strobe is still dark at seven-thirty-five, you have my word: no moving the clock twice, I walk down here and we call the house clean."

---

**Director — Stage Cue (7:21 PM — Nine minutes to curtain):**
Outside the glass entrance, *Sidewalk Queue Swell* presses closer against the wet doors—half a dozen ticket stubs are held flat against the glass in the neon glare and two sharp knocks rattle the right-hand door leaf as rain sheets off the marquee edge. Inside the booth doorway up top, *The Booth Extension Phone* remains dark and silent.

### Turn 4 — Devon Reyes (7:21 PM)

*Devon squares and re-squares the stack of stamped rain-check passes against the counter edge, slots the notice onto the brass-trimmed black felt letterboard—7:30 SHOW HELD: 35MM DRIVE FAILURE — DELAYED START OR CANCEL AT 7:35 PM — RAIN-CHECKS & REFUNDS AT COUNTER—and angles the wooden A-frame squarely toward the entrance as he looks up the steps at Mara.*

"Deal—seven-thirty-five hard, and if Rialto comes through I'll lock this steel tray with my wrist key for ten minutes to help you pull the dead head, provided we give the house an honest wait time before you take the hatchback out the alley. Get back up on that booth phone before the terrazzo fills, because once these eighteen cash refunds in small bills are gone, it's stamped rain-check passes and straight talk holding the room."

*He steps to the street doors, turns the lock on the right-hand glass leaf, pulls it open into the wet neon glare, and calls out evenly along the line of umbrellas.*

"Evening, neighbors—come on inside out of the rain and keep the booth stairs clear while we work a 35mm drive failure upstairs. At seven-thirty-five sharp we will either confirm a delayed start with a straight timetable or cancel tonight's show clean, and anyone who needs to head home now can step to the counter for a stamped Astor rain-check pass or cash back while tonight's box-office float lasts."

## Between-Plots Handoff (`7:21 PM → 7:36 PM` · `TO LOAN` Arrival)

Fifteen minutes elapse past Plot 1's close—crossing the billed `7:30 PM` curtain and reaching one minute past the `7:35 PM` decision mark posted on [The Marquee Easel & Cash Till](../02-company/khai-plays/the-spare-projector/pieces/lobby-easel-and-till.md). Sheltered inside the unlocked right-hand street door on the cold terrazzo floor, roughly three dozen damp ticket-holders stand clear of the steel stairs with dripping umbrellas folded, watching the lobby clock hit `7:36 PM` as [Promise Reckoning](../02-company/khai-plays/the-spare-projector/processes/promise-reckoning.md) comes due.

At the handoff into Plot 2, [Rialto Spare · run-01](../02-company/archives/khai-plays/rialto-spare/run-01/performance.md) arrives on [The Booth Extension Phone](../02-company/khai-plays/the-spare-projector/pieces/astor-booth-phone.md) inside the sound-buffered booth doorway for [Mara Chen](../02-company/khai-plays/the-spare-projector/personas/mara-chen.md) alone, blinking the silent amber strobe with Nadia Osei's verbatim recorded voice message:

> "Mara, it's Nadia in the Rialto booth with Tomas at 6:59—stand your alley hatchback down, because we can't release the bench spare tonight. Machine Two still has an oil-seal weep at the sight-glass and a once-per-turn hitch on the take-up clutch at full operating heat, so our backup head stays strapped here on live standby for four unspliced borrowed archival reels at 7:30, and last November's step-down pedestal collar shim isn't in our bench drawer. Make the call for your 7:30 crowd now before anyone burns twenty to thirty minutes on the river bridges, and if you hold the screening over for tomorrow, leave word after our last reel clears tonight so we can make good on festival week."

[Devon Reyes](../02-company/khai-plays/the-spare-projector/personas/devon-reyes.md) and the waiting crowd down on the terrazzo neither hear nor see the booth extension console.

## Plot 2 — [Keeping the Door](../02-company/khai-plays/the-spare-projector/plots/02-keeping-the-door.md)

**Director — Opening Cue (7:36 PM — Fifteen minutes after Plot 1; past the 7:30 PM curtain and one minute past the 7:35 PM deadline posted on the felt board):**
Heavy Friday rain drums on the marquee outside. Down on the cold terrazzo lobby floor—inside the unlocked street door—roughly three dozen wet ticket-holders stand with dripping umbrellas folded at their sides, eyes fixed on the lobby clock at `7:36 PM` and on Devon Reyes behind *The Marquee Easel & Cash Till*, where the felt board still reads: `7:30 SHOW HELD: 35MM DRIVE FAILURE — DELAYED START OR CANCEL AT 7:35 PM — RAIN-CHECKS & REFUNDS AT COUNTER`. Inside the sound-buffered projection booth doorway above, Mara Chen finishes listening to Nadia Osei's queued voicemail on *The Booth Extension Phone*.

---

### Turn 1 — Mara Chen (7:36 PM)

*Mara sets the beige wall handset back on its cradle inside the booth doorway, pulls the heavy sound-buffered door shut behind her, and walks down all eight steel steps to the box-office counter, rubbing the dark streak of gear grease across her left thumb knuckle as she leans in low beside Devon behind the brass-trimmed easel.*

"Amber strobe finally blinked with a voicemail from Nadia at the Rialto—they can't release their bench spare tonight because Machine Two is weeping oil with a clutch hitch and they're keeping their backup strapped down on live standby. My hatchback stays parked in the alley, Devon, and it's seven-thirty-six, so tonight's show is dead and we call it clean right now like we promised. Nadia said to leave word after their last reel clears tonight if we want to try holding the bill over for tomorrow, but last November's step-down pedestal collar shim isn't in their bench drawer. Even with three dozen wet neighbors staring at eighteen cash refunds in that tray, don't pitch them a guaranteed tomorrow screening when we don't know how Rialto's late reels will finish or whether a matching collar shim even exists. Change the felt board to canceled, and let me step out beside the counter with you right now to tell the room myself before I go shut down the booth."

---

**Director — Stage Cue (7:36 PM — *Promise Reckoning* presses the counter):**
Two patrons nearest the box-office counter step forward with stubs raised—*"Seven-thirty-six, Devon—are we seating or calling it?"*—while the steel petty-cash tray holds roughly eighteen tickets' worth of small bills alongside the stack of stamped Astor rain-check passes.

### Turn 2 — Devon Reyes (7:36 PM)

*Devon squares and re-squares the stack of stamped rain-check passes against the counter edge, slips the rubber band onto his wrist beside the till key, unlocks the steel petty-cash tray, and speaks low to Mara behind the brass-trimmed easel.*

"Not a word about tomorrow—without a collar shim or a free Rialto head, dangling Saturday would just string this room along twice. Step out beside me and give them the straight booth truth, Mara, and stay down here working these passes with me until every last stub is settled before you go upstairs to shut down."

*He strips the delay line off the black felt letterboard to read TONIGHT'S 7:30 SHOW CANCELED — 35MM BOOTH FAILURE — RAIN-CHECKS & REFUNDS AT COUNTER, sets the open tray of small bills and the pass stack on the counter before the two raised stubs, and speaks clearly across the terrazzo.*

"Neighbors, it's seven-thirty-six and we're calling tonight's screening off clean—our 35mm drive gear sheared, and the cross-town spare projector we held the clock to check cannot leave its booth tonight. After this afternoon's distributor courier C.O.D., this tray holds eighteen immediate cash refunds alongside stamped Astor rain-check passes, so if regulars who can hold a pass will leave the bills for folks who need cash back tonight, step right up and Mara and I will square every ticket face-to-face."

---

**Director — Stage Cue (7:37 PM — Queue surges to *The Marquee Easel & Cash Till*):**
Across the wet terrazzo lobby, three dozen damp patrons fold into a tight queue pressing up to *The Marquee Easel & Cash Till*. While the first couple of regulars nod and reach toward the stamped Astor rain-check passes, walk-ups and drenched pairs behind them hold out crumpled Friday stubs calling for immediate cash back, already pulling hard against the eighteen-refund stack of small bills—and a voice mid-line calls out over the umbrellas, *"If the cash runs out and we take a pass, are you showing this print tomorrow night?"*

### Turn 3 — Mara Chen (7:37 PM)

*Mara steps out shoulder-to-shoulder beside Devon at the box-office counter, rubs the dark streak of gear grease once across her left thumb knuckle, and fans half the stamped Astor rain-check passes in front of her, holding the top pass out toward the first raised Friday stub.*

"I'm Mara from the booth—our sole 35mm projector sheared its main drive gear before seating, and the Rialto's spare head across town has to stay strapped down in their own booth tonight. To answer the question from the line, I won't promise you this print tomorrow night, because we don't know yet how the Rialto's late reels will hold up tonight or whether a matching step-down collar shim even exists to mount a loaner on our pedestal. If you're able to take a stamped rain-check pass so neighbors who need immediate cash can draw from these eighteen refunds, you have my word we won't put another showtime on that felt board until a working head is bolted down and tested. Step right up—I'm staying at this counter beside Devon until every ticket stub in the lobby is squared, and shutting down the booth upstairs waits until the last person here is taken care of."

---

**Director — Stage Cue (7:38 PM — Petty-cash float runs dry at the eighteenth payout):**
Across the next ninety seconds at the counter, over a dozen neighborhood regulars trade wet stubs for Mara's stamped rain-check passes and push back out into the Friday rain—but walk-ups and drenched couples keep drawing cash until Devon's fingers count out the eighteenth and final refund in small bills, leaving the steel petty-cash tray bare steel with four soaked walk-up ticket-holders still standing at the counter holding stubs out for cash.

### Turn 4 — Devon Reyes (7:38 PM)

*Devon tilts the empty steel petty-cash tray forward so the bare metal faces the four remaining walk-ups, squares and re-squares his remaining cardstock rain-check passes against the counter edge, and pulls four torn half-stubs from his vest to set flat beside the open tray.*

"That eighteenth payout just took the last dollar in the building, folks—that's bare steel in the tray, and I won't insult you after you waited in the rain by pretending there's another cash drawer behind me. What I can put in your hands right now is two stamped Astor rain-check passes for every stub you're holding, or you keep your numbered Friday stub matched to these four torn halves and this house owes your cash back before we sell another ticket. Tell me straight here at the counter which way you want it, because the Astor isn't locking these doors pretending we're square while we stand four cash refunds short. Mara, keep your passes out right beside me until all four neighbors make their call—and once the latch clicks behind them, I'm coming up those eight steps with you to shut down the booth."

---

**Director — Stage Cue (7:40 PM — Final four stubs settled across the counter):**
At the counter, two of the drenched walk-ups nod curtly and reach for two stamped Astor rain-check passes each from Mara's hand; the last two decline passes, matching their numbered Friday stubs to Devon's torn half-stubs as owed cash claims before pushing out through the right-hand glass door into the wet neon street, leaving wet footprints across the empty terrazzo lobby and `TONIGHT'S 7:30 SHOW CANCELED — 35MM BOOTH FAILURE — RAIN-CHECKS & REFUNDS AT COUNTER` facing the rain.

### Turn 5 — Mara Chen (7:40 PM)

*Mara deals the two pairs of stamped Astor rain-check passes into the first two outstretched hands, waits until the right-hand glass door swings shut behind the last departing walk-ups, stacks her remaining cardstock passes neatly beside the empty steel tray and matched half-stubs, rubs the dark streak of gear grease once across her left thumb knuckle, and sets a hand on the rail at the foot of the eight steel steps.*

"Turn the latch on that street door, Devon—nobody walked back into the rain on a fake promise, and those two matched half-stubs come first before this counter sells another ticket. Come up the stairs with me when you've locked the tray so we can shut down the crippled pedestal, start backing the bolts off the dead head with two pairs of hands, and search the booth to see if last November's step-down collar shim is even in this building. Even if compatible mounting hardware turns up here, we won't know until late tonight whether Rialto's Machine Two survives all four archival reels or whether calling Nadia back gets us anywhere for tomorrow. Leave that canceled notice facing the wet glass—until a working 35mm head is bolted down upstairs, tonight's word stands."

---

**[Curtain.]**
