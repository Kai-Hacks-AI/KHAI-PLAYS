## Plot 1: What We Tell the Line

**Opening Cue (7:18 PM — Twelve minutes to the billed 7:30 PM Friday curtain):**
Steady rain streaks the twin glass street doors of *The Astor Lobby & Booth Stairwell*. Outside under the dripping marquee, nearly fifty wet patrons in the growing Friday queue press umbrellas and advance stubs toward the locked glass; two sharp knuckle-raps hit the pane right beside the blank black felt board of *The Marquee Easel & Cash Till*, where roughly eighteen tickets' worth of small bills and the rubber-banded stack of stamped cardstock rain-check passes sit behind the counter. Twenty minutes ago (`6:58 PM`), the Astor's sole 35mm projector sheared its intermittent drive gear upstairs. Ten minutes ago (`7:08 PM`), Mara called across town to the Rialto Cinema booth asking after their spare 35mm unit. Inside the booth doorway at the top of the eight-step steel stair, *The Booth Extension Phone* and its amber strobe lamp sit completely dark and silent.

### Turn 1 — Devon Reyes
*Devon lifts an open palm with a quick "just a minute" nod toward the wet faces rapping on the street glass, turns back to the box-office counter to tap the rubber-banded stack of cardstock rain-check vouchers square against the edge, then rests a hand on the brass frame of the blank felt easel and looks up the steel stairs toward the booth doorway.*

"Mara, they're knocking on the glass now, and we're twelve minutes off a 7:30 curtain with fifty soaked neighbors standing in the rain. Tell me straight—has anything come back on that booth phone from Rialto yet, or are we still flying blind? Keep in mind I've only got about eighteen cash refunds left in this till after the courier C.O.D., plus the stamped rain-check passes. Unless you can give me firmer ground right now, the exact sentence going onto this board in two minutes is: *'Tonight's 7:30 screening is canceled due to mechanical failure—rain-check passes and box-office refunds at the window.'*"

### Turn 2 — Mara Chen
*Mara glances back over her shoulder at the unlit amber flasher inside the booth doorway, steps out onto the top of the steel stair landing, and rubs a streak of dark gear grease across the knuckle of her left thumb before gripping the rail.*

"The strobe's still dark—nothing from Rialto yet, and our intermittent gear is sheared through for the night. Don't kill a full 35mm house twelve minutes before curtain when their projectionist might just be mid-reel, especially when fifty people rushing your window at once will blow straight past eighteen cash refunds. Unlock the doors to get them out of the wet and put the truth on the felt: *'Main projector down—checking on a cross-town loaner; firm call at 7:35 PM, or claim a refund or rain-check now.'* That lets the impatient ones cash out cleanly while your till can cover them, and gives me fifteen minutes up here by the phone to unwire the dead head—though if Rialto comes through, it'll still take two sets of hands to lift it."

*She taps the carabiner of hatchback keys clipped to her belt once and holds her place at the top of the eight steps, looking down at Devon beside the easel.*

*(7:20 PM — Outside the glass doors, another cluster of walk-ups ducks under the dripping marquee, folding wet umbrellas and craning past the front rank to squint at the still-blank felt letterboard; someone presses an advance ticket stub flat against the glass beside the lock cylinder.)*

### Turn 3 — Devon Reyes
*Devon glances at the advance stub pressed flat against the wet glass, squares the rubber-banded stack of cardstock rain-check passes once against the counter edge, slips the key loop off his wrist to pull out the front-door key beside the plastic letter tray, and steps to the foot of the steel stairs.*

"A hard 7:35 cutoff lets the impatient folks peel off before fifty people rush an eighteen-ticket cash till at once, so I'm with you there—but before I unlock these doors and bring the line onto the terrazzo where every word on these stairs carries, give me the real math. Even if Rialto flashes yes in the next ten minutes, driving across town in Friday rain and mounting a loaner head means no reel hits the screen before 8:15, so nobody can walk in thinking 7:35 means curtain. And I can't leave a full lobby and cash box unattended to ride across town—you'd have to get Rialto's hand to load your hatchback on their end while I hold the house and duck out the alley door for two minutes to help you carry it up. Back me on that clock and that lift plan, and I'll put this exact promise on the felt and open the doors right now: *'Main 35mm unit down—awaiting loaner confirmation; firm go/no-go at 7:35 PM for ~8:15 start, or visit box office now for rain-check passes and cash refunds while register float lasts.'*"

*(7:21 PM — Inside the booth doorway, The Booth Extension Phone and its amber strobe remain completely dark and silent. Outside the glass doors below, rainwater runs down the neon reflection as another patron taps twice on the glass and points at their watch.)*

### Turn 4 — Mara Chen
*Mara comes two steps down the open steel stair, rubs the dark smear of gear grease across the knuckle of her left thumb, and looks past Devon toward the watch-tapping silhouette at the wet glass before meeting his eyes.*

"Make that board say *8:30*, not 8:15—getting across the river and back in Friday rain plus aligning a borrowed head and threading Reel 1 won't happen a minute sooner, and I'm not faking a clock we'll blow past. And I'll need more than a two-minute alley carry from you: if Rialto flashes back by 7:35 with a matching pedestal mount and a hand to load my hatchback on their end, you'll have to lock that cash tray for five minutes before I roll so we can lift our dead head off the studs together, and another five when I'm back to seat the loaner. I'm going inside right now to pull the power leads and loosen the base bolts while I stay right beside that extension. Put *firm go/no-go at 7:35 PM for an ~8:30 start* on the felt and unlock the doors—and if that strobe's still dark at 7:35 sharp, I'll come down these stairs myself and stand beside you when we call it."

*She touches the carabiner of hatchback keys at her hip, climbs back to the top landing, and steps inside the sound-buffered booth doorway beside the still-dark extension phone.*

*(7:22 PM — Umbrellas jostle up to the rain-streaked glass doors as more Friday ticket-holders press close beside The Marquee Easel & Cash Till.)*

### Turn 5 — Devon Reyes
*Devon nods once up toward the booth doorway.*

"Deal, Mara—8:30 on the board, cash tray locked for both five-minute lifts, and 7:35 sharp either way."

*He rapidly slots white letters across the black felt of the marquee easel—`35MM GEAR DOWN — LOANER CHECK IN PROGRESS | FIRM CALL 7:35 PM FOR ~8:30 START | RAIN-CHECKS & CASH REFUNDS (WHILE FLOAT LASTS) AT WINDOW`—sets the A-frame in clear view beside the counter, slips the rubber band off the stamped cardstock rain-check passes next to the petty-cash tray, then crosses to the twin glass doors, turns his key in the deadbolt, loops the key back onto his wrist, and pulls the street door wide to the rain.*

"Evening, everyone—come on inside onto the lobby floor out of the wet so I can give you the straight story up front. Our 35mm projector sheared a drive gear twenty minutes ago, and we're waiting on word from across town for a loaner unit: at 7:35 sharp we'll make a firm go-or-no-go call right here, with an 8:30 curtain if we have our loaner or a clean cancellation on the spot if not. If you'd rather call it a night now instead of waiting until 7:35, step right up to the counter for a stamped Astor rain-check pass or an immediate cash refund while tonight's register float holds out."

*Plot 1 Curtain — Locked at 7:22 PM upon public posting at The Marquee Easel & Cash Till and opening of the lobby street doors.*

- Upstairs inside the sound-buffered booth doorway—where Mara has pulled the power leads and loosened the base bolts on the dead 35mm head—the archived `TO LOAN` Performance [Rialto Spare · run-01](../02-company/archives/khai-plays/rialto-spare/run-01/performance.md) arrives on [The Booth Extension Phone](../02-company/khai-plays/the-spare-projector/pieces/astor-booth-phone.md). Make the call for your 7:30 crowd now before anyone burns twenty to thirty minutes on the river bridges, and if you hold the screening over for tomorrow, leave word after our last reel clears tonight so we can make good on festival week."

## Plot 2: Keeping the Door

**Opening Cue (7:36 PM — Past the `7:30 PM` billed curtain and one minute past the promised `7:35 PM` firm call):**
Heavy rain drums steadily on the marquee outside *The Astor Lobby & Booth Stairwell*. Across the cold terrazzo, roughly forty damp patrons stand crowded around *The Marquee Easel & Cash Till*; a patron in a wet raincoat near the front points from their wristwatch (`7:36 PM`) to `FIRM CALL 7:35 PM` on the felt board as Mara hangs up the booth extension handset inside the upper doorway.

### Turn 1 — Mara Chen
*Inside the booth doorway, Mara sets the scuffed beige handset back onto its wall hook beside the blinking amber lamp, rubs a dark streak of gear grease across the knuckle of her left thumb, unclips the alley hatchback keys from her belt carabiner to shove them into her pocket, and pulls the heavy booth door shut behind her. She walks briskly down all eight steel steps, moves in close beside Devon at the box-office counter, and rests one grease-marked hand on the brass edge of the marquee easel, pitching her voice low between the two of them.*

"Keep your voice close a second—a 6:59 voicemail from Nadia at Rialto just now landed on the booth console, and tonight's dead. Their Machine Two has an oil-seal weep and a hitching take-up clutch on four unspliced archival reels, so their spare head stays strapped on live standby all night, and last November's step-down pedestal collar shim isn't in their bench drawer either. She said to leave word after their last reel clears tonight if we try holding the screening over for tomorrow, so don't promise this room a Saturday show while that collar shim is missing. Tell me how much cash is left in your tray after the early walkouts—it's 7:36, I'm ready to pull *8:30* off this board, and I'll stand right here beside you to call it straight while you work the rain-checks and till."

### Turn 2 — Devon Reyes
*Devon squares and re-squares the stack of stamped cardstock rain-check vouchers sharply against the counter edge, lifts a brief acknowledging hand toward the patron pointing at their wristwatch, and leans in close beside Mara at the brass frame of the easel, keeping his voice low.*

"Six took cash and four took passes—I've only got twelve tickets' worth of small bills left in this tray for forty wet people, and if they think we stalled them, all forty will demand cash at once. Strip *8:30* off that felt right now and tell them straight why Rialto's spare couldn't leave their booth—with zero promises on Saturday—so they know we didn't string them along."

*He turns his wrist key in the steel petty-cash tray to pop the lid, sets the open stack of stamped rain-check passes squarely on the counter ledge, and steps forward to speak clearly across the terrazzo to the waiting crowd.*

"Folks, thank you for holding with us until 7:35—word just landed on our booth line from across town, and tonight's 35mm screening is officially canceled, with no reschedule date confirmed yet. I want to be completely straight with you before we form a line: after an afternoon courier C.O.D., I have twelve immediate cash refunds left in tonight's register alongside a full stack of stamped Astor rain-check passes. If regulars who know us can take a rain-check pass so those twelve cash refunds go first to folks who need cash back tonight, come right up to the window—and Mara's standing right here from the booth to tell you plainly what happened."

*(7:37 PM — A low groan moves across the forty damp patrons on the terrazzo. Half a dozen regulars near the middle nod and step into single file toward the rain-check stack, but a tight knot of wet walk-ups at the front edges straight toward the open cash tray—fifteen or sixteen hands already lifting damp stubs for cash while eyes lock onto `LOANER CHECK IN PROGRESS | FIRM CALL 7:35 PM FOR ~8:30 START` still sitting on the felt easel beside Mara's hand.)*

### Turn 3 — Mara Chen
*Mara sweeps her hand across the black felt of the easel to strip `LOANER CHECK IN PROGRESS | FIRM CALL 7:35 PM FOR ~8:30 START` clean off the board and drops the loose white letters into the plastic tray, leaving `35MM GEAR DOWN` above `RAIN-CHECKS & CASH REFUNDS (WHILE FLOAT LASTS) AT WINDOW`. She rubs the dark streak of gear grease once across the knuckle of her left thumb, picks up half the open stack of stamped cardstock rain-check passes from the counter ledge, and steps out beside the brass A-frame to face the knot of raised ticket stubs head-on.*

"Nobody strung you along—our main projector sheared its intermittent gear right before seven, I called the Rialto booth across the river for their bench spare, and their voicemail telling us to stand down only hit our booth extension a minute ago. Their second machine is fighting a weeping oil seal and a hitching take-up clutch on four unspliced archival reels tonight, so their backup stays strapped down, and the pedestal collar shim we'd need to mount it is missing from their bench anyway. I won't promise you a Saturday makeup screening while that shim isn't in hand—we'll only post a new date if the hardware actually checks out after Rialto's final reel clears tonight. Devon has twelve cash refunds in that tray and we've got stamped Astor rain-checks good for any show right here—if three or four of you up front can take a pass from me at this end of the counter so walk-ups who need cash tonight are covered, step right over, and I'm staying on this floor until every stub is settled before I go upstairs to box the print."

*(7:38 PM — Hearing the specific Rialto breakdown and seeing Mara step out grease-stained with half the voucher stack, four regulars near the front lower their hands from the cash cluster and step sideways to Mara's end of the counter for passes, splitting the forty damp patrons into two moving lines—a dozen walk-ups pressing damp stubs to Devon's open steel petty-cash tray while twenty-eight queue along Mara's end and the counter for stamped Astor rain-checks before pushing back out into the rain.)*

### Turn 4 — Devon Reyes
*Devon pulls a handful of white letters from the tray to slot `TONIGHT CANCELED` onto the black felt just beneath `35MM GEAR DOWN`, squares his half of the stamped cardstock rain-check passes at his elbow, and begins taking damp ticket stubs into his vest pocket while counting small bills briskly out of the open steel petty-cash tray into waiting hands.*

"Appreciate you regulars stepping over to Mara's end for passes—come right up to my window if you're in this front dozen for cash, I've got the bills in the tray to cover every stub here. Here's your refund, folks, and our real apologies for bringing you out in the rain—anyone right behind these twelve cash refunds can grab a stamped Astor rain-check pass good for any show from Mara or right here from my stack."

*Counting out the remaining small bills down toward the steel bottom of the tray as the twin lines move past the counter toward the wet street doors, he glances sideways to Mara beside the easel.*

"Coming down those stairs with the straight booth facts saved this floor, Mara—those four regulars peeling off up front kept this drawer from coming up short. Let's get every last stub settled square, then I'll lock the glass, kill the marquee neon, and come upstairs to help you box the print before you ring Nadia back."

*(7:41 PM — Devon's petty-cash tray sits emptied down to bare steel after the twelfth walk-up pockets their bills; the last two damp patrons at Mara's end of the counter hold out their stubs for stamped rain-check passes as umbrellas pop open outside under the wet marquee and the lobby terrazzo clears.)*

### Turn 5 — Mara Chen
*Mara takes the last two damp stubs offered at the corner of the counter, presses two stamped cardstock rain-check passes into the waiting hands, and sets her remaining passes and collected stubs neatly beside Devon's bare steel cash tray.*

"Here are your rain-checks—thank you both for sticking with us in the wet, and get home safe. Putting a hard 7:35 cutoff on that board is what held this floor together, Devon—nobody walked out that door thinking the Astor lied to them. Lock the glass and kill the marquee neon once the threshold clears; I'm heading up now to cap the loose power leads on the dead head and open the shipping cans. Come on up when the front is bolted so we can wind the print down together and wait out Rialto's last reel—either Nadia turns up that collar shim and a mounting path tonight, or we pack the cans back clean."

*Wiping the dark streak of gear grease from her left thumb knuckle onto her rolled flannel cuff, she turns from the rain-streaked glass doors and climbs the eight steel steps back toward the booth doorway.*

*Final Curtain — 7:41 PM.*
