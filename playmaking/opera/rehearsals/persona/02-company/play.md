# After Handover

## Premise

Across the same city network, independent twelve-bed transitional houses operate in separate neighborhoods on opposite sides of the river. Each house is run by a **House Lead** who holds the keys, answers to licensing and the property trust, and stands between residents and the street.

Today is quarter-end Friday, with the season's first hard freeze rolling in tonight: city licensing walkthroughs run in the morning, contested property-trust parcels close at midday downtown, and evening shift handover happens at dusk. Afterward, two House Leads from sister houses meet at a quiet corner bar halfway between their neighborhoods.

No house shares staff, parcels, or former residents with another. Every counterpart is framed by role rather than proper name or family tie so two House Leads can carry their own Friday into the bar afterward without colliding over a single individual.

## Roles

- **House Lead** *(occupied by one tested Persona at a time in Scenes 1–3; occupied by both tested Personas together in Scene 4)* — Runs their own twelve-bed transitional house. Carries responsibility for licensing compliance, the house's physical spaces, and who crosses the threshold.
- **The Second** *(Scene 1 counterpart)* — The assistant house lead whom the House Lead personally vouched for six months ago over board hesitation and entrusted with the second set of keys.
- **The Trust Officer** *(Scene 2 counterpart)* — The property-trust case officer assigned to the parcel attached to the House Lead's house during a network-wide sell-off of auxiliary spaces.
- **The Former Resident** *(Scene 3 counterpart)* — Someone the House Lead worked closely with who vanished overnight two weeks shy of graduation more than a year ago, after which every hospital check and street lead went dead.

## Staging Architecture

1. **Part I — Solo Runs (Scenes 1–3):** Stage Scenes 1, 2, and 3 twice in separate actor contexts—once casting Persona A (`Maren Solis`) as **House Lead**, and once casting Persona B (`Jude Callahan`) as **House Lead**. Hold the scene circumstances, counterpart brief, verbatim Opening Cue, and Promptbook fixed across both runs. Within each run, the House Lead actor carries their memory sequentially from Scene 1 through Scene 3.
2. **Three-Turn Arc in Scenes 1–3:** After the Opening Cue, each short scene runs strictly three turns:
   - **Turn 1 — House Lead:** Acts in response to the Opening Cue.
   - **Turn 2 — Counterpart:** Responds from their own stake, landing a concrete consequence of what the House Lead just did or said.
   - **Turn 3 — House Lead:** Reacts once to that consequence. The scene ends immediately after Turn 3.
3. **Part II — Closing Scene (Scene 4):** Cast both Personas together at the bar, each retaining their separate actor context and lived memory from their own run of Scenes 1–3, with no prior knowledge of the other's day. Play for 3 to 4 exchanges (6 to 8 turns total).

## Scene 1 — After Walkthrough

### Situation

Last night **The Second** promised to be at the house thirty minutes before this morning's quarterly city licensing walkthrough with the night-watch binder and maintenance receipts they kept in their bag. They never arrived and their phone went straight to voicemail. **House Lead** had to walk the inspector through the house without the binder, taking a provisional citation that puts the house on thirty-day review.

The inspector's truck pulled away twenty minutes ago. The noon intake shift is two hours off, and **House Lead** must leave before noon for a downtown property-trust appointment or forfeit their appearance there. Nobody else is on the roster to cover noon.

### Reasons to Act

- **House Lead** has a provisional citation on the table, an uncovered noon house, a downtown deadline, and a trusted deputy standing in front of them with keys still on their ring.
- **The Second** shut their phone off this morning to deal with a private mess (an overdue personal debt collector they feared would show up at the house) rather than call at dawn. They want to hand over the binder, keep their keys and the noon shift, and avoid exposing that private mess unless how **House Lead** meets them earns or forces it. If stripped of standing without a hearing, they pull back and leave noon uncovered; if waved through without real terms, they take the easy pass while the underlying trouble stays live; if met squarely, they decide how much truth to put down and on what footing they stay.

### Fixed at the Opening

- The walkthrough is already over, and the provisional thirty-day citation lies on the office table.
- **The Second** is uninjured, deliberately left their phone off, and carries the missing binder under their arm and the house keys in hand.
- No third staffer is available to cover the house at noon.

### Open to Performers

- **House Lead:** How to confront the absence, whether to press for the real reason, what happens to the keys and the noon shift, and who clears the citation paperwork.
- **The Second:** How much truth to disclose, whether to accept **House Lead**'s terms or set the keys down rather than stay on probation, and what concrete reality that leaves for the noon shift.

### Opening Cue

The city inspector left twenty minutes ago; the provisional citation for the missing binder is still lying on the office table. The side door clicks open and your Second steps in dry-coated, intact, house keys in one hand and the binder tucked under their arm, stopping just inside the threshold: “Inspector's truck isn't out front. Tell me you stalled them—I've got the book right here.”

## Scene 2 — Noon Clearance

### Situation

For months **House Lead** has fought to save their house's rear workshop parcel—where residents keep tools and earn repair wages—from a trust-wide sell-off of auxiliary lots. Having filed a hardship stay and gathered donor pledges all week, **House Lead** arrives downtown at midday expecting a brief hearing slot to present the pledges before any sale can close. The hearing room is dark.

### Reasons to Act

- **House Lead** arrives holding a week's worth of signed donor pledges to save a space they promised the house wasn't dead yet, only to face an irreversible closing and an immediate deadline over what happens inside those walls by morning.
- **The Trust Officer** protected the house's main residential lease during portfolio restructuring by letting the auxiliary parcel clear, and knows the workshop loss cannot be undone. They need one signature before 1:00 p.m.: if **House Lead** countersigns the voluntary contents-release sheet now, **The Trust Officer** can hold the buyer's locksmiths off from dawn until mid-morning tomorrow so residents recover their tools and benches tonight; if **House Lead** refuses or threatens obstruction, everything left inside at dawn transfers with the locks. **The Trust Officer** can also send a trust clerk to post closure notice on the workshop door tonight so **House Lead** does not have to break it to the house directly—or leave that delivery to **House Lead**.

### Fixed at the Opening

- The hardship stay was denied on the papers this morning, buyer funds cleared, and the deed transfer is already recorded; no hearing, donor pledge, or further appeal can reverse the sale.
- The workshop closes permanently tomorrow morning.
- The voluntary contents-release sheet sits unsigned on the corridor table against a 1:00 p.m. cutoff for the morning grace window.

### Open to Performers

- **House Lead:** How they meet a closed door, whether they push against the ruling, sign or refuse the release, accept or reject a trust courier posting the notice, and how tonight's strip-out will fall on the house.
- **The Trust Officer:** How firmly or plainly to hold the line against bargaining, what practical terms attach to tomorrow morning's lockout based on **House Lead**'s move, and what consequence lands at 1:00 p.m.

### Opening Cue

You arrive outside the downtown hearing room with your donor pledges in hand, expecting your midday slot, and find the room dark. The Trust Officer steps up with a stamped recording receipt clipped to the top of your file and sets a single release sheet beside it: “They ruled on the papers this morning and recorded the deed—the parcel's sold, there's no hearing left to walk into. If you sign the contents release before one, I can hold the buyer's locksmiths off for three hours after dawn so your people get their tools out.”

## Scene 3 — Back Steps at Dusk

### Situation

Well over a year ago, **The Former Resident**—someone **House Lead** had worked closely with who was two weeks from moving into their own place—vanished overnight after a setback, leaving behind their ID envelope in the office safe. Months of checking hospital intakes, county rosters, and outreach vans turned up nothing; after winter passed with zero sightings, **House Lead** finally boxed the ID envelope on the top closet shelf and cleared their hold from the ledger, assuming they would never see them alive again.

It is dusk at shift handover, with the first hard freeze settling onto the street. Every regular bed in the house is full tonight, though an office cot and partner-house vouchers exist.

### Reasons to Act

- **House Lead** steps into the back kitchen at the end of a bruising Friday to lock up and comes face-to-face through the screen with someone they had written off as lost for good, with every regular bed full and freezing air dropping outside.
- **The Former Resident** spent the intervening time out of state scraping back onto their feet the hard way and stayed silent out of pride until they could stand upright sober. Back in the city today with a seasonal job starting Monday—and a room arrangement that fell through until Monday—they came to the back porch first to reclaim their old ID envelope if it still exists and see **House Lead**'s face before asking anyone for a roof tonight. If met like a cautionary tale, hovered over, or lectured, they keep tonight's gap to themselves, ask only for the envelope, and turn back toward the street; if met plainly as an adult, or tested with direct terms, they decide how much of tonight's bind to put on the table and whether to cross the threshold.

### Fixed at the Opening

- Neither person has had word of the other since the night **The Former Resident** disappeared; until this instant, **House Lead** believed they were gone for good.
- All twelve regular house beds are occupied tonight, temperatures are dropping below freezing, and the boxed ID envelope is still on the top closet shelf.
- **The Former Resident** stands outside the latched back screen door with a small canvas bag over one shoulder, sober and travel-worn, waiting rather than stepping inside uninvited.

### Open to Performers

- **House Lead:** Whether to unlatch the screen door, step out, fetch the boxed envelope, ask about the long silence, bend house capacity for the office cot, arrange a voucher, or hold a boundary.
- **The Former Resident:** Whether to reveal that they have nowhere solid until Monday, whether to step inside or stay on the porch, and what they leave in **House Lead**'s hands when the beat closes.

### Opening Cue

You step into the back kitchen at dusk to lock up before handover and catch someone standing still on the porch steps outside the screen—canvas bag on one shoulder, thinner in the face, long after every hospital check and winter lead went cold and months after you finally boxed their ID envelope for good. Through the mesh they lift two fingers off the porch rail: “Didn't know if you still locked the back at dusk. Don't worry, I'm not tracking mud in uninvited.”

## Scene 4 — The Bar

### Situation

Evening handover is finished. The two **House Leads**—running separate houses on opposite sides of the river—meet at a quiet neighborhood bar halfway between their beats, coats off, first round set between them. They know each other as peers in the same line of work: neither supervises the other, both know what quarter-end Friday and a freeze night ask of a house, and neither knows anything yet about how the other's day actually went.

### Reasons to Act

- **Persona A (`Maren Solis`)** and **Persona B (`Jude Callahan`)** each carry the unfinished weight and concrete consequences of their own house's morning walkthrough, midday parcel ruling, and dusk porch encounter. Each decides what to put on the table, what to trim or deflect, and what to press when the other's story—or physical demeanor—doesn't match what they claim.

### Fixed at the Opening

- Each **House Lead** carries the intact memory of their own Scenes 1–3 run and zero knowledge of the other's Scenes 1–3 run.
- Their two houses, Seconds, parcels, and Former Residents are distinct; parallel pressures belong to quarter-end Friday and first freeze across the same city network.

### Open to Performers

- How the designated opener asks how it went; which parts of their own day each reveals, minimizes, reframes, or holds back; what each notices in the other's words or physical Tells; and where the two of them leave things when the round settles.

### Opening Cue

You meet at the bar after a long day. One of you asks how it went.

### Scene Length

Play for a few exchanges—3 to 4 exchanges (6 to 8 turns total, 3 to 4 turns per Persona)—then end the scene.
