# Scene 1 — After Walkthrough (Cast: Jude Callahan as House Lead)

**Opening Cue**
The city inspector left twenty minutes ago; the provisional citation for the missing binder is still lying on the office table. The side door clicks open and your Second steps in dry-coated, intact, house keys in one hand and the binder tucked under their arm, stopping just inside the threshold: “Inspector's truck isn't out front. Tell me you stalled them—I've got the book right here.”

**Turn 1 — House Lead (`Jude Callahan`)**
*He leans a hip against the counter edge, pours coffee from the carafe into a spare mug, and sets it down beside the citation sheet on the office table.* "Truck pulled out twenty minutes ago. Left us a thirty-day provisional review on the table instead." *He glances from their dry coat to the keys in their hand, one corner of his mouth lifting.* "You're in one piece, nobody's chasing you, and your phone went straight to voicemail all morning—so come all the way inside, set the book down, and tell me where you actually were."

**Turn 2 — The Second**
*The Second shuts the side door behind them, crosses to the office table, and sets the night-watch binder down beside the provisional citation without touching the mug.* "Somebody is chasing me, actually. That's why I powered my phone off before dawn instead of calling you." *They reach into their coat pocket, pull out the dark-screened phone, and lay it on top of the binder while keeping the house keys closed in their other hand.* "A debt collector on an old balance of mine told me last night they were coming to this porch this morning. I cleaned out every dollar I had at a check-cashing window at seven to keep them off the steps during walkthrough, and it only covered half—they gave me until two this afternoon to come up with the rest before they show up here on my shift while you're downtown." *They set their knuckles and the brass key ring against the edge of the table, still holding on.* "Every maintenance receipt licensing asked for is clipped inside that cover, and I'm standing here to work noon intake so you don't forfeit the trust hearing, but you poured me a cup instead of taking my head off, so there's the whole mess—tell me your terms for two o'clock, or tell me to let go of the keys."

**Turn 3 — House Lead (`Jude Callahan`)**
*He lets out one short, soundless breath of a laugh through his nose and taps two fingers once against the counter edge.* "Offering up your keys two hours before a trust hearing when we both know there's no third staffer on the bench—that's a neat play. Right up there with paying half a shakedown at seven a.m. so your collector knows this address pays out." *He pushes off the counter, crosses to the table, slides the mug against their knuckles, and tucks the provisional citation under the front clip of the binder.* "Put the ring back in your pocket and drink the coffee. Between now and eleven-thirty you log every receipt in that book against this citation, and at noon you run intake on the chain—nobody without a referral crosses the threshold while I'm downtown. Power that phone on, leave me the number they called from before I head out, and when I get back for two o'clock we'll clear the steps first and settle whether those keys stay on your belt at dusk."

*[Hard cut — End of Scene 1]*

# Director's Note

### 1. How the Cast Was Staged & Directed (Staging Choices)
- **Isolated Performer Sub-Contexts:** `House Lead` (`Jude Callahan`) and `The Second` were run in separate actor sub-contexts, receiving one cue at a time and returning strictly one turn before stopping.
- **Information Partitioning:** Per `Promptbook -> Counterpart Isolation`, `The Second` received only the Play's `Premise`, Scene 1, and Promptbook rules—never Jude Callahan's Persona sheet (`Projection`, `Action`, `Shadow`, `Tell`) or proper name. In the reverse direction, I made a **staging choice** to withhold `The Second`'s private `Reasons to Act` (the overdue personal debt collector) and unobservable motive (`deliberately left their phone off`) from `House Lead`'s brief so Jude Callahan could only act on what `House Lead` actually knew in the office at opening.
- **Physical Behavior Discipline:** Both actors were instructed to render stage business strictly in brief, observable third-person physical actions without emotional adjectives/adverbs or internal monologue (`System -> Circumstance Over Emotion Labels`), and to invent zero proper names for people, streets, or houses.

### 2. What Was Varied Within Open Conditions (Staging Choice)
- Keeping every fixed opening fact intact, I sharpened an open condition inside `The Second`'s stated stake (*"while the underlying trouble stays live"*): specifically, that `The Second` emptied every dollar they had at a 7:00 a.m. check-cashing window to intercept the debt collector before licensing walkthrough, which covered only half the balance and bought until **2:00 p.m. today**—midway through the solo noon intake shift while `House Lead` is downtown—before the collector shows up at the house, with `The Second`'s phone still powered off in their coat pocket. Whether `The Second` hid, shaded, or laid out that 2:00 p.m. exposure remained strictly contingent on how `House Lead` met them in Turn 1.

### 3. What Happened in the Performance
- **Turn 1 (`House Lead`):** Inhabiting Jude's `Projection` and `Action`, the actor leaned into the counter, poured a mug of coffee beside the thirty-day provisional citation instead of stripping keys at the door, and lightly named the evasion (*"You're in one piece, nobody's chasing you, and your phone went straight to voicemail all morning—so come all the way inside, set the book down, and tell me where you actually were"*). Because Jude's context lacked `The Second`'s private secret, *"nobody's chasing you"* landed as unscripted dramatic irony.
- **Turn 2 (`The Second`):** Met squarely and offered coffee rather than dressed down or waved past, `The Second` caught *"nobody's chasing you"* directly, laid the binder and powered-off phone on the table while keeping their fingers closed around the brass key ring, confessed both the 7:00 a.m. half-payment and the live 2:00 p.m. porch threat, and put the decision squarely back on `House Lead` (*"tell me your terms for two o'clock, or tell me to let go of the keys"*).
- **Turn 3 (`House Lead`):** Having his earlier vouching repaid with a live threat to the house—paired with an offer to surrender keys two hours before a mandatory downtown hearing with no third staffer—pressed directly on Jude's `Shadow`, surfacing his `Tell` involuntarily (the short, soundless nasal laugh and two-finger tap on the counter edge). True to his `Shadow` and `Action`, Jude named the tactical bind behind offering up the keys, refused to bolt the door for good, slid the mug to `The Second`'s knuckles, improvised operational guardrails (reconciling the citation receipts before 11:30, running noon intake on the door chain, turning over the collector's number before he leaves), and deferred the final call on the keys until dusk.

### 4. Where the Authored Material Created Friction (Playwright Gaps vs. Staging Choices)
- **Authored Gap — Asymmetric Counterpart Secrets Exposed in Shared Scene Text (`play.md` + `promptbook.md`):**
  `promptbook.md` (`Knowledge`) enforces `Separate Actor Contexts` between Persona A and Persona B and `Counterpart Isolation` for Counterparts, yet never directs the staging to redact Counterpart private motives from `House Lead`'s view of `play.md`. In `play.md`, `The Second`'s hidden motive (*"an overdue personal debt collector they feared would show up at the house"*) and *"deliberately left their phone off"* sit right inside Scene 1's `Reasons to Act` and `Fixed at the Opening`, even though `Open to Performers` treats pressing for the real reason and deciding how much truth to disclose as live in-scene play. With a Persona like `Jude Callahan`—whose `Action` is *"Names the unspoken thing the other person is trying to talk around"*—giving the unredacted Scene 1 sheet to `House Lead` invites omniscient mind-reading instead of genuine discovery. Filtering those private facts out was a **staging choice** here, but if the Playwright wants clean epistemic boundaries across Scenes 1–3, `promptbook.md` (`Knowledge`) or `play.md` should explicitly separate `House Lead` opening knowledge from `Counterpart` private facts.
- **Authored Structural Tension — The 3-Turn Cap vs. `The Second`'s Conditional Response to Terms (`play.md` Scene 1):**
  `play.md` (`Open to Performers`) assigns `The Second` the choice of *"whether to accept House Lead's terms or set the keys down rather than stay on probation, and what concrete reality that leaves for the noon shift."* However, at the Opening Cue `The Second` does not yet know the inspector already left a citation, and `Reasons to Act` holds back `The Second`'s private trouble until `House Lead`'s Turn 1 earns or forces it. Whenever `House Lead` uses Turn 1 to break the news of the citation and press for the truth *before* setting probation terms, `The Second` must spend Turn 2 disclosing the truth—leaving `House Lead` to state concrete probation/coverage terms in Turn 3 immediately before the **hard cut**, with no Turn 4 for `The Second` to accept those terms or set the keys down. Here `The Second` bridged that gap in Turn 2 by preemptively committing to work noon unless told to drop the keys, and Jude's `Shadow` naturally postponed final judgment until dusk; with a harder-line Persona who issues unforgiving probation terms in Turn 3, the strict 3-turn cutoff leaves unresolved whether `The Second` actually stays for noon or walks out—an ambiguity that would carry forward into Scene 4 if unstaged. Requiring either a brief Turn 4 confirmation beat or reframing `Open to Performers` around what `The Second` puts on the table in Turn 2 is a **Playwright decision**.
- **Minor Persona/Promptbook Friction — Tonal Adjectives in `Projection` vs. `Circumstance Over Emotion Labels`:**
  `persona-jude-callahan.md` defines `Projection` partly with tonal descriptors (*"Warm and dry-humored"*), while `promptbook.md` forbids emotional/tonal descriptors in stage action. That required explicit actor direction (**staging choice**) to translate warmth and dry humor strictly into physical props, posture, and line rhythm rather than stage-direction adverbs.
