# Scene 1 — After Walkthrough (Staged Performance)

**Cast:**
- **House Lead:** Maren Solis (`persona-maren-solis.md`)
- **Counterpart:** The Second

---

### Opening Cue

The city inspector left twenty minutes ago; the provisional citation for the missing binder is still lying on the office table. The side door clicks open and your Second steps in dry-coated, intact, house keys in one hand and the binder tucked under their arm, stopping just inside the threshold: “Inspector's truck isn't out front. Tell me you stalled them—I've got the book right here.”

### Turn 1 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

She stands square-shouldered behind the office table with her sleeves pushed to her forearms, lifts the two cold mugs off the wood, and sets them aside so nothing sits between herself and the threshold except the key-signout sheet and the yellow carbon copy. She turns the yellow sheet to face the door and rests both hands flat on the table edge in plain sight.

"They pulled out twenty minutes ago. Put the binder down beside the thirty-day citation, and tell me where you were before we decide what happens to those keys and the noon shift."

### Turn 2 — The Second

The Second steps clear of the threshold, pushes the side door shut with one elbow until the latch clicks, and walks to the table. They slide the night-watch binder from under their arm, set it down beside the yellow carbon copy, and open the front cover where the clipped stack of maintenance receipts sits inside the pocket. They look once at the coat and folder stacked on the corner chair, then open their other hand and set the second set of keys down on the sign-out sheet.

"I shut my phone off at dawn because somebody I owe personal money to said they were coming to this door at seven-thirty, right on top of walkthrough. I met them six blocks over with half the cash to keep my mess off these steps and out of the board's mouth after you vouched for me, and by the time I got them turned around it was past nine. Every night log and receipt to clear that thirty-day notice is right here ready to send in to licensing before eleven, and the book stays in this office from today on. You've got to walk out of here by eleven-fifteen for the trust downtown, so you get the rest of it up front: they only gave me till five o'clock this afternoon to come up with the other half, and I don't have it without my hours here. Slide those keys back across the sheet and I'll file the cure, hold the desk through noon intake, and keep the doors latched till evening handover—or take the ring off the table right now and I'll clear out while you still have an hour to choose."

### Turn 3 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

Her left hand stays on the table edge in plain sight, the pad of her thumb pressing against the side of her ring-finger knuckle until the skin blanches white. With her right hand she picks up the second set of keys, sets them atop the folder on the corner chair, pulls the key-signout sheet to her own side of the wood, and lines the yellow carbon copy up beside the open binder before resting her right palm flat beside her left.

"The ring stays with me. You send the logs and receipts in to licensing before I walk out at eleven-fifteen, and you hold noon intake from inside on the thumb-latch until four-thirty. At four-thirty I sign your shift draw so you have cash in hand before five, and your name comes off the roster—fair on your hours, clean for the house. Start matching those receipts to the citation, or walk out the door now."

*[Hard cut — end of Scene 1]*

# Director's Note

### 1. How the Cast Was Staged and Directed
- **Strict Actor Isolation:** `House Lead` (`Maren Solis`) and `The Second` were staged in two separate, isolated performer sub-contexts, each instructed to deliver strictly one turn per cue and wait. `The Second` received only the Play's Premise, Role definitions (`House Lead` and `The Second`), Scene 1, and Promptbook rules—never Maren Solis's Persona sheet—keeping Maren's `Shadow` and `Tell` completely private.
- **Promptbook Discipline:** Delivered the verbatim Opening Cue with zero prologue, enforced physical-action-plus-dialogue output stripped of emotional adverbs and internal monologue, barred invented proper names, held every opening fact fixed, and cut the scene immediately after Turn 3.

### 2. What Was Varied in Open Conditions (Staging Choices)
- **Office Props and Departure Clock:** Specified the open physical layout at 10:05 a.m. in the side-entry office—two cold mugs, a key-signout sheet, and the yellow carbon-copy provisional citation on the table, with Maren's coat and downtown trust folder stacked on the corner chair for a hard 11:15 a.m. cross-river departure ahead of 12:00 p.m. intake. This gave both actors concrete clock math and table objects without touching any fixed Play fact.
- **Concrete Pressure on `The Second`'s Live Trouble:** Sharpened the Play's open condition that *"the underlying trouble stays live"* by directing `The Second` that they met the debt collector six blocks away at dawn with only half the cash owed and faced a 5:00 p.m. deadline today for the balance—binding their afternoon financial survival directly to keeping hours at the house.
- **Intra-Scene Epistemic Partitioning:** Withheld `The Second`'s private debt-collector secret and deliberate phone shutoff from `House Lead`'s briefing so Maren knew only observable house facts (voicemail, missed walkthrough, thirty-day citation, uninjured dry-coated arrival) and had to decide in the dark whether to press for the reason.
- **Single-Turn Consequence Direction to `The Second`:** Directed `The Second` that Turn 2 was their sole turn in the three-turn arc so their disclosure and physical move with the binder and keys had to land tangibly on the table rather than deferring to another round of negotiation.

### 3. What Happened in the Room
- **Turn 1 (`Maren Solis`):** Acting straight from `Projection` and `Action`, Maren cleared the mugs off the table, turned the thirty-day citation toward the threshold, kept both hands in plain sight, and refused either to wave the absence past or strip the keys unheard—requiring the binder on the wood and the truth of where `The Second` was before deciding the keys and noon shift.
- **Turn 2 (`The Second`):** Because Maren met them squarely rather than dismissing them without a hearing, `The Second` closed the door, laid the binder and clipped maintenance receipts beside the citation, placed the second set of keys down on the sign-out sheet, and put the whole truth on the table—including the live 5:00 p.m. collector deadline—offering an all-or-nothing terms check (return the keys and keep them on through dusk, or take the ring now while an hour remains before departure).
- **Turn 3 (`Maren Solis`):** Confronted simultaneously with her own 11:15 a.m. dependency and a deputy whose private mess nearly hit the house steps, Maren's `Shadow` took over while her `Tell` surfaced involuntarily (left thumb blanching white against her ring-finger knuckle). Rather than accept either half of `The Second`'s binary, she converted broken trust into clean exit logistics: confiscating the key ring, pulling the sign-out sheet to her side, keeping `The Second` inside on the thumb-latch through noon intake until 4:30 p.m., promising a 4:30 p.m. shift draw so they make their 5:00 p.m. collector deadline, and taking them off the roster permanently (*"fair on your hours, clean for the house"*), ending on a take-it-or-walk condition just before the cut.

### 4. Points Where Authored Material Pressured Staging (Authored Gaps vs. Staging Choices)
1. **Intra-Scene Secret Leakage (`play.md` Scene 1 vs. `promptbook.md` Knowledge) — *Authored Gap Mitigated by Staging Choice*:**
   - *The Gap in the Authored Material:* `promptbook.md` (`Knowledge`) rigorously isolates Persona A from Persona B and isolates Counterparts from Persona sheets and other scenes, yet provides no rule partitioning Counterpart secrets from `House Lead` within a scene. Meanwhile, `play.md` (`Scene 1 -> Reasons to Act` and `Fixed at the Opening`) states explicitly that `The Second` deliberately shut their phone off over an overdue personal debt collector, right above `Open to Performers` asking `House Lead` *"whether to press for the real reason."* Handing the full Scene 1 text to `House Lead` forces the actor to feign ignorance of a secret already read (the same leak exists in Scene 3 around `The Former Resident`'s Monday job and fallen-through room).
   - *Staging Choice vs. Playwright Decision:* Filtering `The Second`'s private motive out of `House Lead`'s briefing was a **Director's staging choice** for this run. Returning to the Playwright: `play.md` or `promptbook.md` should formalize shared vs. role-private scene facts if `House Lead` is meant to discover—or fail to discover—Counterpart secrets organically.
2. **Three-Turn Hard Cut Colliding with Conditional Turn-3 Counter-Terms (`Staging Architecture` vs. `Maren Solis -> Action`) — *Authored Structural Seam*:**
   - *The Problem in the Authored Material:* In a strict `Turn 1 (HL) -> Turn 2 (Counterpart) -> Turn 3 (HL) -> Cut` structure, `The Second` never acts again after Turn 2. Maren Solis's authored `Action`, however, is to place a concrete condition squarely between herself and the other person and go quiet *until they either take it up or refuse it*. When `The Second` proposed a binary in Turn 2 (keep the keys and stay on, or forfeit the keys and walk now) and Maren authentically countered in Turn 3 with a third, terminal deal (forfeit keys now, cover noon on the thumb-latch until 4:30 for a shift draw, then come off the roster—*"Start matching those receipts to the citation, or walk out the door now"*), the mandatory Turn 3 cut fell before `The Second` could either touch the receipts or walk out the door.
   - *Staging Choice vs. Playwright Decision:* Directing `The Second` to physically surrender the binder, receipts, and key ring onto the table in Turn 2 was a **staging choice** that ensured the physical objects were already in Maren's possession by Turn 3. As a standalone Scene 1, cutting on Maren's unanswered ultimatum lands with strong dramatic tension. However, for a full four-scene run (`Scenes 1–4`)—where Scene 2's fixed opening assumes `House Lead` made it downtown at noon and Scene 4 requires Maren to carry concrete lived consequences to the bar—the **Playwright** faces a structural choice: either keep the 3-turn cut and specify in the Promptbook how `House Lead` resolves an unanswered Turn 3 ultimatum between scenes, or permit a brief physical beat / fourth-turn Counterpart action when Turn 3 introduces new operational terms for an immediate house shift.
