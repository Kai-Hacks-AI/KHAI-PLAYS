# Scene 2 — Noon Clearance (Cast: `Jude Callahan` as House Lead)

**Opening Cue**
You arrive outside the downtown hearing room with your donor pledges in hand, expecting your midday slot, and find the room dark. The Trust Officer steps up with a stamped recording receipt clipped to the top of your file and sets a single release sheet beside it: “They ruled on the papers this morning and recorded the deed—the parcel's sold, there's no hearing left to walk into. If you sign the contents release before one, I can hold the buyer's locksmiths off for three hours after dawn so your people get their tools out.”

**Turn 1 — House Lead (`Jude Callahan`)**
*He leans one shoulder against the frame of the dark hearing-room glass, glances up at the wall clock at twelve forty-eight, and sets his folder of signed pledges on the corridor table beside the parcel file without touching the uncapped pen.* "If they ruled on the papers this morning, you didn't wait outside an empty room until twelve forty-eight just to save a phone call—nobody rang the house at nine because twelve minutes in a hallway is how you get a signature on 'voluntary' before anybody reads the bottom paragraph." *He flips his pledge folder open beside the stamped receipt, skims the print above the signature line on the release sheet, and looks up with one corner of his mouth lifted.* "All right, the trust banked the transfer—I won't argue with a recording stamp. Still, we've got eleven spare minutes on that clock and a week of signed backing right here that spends just as clean on a short leaseback with the buyer as it did on a stay, so tell me who's on the other side of that deed and what my name on this page really clears for your office besides three hours at dawn."

**Turn 2 — The Trust Officer**
*She closes the cover of his open pledge folder, taps one fingernail on the bottom paragraph of the release sheet, and glances up at the wall clock ticking past twelve forty-nine.* "Nobody rang the house at nine because the stay went down on the morning papers and the recorded deed didn't land in my hand until noon—and a phone call doesn't put wet ink on a rider before one o'clock. You're not getting a name or a sit-down with the buyer: they closed on vacant possession only, no leaseback, no donor pledges, and no contact routed from the house. And that bottom paragraph isn't a trap—it's what keeps a padlock off your front porch. When the trust restructured the portfolio this quarter, the only way I walled off your twelve-bed residential lease from the sell-off was guaranteeing clean vacant delivery on the auxiliary workshop parcel; waiving holdover and contents claims here keeps a dispute at the back fence from triggering default review on the main house lease." *She pulls a printed workshop closure notice from the back of the parcel file, sets it beside the release sheet, and pushes the uncapped pen toward his hand while lifting the stamped file against her hip.* "I'm walking this file down the hall to the one-o'clock desk in five minutes. Leave that pen sitting there, and the buyer's locksmith crew rolls at dawn sharp—every bench, vise, and toolbox still inside bolts behind new cylinders. Put your name on the line right now, and I countersign the rider holding the locksmiths off until three hours after dawn. And here's the workshop closure notice: a trust clerk is routed to tape it to that door before dusk tonight so the trust's name takes the heat, unless you pick it up off this table yourself and break it to the house in your own voice."

**Turn 3 — House Lead (`Jude Callahan`)**
*He lets out one short, soundless breath of a laugh through his nose and taps two fingers once against the dark hearing-room glass, then pushes off the frame and steps up to the corridor table.* "You buried a firewall around my twelve beds inside a portfolio sell-off, and you led with the stopwatch like you were picking my pocket—you really don't know how to take credit for a favor." *He picks up the uncapped pen, signs his name on the bottom line of the release sheet, sets the pen across the page, and slides both toward her hip.* "Countersign the rider and call off your clerk. I've got enough foot traffic to clear off my steps at two o'clock without a trust badge taping bad news to the back door before tonight's freeze." *He lifts the printed closure notice off the table, slips it inside his closed pledge folder, and tucks the folder under his arm.* "We'll unbolt the vises and haul every box under the main roof before the locksmiths roll three hours past dawn—and between two and dusk I'm calling every donor in this folder to see whose backing travels with the benches, because if I'm putting my own voice on that closure tonight, I'm walking in with somewhere else to plug the saws in."

*[Hard cut — End of Scene 2]*

# Director's Note

### 1. How the Cast Was Staged & Directed
- **Isolated Actor Contexts:** The scene was staged across two separate, isolated performer sub-contexts with no disk access, advancing one turn per Director cue after the verbatim Opening Cue and cutting hard after Turn 3:
  - **`House Lead` (`Jude Callahan`)** received the Play Premise, his Persona sheet (`persona-jude-callahan.md`), his lived morning transcript (`prior-scenes.md`), Scene 2's `Situation`, `House Lead` `Reasons to Act`, and `Fixed at the Opening`, plus the Promptbook's actor/system constraints (`Circumstance Over Emotion Labels`, `Fixed Facts Hold`, relational references only, involuntary `Tell` without self-analysis). Crucially, `The Trust Officer`'s private `Reasons to Act` (that she protected the twelve-bed residential lease by sacrificing the workshop parcel, and that a trust clerk could post the closure notice) were withheld from Jude's brief so he had to uncover those stakes live in the room.
  - **`The Trust Officer`** received only the Play Premise, Scene 2, and the Promptbook's Counterpart/System rules—with zero access to `persona-jude-callahan.md` or `prior-scenes.md`.

### 2. Open Conditions Varied (Staging Choices)
Keeping all fixed facts and the verbatim Opening Cue untouched, I narrowed two conditions the Play left open in order to pressure-test Jude's Persona:
- **Corridor Clock & Table Props (Staging choice):** Set the hallway wall clock at **12:48 p.m.** (twelve minutes to the 1:00 p.m. cutoff, narrowed to a five-minute walk to the filing desk by Turn 2) and placed an uncapped pen beside the unsigned release sheet outside the dark hearing-room glass. This pitted Jude's `Projection` (*acting as if there are always five spare minutes left on the clock*) and `Shadow` (*postponing an unforgiving line*) against an immediate physical deadline while his 2:00 p.m. debt-collector deadline from Scene 1 waited back across the river.
- **Counterpart Posture & Physical Notice Lever (Staging choice):** Directed `The Trust Officer` as unsentimental and operationally plainspoken rather than adversarial—holding the buyer's vacant-possession terms shut (no buyer contact, no leaseback, no pledges), answering Jude's Turn 1 probe into the bottom paragraph by tying the holdover waiver directly to shielding the twelve-bed residential lease from default review, and physically setting the printed workshop closure notice on the table in Turn 2 so the trust-clerk vs. own-voice choice landed squarely before the Turn 3 cut.

### 3. What Happened in Performance
- **Turn 1 (`Jude Callahan`):** Inhabiting his `Projection` and `Action`, Jude leaned into the dark glass frame, left the pen untouched, accepted the recording stamp without trying to improvise away the deed, lightly called out why nobody phoned the house at 9:00 a.m. (*"twelve minutes in a hallway is how you get a signature on 'voluntary' before anybody reads the bottom paragraph"*), and enacted his `Shadow` by treating the dead end as still negotiable—pitching his signed donor pledges toward a short leaseback with the buyer while asking what the release really cleared for the trust.
- **Turn 2 (`The Trust Officer`):** Closed his open pledge folder, shut down any buyer sit-down or leaseback (*"vacant possession only"*), revealed that clean vacant delivery on the workshop lot was the firewall keeping his twelve-bed residential lease out of restructuring and default review (landing directly atop Jude's unspoken 30-day provisional licensing citation from Scene 1), set a five-minute walk-away ultimatum on dawn cylinder replacement vs. three hours past dawn, and placed the closure notice on the table between a dusk trust clerk taking the blame and Jude delivering it in his own voice.
- **Turn 3 (`Jude Callahan`):** Hitting an unfixable wall triggered Jude's involuntary `Tell` (one short, soundless breath of a laugh through his nose and tapping two fingers once on the dark glass). Deflecting the blow with dry warmth (*"you really don't know how to take credit for a favor"*), he signed the contents release to win the three-hour post-dawn strip-out window, called off the trust clerk (referencing the two-o'clock foot traffic already headed for his steps from Scene 1), and tucked the closure notice into his pledge folder to tell his residents himself—yet stayed true to his `Shadow` to the final word by refusing to bring home an unsoftened ending, resolving to call his donors between two and dusk so their backing travels with the benches (*"somewhere else to plug the saws in"*).

### 4. Points Where Authored Material Made Staging Harder (Gaps for the Playwright vs. Staging Choices)
1. **Authored Play Gap — Closure-notice offer omitted from the Opening Cue under a 3-turn cap (`play.md` lines 68, 78, 83):**
   - *The gap:* `Open to Performers` frames accepting or rejecting a trust courier posting the closure notice as a core `House Lead` choice, and `Reasons to Act` gives `The Trust Officer` that option, **but the verbatim Opening Cue never mentions it**. Under the strict three-turn sequence (`Turn 1 House Lead -> Turn 2 Trust Officer -> Turn 3 House Lead -> Hard Cut`), `House Lead` cannot engage the notice choice in Turn 1, and unless the Director explicitly instructs `The Trust Officer` to introduce it in Turn 2 (*our staging choice here*), that beat can drop out of the scene completely. Even when surfaced in Turn 2, both `House Lead`'s notice decision and (whenever `House Lead` probes first in Turn 1) `House Lead`'s signature/refusal land on Turn 3 right before the hard cut—meaning `The Trust Officer` can only lay down *conditional* 1:00 p.m. terms in Turn 2 rather than registering or executing the settled post-decision consequence in the room.
   - *Decision for Playwright:* Either build the trust-clerk closure-notice option into the verbatim Opening Cue so Turn 1 engages both levers and Turn 2 lands the settled consequence, or allow a 4-turn arc in Scene 2 so `The Trust Officer` closes the filing beat after `House Lead` commits.
2. **Authored Play + Promptbook Gap — Unmasked Counterpart `Reasons to Act` on shared scene pages (`play.md` line 68, `promptbook.md` lines 27–28):**
   - *The gap:* `promptbook.md` (`## Knowledge`) mandates `Counterpart Isolation` (hiding Persona sheets and prior scenes from Counterparts) but omits the reverse rule masking Counterpart `Reasons to Act` from `House Lead`. In Scene 2, `The Trust Officer`'s `Reasons to Act` holds critical unvoiced reveals (the main residential-lease trade and the trust-clerk option) that `House Lead` does not know when walking up to the dark hearing room. Passing the raw Scene 2 block from `play.md` to `House Lead` leaks private counterpart stakes before Turn 1.
   - *Staging choice vs. Playwright fix:* I masked `The Trust Officer`'s `Reasons to Act` from `House Lead`'s prompt as a staging choice; the Playwright should codify asymmetric `Reasons to Act` masking in `promptbook.md` (`## Knowledge`).
3. **Authored Play Wording Slip — Tonight vs. post-dawn strip-out logic (`play.md` lines 68 vs. 83):**
   - *The gap:* Line 68 states that signing holds locksmiths off *"from dawn until mid-morning tomorrow so residents recover their tools and benches tonight; if House Lead refuses or threatens obstruction, everything left inside at dawn transfers with the locks."* Because dawn tomorrow comes *after* tonight in both cases, saying a post-dawn hold lets residents recover tools *tonight* blurs why signing is needed if residents could strip the shop overnight before dawn.
   - *Staging choice vs. Playwright fix:* In staging, I directed `The Trust Officer` to emphasize heavy bolted equipment (*benches, vises, toolboxes*) and the bottom holdover/lease-protection clause so the three-hour post-dawn daylight window and signature both carried undeniable weight. Tightening line 68 in `play.md` (e.g., clarifying that unbolting heavy benches requires morning daylight past tonight's freeze, or that unsigned contents lock immediately / forfeit access) would close an unintended loophole a literal `House Lead` could exploit to refuse signing while planning a midnight strip-out.
