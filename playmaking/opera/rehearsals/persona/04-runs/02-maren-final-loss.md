# Scene 2 — Noon Clearance (Staged Performance)

**Cast:**
- **House Lead:** Maren Solis (`persona-maren-solis.md`)
- **Counterpart:** The Trust Officer

---

### Opening Cue

You arrive outside the downtown hearing room with your donor pledges in hand, expecting your midday slot, and find the room dark. The Trust Officer steps up with a stamped recording receipt clipped to the top of your file and sets a single release sheet beside it: “They ruled on the papers this morning and recorded the deed—the parcel's sold, there's no hearing left to walk into. If you sign the contents release before one, I can hold the buyer's locksmiths off for three hours after dawn so your people get their tools out.”

### Turn 1 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

She stops square-shouldered at the corridor table with her coat open and sleeves pushed to her forearms, looks once up at the wall clock at 12:48, and lifts the stamped recording receipt clipped to her parcel file, running one finger under the recording stamp. She sets the parcel file and her stack of signed donor pledges off to the far end of the wood, slides the unsigned contents-release sheet and the uncapped black pen into the cleared space between herself and the Trust Officer, and rests both hands flat on the table edge in plain sight.

"The deed is recorded, so the pledges stay shut. Before my name goes on a release, write the exact three-hour dawn window and rear-gate access onto this sheet over your initials, and list our bolted bench tools and compressor as resident property, not buyer fixtures. Put that in ink while we have twelve minutes, or tell me why it isn't already on the page."

### Turn 2 — The Trust Officer

The Trust Officer looks up at the wall clock as the minute hand clicks to 12:50, picks up the uncapped black pen, and pulls the contents-release sheet halfway back across the wood. Resting one palm on the upper corner, the Trust Officer writes three numbered lines into the side margin, signs initials beside each line, draws an empty box next to the dispatch line at the bottom, and turns the sheet back toward her with the pen laid across the countersignature blank.

"It wasn't on the page because letting your auxiliary lot clear on clean boilerplate this morning was how I kept the portfolio committee from opening your twelve-bed residential lease. I can't rewrite fixture title on a recorded deed without buyer's escrow voiding the whole grace window, so here is every inch my initials actually cover: locksmiths held off from six-thirty to nine-thirty tomorrow morning, rear-gate access for load-out, and your bench tools and compressor released as removable trade gear only if every lag bolt comes out clean and nothing is still wired into the panel or breaking masonry when their crew walks in at nine-thirty. Check that bottom box before you sign if you want a trust clerk posting the closure notice on the workshop door at dusk so downtown takes the heat, or cross it out and break it to the house yourself—in ten minutes I walk this down to dispatch whether your name is on it or not."

### Turn 3 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

With her left hand resting on the lower edge of the release sheet in plain sight, the pad of her thumb pressing hard against the side of her ring-finger knuckle until the skin blanches white, she picks up the black pen in her right hand, draws a straight line through the empty box at the bottom, and signs her name on the countersignature blank. Keeping her left palm flat over the signed sheet, she unclips the stamped recording receipt from her parcel file, sets it blank-side-up in the cleared space between them, and lays the pen across it.

"Keep your clerk off my steps at dusk—I told my house that shop wasn't dead, so I tell them at handover myself, and every lag bolt and panel wire comes out clean by nine-thirty. I don't carry favors made behind dark glass; if a committee touches our twelve-bed lease, put the notice on the table in front of me so we keep it fair. Copy those three lines and your initials onto the back of my receipt so I hold the same ink at six-thirty, or my signature stays under my hand."

*[Hard cut — end of Scene 2]*

# Director's Note

### 1. How the Cast Was Staged & Directed
- **Strict Context Isolation:** `House Lead` (`Maren Solis`) and `The Trust Officer` were staged in two strictly separate performer sub-contexts, instructed to send one turn only and wait on cue.
  - **`House Lead` context:** Received `persona-maren-solis.md`, `prior-scenes.md` (her lived Friday morning in Scene 1, where she took back her Second's keys and scheduled her Second's removal from the roster at 4:30 p.m.), the Play's Premise, the Promptbook rules for `House Lead`, and only the player-facing opening facts of Scene 2. `The Trust Officer`'s private `Reasons to Act` (the residential-lease restructuring trade and the dusk trust-clerk option) were withheld from `House Lead`'s Turn 1 brief so she could not anticipate Turn 2.
  - **`The Trust Officer` context:** Received only the Play's Premise, Scene 2, the Promptbook rules for Counterparts, and `House Lead`'s Turn 1—with zero access to `prior-scenes.md` or `persona-maren-solis.md` (including no access to Maren's name, `Shadow`, or `Tell`).
- **Direction on Turn 2:** After `House Lead` demanded written fixture reclassification in Turn 1, I directed `The Trust Officer` neither to stall nor to rubber-stamp a deed change that recorded closing law would forbid, but to answer directly from institutional authority and blind spot: disclosing *why* the sheet was boilerplate (the main residential-lease protection trade), granting in initialed ink only what dispatch/escrow could honor (a hard 6:30–9:30 a.m. locksmith hold, rear-gate load-out, and removable-gear status strictly conditioned on clean lag-bolt and panel removal without masonry damage), and putting the dusk closure-notice clerk offer onto the sheet as a check-box against a hard dispatch walkaway.

### 2. What Was Varied (Director's Staging Choices Within Open Conditions)
- **Countdown Clock Compression:** The Play leaves exact arrival time within "midday" open against a fixed "1:00 p.m. cutoff." I set the corridor wall clock at **12:48 p.m.** at Turn 1 (advancing to **12:50 p.m.** in Turn 2) so the 1:00 p.m. deadline operated as an immediate twelve-to-ten-minute physical constraint in the corridor without altering a word of the verbatim Opening Cue.
- **Concrete Table Objects:** I placed an uncapped black pen beside the parcel file, clipped recording receipt, and unsigned release sheet on the corridor table so Maren's `Projection` (clearing table clutter, hands flat in plain sight) and `Action` (placing a concrete condition squarely between herself and her counterpart) could play through observable physical handling rather than abstract negotiation.

### 3. What Happened in Performance
- **Turn 1:** True to her `Projection` and `Action`, Maren did not plead with the week's donor pledges once she verified the recording stamp; she set the pledges and file aside to clear the wood and demanded initialed ink amendments defining the three-hour window, rear-gate access, and resident-property status for bolted shop gear before signing. Notably, her `Tell` stayed dormant in Turn 1 because a recorded legal fact alone only triggered her logistical discipline, not yet her `Shadow`.
- **Turn 2:** `The Trust Officer` met her demand with an institutional trade-off: revealing that letting the workshop clear cleanly had protected the twelve-bed residential lease in restructuring, setting strict physical conditions on unbolting/unwiring gear before 9:30 a.m. lest escrow void the hold, and offering a trust clerk at dusk so downtown would absorb the residents' anger.
- **Turn 3:** Learning that an unannounced backroom favor had been made over her roof—paired with an offer to have an outsider deliver bad news at her own threshold—pressed directly on Maren's `Shadow` (*treating needing anyone as a structural fault; locking people out and calling the distance fairness*). Her `Tell` surfaced involuntarily (left thumb blanching her ring-finger knuckle white while her voice stayed level). Compounding the solitary load she already assumed in Scene 1 (cutting her Second loose at 4:30 p.m.), she crossed out the dusk clerk box to break the loss to the house at handover herself, rejected covert favors, signed the release, and pinned the signed sheet under her left palm until `The Trust Officer` copied the three initialed terms onto the back of her recording receipt.

### 4. Authored Material Friction & Gaps for the Playwright (Distinguished from Staging Choices)
1. **Gap in the Play — Legal/Physical Overlap Between Tonight's Access and the Post-Dawn Locksmith Hold (`play.md`, Scene 2):**
   - *Authored friction:* Line 68 states that signing the contents release lets `The Trust Officer` *"hold the buyer's locksmiths off from dawn until mid-morning tomorrow so residents recover their tools and benches tonight; if House Lead refuses or threatens obstruction, everything left inside at dawn transfers with the locks,"* whereas the Opening Cue (line 83) frames the three-hour post-dawn hold as *"so your people get their tools out."* Strictly speaking, if locksmiths do not arrive until dawn either way and residents still have shop keys tonight, a stubborn `House Lead` might assume they can strip loose tools tonight *without* signing—unless refusing the contents release either forfeits legal right to remove shop equipment/bolted benches or brings immediate/dawn seizure of fixtures.
   - *Distinction from our staging choice:* We kept the Play fixed and bridged this naturally in performance via `House Lead` raising bolted bench tools/wired compressors in Turn 1 and `The Trust Officer` tying both the 6:30–9:30 a.m. rear-gate load-out and removable trade-gear release to the countersigned sheet in Turn 2. If the Playwright wants that leverage airtight even when a different Persona ignores fixtures and says *"We'll just empty it before dawn without signing,"* clarifying why the contents release is legally/physically required for tonight-into-morning removal would close that loophole.
2. **Gap in the Promptbook — `House Lead` Packet Filtering Within Each Scene (`play.md` & `promptbook.md`):**
   - *Authored friction:* `promptbook.md` explicitly defines `Counterpart Isolation` (*"receives only the Play's Premise, their single scene, and this Promptbook"*) and cross-run Persona isolation, but does not specify that Counterpart-side `Reasons to Act` and unannounced Counterpart offers inside `Open to Performers` must be withheld from `House Lead` at Turn 1. In Scene 2 of `play.md`, `Open to Performers — House Lead` (line 78) explicitly mentions *"accept or reject a trust courier posting the notice,"* even though the Opening Cue never mentions a courier/clerk. Passing Scene 2 raw to `House Lead` before Turn 1 risks `House Lead` answering an offer `The Trust Officer` hasn't made yet.
   - *Distinction from our staging choice:* As a staging choice, we filtered `The Trust Officer`'s private restructuring motive and clerk-posting option out of `House Lead`'s Turn 1 brief so Turn 2 landed fresh. Adding one sentence under `Promptbook -> Knowledge` specifying that `House Lead` receives only `Situation`, `Fixed at the Opening`, their own `Reasons to Act` bullet, and the `Opening Cue` would codify this cleanly.
3. **Structural Interaction — Conditional Turn 3 Actions Under the Hard 3-Turn Cap (`persona-maren-solis.md` + `promptbook.md`):**
   - *Observation (not a defect, flagged for multi-scene continuity):* Maren Solis's authored `Action` is placing a concrete condition between herself and the other person and waiting until they take it up or refuse it. Because Scenes 1–3 enforce an immediate hard cut after Turn 3 (`promptbook.md`, line 35), when Maren executes her `Action` on Turn 3—just as she did at the end of Scene 1 in `prior-scenes.md`, and here by holding her palm over her signature until `The Trust Officer` copies the three initialed lines onto her receipt—the cut lands right before the Counterpart's physical compliance. Dramatically, this produces a taut ending; operationally, before staging Scene 3 and Scene 4, the Playwright or Promptbook may want a brief continuity rule noting how conditional Turn 3 closes carry forward into later scenes when the condition is mechanically within the Counterpart's stated stake (here, `The Trust Officer` wants the signed sheet before 1:00 p.m. and already initialed those exact three lines).
