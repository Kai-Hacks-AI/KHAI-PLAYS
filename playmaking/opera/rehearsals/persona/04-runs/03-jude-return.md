# Scene 3 — Back Steps at Dusk (Cast: `Jude Callahan` as House Lead)

**Opening Cue**
You step into the back kitchen at dusk to lock up before handover and catch someone standing still on the porch steps outside the screen—canvas bag on one shoulder, thinner in the face, long after every hospital check and winter lead went cold and months after you finally boxed their ID envelope for good. Through the mesh they lift two fingers off the porch rail: “Didn't know if you still locked the back at dusk. Don't worry, I'm not tracking mud in uninvited.”

**Turn 1 — House Lead (`Jude Callahan`)**
*He stops with his hand an inch from the deadbolt, holds still for a beat looking through the mesh from the canvas bag on their shoulder to their face, then flips the brass hook out of the screen eye and props the outer door open with his boot.* "Ground's freezing solid out there—you couldn't track mud in tonight if you tried." *He reaches back to the stove, pours coffee from the pot into a clean mug, sets it on the corner of the kitchen table, and leans one shoulder into the open doorframe with half a grin.* "I checked hospital intakes and outreach vans on both sides of the river all last winter before I finally boxed your ID envelope up on the top closet shelf. You're not waiting outside the mesh after a year and change because you're worried about the floor—you're checking whether the latch holds before you say what you walked back here for. Come inside before my second asks why I'm heating the yard, put your hands on that mug, and tell me."

**Turn 2 — The Former Resident**
*They step over the threshold past his boot, let the canvas bag slide off their shoulder against the table leg, and wrap both palms around the mug without pulling out a chair.* "You wouldn't have found me on either side of the river—I left the state that week and spent the whole stretch scraping back onto my feet the hard way. I stayed quiet because I wasn't going to call here until I was standing upright sober, not after walking out on you two weeks shy of graduation." *They lift the mug off the wood, take a swallow of the hot coffee, set it back down on the corner, and look straight at him.* "I got back into the city today clean, with a seasonal job starting Monday morning that needs the ID in that envelope, and a room arrangement that fell through until Monday with the freeze coming down. If your beds are full tonight or vanishing burned my standing with the trust, pull that box off the top shelf and I'll clear the steps before your second walks in for handover—if there's a way across this floor till Monday, put the test in front of me and name the terms."

**Turn 3 — House Lead (`Jude Callahan`)**
*He lets out one short, soundless breath of a laugh through his nose and taps two fingers once against the doorframe, then steps back so the screen falls shut and pushes the inner door closed against the draft without turning the deadbolt.* "Staying quiet a whole year until you could walk in standing tall isn't sparing the house—that's just pride letting me check winter rosters while you scraped by without a net." *He crosses to the closet, reaches up onto the top shelf, lifts down a cardboard box, and pulls a manila envelope from inside before setting it flat on the table beside their mug.* "Take your envelope now—Monday's job doesn't ride on anything else in this room. All twelve beds upstairs are full tonight, and licensing left a thirty-day provisional review on my office table this morning, so I can't unfold the office cot without risking the twelve people upstairs." *He opens the counter drawer, sets a sealed test cup and the voucher pad next to the envelope, clicks a pen open, and slides the cup an inch toward their hand before leaning a hip against the counter edge.* "Run the test at the sink, finish your coffee before my second comes down for handover, and I'll write you a partner-house voucher across the river till Monday. The trust sold our workshop at noon and the buyer's locksmiths roll three hours past dawn—so if you want to square walking out two weeks shy of graduation, come back through this door at first light and put a hand on a wrench."

*[Hard cut — End of Scene 3]*

# Director's Note

### 1. How the Cast Was Staged & Directed (and What Was Varied)
- **Strict Sub-Context Isolation & Asymmetric Briefing (Staging Choice):** I staged `House Lead` (`Jude Callahan`) and `The Former Resident` in two isolated performer sub-contexts, delivering one turn per cue after the verbatim Opening Cue and cutting hard after Turn 3.
  - `House Lead` received the Premise, `persona-jude-callahan.md`, his verbatim lived transcripts from Scenes 1 and 2 (`prior-scenes.md`), the Promptbook's acting constraints, and *only* the Scene 3 facts `House Lead` knows at dusk—withholding `The Former Resident`'s private `Reasons to Act` (out-of-state recovery, Monday's job, and the fallen-through weekend room).
  - `The Former Resident` received only the Premise, the Promptbook's acting rules, and their own Scene 3 history and stake—never seeing `prior-scenes.md`, `persona-jude-callahan.md`, or unrevealed house-interior facts (whether the ID envelope still existed or whether the twelve regular beds were full).
- **Keeping Earlier Friday Pressure Live Across the Afternoon Gap (Varied Open Condition):** Rather than inventing offstage victories between Scene 2 (1:00 p.m.) and dusk handover, I directed `Jude Callahan` not to fabricate tidy afternoon resolutions—keeping Scene 1's thirty-day provisional licensing review and Scene 2's dawn workshop strip-out live on his back as he stepped into the kitchen at dusk. That made the office cot an active compliance risk to the twelve upstairs residents rather than a cost-free gesture of mercy.
- **Sharpening Counterpart Leverage Against Charm (Directorial Direction):** Within the Play's fixed stakes, I directed `The Former Resident` to play directly from sober pride and concrete Monday urgency (tying Monday morning's job start to the physical IDs in that envelope) and not to stall on the porch once Jude met them plainly: cross or refuse the threshold decisively, answer last winter's search directly, and force a concrete decision in Jude's hands before handover.

### 2. What Happened in Performance
- **Turn 1 (`Jude Callahan`):** Inhabiting his `Projection` and `Action`, Jude stopped short of the deadbolt, unhooked and boot-propped the screen door, poured coffee onto the kitchen table, mentioned last winter's river-wide hospital and outreach-van checks without heat, and named the unspoken stake—telling `The Former Resident` they weren't standing outside out of concern for mud, they were testing whether the latch still held before stating why they came back.
- **Turn 2 (`The Former Resident`):** Met as an adult rather than lectured, `The Former Resident` stepped past Jude's boot and warmed their palms on the mug without taking a chair—physically refusing to settle in before terms were square. They owned leaving the state and staying silent out of pride after blowing graduation two weeks out, put both concrete needs on the table (the ID envelope needed for Monday morning's seasonal job, plus a fallen-through room through the weekend freeze), and put a clean fork in Jude's lap: hand down the box so they could clear the steps before handover if beds were full or trust standing was burned, or put a test and real terms on the floor.
- **Turn 3 (`Jude Callahan`):** The revelation that pride kept them silent through a winter of hospital checks—colliding with a full house under provisional citation—triggered Jude's involuntary `Tell` (the short, soundless breath of a laugh through his nose and two fingers tapped once on the doorframe). Closing the inner door against the draft *without turning the deadbolt*, Jude separated survival from penance: he pulled down the boxed ID envelope unconditionally so Monday's job stayed safe, drew a firm licensing boundary against unfolding the office cot under this morning's thirty-day review, and—true to his `Shadow` refusing to bolt a door for good—improvised a two-part side angle: a sink sobriety cup for a partner-house voucher till Monday, plus an invitation back at dawn with a wrench to help strip out the sold workshop before the locksmiths arrive.

### 3. Authored Material Friction & Gaps (Distinguished from Staging Choices)
1. **Bilateral Leakage Inside `play.md` Scene Blocks vs. `promptbook.md` Isolation Rules (Authored Gap):**
   - *Gap in Play/Promptbook:* `promptbook.md` (§ Knowledge) isolates Persona A from Persona B and restricts Counterparts to "only the Play's Premise, their single scene, and this Promptbook," but omits any rule filtering the Counterpart's private `Reasons to Act` and conditional `if/then` triggers out of `House Lead`'s view of `play.md`. Handing `play.md` Scene 3 raw to `House Lead` spoils Jude's `Action` by telling the actor in advance about the out-of-state stretch, Monday's seasonal job, and the fallen-through room. Conversely, handing Scene 3 raw to `The Former Resident` exposes `Situation` and `Fixed at the Opening` facts (`House Lead`'s winter hospital checks, all twelve beds full, the envelope still boxed on the top closet shelf) that `Reasons to Act` says `The Former Resident` does not yet know (*"if it still exists"*).
   - *Distinction:* Partitioning those facts into asymmetric actor briefings was my **staging choice**; if the Playwright wants consistent isolation across Directors, `promptbook.md` (or `play.md`'s layout) needs explicit role-scoped visibility rules within each scene.
2. **Unbridged Afternoon Gap Between Scene 2 (1:00 p.m.) and Scene 3 (Dusk) (`play.md` Structural Gap):**
   - *Gap in Play:* Scenes 1 and 2 push `House Lead` to set afternoon commitments (in Jude's `prior-scenes.md`: a 2:00 p.m. debt-collector confrontation, whether `The Second` keeps keys at dusk, afternoon donor calls, and hauling workshop benches under the main roof), yet Scene 3 jumps straight to dusk handover assuming an unchanged baseline (active shift handover, an unobstructed office cot) with no Promptbook guidance on what happened between 1:00 p.m. and dusk.
   - *Distinction:* Holding the morning citation and morning workshop lockout live while leaving afternoon specifics uninvented was my **staging choice**; if a `House Lead` fires `The Second` in Scene 1 Turn 3, Scene 3's fixed premise (*"dusk at shift handover"*) can collide directly with Scene 1 unless the Playwright clarifies who relieves `House Lead` at dusk when `The Second` is gone.
3. **Three-Turn Hard Cap vs. Threshold/Voucher Resolution and Closet Geography (`play.md` + `promptbook.md` Tension):**
   - *Tension in Authored Material:* Because `The Former Resident` only reveals the weekend housing gap in Turn 2 and the scene cuts hard immediately after `House Lead`'s Turn 3, any conditional terms `House Lead` offers in Turn 3 (here: taking the test cup for a voucher and returning at dawn) cannot be accepted or refused onstage. This leaves an ambiguity heading into Scene 4 (`The Bar`): at the bar after handover, does `House Lead` know whether `The Former Resident` passed the cup and took the voucher, or did handover interrupt before it resolved? In addition, `play.md` moves the ID envelope from the *office safe* to *"the top closet shelf"* while staging Scene 3 at the *back kitchen* screen door; treating that closet as reachable from the kitchen was a **staging choice** to fit Turn 3's single-room beat, whereas if the Playwright intends an office closet down the hall, retrieving it mid-turn stretches physical staging under a 3-turn cap.
4. **Network Non-Overlap Premise vs. Partner-House Vouchers (`play.md` Watchpoint for Scene 4):**
   - *Tension in Play:* The Premise specifies *"No house shares staff, parcels, or former residents with another"* across opposite sides of the river, while Scene 3 explicitly introduces *"partner-house vouchers"* on a night when regular beds are full. Jude's line (*"a partner-house voucher across the river"*) fits a multi-house city network without naming the other Persona's house, but in a full Scene 4 staging the Promptbook may want a guardrail reminding both `House Leads` not to treat a cross-river voucher as landing at the other's specific twelve-bed house.
