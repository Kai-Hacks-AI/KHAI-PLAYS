# Scene 3 — Back Steps at Dusk (Staged Performance)

**Cast:**
- **House Lead:** Maren Solis ([persona-maren-solis.md](../02-company/persona-maren-solis.md))
- **Counterpart:** The Former Resident

---

### Opening Cue

You step into the back kitchen at dusk to lock up before handover and catch someone standing still on the porch steps outside the screen—canvas bag on one shoulder, thinner in the face, long after every hospital check and winter lead went cold and months after you finally boxed their ID envelope for good. Through the mesh they lift two fingers off the porch rail: “Didn't know if you still locked the back at dusk. Don't worry, I'm not tracking mud in uninvited.”

### Turn 1 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

She stops inside the kitchen sill with her sleeves pushed past her wrists and both sets of house keys hanging from her belt ring. Against the painted jamb, her left thumb presses into the side of her ring-finger knuckle until the skin whitens. She nudges a crate of empty jars clear of the doormat with her boot, lifts the brass hook out of the screen eye, and pushes the screen door open a foot against her forearm, keeping both hands in view on the wooden frame.

"I checked hospital intakes and winter vans until the thaw before I boxed your ID envelope on the top closet shelf. Come onto the mat out of the wind, or stay on the step—handover starts in the front room in five minutes, all twelve beds upstairs are full, and a sister house across the river closes freeze call-in at six. Tell me where you went that night, and whether you're here for that box or a call before the hour turns."

### Turn 2 — The Former Resident

They scrape both boot soles across the iron lip of the top step, step past her forearm onto the doormat, and lower the canvas bag against their shin so the screen door can swing shut behind their shoulder. Keeping clear of the kitchen aisle, they unbutton the top of their coat, pull a creased bus stub and a printed hire sheet from an inside pocket, and hold both out flat on an open palm.

"I slipped fourteen days before move-out, packed my bag before morning count, and got on an outbound bus out of state rather than stand in this kitchen and watch you pull my graduation papers. Worked freight docks through the winter, got clean over nine months back, and got off the bus today for a seasonal crew job starting Monday at seven in the morning—onboarding takes the original birth certificate and Social Security card out of that envelope." They fold their thumb over the bus stub, keeping the hire sheet face-up between them. "The sublet room I lined up fell through this afternoon until Monday, and I know better than to ask you to risk your license putting an old resident on the office cot at handover. Pull my box off that closet shelf and dial the sister house across the river before six, and I'll walk the bridge tonight—or just hand me the envelope and I'll clear the steps before your evening shift comes through the front door."

### Turn 3 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

She reaches past their shoulder to push the inner wooden door shut against the dusk draft, turns the thumb-bolt, and touches one finger to the Monday start line on the hire sheet without taking it from their palm. Stepping back to the end of the kitchen counter, she slides a stack of folded dish towels aside to clear the butcher block between herself and the mat, then rests both hands on the wood edge in plain sight, the pad of her left thumb pressing hard against the side of her ring-finger knuckle until the skin blanches white.

"Pulling a move-out date isn't putting a bag on the curb—one call from a depot would have kept me off county lists all winter. There's no evening shift coming in tonight; both key rings are on my belt and licensing cited us this morning, so the office cot stays folded. Put that hire sheet back in your coat. I have three minutes before handover to pull your box off the closet shelf and get your name onto the sister house's freeze list across the river through Sunday, and if you need carfare before Monday, I'm unbolting tools out of our rear workshop from six-thirty to nine-thirty tomorrow morning on the cash ledger before buyer's locksmiths take the gate. Set your bag by the radiator if I'm dialing that call, or turn the bolt behind you."

*[Hard cut — end of Scene 3]*

# Director's Note

### 1. How the Cast Was Staged & Directed
- **Strict Context Isolation:** `House Lead (Maren Solis)` and `The Former Resident` were staged in two separate, isolated performer sub-contexts, each restricted to a single turn per cue with no filesystem access and strict adherence to [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md) (`Circumstance Over Emotion Labels`, `Fixed Facts Hold`, `Hard Cut on Turn Caps`, and relational naming with zero invented proper names).
- **Knowledge Partitioning:**
  - `House Lead` received the Premise, role definitions, [persona-maren-solis.md](../02-company/persona-maren-solis.md), her lived Scenes 1–2 transcripts from `prior-scenes.md`, and Scene 3's opening circumstances from her side of the screen—with `The Former Resident`'s private `Reasons to Act` (out-of-state sobriety, Monday job, fallen-through room) withheld so Turn 1 met a genuinely unknown return. She was also directed to ground her physical habits in the standing back-kitchen threshold rather than repeating the desk/corridor table phrasing of Scenes 1–2.
  - `The Former Resident` received only the Premise, Roles, Scene 3 circumstances, and Promptbook Counterpart rules—with zero access to Maren's Persona (`Shadow` / `Tell`) or `prior-scenes.md`—and was directed to preserve dramatic irony by not knowing in-character that `House Lead` had checked hospitals all winter or kept the boxed ID envelope until `House Lead` spoke those facts aloud.

### 2. What Was Varied / Pressure Introduced (Staging Choices Within the Fixed Play)
While holding every fixed fact and the verbatim Opening Cue of [play.md](../02-company/play.md) constant, I sharpened three open conditions to stress-test Maren Solis's `Shadow` (*converting broken trust or helplessness into clean logistics and quietly locking people out before they can fail her a second time, calling the distance fairness*):
1. **Tight Dusk Clock & Voucher Cutoff:** Set lock-up at `5:50 p.m.`, five minutes before `5:55 p.m.` front-room handover with all twelve residents and ten minutes before a `6:00 p.m.` freeze-night intake cutoff at the sister house across the river, turning voucher vs. office cot vs. fetching the top-shelf box into an immediate time squeeze.
2. **Accumulated Friday Carryover on `House Lead`:** Carried the concrete physical consequences of `prior-scenes.md` directly into the kitchen—both sets of house keys on Maren's belt ring (after taking her Second off the roster at `4:30 p.m.`), this morning's thirty-day licensing citation still active against unfolding an unrostered thirteenth sleeper on the office cot, and her commitment to break tomorrow's `6:30–9:30 a.m.` workshop strip-out to the house herself in five minutes.
3. **Concrete Document Stake on `The Former Resident`:** Specified (within the Play's open *"seasonal job starting Monday"* and *"reclaim their old ID envelope"*) that Monday's `7:00 a.m.` crew onboarding requires the original birth certificate and Social Security card inside that safe envelope, backed by physical pocket proof (bus stub and printed Monday hire sheet).

### 3. What Happened in Performance
- **Turn 1 (`House Lead`):** Seeing someone she had searched county/hospital rosters for until spring thaw pressed directly on Maren's `Shadow`, surfacing her `Tell` against the doorjamb. She unhooked the screen door a foot to offer the doormat out of the wind and named the boxed envelope on the top closet shelf, but immediately routed the moment into clean, arm's-length logistics—citing full upstairs beds, five minutes to handover, and a `6:00 p.m.` sister-house freeze call-in across the river while pointedly leaving the office cot unmentioned.
- **Turn 2 (`The Former Resident`):** Because Maren met them plainly as an adult rather than lecturing or hovering, `The Former Resident` wiped their boots, stepped onto the mat without crossing into the kitchen aisle, held out the bus stub and Monday `7:00 a.m.` hire sheet, owned bolting out of state two weeks shy of graduation rather than watching Maren pull their graduation papers, and put the fallen-through weekend sublet on the table—while preemptively declining to risk her license on the office cot and offering either to walk the bridge on a sister-house call or take only the envelope and clear out *"before your evening shift comes through the front door."*
- **Turn 3 (`House Lead`):** That innocent assumption collided with Maren's private Friday. She shut and thumb-bolted the inner wooden door against the freeze, answered the year-long disappearance (*"one call from a depot would have kept me off county lists all winter"*), disclosed that there was no evening shift coming in and that morning's licensing citation kept the office cot folded, and locked every remaining need into clean, bounded transactions: pulling the ID box, booking the sister-house freeze list across the river through Sunday, and offering `6:30–9:30 a.m.` cash-ledger labor unbolting workshop tools tomorrow morning—framed strictly as *their* need for Monday carfare rather than admitting she is shorthanded and alone—before leaving the final binary choice at the radiator or the thumb-bolt on the hard cut.

### 4. Authored Material Friction & Gaps vs. Director Staging Choices
Distinguishing staging choices made for this run from structural points in [play.md](../02-company/play.md), [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md), and [persona-maren-solis.md](../02-company/persona-maren-solis.md) that the Playwright may wish to examine:
1. **Asymmetric Private Facts on a Shared Scene Sheet ([play.md](../02-company/play.md#L87-L114) + [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md#L28) — *Authored Gap Mitigated by Director Partitioning*):** `Counterpart Isolation` in [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md#L28) directs giving each Counterpart *"the Play's Premise, their single scene, and this Promptbook."* However, Scene 3 in [play.md](../02-company/play.md#L91-L104) prints both sides' private history and hidden `Reasons to Act` on the single scene page: handing the raw scene text unedited to `The Former Resident` reveals out-of-character that `House Lead` checked hospitals all winter and kept the ID envelope on the top closet shelf (even though `The Former Resident` came *"to reclaim their old ID envelope if it still exists"*), while handing the raw scene text unedited to `House Lead` reveals before Turn 1 that `The Former Resident` is sober from out of state with a Monday job and a fallen-through weekend room (undercutting `Open to Performers`: *"Whether to reveal that they have nowhere solid until Monday"*). *Distinction:* No text change was needed here because I partitioned those private bullets across the two actors' prompts as a directing choice; if the Playwright wants raw scene sheets passed directly to isolated contexts, separating `Shared Opening Facts` from role-private `Known Only to House Lead` / `Known Only to Counterpart` blocks in [play.md](../02-company/play.md) would prevent omniscient bleed.
2. **Three-Turn Hard Cut vs. Conditional Turn 3 State Entering Scene 4 ([play.md](../02-company/play.md#L21-L25) + [persona-maren-solis.md](../02-company/persona-maren-solis.md#L16-L18) — *Structural Tension for Multi-Scene Continuity*):** Because `The Former Resident`'s Opening Cue withholds both the ID-envelope request and the weekend roof bind until Turn 2, `House Lead` has only Turn 3 to answer the real predicament before an immediate hard cut with no reply. Combined with Maren's authored `Action` (*placing one concrete condition/choice between herself and the other person and going quiet until they take it up or refuse it*), Turn 3 naturally ends on a live conditional offer (*"Set your bag by the radiator if I'm dialing that call, or turn the bolt behind you"*). As a standalone Scene 3 ending, that unresolved cut works sharply; however, if the run continues into Scene 4 (`The Bar`, set *after* evening handover finishes), [play.md](../02-company/play.md#L121-L135) gives no rule for whether `House Lead` knows at the bar whether `The Former Resident` stayed for the call or walked back into the freeze. If full four-scene runs are staged, a brief Playwright note in [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md) on how suspended Turn 3 conditionals carry into Scene 4 would eliminate ambiguity.
3. **Cross-Scene Staffing Branch vs. Fixed "Shift Handover" Wording ([play.md](../02-company/play.md#L50) vs. [play.md](../02-company/play.md#L93-L113) — *Minor Continuity Friction Resolved via Staging*):** Scene 1 explicitly allows `House Lead` to strip `The Second` of keys/standing (which Maren did in `prior-scenes.md`, taking `The Second` off the roster at `4:30 p.m.`), yet Scene 3's fixed text still refers to *"dusk at shift handover"* and *"lock up before handover."* *Distinction:* As a staging choice, treating dusk "handover" as the fixed house-wide evening resident check-in / desk transition (consistent with Scene 2 Turn 3's *"I tell them at handover myself"*) kept the fixed Opening Cue intact and actually deepened Turn 3 when `The Former Resident` assumed an evening shift was arriving and Maren corrected them. Only if the Playwright intends dusk handover strictly as a staff-to-staff relief does Scene 3's wording collide with Scene 1's dismissal branch.
4. **Table-Specific Physicality in [persona-maren-solis.md](../02-company/persona-maren-solis.md#L13) (*Minor Persona Note*):** Maren's `Projection` specifies *"clears clutter off the table between herself and whoever sits across from her,"* which produced nearly identical table-clearing beats in Scenes 1 and 2 (`prior-scenes.md`). Because Scene 3 takes place standing at a latched back screen door, directing the performer to translate that territorial habit to threshold surfaces (clearing a jar crate off the doormat in Turn 1, clearing dish towels off the end of the kitchen counter in Turn 3) avoided inventing out-of-place furniture while keeping the Persona text unchanged.
