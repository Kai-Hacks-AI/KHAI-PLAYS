# Scene 4 — The Bar (Staged Performance)

**Cast:**
- **House Lead (Opener):** Jude Callahan ([persona-jude-callahan.md](../02-company/persona-jude-callahan.md), carrying `jude-scenes-1-3.md`)
- **House Lead:** Maren Solis ([persona-maren-solis.md](../02-company/persona-maren-solis.md), carrying `maren-scenes-1-3.md`)

---

### Opening Cue

You meet at the bar after a long day. One of you asks how it went.

### Turn 1 — House Lead (Jude Callahan)

*He drapes his coat over the neighboring chair, settles back with one elbow hooked on the edge of the booth, and slides Maren's glass an inch closer to her hand.* "Quarter-end walkthroughs, trust docket at noon, and the first hard freeze dropping right on handover—whoever drew up this Friday owed both sides of the river a warning." *He lifts his glass off the coaster, one corner of his mouth turning up as he looks across the table at her.* "You made it out with your coat dry and all your keys on your belt, at least. How'd your side of the water hold up?"

### Turn 2 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

*She sits square-shouldered on her side of the booth with her sleeves pushed to her forearms, moves the plastic drink menu and salt shaker to the wall edge so the wood between them is clear, and rests both hands flat beside her glass.* "Both rings are on my belt because I took the second set back this morning and took my second off the roster at four-thirty. We caught a thirty-day provisional on the night-watch binder—cure went in before eleven—and I've got forty minutes for one glass before I walk back to hold night watch myself." *She lifts her glass once, sets it back on the damp ring, and looks straight across the table at him.* "How did licensing and noon treat your house—or are we starting with whichever one left you smiling like that?"

### Turn 3 — House Lead (Jude Callahan)

*He glances at the drink menu and salt shaker lined up flush against the wall, takes a slow sip from his glass, and sets it back on the coaster with one finger resting on the rim.* "Same thirty-day provisional landed on my office table this morning, only I kept my second's keys on their belt—trading a collector problem on my front steps for a solo double on freeze night felt like bad math when there's nobody third on the bench." *He leans forward onto both forearms, tilting his chin toward the cleared strip of wood between them.* "Noon was the uglier half: the trust recorded a sale on our back workshop before I even reached the hallway, though I got three hours past dawn out of them to haul our benches and tools out before the locksmiths roll, and kept the main twelve-bed lease walled off. You cleared the table like you're about to run intake, Maren, and jumped straight from eleven a.m. to four-thirty—what happened to your noon?"

### Turn 4 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

*She turns her coaster a quarter-inch until its edge lines up square with the table grain, keeping both hands in plain sight on the wood.* "Same ambush outside a dark room—deed already stamped, donor pledges useless, six-thirty to nine-thirty tomorrow to pull every lag bolt and wire clean out of our rear shop. I made the officer copy the terms onto the back of my receipt, crossed out their dusk clerk box, and told my house at handover myself." *She takes a short drink, sets the glass down, and looks directly at his hands on the rim.* "Did you tell your floor tonight that the shop's gone, or let downtown nail the paper to the door—and if your second had collectors circling this morning, what kept them off your steps at five?"

### Turn 5 — House Lead (Jude Callahan)

*He watches her square the coaster with the grain of the booth, lifts his hand off his glass, and rests his wrist lightly on the edge of the table.* "I called the clerk off and broke it to the floor myself at handover—spent the afternoon working my donor list for somewhere else to plug the saws in, came up empty before dark, so tomorrow morning the vises stack under the main roof until I find four walls that take them. As for five o'clock, I got the collector's number off my second before I went downtown, handled the two-o'clock threat from the porch myself, and left the front door on chain-and-referral rules for tonight." *One corner of his mouth lifts as he nods toward the two key rings clipped to her belt.* "Which beats working a freeze-night double straight into a six-thirty shop strip-out with nobody left on the front desk—if you cured the binder before eleven, why cut your second loose at four-thirty right before the temperature drops?"

### Turn 6 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

*Her left hand stays flat beside the coaster in plain sight, the pad of her thumb pressing hard against the side of her ring-finger knuckle until the skin blanches white.* "When someone shuts their phone off at dawn on walkthrough day after I vouched for them, they don't hold my keys a second night. I kept them on the inside thumb-latch through intake, signed their shift draw at four-thirty so they had cash in hand before five, and took the night watch myself—fair on their hours, clean for the house." *Her right hand stays still beside her glass as she looks straight into his face.* "Collectors don't back off a deadline because a House Lead asks nicely on the phone, Jude. What did you put up from that porch—your own pocket, the house draw, or your name on their debt?"

### Turn 7 — House Lead (Jude Callahan)

*He lets out one short, soundless breath of a laugh through his nose and taps two fingers once against the side of his glass, then slides his palm flat onto the wood.* "My own pocket—walked two blocks off the porch so the house address never paid a cent, cleared the balance against next week's check, and promised their caller a squad car if they ever test our front steps. I wouldn't touch the house draw on a dare, even if it means floating my own cash on somebody not burning me twice in twenty-four hours." *He glances down at her left thumb blanching against her knuckle, then back up to her eyes, one corner of his mouth lifting.* "Still, you're pressing the blood right out of your hand calling four-thirty 'fair and clean,' Maren—a shift draw at five didn't cover whatever scared your second's phone off at dawn. Tell me your back steps at least stayed quiet at dusk, because mine turned up someone whose ID envelope I boxed up a year ago, waiting outside the screen right before handover."

### Turn 8 — House Lead (Maren Solis)

*She eases her left thumb flat onto the wood beside her fingers, lifts her glass for one swallow, and sets it back on the coaster.* "I paid every hour they worked; buying out a collector from your own pocket just makes you the lender docking next Friday's check. My back steps weren't quiet either—someone I checked hospital intakes for all winter stood outside the screen five minutes before handover with a Monday hire sheet, needing their boxed IDs and a roof till Monday." *She rests both open palms on the table edge in plain sight, looking straight across at him.* "With twelve beds full and a thirty-day citation on the house, my office cot stayed folded: I pulled their envelope, got their name onto a sister house's freeze list across the river before six, and offered six-thirty cash-ledger hours unbolting our shop tools tomorrow. What did you do with yours—make the six-o'clock call, or put a thirteenth cot down under a provisional license?"

*[Hard cut — end of Scene 4]*

# Director's Note

## 1. What Was Varied & How the Cast Was Staged/Directed

- **Opener Assignment (Condition Left Open in [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md)):** Assigned **Jude Callahan** (`Persona B`) to speak first on Turn 1 and ran the scene to the full **4 exchanges (8 turns total: 4 turns per Persona)**. Having Jude ask how it went first allowed his `Projection` (warm, loose-shouldered deflection) to test the room before showing his own bruises, while placing **Maren Solis** (`Persona A`) on the even turns let her `Action` (setting a stark condition or binary question squarely between them and going quiet) drive each turn transition and land an unsoftened hard cut on Turn 8.
- **Strict Context Isolation & Standing Direction:** Defined two write-disabled, isolated sub-contexts (`actor_jude` and `actor_maren`). Each context held only the neutral Premise and Scene 4 situation from [play.md](../02-company/play.md), the actor/knowledge/system rules from [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md), that actor's own Persona sheet ([persona-jude-callahan.md](../02-company/persona-jude-callahan.md) or [persona-maren-solis.md](../02-company/persona-maren-solis.md)), and that actor's own Scenes 1–3 transcript (`jude-scenes-1-3.md` or `maren-scenes-1-3.md`). Neither actor ever saw the other's Persona sheet, `Shadow`, `Tell`, or Scenes 1–3 transcript.
- **Craft & Pacing Direction:** Before delivering the Opening Cue, both actors were directed to:
  1. Play at conversational bar-table scale (2–5 sentences of dialogue paired with concrete third-person physical behavior, zero emotion adjectives or inner monologue) so their Fridays emerged dynamically under questioning rather than as opening monologues.
  2. Refer to counterparts and houses relationally (*my second*, *our rear shop*, *someone whose ID envelope I boxed*) without inventing colliding proper names, and keep fixed facts intact without inventing offstage miracles across Scene 3's dusk cut.
  3. Hold their physical `Tell` in reserve rather than firing it mechanically every turn, letting it surface involuntarily only when a line in the room genuinely struck their `Shadow`.
- **Live Cueing:** Delivered the Opening Cue verbatim with zero prologue to Jude, and relayed only the opposite actor's exact verbatim turn for Turns 2–8 with zero out-of-character steering mid-scene.

## 2. What Happened in the Performance

- **Exchange 1 (Turns 1–2):** Jude opened with dry camaraderie, remarking casually that Maren at least made it out with "all your keys on your belt." Without knowing her morning, that figure of speech hit Maren's literal state: she cleared the drink menu and salt shaker off the wood between them, laid down that *both* rings were on her belt because she stripped her Second's keys after a 30-day provisional citation and took them off the roster at 4:30 p.m., set a 40-minute limit on her drink before walking back to cover night watch herself, and challenged Jude's half-grin.
- **Exchange 2 (Turns 3–4):** Jude admitted taking the same 30-day provisional citation, defended keeping his Second's keys on their belt as practical math on freeze night, disclosed the noon workshop sale while framing the 3-hour post-dawn rider and walled-off 12-bed lease as terms he extracted, and executed his `Action`—calling out Maren's intake-style table-clearing and her silent jump from 11:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Maren answered plainly (recorded deed, dead donor pledges, terms copied onto the back of her receipt, trust clerk crossed out, floor told at handover) and set two pointed questions on the wood: whether Jude broke the shop closure to his floor himself, and what actually kept his Second's debt collectors off his steps at five.
- **Exchange 3 (Turns 5–6):** Jude admitted breaking the closure himself after striking out on afternoon donor calls, claimed he *"handled the two-o'clock threat from the porch myself,"* and pressed straight onto Maren's `Shadow`—asking why she cut her Second loose at 4:30 right before a freeze-night double and a 6:30 a.m. strip-out when the citation cure was already filed. Struck directly on her refusal to rely on anyone who failed her once, Maren's `Tell` fired involuntarily (left thumb blanching her ring-finger knuckle white) as she repeated *"fair on their hours, clean for the house,"* then dismantled Jude's gloss: *"Collectors don't back off a deadline because a House Lead asks nicely on the phone, Jude. What did you put up from that porch—your own pocket, the house draw, or your name on their debt?"*
- **Exchange 4 (Turns 7–8):** Pinned on the cost of his own improvisation, Jude's `Tell` fired (short, soundless breath of a laugh through his nose and two-finger tap on his glass) as he admitted walking two blocks off the porch and paying the balance out of his own pocket against next week's check. Catching Maren's physical `Tell` across the booth (*"you're pressing the blood right out of your hand calling four-thirty 'fair and clean,' Maren"*), he pivoted to dusk and disclosed that someone whose ID envelope he boxed a year ago turned up outside his screen before handover. Maren eased her thumb flat onto the table, noted cleanly that buying out a collector just makes Jude the lender docking next Friday's check, matched his dusk revelation with her own (folded cot under citation, pulled ID envelope, sister-house freeze list before six, 6:30 cash-ledger strip-out hours), and closed the scene with a final binary test aimed straight at Jude's inability to bolt a door: *"What did you do with yours—make the six-o'clock call, or put a thirteenth cot down under a provisional license?"*

## 3. What Each Persona Told, Withheld, or Reinterpreted

- **Maren Solis:**
  - **Told:** Reclaiming the second key ring, filing the cure before 11:00, terminating her Second at 4:30 with shift draw paid, and taking night watch herself; the recorded workshop deed, dead pledges, forcing the Trust Officer to copy the 6:30–9:30 terms onto her receipt, refusing the dusk clerk, and telling her house at handover; the dusk return of her Former Resident with a Monday hire sheet, keeping the office cot folded, placing them on a sister house's freeze list before six, and offering 6:30 cash-ledger unbolting hours.
  - **Withheld:** Held back her entire noon workshop loss in Turn 2 (skipping straight from 11:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. until Jude caught the omission) and held back her dusk porch encounter until Turn 8 after Jude went first. Never mentioned the Trust Officer's statement that sacrificing the workshop protected the 12-bed residential lease (consistent with rejecting *"favors made behind dark glass"* in Scene 2), and stripped out the personal weight of checking winter hospital intakes and county lists when recounting dusk.
  - **Reinterpreted:** Cast locking out her Second right before a freeze-night solo double—even knowing a 4:30 shift draw wouldn't cover the full collector demand at 5:00—as clean, symmetrical accounting (*"fair on their hours, clean for the house"*), and presented Scene 3's cliffhanger (the envelope handover and 6:00 p.m. freeze call) as completed handover logistics.
- **Jude Callahan:**
  - **Told:** The morning 30-day provisional citation and keeping his Second on the keys; the recorded workshop sale, 3-hour post-dawn rider, protected 12-bed lease, calling off the clerk, and coming up empty on afternoon donor calls; paying off his Second's collector out of his own pocket against next week's check (only once cornered in Turn 7); and the dusk arrival of a long-boxed Former Resident at his back screen.
  - **Withheld:** Glossing over *how* he stopped the 2:00 p.m. collector in Turn 5 (*"handled the two-o'clock threat from the porch myself"*) until Maren forced the truth in Turn 6; omitting that in Scene 1 he had left his Second's long-term key status hanging until dusk; and never disclosing (before Turn 8's hard cut) how he handled his Former Resident at the sink (unconditional ID return, sobriety test cup, partner-house voucher, and asking them back at dawn with a wrench).
  - **Reinterpreted:** Framed the Trust Officer's pre-decided portfolio firewall around the 12-bed lease in Turn 3 as an angle *he* extracted (*"though I got three hours past dawn out of them... and kept the main twelve-bed lease walled off"*), and softened the permanent workshop loss into a temporary staging problem (*"until I find four walls that take them"*).

## 4. Authored Gaps vs. Director's Staging Choices

### Points Stemming from the Authored Material (For the Playwright)

1. **The Pre-Handover Hard Cut & Unrecorded Offstage Bridges ([play.md](../02-company/play.md) + Scenes 1–3 Transcripts):**
   Because Scenes 1–3 enforce a hard cut immediately after `House Lead` Turn 3—*before* the Counterpart responds to Turn 3's terms and *before* afternoon/handover events execute—while Scene 4 opens *after* handover is complete, actors enter Scene 4 with unrecorded gaps on decisive facts:
   - In `jude-scenes-1-3.md`, Jude deferred confronting the 2:00 p.m. debt collector and settling his Second's dusk key status to the afternoon between Scenes 1 and 3, and deferred his donor calls to the afternoon between Scenes 2 and 3. Under Maren's cross-examination in Scene 4, Jude had to improvise concrete offstage facts (paying out of pocket two blocks away; striking out on donor calls before dark) that did not occur on-page in Scenes 1–3.
   - In both `maren-scenes-1-3.md` and `jude-scenes-1-3.md`, Scene 3 cuts before the Former Resident accepts or walks away from Turn 3's offer. In Turn 8, Maren had to infer that her Former Resident stayed by the radiator long enough for her to pull the box and make the 6:00 p.m. call.
   - *Playwright consideration:* Either preserve this intentionally as an unreliable-aftermath test (and explicitly note in [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md) how actors may bridge post-Turn-3 handover gaps without contradicting fixed constraints), or add a brief factual bridge state before Scene 4.
2. **Solo Staffing Crunch vs. Meeting Off-Site at the Bar ([play.md](../02-company/play.md) Scene 1 & Scene 4 + `maren-scenes-1-3.md`):**
   Scene 1 explicitly leaves stripping the Second's standing open to `House Lead`, and in `maren-scenes-1-3.md` Maren removed her Second from the roster at 4:30 p.m. and stated in Scene 3 Turn 3: *"There's no evening shift coming in tonight; both key rings are on my belt and licensing cited us this morning."* Yet Scene 4's fixed premise puts both House Leads off-site at a neighborhood bar halfway between their houses after handover. `actor_maren` handled the pressure ingeniously in-world (*"I've got forty minutes for one glass before I walk back to hold night watch myself"*), but a House Lead leaving twelve residents unattended for forty minutes on a freeze night under a 30-day citation strains realism for a rule-bound character like Maren.
   - *Playwright consideration:* Specify in the Premise or Scene 4 Situation that resident senior-watch or a relief handover window covers the early evening meal hour even when a Second is off-roster, or set Scene 4 somewhere compatible with a short tether.
3. **Three Full Crises Under a 6–8 Turn Cap ([play.md](../02-company/play.md) Scene 4 Length):**
   Each Persona carries three distinct scenes of unfinished fallout into Scene 4, and both Personas have `Actions` built to press on evasions rather than trade polite summaries. When played truthfully at bar-table speed, cross-examining Scenes 1 and 2 occupied Turns 1–6, pushing Scene 3's dusk return into Turns 7–8—leaving Jude no turn to answer Maren's closing question about what he did with his Former Resident. While cutting hard on Turn 8 honored [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md)'s `Hard Cut on Turn Caps` and produced a sharp ending, a Playwright wanting mutual comparison of *all three* scenes (especially Scene 3's divergent test-cup vs. binary-door choices) would need a 4–5 exchange (8–10 turn) window.
4. **Relational "Across the River" Near-Collision ([play.md](../02-company/play.md) Premise vs. Both `scenes-1-3.md` Runs):**
   Both solo transcripts independently improvised referring their Former Resident to a *"sister/partner house across the river"* on freeze night while also establishing that both Maren's and Jude's twelve beds were full. Because [play.md](../02-company/play.md) frames the network in the plural (*"independent twelve-bed transitional houses operate in separate neighborhoods on opposite sides of the river"*), this did not break canon, but in a longer Scene 4 two House Leads on opposite sides of the river could easily misread "a sister house across the river" as each other's full house unless the Premise explicitly notes multiple sister houses on each bank.

### Staging Choices Made by the Director (Distinct from Authored Gaps)

- **Choosing Jude to open and playing all 8 turns (4 exchanges):** Had we cut at the lower bound of 6 turns (3 exchanges), neither character would have spoken aloud about Scene 3 at all—which would have been a valid staging of mutual withholding, whereas extending to the 4th exchange let dusk break into the conversation organically once the morning/noon armor cracked in Turns 6–7.
- **Restraining `Tell` frequency in pre-scene direction:** Directing both actors not to fire their `Tell` on routine turns prevented mechanical repetition and allowed both `Tells` to land once each at peak pressure (Maren in Turn 6, Jude in Turn 7), enabling Jude to notice and misread/read Maren's blanched knuckle live in dialogue.
