# Performance — *After Handover*, Scene 1 (*After Walkthrough*)

**Cast:**
- **House Lead:** `Maren Solis`
- **Counterpart:** `The Second`
- **Piece in Play:** `The Second's Key Ring`

---

**Opening Cue:**
Setting the night-watch binder beside the thirty-day provisional citation, your Second opens their other hand and sets the second set of house keys down on the office table within reach between you: “Every log and receipt licensing asked for is inside that cover, and I don't make five o'clock's balance without my hours here. Slide those keys back across the wood and I'll clear the citation and hold noon intake through handover—or take the ring off the table right now and I'll clear out while you still have an hour before you head downtown.”

**Turn 1 — House Lead (`Maren Solis`):**
Maren stands square-shouldered across the office table, sleeves pushed to her forearms, and pushes a loose notepad and pen tray to the corner until only the citation, the binder, and the brass key ring lie on the wood between them. Her left hand rests in plain sight on the tabletop, the pad of her thumb pressing hard against the side of her ring-finger knuckle until the skin blanches white. With her right hand, she picks up the key ring and snaps its clip onto her own belt loop.

"The ring stays on my belt. Stay through five, set your phone on the wood powered on, file those receipts against the citation, and hold noon intake off the inside latch while I'm downtown. At five I sign your hours and take you off the roster—or leave the binder and take the door now."

She sets her right hand flat on the table beside her left and waits without speaking.

**Turn 2 — The Second:**
The Second looks at the brass ring clipped to the House Lead's belt loop, presses a palm against the outside of their closed coat pocket instead of drawing the phone out, and leaves the night-watch binder and clipped receipts untouched beside the citation.

"Signing my hours at five doesn't put cash six blocks over by five, and turning my phone on behind an inside latch with no keys just brings that collector to these steps before you cut me from the roster anyway."

They button their coat at the throat, walk to the side-entry door, and pull it open, stepping across the threshold onto the stoop with one hand on the outer edge of the door.

"Everything from last night is under that binder clip, still unfiled against the citation. You've got both sets of keys on one belt now, nobody on noon intake, and under two hours before downtown closes."

**Turn 3 — House Lead (`Maren Solis`):**
Maren keeps her left hand flat in plain sight on the tabletop, the pad of her thumb pressing hard against the side of her ring-finger knuckle until the skin blanches white. With her right hand, she slides the night-watch binder across the wood, slips the metal clip off the maintenance receipts, and squares the stack beside the thirty-day citation. As she reaches to the corner of the table for the pen tray, the second brass key ring clicks once against her own set at her belt.

"Your hours through last night go out Monday so the sheet stays even. Pull that latch shut from the stoop. I've got twenty minutes to log this binder before I lock up for downtown."

She takes a pen from the tray, sets its point to the top line of the citation, and keeps her eyes on the page.

*[Hard cut]*

# Director's Note

### 1. How the Cast Was Staged & What Was Varied
- **Strict Context Isolation:** Two write-disabled performer sub-contexts (`actor-house-lead` and `actor-the-second`) were staged with one-turn-per-cue messaging and zero filesystem access. `House Lead` received the Play's Premise and Roles (`House Lead`, `The Second`), Scene 1's shared circumstances and fixed spoken facts, `Promptbook` acting/system rules, `persona-maren-solis.md` verbatim (kept strictly private to `House Lead`), and `piece-second-key-ring.md` verbatim. `The Second` received only the Play's Premise, Roles (`House Lead`, `The Second`), Scene 1 (`Counterpart Isolation`—no Scenes 2–4), `promptbook.md` verbatim, the fixed spoken pre-cue facts, and `piece-second-key-ring.md` verbatim, with zero access to `persona-maren-solis.md`.
- **What Was Varied:**
  1. *Mid-scene entry point (per Human brief):* Rather than starting at `play.md`'s threshold Opening Cue with `The Second`'s debt still concealed, the scene entered twenty minutes post-walkthrough at the exact table configuration anchored by `piece-second-key-ring.md`, with the dawn half-payment and 5:00 p.m. collector deadline already spoken aloud.
  2. *Unscripted key call & asymmetric stake shielding (Director staging choice):* `House Lead` was directed to make an unhedged call on the keys in Turn 1 without tilting between keeping/leaving, taking, or returning them, and `The Second`'s internal conditional rubric from `play.md` (*"If stripped of standing... if waved through..."*) was withheld from `House Lead`'s sheet so Maren had to act purely from her Persona and the room's material constraints.
  3. *Observable action grammar (Director staging choice):* Both actors were directed to use third-person present-tense physical stage behavior paired with direct spoken dialogue, stripping emotional adjectives and internal monologue per `Promptbook` and delivering cues verbatim without Director prologue.

### 2. What Happened in Performance
- **Turn 1 (`Maren Solis`):** Rather than accepting `The Second`'s clean binary (*slide the keys back* vs. *take the ring and I leave now*), Maren acted squarely out of her `Action` and `Shadow`—converting broken trust into stripped-down logistics that lock the deputy out before a second failure while calling the terms even. Clearing the tabletop clutter, she **took the brass key ring onto her own belt loop** and set a single take-it-or-leave-it condition: work through 5:00 p.m. without keys off the inside latch (pulling directly from `piece-second-key-ring.md`'s `Load Bearing` constraint), keep the phone powered on on the table, file the receipts against the citation, and get cut from the roster at five with hours signed.
- **Turn 2 (`The Second`):** `The Second` met Maren's severance terms from their real 5:00 p.m. stake and refused the trap: signed timesheet hours at five do not put cash six blocks over by five, and powering on their phone while locked behind an inside latch without keys only brings the collector to the house steps before they get cut anyway. Walking onto the side stoop, `The Second` landed the concrete consequence in the room: receipts left unfiled beside the citation, both sets of keys stranded on Maren's single belt, and noon intake completely uncovered with under two hours until the downtown parcel closing.
- **Turn 3 (`Maren Solis`) -> Hard Cut:** Refusing to bargain or call `The Second` back inside, Maren absorbed the collapse onto her own shoulders—the second brass ring clicking once against her own keys at her belt as she unclipped the receipts herself, promised Monday payroll through last night *"so the sheet stays even,"* told `The Second` to pull the latch shut from the stoop, and put pen to the citation with twenty minutes left before locking the house up to head downtown.

### 3. Authored Material Assessment: Gaps vs. Staging Choices
- **Authored Material Collision (Playwright decision needed) — `play.md` Scene 1 Opening vs. `piece-second-key-ring.md` (`Yearbook` & `Place`):**
  - In `play.md`, Scene 1's verbatim Opening Cue begins at the side-door threshold with keys in `The Second`'s hand, and `Reasons to Act` states that `The Second` wants to *"avoid exposing that private mess [the debt collector] unless how House Lead meets them earns or forces it."*
  - By contrast, `piece-second-key-ring.md` places the keys already on the office table (`Place`) and writes the collector threat, the dawn off-site half-payment, and the afternoon balance directly into `Yearbook`. In a standard run starting from `play.md`'s authored Opening Cue, supplying `piece-second-key-ring.md` verbatim to both cast contexts would leak `The Second`'s hidden motive to `House Lead` before Turn 1 and contradict `Fixed at the Opening`. This run avoided that leak only because the Human brief superseded `play.md`'s Opening Cue with a later mid-scene cue—something `promptbook.md` (*"Delivers every Opening Cue verbatim"*) forbids a Director from doing on their own. The Playwright should either move `play.md` Scene 1's opening to the table-key beat or pare `piece-second-key-ring.md`'s `Yearbook` back to shared pre-scene history so asymmetric secrets remain exclusively in the counterpart brief.
- **Authored Material Gap (Playwright decision needed) — `promptbook.md` `Knowledge` omits `Piece` sheets:**
  - `promptbook.md` (`Knowledge`) specifies exact document boundaries (`Counterpart Isolation`: *"Each Counterpart actor receives only the Play's Premise, their single scene, and this Promptbook"*), with no rule for standalone `Piece` files or whether `Yearbook` entries are shared knowledge across both roles. Adding an explicit `Piece` visibility rule to `promptbook.md` would remove ambiguity.
- **Minor Worldbuilding Tension (Optional Playwright clarification) — Shift hours vs. 5:00 p.m. cash liquidity:**
  - The Opening Cue states *"I don't make five o'clock's balance without my hours here,"* without specifying how Friday afternoon shift hours convert into cash before 5:00 p.m. (e.g., same-day petty-cash draw, pay-stub proof to buy time from the collector, or end-of-quarter payout). In Turn 2, `The Second` leveraged that ambiguity (*"Signing my hours at five doesn't put cash six blocks over by five"*) when Maren paired signing hours with firing them at five. It played convincingly here—getting cut at five kills any wage-advance or ongoing-pay leverage—but if the Playwright intends afternoon shift hours alone to satisfy the 5:00 p.m. balance mechanically, a half-line in Scene 1 establishing how quarter-end Friday pay clears would tighten the bind.
- **Director Staging Choices (Not Gaps in the Play/Persona/Piece):**
  - *Verbatim relay of `Maren` in Turn 1 action lines:* `House Lead` wrote third-person action lines using `Maren`, which was relayed verbatim to `The Second` for transcript integrity; `The Second` stayed strictly in role-framing (`the House Lead`) per `Promptbook`, and `persona-maren-solis.md` remained completely private.
  - *Back-to-back `Tell` activation:* Because both Turn 1 (locking `The Second` out of the keys) and Turn 3 (bearing the uncovered house alone) pressed squarely on Maren's `Shadow`, the performer surfaced her left-thumb knuckle `Tell` on both turns. In a 3-turn micro-scene, a Director can easily modulate that in rehearsal notes (*let the Tell fire once on the sharpest turn*) without changing a word of `persona-maren-solis.md`, whose `Projection`, `Action`, and `Shadow` drove Maren's exact choices cleanly.
