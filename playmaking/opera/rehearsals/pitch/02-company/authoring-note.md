# Authoring Note — *The Giant’s Shoe*

## Key Authoring Choices

1. **One Boot, No Giant (`piece-shoe.md`, `play.md`, `promptbook.md`):**
   To make the child’s fixed question (“Is the giant coming?”) press meaningfully on both volunteers without scripting their answer, the Third Ward workshop built **only** the eight-foot left boot after timber and shed space ran short. Both carriers know there is no giant rigged behind them—only a flatbed waiting thirty yards ahead beyond the east arch. Under **Dry comedy**, that gap frames a deadpan haulage reality (an ankle-down budget entry whose heel is folding mid-route); under **Quiet wonder**, that same absence makes whatever presence the giant has in the square depend entirely on how the carriers hold the boot and treat the question.

2. **Opposing Physical Stakes Across the Frame (`persona-toe-carrier.md`, `persona-heel-carrier.md`):**
   - **Maren (Toe Carrier)** cares about **civic schedule and public follow-through**: she co-signed for the Third Ward’s lead marshaling berth after last year’s forfeit, faces the east arch, and reads steady forward cadence as control (`Shadow`), feeling the rear trouble only as drag through the wooden welt.
   - **Tomas (Heel Carrier)** cares about **workmanship and material integrity**: he ribbed and repatched the rear counter himself in the parish shed after promising the workshop crew their entry would ride upright, sees and feels Tuesday night’s damp flour-paste seam folding directly over his knuckles, and resists admitting aloud in public that his patch is giving way (`Shadow`).
   Putting schedule-keeper at the blind front end and proud builder at the failing rear end generates immediate physical disagreement through the object itself without tilting either Persona toward wisecracks or lyricism.

3. **Spatial Pressure for Non-Verbal Choices (`place-square.md`):**
   `Market Square` withholds cover along the thirty-yard chalk straightaway while offering a waist-high stone plinth beside the dry fountain three paces off-line. Because shifting grip, checking pace, pressing straight for the arch, or angling toward the plinth are all concrete spatial moves in full view of the chalk edge, **acting** without speech and **staying silent** carry as much stage weight as **speaking**.

4. **Four-Beat Arc and Child Handling (`play.md`, `promptbook.md`):**
   The Stage/Director voice carries the child cleanly on a single fixed cue after one brief crossing-strain beat from each carrier (Turns 1–2), delivers “Is the giant coming?” verbatim with zero extra child lines, opens **one short exchange** by the scene (Turns 3–4, up to Turn 6 maximum across the Play), and closes immediately with the physical and human consequence.

## Seams and Tensions Noticed While Authoring `TO TUNE`

1. **`Undertow` Under Pure `Tenor` Substitution:**
   In `Pitch — TO TUNE`, `Undertow` is defined relationally: *“Name the counter-current that pulls against the Tenor.”* When holding `Undertow`, `Nerve`, and `Echo` byte-for-byte identical across two Pitches to isolate `Tenor` (*dry practicality of a parade logistics problem* vs. *quiet seriousness*), writing `Undertow` as the direct tonal opposite of one `Tenor` would make it redundant or self-contradictory under the other. To stay faithful to `TO TUNE` under strict `Tenor` isolation, `Undertow` had to hold both halves of the scene’s material tension—failing papier-mâché in tired hands *and* an onlooker taking the boot at face value—so whichever pole `Tenor` foregrounds, the remaining pole supplies a live counter-pull.

2. **Preventing Register Leakage in `Piece` and `Persona`:**
   `Yearbook` in `TO PLAY` and `Projection`/`Tell` in `TO PAST` naturally invite small stylistic flourishes during authoring (e.g., a punny float title immediately pre-loads dry comedy; reverent craft phrasing pre-loads quiet wonder). Keeping `Persona`, `Place`, and `Piece` strictly somatic, spatial, and institutional—wet flour paste, chalk marshaling lines, wooden carry-welts, finger-counting, lip-biting—was essential so register stayed genuinely unallocated until `Pitch` tuned the scope.

3. **Guarding Open Action/Silence Against Model Speech Priors:**
   Both registers tempt language models toward verbal performance—deadpan quips under dry practicality or gentle spoken assurances under quiet seriousness—which can crowd out `act` and `stay silent`. `Promptbook` (`Agent` and `System`) had to specify that an unspoken turn is still rendered as observable physical work with the shoe and breath so `Pitch` tunes *how* silence or movement lands rather than collapsing the open choice into dialogue.

4. **Where `Pitch` Attaches Between `Play` and `Promptbook`:**
   `TO TUNE` specifies that *“A Pitch tunes the scope that links it,”* while the five fixed HACKS chapters of `Promptbook` do not dedicate a standalone structural slot to `Pitch`. Linking `[pitch.md](pitch.md)` in `play.md` (to bind the Play’s scope) and referencing `[pitch.md](pitch.md)` across `Human`, `Agent`, and `System` in `promptbook.md` (to govern selection, role delivery, and register-only bounds) bridged the two cleanly without exposing multiple Pitch variants inside the production files.
