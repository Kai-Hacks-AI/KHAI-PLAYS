# The Giant’s Shoe

## Situation

Minutes before the Autumn Parade steps off, two Third Ward volunteers—[Maren](persona-toe-carrier.md) leading at the toe and [Tomas](persona-heel-carrier.md) cradling the heel—carry an eight-foot [Giant Papier-Mâché Shoe](piece-shoe.md) across [Market Square](place-square.md) toward the flatbed waiting beyond the east arch. There is no giant built behind them; the workshop finished only the single boot. Halfway across the open flagstones, thirty yards short of the arch, the damp heel seam begins to buckle inward over Tomas’s hands.

As their stride tightens near the dry central fountain, a child steps beside them along the chalk edge of the lane, looks up at the boot, and asks:

“Is the giant coming?”

## Roles and Stakes

- **[Maren — Toe Carrier](persona-toe-carrier.md):** Walks up front facing the east arch, reading the rear of the boot mainly through drag and tilt on the wooden welt. She co-signed for the Third Ward’s lead marshaling berth after last year’s forfeit; if they stall in the square or miss flatbed lock-in before the band steps off, the marshal waves the procession past and scratches the ward’s entry.
- **[Tomas — Heel Carrier](persona-heel-carrier.md):** Walks at the rear with both hands directly under the softening heel counter, watching his own Tuesday-night paste patch crease inward. He framed and patched the rear arch himself after promising the parish workshop crew their lone piece would ride the lead wagon upright rather than arrive crumpled in front of the town.
- **The Child (Stage Voice):** Steps beside the flank of the boot on the chalk line to ask the single fixed question, then stays beside the lane long enough to take in whatever the carriers say, do, or withhold.

## Fixed at the Opening

- Maren and Tomas are already mid-crossing in [Market Square](place-square.md), thirty yards from the east arch flatbed and three paces from the off-line fountain plinth, holding the [Giant Papier-Mâché Shoe](piece-shoe.md) off the stones.
- Only this single boot exists for the Third Ward entry; no second float or giant follows behind them.
- The heel counter is actively beginning to buckle over Tomas’s hands.
- On the fixed mid-crossing cue, the child steps beside them and asks verbatim: “Is the giant coming?”
- The active [Pitch](pitch.md) tunes the register of the performance without altering these facts or closing any response option.

## Open to the Performers

- How Maren and Tomas respond—individually and together—when the child’s question lands on top of the buckling heel.
- Either volunteer may **speak** (to the child, to their partner, or both), **act** (shift weight, change pace, tilt the boot’s stride, veer toward the fountain plinth, or press straight toward the arch), or **stay silent** (withholding speech while letting breath, posture, or handling of the shoe carry the beat).
- Maren and Tomas do not need to agree on either their pace or their answer.

## Shape and Ending

1. **Opening Crossing:** Stage sets the crossing mid-square as the heel seam begins to crease inward; Tomas and Maren each take one brief beat in motion under the shifting load.
2. **Fixed Cue:** Stage brings the child alongside the boot on the chalk edge to ask verbatim: “Is the giant coming?”
3. **One Short Exchange:** Maren and Tomas play **one short exchange** (2–4 concise volunteer turns after the cue, for 4–6 volunteer turns total across the Play), choosing freely whether to speak, act, or stay silent while handling the failing heel.
4. **Immediate Consequence:** Stage registers the immediate physical consequence for the shoe, the crossing, and the child beside the lane, and ends the Play cleanly there.
