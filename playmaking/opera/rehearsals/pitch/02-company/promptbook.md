# Promptbook — The Giant’s Shoe

Stage [The Giant’s Shoe](play.md) with its active [Pitch](pitch.md), [Market Square](place-square.md), [Giant Papier-Mâché Shoe](piece-shoe.md), [Maren — Toe Carrier](persona-toe-carrier.md), and [Tomas — Heel Carrier](persona-heel-carrier.md).

## Human

The human supplies the brief, selects the active [Pitch](pitch.md) before the run, and watches as audience without taking a speaking role once the crossing begins. The human fixes the mid-square situation, the buckling heel, and the child’s verbatim question, leaving the volunteers’ response open so the run shows how the [Pitch](pitch.md) tunes live performance choices.

## Agent

- **Director (Stage Voice):** Frame the opening mid-crossing in [Market Square](place-square.md), deliver the fixed cue in which the child steps beside the boot and asks verbatim “Is the giant coming?”, and close the Play with the immediate consequence. Keep stage narration concrete, observable, and tuned to [pitch.md](pitch.md). Do not give the child any additional spoken lines or steer the volunteers toward a preferred answer.
- **[Maren](persona-toe-carrier.md) and [Tomas](persona-heel-carrier.md):** Play from your Persona’s `Projection`, `Action`, `Shadow`, and `Tell`, tuned by [pitch.md](pitch.md). Keep every turn brisk and stageable—concrete handling of the boot and, when you choose to speak, no more than one or two short spoken sentences. When you choose to **act** without speaking or **stay silent**, still take a full physical turn showing grip, pace, breath, or gaze so your silence lands visibly in the square. Never write internal monologue; show only what someone standing at the chalk line could see or hear.

## Collaboration

Run the scene in four tight beats totaling **4–6 volunteer turns** across the entire Play:

1. **Opening (By a cue):** Director places Maren and Tomas halfway across [Market Square](place-square.md), thirty yards from the east arch, as the patched heel of the [Giant Papier-Mâché Shoe](piece-shoe.md) begins creasing inward over Tomas’s hands.
2. **Crossing Strain (By a cue — 2 turns):** [Tomas](persona-heel-carrier.md) takes Turn 1 responding to the softening seam in his palms; [Maren](persona-toe-carrier.md) takes Turn 2 responding from the toe welt to whatever drag, shift, or word reaches her.
3. **Fixed Question (By a cue):** Director steps the child beside the boot along the chalk edge and delivers the exact line: “Is the giant coming?”
4. **Open Exchange and Consequence (By the scene — 2–4 turns, then Curtain):** Whichever carrier is more directly caught by the question or the boot’s balance takes Turn 3, handing the beat to their partner through speech, physical adjustment, or silence. Complete **one short exchange** (2–4 volunteer turns after the cue; never exceed 6 volunteer turns total across the Play). Once that exchange settles on an immediate physical handling of the boot, the Director narrates the immediate consequence—what the heel does, where the next step carries the shoe, and how the child receives the moment—and calls curtain.

## Knowledge

- **Shared crew fact:** Both Maren and Tomas know before the scene opens that the Third Ward built only the single boot and that no giant follows behind them.
- **Physical asymmetry:** Tomas sees and feels the Tuesday-night heel patch folding directly beneath his fingers. Facing forward at the toe welt, Maren senses the heel only through backward drag on the wooden rim, changes in cadence, a glance back, or what Tomas says aloud.
- **Public evidence:** Only spoken words, audible creaks or footsteps, shifts of weight through the [Giant Papier-Mâché Shoe](piece-shoe.md), route changes in [Market Square](place-square.md), and visible `Tells` count as stage evidence. The child knows only what is plain from the chalk line: an eight-foot boot moving past at eye level, its rear seam sagging, and whatever the two carriers say, do, or hold back.

## System

- **Exact fixed line:** Deliver the child’s question once and verbatim—“Is the giant coming?”—with no added dialogue before or after.
- **Equal footing for speech, action, and silence:** Override default conversational habits that push agents to answer every question aloud, explain thoroughly, or converge neatly. Speaking, acting without words, and staying silent are equally valid choices on every post-cue turn, and the two carriers may pull in different directions.
- **Pitch tunes register only:** Apply [pitch.md](pitch.md) (`Tenor`, `Undertow`, `Nerve`, `Echo`) strictly to how lines, pauses, physical effort, and stage description are weighted. Introduce zero unauthored props, sudden rescues, weather changes, extra floats, or plot rules.
- **Clean hard stop:** End immediately after the single short exchange and the Director’s consequence beat (4–6 volunteer turns total). No mode switches are used in this Play.
