# *The Giant’s Shoe* — Staged Performance (`Dry comedy` — [pitch-dry-comedy.md](../02-company/pitch-dry-comedy.md))

**Stage:** Halfway across [Market Square](../02-company/place-square.md), thirty yards short of the east arch where the lead flatbed waits and brass instruments warm up out of sight, [Maren](../02-company/persona-toe-carrier.md) and [Tomas](../02-company/persona-heel-carrier.md) carry the Third Ward’s lone entry knee-high between white chalk marshaling lines: a single eight-foot russet papier-mâché left work boot ([piece-shoe.md](../02-company/piece-shoe.md)) with wrist-thick rope laces and painted brass eyelets, no giant or second float behind them. Early onlookers watch over the low rope barriers along the north and south flanks; three paces outside the chalk line sits the waist-high stone plinth of the dry central fountain. In the damp autumn air, as their boots take the gentle slope of uneven granite flagstones, the repatched seam under the rear heel counter goes soft over Tomas’s hands and begins creasing inward toward the stone.

**Tomas:** *Tomas draws his lower lip between his teeth. Keeping his shoulders squared low beneath the rear counter, he slides one broad, paste-flecked palm inward off the wooden carry-welt to press flat under the creasing heel seam, spreading his fingers wide against the damp cardboard and checking his stride on the sloped granite by half a pace.*

**Maren:** *Her right index finger taps three quick beats against the wooden carry-welt at the sudden drag; keeping her eyes fixed ahead on the east arch, she slides both hands forward along the painted toe rim, bears down slightly on the nose to counter the rear dip, and holds an even stride onto the next sloped flagstone.* "Thirty yards to the flatbed, Tomas—stay on the step."

**Stage:** Near the dry central fountain, three paces inside the waist-high stone plinth, Maren’s steady forward pull tautens the wooden carry-welt against the sagging rear counter—drawing the damp crease tighter over Tomas’s spread palm. A child ducks beneath the low rope barrier onto the white chalk edge beside the flank of the boot, matches their tightened stride at eye level with the russet shell and wrist-thick laces, looks up at the shoe, and asks:

“Is the giant coming?”

**Tomas:** *Tomas flicks a glance sideways toward the stone plinth beside the dry fountain, then draws his lower lip hard between his teeth as the tautened seam creases deeper over his knuckles. Dropping his shoulders lower, he clamps his left hand tight to the rear wooden carry-welt and wedges his paste-flecked right wrist and forearm flat beneath the bowing heel arch, lengthening his step to keep Maren’s cadence even as his boots scrape outward onto the white chalk edge nearest the plinth. He looks briefly down toward the child beside the flank and speaks on a short, tight breath.* "His boot's riding the lead wagon first—watch your toes on the chalk."

**Maren:** *Her right index finger taps a quick four-count against the wooden toe-welt as the shoe dips and pulls toward the fountain side; without looking back, she braces her left hip against the rim, hauls the nose squarely down center lane past the stone plinth, and slides both hands forward on the painted welt without shortening her stride. She cuts a brief, level glance down at the child beside the russet flank before fixing her eyes on the east arch again.* "East arch won't clear the rest of him—marshal's making him meet the wagon on the far side. Inside the chalk, Tomas."

**Tomas:** *Tomas draws his lower lip tight between his teeth as Maren’s haul jerks the frame inward and presses a deeper crease into the damp cardboard over his knuckles. Saying nothing, he steps off the chalk edge back inside the lane past the stone plinth, jams the rear curve of the wooden carry-welt hard against his hip against the forward tug of the frame, and slides his paste-flecked right palm and forearm higher beneath the bowing heel seam while his left hand locks onto the inner rim. Letting out a long, tight breath through his nose, he plants his boots a heavy half-pace shorter on the uneven granite, holding the rear counter knee-high off the stone.*

**Stage:** Caught between Maren’s forward haul at the painted toe and Tomas’s shortened, hip-braced plant at the rear, the damp heel seam groans softly and molds a deep trough along Tomas’s paste-flecked forearm—bowed low over the cracked inner rib, yet held clear of folding onto the flagstones by the flat of his arm. Their next uneven stride carries the eight-foot boot past the dry fountain’s stone plinth—leaving the last off-line rest behind—and straight down the open center lane toward the east arch twenty-five yards ahead. Stepping half a pace clear of the white chalk as warned, the child stands quiet beside the rope barrier, looking from the creased heel riding upright on Tomas’s sleeve to the top of the east arch ahead as the single oversized shoe crosses the open stone. *Curtain.*

# Director’s Note

### (a) How [pitch-dry-comedy.md](../02-company/pitch-dry-comedy.md) Shaped the Question and Performance
- **`Tenor` (*dry practicality of a parade logistics problem*):** When `"Is the giant coming?"` landed mid-stride, neither carrier punctured the illusion or stopped to explain the workshop’s timber shortage; instead, both translated the myth straight into freight, foot-traffic, and route-clearance logistics. Tomas answered on a compressed breath as a loading-order and safety warning (*"His boot's riding the lead wagon first—watch your toes on the chalk"*), and Maren immediately extended that premise into marshaling clearance while correcting her partner’s line (*"East arch won't clear the rest of him—marshal's making him meet the wagon on the far side. Inside the chalk, Tomas."*).
- **`Undertow` (*failing papier-mâché in tired hands beside an onlooker taking the boot at face value*):** Beneath their brisk answers, the physical strain stayed asymmetric and unspoken. Tomas guarded his softening Tuesday-night patch without words on Turns 1 and 5—drawing his lower lip between his teeth and feeding first his palm and then his entire right wrist and forearm under the damp crease rather than confessing aloud in the open square that his rear arch was folding. Up front, Maren never looked back, registering the failure only as drag and lateral drift tapped out in three- and four-counts by her right index finger against the wooden welt while keeping her eyes locked on the berth beyond the arch.
- **`Nerve` (*suspense over whether the softening heel survives whatever answer, gesture, or silence they risk mid-step*):** Every turn ratcheted mechanical tension across the wooden carry-welt. Maren’s Turn 2 forward press (*"stay on the step"*) pulled the damp crease tighter over Tomas’s palm right as the child stepped onto the chalk; Tomas’s Turn 3 drift onto the fountain-side chalk edge opened a fleeting line toward the waist-high stone plinth; Maren’s Turn 4 hip-brace hauled the nose back down center lane past the plinth; and Tomas’s silent Turn 5 compliance—stepping back inside the chalk while jamming the carry-welt into his hip a half-pace shorter—left the heel’s survival hanging on one forearm bearing opposing pulls over twenty-five yards of stone.
- **`Echo` (*one oversized shoe crossing open stone, and how much of a giant depends on how two tired people carry the weight*):** Pulling in opposite mechanical directions—Maren protecting the ward’s marshaling slot, Tomas shielding his parish-shed workmanship—the two volunteers ended up jointly constructing a giant large enough to be rerouted around the east arch, carried entirely by how two tired people refused to set one sagging boot down in front of the child.

### (b) Additional Direction Supplied as Director
- **Perceptual asymmetry across isolated sub-contexts:** Kept each Persona’s sheet private and filtered turn-by-turn cues strictly by position on the shoe—Maren received only the backward drag, half-pace cadence check, lateral scrape toward the fountain-side chalk edge, and Tomas’s tight-breathed words (noting she could see the rear counter only if she turned her head, which she never did), while Tomas felt Maren’s forward pulls and center-lane hip-haul directly in the creasing cardboard over his knuckles.
- **Turn routing, silence parity, and clean stop:** Explicitly weighted speech, wordless physical adjustment, and silence equally on every cue and barred internal monologue or outcome-narration beyond each carrier’s own step. After the fixed cue, I routed Turn 3 to Tomas first (caught simultaneously at the rear flank by Maren’s Turn 2 pull and the child stepping onto the chalk), handed Turn 4 to Maren, and gave Tomas Turn 5 to answer Maren’s haul past the stone plinth—calling curtain cleanly at 5 volunteer turns (2 pre-cue, 3 post-cue) once Tomas stepped back inside the chalk and braced the heel on his forearm.

### (c) Fixed Opening, Cue, and Staging Fit
- **Zero changes or strain in the authored Play:** The fixed mid-crossing opening, Company, and verbatim cue (`“Is the giant coming?”`) staged cleanly without modification. The spatial geometry in [place-square.md](../02-company/place-square.md) (three paces off-line to the fountain plinth versus thirty open yards to the east arch) paired tightly with Maren’s and Tomas’s opposing `Actions` and `Shadows`, giving Tomas’s Turn 3–5 chalk-edge drift and return real physical stakes without needing extra dialogue.
- **Staging choice vs. Play gap:** [piece-shoe.md](../02-company/piece-shoe.md) bundles shared workshop build history with active rear-heel failure details (`Apparent` and `Yearbook`). That is an asset sheet rather than a flaw in the Play, so as a staging choice to honor [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md)’s physical-asymmetry rule, I supplied Maren with the shared crew build baseline (the single-boot scaling decision and Tuesday roof-leak repatch) while keeping the west-curb inner-rib crack and active inward folding out of her view unless she glanced back or Tomas named it aloud.
