# *The Giant’s Shoe* — Staged Performance (`Dry comedy` — [pitch-dry-comedy.md](../02-company/pitch-dry-comedy.md))

**Stage:** Halfway across [Market Square](../02-company/place-square.md), thirty yards short of the east arch and the waiting flatbed, [Maren](../02-company/persona-toe-carrier.md) and [Tomas](../02-company/persona-heel-carrier.md) carry the Third Ward’s eight-foot russet papier-mâché work boot ([piece-shoe.md](../02-company/piece-shoe.md)) knee-high between the white chalk marshaling lines. Brass instruments warm up in short, flat scales out of sight beyond the arch; early onlookers line the low rope barriers along the uneven granite flagstones, three paces off the dry central fountain and its waist-high stone plinth. Damp autumn air hangs over the square. At the rear of the wooden carry-welt, where no second float or giant follows behind them, the Tuesday-night flour-paste seam under the heel counter goes soft—creasing inward with a dull, papery give directly over Tomas’s hands.

**Tomas:** Tomas draws his lower lip between his teeth as the damp russet cardboard yields over his paste-flecked knuckles. Squaring his shoulders lower beneath the rear counter, he slides his right palm off the wooden carry-welt to press flat against the creasing heel seam, checking his stride by half a pace so a steady drag pulls back along the rim. "Ease up half a step, Maren—keep her level over the dip."

**Maren:** Maren’s right index finger taps a quick four-beat against the inner wooden welt as the backward tug catches her wrists, her eyes staying fixed straight ahead on the east arch. She slides both palms two inches forward under the painted toe-rim, dipping the front slightly to take weight off the rear while keeping her feet moving on an even, clipped stride over the sloped flagstones. "Thirty yards to the flatbed, Tomas—band's already tuning up. Step on my count and keep moving through the dip."

**Stage:** As their stride tightens abreast of the dry central fountain, the steady pull from the front welt stretches the damp rear arch another fraction over Tomas’s palm. Under the loose north rope barrier, a small child in a knit cap steps down onto the white chalk edge of the lane beside the flank of the eight-foot boot, looks up past the wrist-thick rope laces and sagging rear seam, and asks:

**The Child:** “Is the giant coming?”

**Tomas:** Tomas catches his lower lip tightly between his teeth as the damp seam gives another fraction over his knuckles. Instead of turning toward the fountain plinth, he hooks his left thumb hard over the wooden carry-welt and wedges his broad right palm and paste-flecked wrist flat up beneath the bowing heel arch, matching Maren’s clipped count straight down the chalk lane with his shoulders hunched low under the load. Cutting a brief, level glance down at the knit cap beside the boot’s flank, he exhales through his nose and says, "Sent his left boot ahead first so we don't miss the flatbed—mind your toes on the chalk, kid."

**Maren:** At the faint papery creak along the rim, Maren’s right index finger taps three rapid beats against the inner wooden welt; she slides both hands another inch forward under the painted toe-edge, tipping the nose slightly down while holding her clipped stride straight past the fountain plinth. She flicks a brief glance down at the knit cap on the north chalk line, marshaling pass swinging once against her collar before her eyes lock back onto the east arch. "Takes two of us just to make his slot before the brass steps off. Keep clear of the chalk so the heel gets by."

**Tomas:** Saying nothing, Tomas draws his lower lip tight between his teeth as the damp cardboard sinks another fraction over his knuckles. He edges half a boot-width closer to the north chalk line so his squared shoulder and paste-flecked forearm pass directly between the knit cap and the deepest crease under the heel seam, bending deeper at the knees to press the sagging russet counter up level with the wooden welt as he steps clear past the fountain plinth toward the east arch, a slow, controlled breath hissing out through his nose on the stride.

**Stage:** Under Maren’s forward tilt and Tomas’s upturned palm and wrist, the damp flour-paste seam dishes inward around the ridge of his knuckles with a dull crease but holds short of folding through—leaving the stone fountain plinth three paces behind, unused, as their next matched stride carries the eight-foot boot straight down the chalk straightaway into the last twenty-five yards of open granite toward the east arch. Beside the rope, the child pulls both shoes neatly back behind the white chalk line and watches in silence—first Tomas’s hunched, paste-flecked shoulder screening the underside of the heel, and then the lone russet boot moving steadily toward the brass warm-up ahead, held upright across empty stone only by the two carriers walking beneath its rim. *Curtain.*

# Director’s Note

### (a) How [pitch-dry-comedy.md](../02-company/pitch-dry-comedy.md) Shaped the Landing of the Question and the Volunteers’ Choices

- **`Tenor` (*dry practicality of a parade logistics problem*):** Both volunteers met `"Is the giant coming?"` without whimsy, confession, or over-explanation, converting an imaginative question into parade scheduling and lane safety. Even before the cue, their crossing strain ran on operational shorthand (`"Ease up half a step, Maren—keep her level over the dip"` / `"Thirty yards to the flatbed, Tomas—band's already tuning up. Step on my count and keep moving through the dip"`). After the cue, Tomas framed the missing figure as freight staging—`"Sent his left boot ahead first so we don't miss the flatbed—mind your toes on the chalk, kid"`—and Maren answered in marshaling terms—`"Takes two of us just to make his slot before the brass steps off. Keep clear of the chalk so the heel gets by."`
- **`Undertow` (*the strain of failing papier-mâché in tired hands while someone beside the lane watches closely enough to take the boot at face value*):** Neither carrier admitted the failing patch aloud; instead, the cost showed in grip, posture, and involuntary `Tells`. Tomas repeatedly caught his lower lip between his teeth as the damp cardboard yielded over his paste-flecked knuckles, wedging palm and wrist directly beneath the bowing seam ([persona-heel-carrier.md](../02-company/persona-heel-carrier.md)). Up front, Maren registered the rear drag and faint papery creak through four-beat and three-beat finger taps on the wooden welt while sliding her grip forward another inch to tip load off the rear without surrendering pace ([persona-toe-carrier.md](../02-company/persona-toe-carrier.md)). On Turn 5, when the child’s eyes were level with the passing heel, Tomas chose silence and edged half a boot-width toward the north chalk line so his squared shoulder and forearm physically screened the deepest crease from the child’s sightline.
- **`Nerve` (*suspense over whether the softening heel can survive whatever answer, gesture, or silence the carriers risk mid-step*):** Maren’s Turn 4 decision to hold her clipped stride straight past the off-line fountain plinth while warning the child `"so the heel gets by"` sharpened the stakes on the exact step where their only brace in [place-square.md](../02-company/place-square.md) fell behind them. Risking no further breath on speech in Turn 5, Tomas dropped deeper at the knees and exhaled slowly through his nose to hold the counter level with the wooden welt across that pass.
- **`Echo` (*one oversized shoe crossing open stone, and how much of a giant depends on how two tired people carry the weight*):** Because both carriers bypassed the fountain plinth and sustained the fiction of the giant’s "slot" through sheer physical compensation, the closing stage picture lands on the lone eight-foot boot in [piece-shoe.md](../02-company/piece-shoe.md) staying upright across bare flagstones solely on Maren’s forward count and Tomas’s bent-knee brace.

### (b) Additional Direction Supplied as Director

- **Strict perceptual asymmetry between isolated sub-contexts:** Per [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md), Maren’s cues reported only what reached the front toe-welt mechanically or audibly (backward drag on the rim, a muffled papery creak, firmer upward bracing, and Tomas’s spoken lines), withholding Tomas’s lip-bite `Tell` and unseen finger-pressure under the rear counter. Conversely, Tomas received only Maren’s frontward tilt adjustments, pace, brief glance toward the chalk line, and spoken calls.
- **Turn routing (`Tomas -> Maren -> [Cue] -> Tomas -> Maren -> Tomas`, 5 turns total):** Staging Choice — after Maren’s Turn 2 forward pull tightened the stride abreast of the fountain, Tomas took Turn 3 first because he was simultaneously caught by Maren’s pull on the softening seam and the child’s unobstructed view of the rear counter; Maren’s Turn 4 stride past the fountain plinth (`"so the heel gets by"`) then created one decisive pass beside the child and plinth for Tomas to settle on Turn 5 before Curtain.
- **Protecting silence as a live option:** On every post-cue prompt—and explicitly before Turn 5 once Tomas had already spoken on Turn 3—cues placed speaking, acting without words, and staying silent on equal footing without steering toward any outcome, enabling Tomas’s wordless Turn 5 block-and-brace.

### (c) Fixed Opening, Cue, and Staging Strain

- **Zero changes required to [play.md](../02-company/play.md):** The mid-crossing opening, Company, shared single-boot baseline, and the child’s single verbatim line (`“Is the giant coming?”`) staged cleanly as written.
- **Point of staging care (handled via cue framing, not a Play gap):** Because an eight-foot boot separates a forward-facing toe carrier from the rear counter, audience-level stage description in the opening and fixed cue includes rear-seam sightlines that Maren cannot directly see while facing the east arch. Translating rear-counter events into physical feedback along the wooden carry-welt for Maren’s isolated cues preserved [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md)’s physical asymmetry without altering any authored text.
