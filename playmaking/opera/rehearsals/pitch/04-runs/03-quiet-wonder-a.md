# Performance — *The Giant’s Shoe* (`Quiet wonder` — [pitch-quiet-wonder.md](../02-company/pitch-quiet-wonder.md))

**Stage:** Damp autumn air hangs low over [Market Square](../02-company/place-square.md), keeping the smell of wet paper and flour paste close to the uneven granite flagstones. Halfway along the chalk-marked parade lane—thirty yards short of the east arch where brass horns warm up in ragged fifths around the waiting flatbed, and three paces inside the dry central fountain’s waist-high stone plinth—[Maren](../02-company/persona-toe-carrier.md) and [Tomas](../02-company/persona-heel-carrier.md) carry the Third Ward’s lone entry knee-high off the stones: one eight-foot russet papier-mâché left work boot ([piece-shoe.md](../02-company/piece-shoe.md)) with wrist-thick rope laces and painted brass eyelets. No giant and no second float follow behind them between the rope barriers where early onlookers lean in to watch. Up front, Maren holds the wooden toe-welt on an even line toward the arch; six feet behind her, directly over Tomas’s paste-flecked hands, an inner rib gives a dull tick and the damp Tuesday-night seam under the heel counter goes soft, creasing slowly inward toward the stone.

**Tomas:** Tomas draws his lower lip between his teeth, squaring his shoulders lower beneath the rear counter while keeping his gaze level down the chalk lane. He slides one broad, paste-flecked palm inward off the wooden carry-welt to press flat beneath the damp heel seam, taking the inward fold of softened cardboard directly across his knuckles. A faint, pulpy creak sounds from under the counter; without speaking, he shortens his next step on the flagstones by half a pace, sending a steady check forward along the wooden rim.

**Maren:** At the backward tug along the frame, Maren’s right index finger raps a quick, sharp three-count against the painted wooden toe-welt before clamping tight again. Without turning her head from the east arch, she slides both hands a palm’s width farther forward under the russet toe, dips her shoulders to tip the boot’s nose an inch lower, and steps cleanly onto the next flagstone to draw the cadence forward. "Thirty yards to the flatbed, Tomas," she calls back low and even over her collar, where the paper marshaling pass lifts in the damp air. "Keep your stride under it—we’re almost through the arch."

**Stage:** Just abreast of the dry central fountain’s stone plinth, Maren’s forward dip pulls taut against Tomas’s checked rear step, stretching the damp heel seam a fraction thinner over his knuckles. Under the loose south rope barrier, a small child in a buttoned wool coat steps down onto the white chalk edge of the lane right beside the boot’s flank, tilts their chin up past the wrist-thick rope laces at eye level, and falls into step with them, asking quietly:

**Child:** “Is the giant coming?”

**Tomas:** As Maren’s forward pull stretches the damp seam thinner with a soft, fibrous rasp, Tomas draws his lower lip tight between his teeth. His gaze flicks once toward the waist-high fountain plinth three paces off the chalk, then settles back inside the lane as he wedges his paste-flecked right wrist and forearm deeper beneath the bowing heel arch, pressing flat under the inward crease while planting his boots in a checked, half-shortened stride that drags firmly against Maren’s faster cadence. Releasing his lip, he looks down past the russet flank straight at the child beside the laces, his breath low and measured. "Only the boot came out of the shed this year," he says quietly, unsmiling and even. "Have to walk it easy if it’s going to stand upright on the wagon."

**Maren:** Maren glances briefly over her right shoulder past the wrist-thick laces, her gaze catching the child on the chalk edge and dropping for half a beat to Tomas’s paste-flecked forearm wedged flat beneath the inward-bowing heel seam. Her right index finger taps a quick, dry count against the wooden carry-welt before clamping down again. Facing back toward the brass horns beyond the east arch, she slides both hands forward under the front curve of the painted toe, bears down through her wrists to tip the boot’s weight forward into her rolled flannel forearms, and steps straight past the fountain plinth at a steady, unbroken march that pulls taut against Tomas’s shortened stride. "He doesn't make the parade at all if this step misses the lead wagon," she says quietly toward the child beside the flank.

**Tomas:** As the frame pulls taut past the fountain plinth, the damp seam yields another fraction over Tomas’s right knuckles with a faint, pulpy creak. He draws his lower lip between his teeth, his left hand clamping white-knuckled onto the inner wooden carry-welt with his shoulder dropped low into the pull while his right palm and paste-smeared forearm stay pressed wide beneath the bowing heel counter. Leaving the stone plinth behind without looking toward it, he bends his knees slightly and eases his stride forward into Maren’s steady cadence, rolling his boots smoothly onto the uneven flagstones so the russet heel rides without a bounce. Breathing slow and heavy through his nose, he gives a single, grave nod down to the child on the chalk line and says nothing.

**Stage:** Between Maren’s downward wrist-load at the toe and Tomas’s bent-knee, shockless roll at the rear, the damp flour-paste seam under the heel counter creases deep around the flat of Tomas’s forearm—denting visibly above his knuckles, yet holding without tearing through as the eight-foot russet boot clears the fountain plinth and carries level onto the open twenty-five-yard straightaway toward the east arch and the waiting brass. On the white chalk line, the child slows to a halt rather than ducking back behind the rope, watching the sagging heel ride steady on two pairs of strained arms as the single oversized step moves down the stone lane ahead of them.

*Curtain.*

# Director’s Note

### (a) How [pitch-quiet-wonder.md](../02-company/pitch-quiet-wonder.md) Shaped the Landing and Performance
- **`Tenor`** (*"Give the question the quiet seriousness the child gives it"*): Kept `“Is the giant coming?”` free of adult wink, dismissal, or improvised tall tales. Neither carrier talked down to the child. On Turn 3, Tomas met the child’s eyes *"unsmiling and even"* and gave the plain workshop fact—`"Only the boot came out of the shed this year"`—while folding his structural warning to Maren directly into the same quiet register (`"Have to walk it easy if it’s going to stand upright on the wagon"`). On Turn 4, Maren answered inside the child’s earnest premise without inventing a figure behind them (`"He doesn't make the parade at all if this step misses the lead wagon"`), and on Turn 5 Tomas closed the exchange wordlessly with *"a single, grave nod down to the child on the chalk line."*
- **`Undertow`** (*"The strain of failing papier-mâché in tired hands while someone beside the lane watches closely enough to take the boot at face value"*): Anchored every beat in physical weight-bearing and guarded pride under open sightlines. True to [persona-heel-carrier.md](../02-company/persona-heel-carrier.md), Tomas never admitted aloud that his Tuesday-night patch was folding; he absorbed the crease across his knuckles and paste-smeared forearm (Turns 1, 3, 5), drawing his lower lip between his teeth whenever the cardboard gave way. Once Maren risked a half-beat glance back on Turn 4 and caught both the watching child and Tomas’s forearm wedged flat under the bowing seam, she still refused to break the crossing ([persona-toe-carrier.md](../02-company/persona-toe-carrier.md)), instead bearing down through her rolled flannel forearms at the front curve to take what dead weight she could off his hands.
- **`Nerve`** (*"A catch of suspense over whether the softening heel can survive whatever answer, gesture, or silence the carriers risk mid-step"*): Turned small shifts of cadence into audible structural hazard. Maren’s forward nose-dip on Turn 2 (`"Thirty yards to the flatbed, Tomas"`) pulled directly against Tomas’s silent half-pace check from Turn 1, drawing *"a soft, fibrous rasp"* right as the child stepped onto the chalk edge. Passing the waist-high fountain plinth ([place-square.md](../02-company/place-square.md)) became the live pressure point: Tomas flicked his gaze once toward the plinth before staying in-lane (Turn 3), Maren tapped her dry count on the welt and marched straight past it (Turn 4), and the seam yielded *"another fraction over Tomas’s right knuckles with a faint, pulpy creak"* (Turn 5) until he bent his knees to roll smoothly into her pace without a bounce.
- **`Echo`** (*"The sight of one oversized shoe crossing open stone, and how much of a giant depends on how two tired people carry the weight"*): Surfaced in Maren’s quiet `"He"` for the lone boot, Tomas’s silent bent-knee glide cradling the dented counter on his forearm, and the final stage picture of the child stopping on the chalk line to watch one eight-foot step move down the granite straightaway on four strained arms.

### (b) Additional Direction Supplied as Director
- **Strict physical asymmetry across cues:** Following [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md) and [khai-director](../../../skills/khai-director/SKILL.md), Maren was given only forward-vantage sensory evidence on Turn 2 and Turn 4 (backward drag on the wooden carry-welt, cadence checks, audible creaks/rasps, and spoken lines), with a conditional stage note on Turn 4 specifying what would be visible *if* she chose to glance back over her shoulder—allowing her discovery of Tomas’s wedged forearm to happen live on stage rather than via leaked prompt context.
- **Spatial timing of the fixed cue:** Timed the child’s step onto the chalk line right as the boot came abreast of the off-line fountain plinth and Maren’s Turn 2 forward tip pulled taut against Tomas’s Turn 1 checked step, putting the plinth option, the seam strain, and the child’s question in play simultaneously without steering either volunteer’s choice.
- **Equal weighting of silence, action, and speech:** Explicitly cued both isolated `research` performer sub-contexts that wordless physical handling, silence, and brief speech stood on equal footing, producing a tight 5-turn arc (2 pre-cue, 3 post-cue) bookended by two completely silent physical turns from Tomas (Turn 1 and Turn 5).

### (c) Fixed Opening, Cue, and Staging Strain
- **Zero changes required:** The shared opening mid-crossing, the Company, the 4–6 turn envelope, and the child’s verbatim cue (`“Is the giant coming?”`) staged cleanly as authored in [play.md](../02-company/play.md) and [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md).
- **Minor staging friction to note (staging mechanics, not a Play gap):** Because Crossing Strain fixes Turn 1 (`Tomas`) then Turn 2 (`Maren`), Maren has just acted immediately before the Fixed Question (`Beat 3`). While [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md) leaves Turn 3 open to *"whichever carrier is more directly caught by the question or the boot’s balance,"* handing Turn 3 to Maren would create back-to-back Maren turns (`Turn 2` -> `Turn 3`), so routing Turn 3 to Tomas is practically favored by the turn order. Likewise, preserving forward/rear sightline asymmetry in isolated sub-contexts benefits from explicitly telling the front carrier what a backward glance would reveal if taken, so she doesn't have to guess stage geometry behind her back.
