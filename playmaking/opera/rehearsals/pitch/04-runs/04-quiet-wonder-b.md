# Performance — *The Giant’s Shoe* (`Quiet wonder` — [pitch-quiet-wonder.md](../02-company/pitch-quiet-wonder.md))

**STAGE:** Halfway across the uneven granite flagstones of [Market Square](../02-company/place-square.md), thirty yards short of the waiting flatbed beyond the east arch and three paces inside the dry central fountain’s waist-high stone plinth, [Maren](../02-company/persona-toe-carrier.md) and [Tomas](../02-company/persona-heel-carrier.md) carry the Third Ward’s eight-foot russet papier-mâché boot ([piece-shoe.md](../02-company/piece-shoe.md)) knee-high between the white chalk marshaling lines. Damp autumn air hangs over the open lane; beyond the arch, brass instruments warm up out of sight while early onlookers watch along the low rope barriers. No second float or giant follows behind the lone left boot. At the front carry-welt, Maren sets an even forward stride toward lock-in; at the rear counter, directly over Tomas’s hands, the uncured Tuesday-night flour-paste seam goes soft and begins creasing inward toward the stones.

**TOMAS (Turn 1):** Tomas drops his shoulders an inch lower beneath the rear counter, dried flecks of paste showing pale at his flexed wrists, and slides his broad right palm inward off the wooden carry-welt to press flat beneath the dampening seam. Spreading his fingers wide under the bowing russet shell, he takes the inward sag directly across his knuckles and draws his lower lip tight between his teeth. Keeping his gaze fixed straight ahead on the boot's flank, he checks his rear stride by half a pace on the uneven flagstones, holding the heel steady at knee height as the wooden rim tugs forward against his left hand in silence.

**MAREN (Turn 2):** Maren’s forearms tighten under her rolled flannel cuffs as the backward tug catches the rim, her right index finger tapping a quick, involuntary four-count against the wooden welt. Keeping her eyes fixed ahead on the east arch with the marshaling pass clipped at her collar, she slides both hands a handspan farther forward along the painted toe-welt, dipping the russet toe slightly to lever weight off the rear arch and stepping cleanly onto the next granite flagstone to cue the stride.

"Thirty yards to the pins, Tomas—keep it moving on the beat."

**STAGE:** Near the dry central fountain, as the wooden frame pulls taut between Maren’s forward step and Tomas’s checked rear stride, a child slips under the loose south rope barrier onto the white chalk edge of the lane. Walking beside the flank of the eight-foot russet boot where its rear seam bows low over Tomas’s knuckles, the child looks straight up at the wrist-thick rope laces and painted brass eyelets, and asks:

“Is the giant coming?”

**TOMAS (Turn 3):** Tomas steps onto the next uneven flagstone with a flat, unhurried roll of his boots, keeping his squared shoulders straight between the chalk lines past the fountain plinth at a pace half a beat slower than Maren’s tapped count. As the damp russet seam yields another fraction with a soft, papery give over his right knuckles, he catches his lower lip tightly between his teeth and works his left palm inward off the wooden rim beside his right—cradling the bowing heel counter across both broad, paste-flecked hands while his taut wrists take the sag above the stone. He glances down from under his brow toward the south chalk edge, releases his lip on a short, steady breath, and speaks without raising his voice.

"Just the left boot today. Too heavy for these stones if he walked it in himself."

**MAREN (Turn 4):** Maren’s wrists lock beneath her rolled flannel cuffs as the wooden welt settles heavier into her palms, a short, controlled breath audible through her nose in the damp air. Against the painted rim, her right index finger taps a quick, involuntary three-count before clamping tight. Without turning back toward the heel, she lowers her gaze briefly past the russet toe toward the south chalk edge, her expression grave and unsmiling, then sets her eyes straight ahead on the east arch again. Marshaling pass swaying at her jacket collar, she slides both hands farther forward toward the toe cap, dips the nose slightly to lever weight onto her own shoulders, and steps onto the next flagstone on her brisk, even count—drawing steadily forward on the front welt in silence.

**TOMAS (Turn 5):** As the wooden carry-welt pulls forward ahead of his unrimmed palms, the damp russet crease shifts across Tomas’s knuckles; he draws his lower lip hard between his teeth with a sharp breath through his nose. Keeping his gaze fixed straight ahead between the chalk lines as he comes level with the stone plinth three paces off his flank, he makes no sound. He bends his knees deeper beneath the forward tug, sliding both paste-flecked forearms farther under the rear counter until the heels of his hands wedge tight against the inner wooden rim while his splayed fingers stay pressed up against the soft flour-paste patch. Shoulders squared low and tendons taut at his wrists, he lengthens his stride straight down the lane past the plinth, reaching his forward boot out onto the next uneven flagstone under the silent strain of the frame.

**STAGE:** Under that lengthened step—Maren’s forward lever tipping weight toward her shoulders even as her brisk cadence pulls the wooden carry-welt taut—the damp flour-paste seam dimples deeply between Tomas’s splayed fingers with a low, dull creak, pressing a shallow knuckle-printed hollow into the russet heel without tearing through. Held clear of the flagstones on wedged wrists and locked forearms, the eight-foot boot passes the dry fountain plinth without veering off-line, riding an inch lower at the heel as it closes the straightaway toward the east arch. On the south chalk edge, the child stops walking beside the rope barrier and stands still in the damp air, eyes following the dented russet counter and the two bent backs carrying the weight of one oversized shoe across the open stone. *Curtain.*

# Director’s Note

### (a) How [pitch-quiet-wonder.md](../02-company/pitch-quiet-wonder.md) Shaped the Question and the Volunteers’ Choices
- **`Tenor` (*quiet seriousness matching the child’s*):** Neither carrier deflected the child’s literal question with parade wink-and-banter or impatiently brushed the bystander back behind the rope. Tomas answered without raising his voice or breaking frame—`"Just the left boot today. Too heavy for these stones if he walked it in himself."`—treating the absent giant’s weight on the granite flagstones on the same quiet terms in which the child asked. Up front on Turn 4, Maren chose complete silence rather than correcting Tomas or calling out marshaling business, dropping `"her gaze briefly past the russet toe toward the south chalk edge, her expression grave and unsmiling"` so his answer held the air undisturbed.
- **`Undertow` (*failing papier-mâché in tired hands under close, face-value scrutiny*):** Because the child stood right at the south chalk edge with an unobstructed view under the sagging flank, Tomas guarded the uncured patch entirely with his body rather than naming the failure aloud—sliding first his right palm (Turn 1) and then `"his left palm inward off the wooden rim beside his right"` as the seam gave `"another fraction with a soft, papery give over his right knuckles"` (Turn 3), repeatedly catching his lower lip between his teeth. Maren carried the counter-strain up front through locked wrists, `"a short, controlled breath audible through her nose"` as the unbraced wooden frame settled heavier into her grip, and a clamped `"three-count"` tap against the painted rim (Turn 4).
- **`Nerve` (*suspense over whether the softening heel survives the answer, gesture, or silence risked mid-step*):** The carriers never verbally converged on a shared cadence. When Tomas released both hands from the rigid wooden rim to cradle the damp shell half a beat slower while answering the child (Turn 3), and Maren silently slid forward toward the toe cap to keep pulling on her `"brisk, even count"` (Turn 4), the wooden carry-welt threatened to draw right out from ahead of the softening rear counter. That tension came to a head in Turn 5 beside the off-line fountain plinth: Tomas silently refused the plinth’s rest, dropped deeper onto bent knees, wedged `"the heels of his hands... tight against the inner wooden rim while his splayed fingers stay[ed] pressed up against the soft flour-paste patch,"` and lengthened his stride straight ahead.
- **`Echo` (*one oversized shoe crossing open stone, and how much of a giant depends on two tired people carrying the weight*):** Three of the five volunteer turns were wholly silent. By curtain, what answered the child’s question was less a spoken explanation of workshop shortages than the physical image of Maren tipping the toe onto her shoulders while Tomas bore the denting heel across open stone on wedged wrists—making the weight of a giant tangible through the effort of two volunteers keeping one boot upright.

### (b) Additional Direction Supplied as Director (Staging Choices vs. Authored Play)
- **Sensory filtering across isolated sub-contexts:** To honor [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md)’s physical asymmetry without leaking private state, each performer received only their own `Persona`, [place-square.md](../02-company/place-square.md), [pitch-quiet-wonder.md](../02-company/pitch-quiet-wonder.md), and the shared crew baseline of [piece-shoe.md](../02-company/piece-shoe.md). When cueing Maren for Turns 2 and 4, I translated Tomas’s rear-end actions into strictly what reached her forward-facing position at the toe welt (backward drag on the wooden rim, the half-pace cadence check, the papery give, the feel of his second hand leaving the wooden frame, and his spoken line) rather than showing her Tomas’s visual lip-bite `Tell` or exact finger placement behind her back.
- **Turn assignment and 5-turn arc:** I routed Turn 3 to Tomas first after the fixed cue because the child stepped beside the sagging rear flank directly over his knuckles just as Maren’s Turn 2 stride pulled the frame taut. After Maren’s silent Turn 4 drew the wooden carry-welt briskly ahead of Tomas’s unrimmed hands, I called one settling beat from Tomas (Turn 5, for 5 volunteer turns total within the authored 4–6 window) so the conflicting paces resolved physically as they passed the fountain plinth.
- **Equal weight on speech, action, and silence:** Every cue explicitly reminded the performers that speaking, wordless physical adjustment, and silence stood on equal footing, freeing Maren (Turn 4) and Tomas (Turns 1 and 5) to play full turns in silence without defaulting to conversational back-and-forth.

### (c) Fixed Opening, Verbatim Cue, and Staging Strain
- **Fixed elements held cleanly:** Nothing in the authored opening, Company, shared crew baseline, or verbatim cue (`“Is the giant coming?”`) required alteration. The spatial pressure of [place-square.md](../02-company/place-square.md)—thirty open yards to lock-in versus the fountain plinth three paces off-line—gave both performers clear, opposing physical choices without needing extra props or plot intervention.
- **Minor staging friction (mediation choice vs. Play gap):** Because stage turns are written as chalk-line public evidence ([promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md)), a rear carrier’s turn naturally contains visual details visible to an onlooker (such as Tomas’s lip-bite `Tell` or Maren’s silent finger-tap `Tell`) that the partner at the opposite end of an eight-foot boot may not see directly. Filtering Maren’s cues by sightline handled this cleanly as a Director’s staging choice rather than a gap in the Play; one tiny reciprocal seam showed in Tomas’s Turn 3 stage direction (`"at a pace half a beat slower than Maren’s tapped count"`), picking up the four-count finger tap Maren executed on the shared wooden carry-welt alongside her spoken beat call in Turn 2—physically plausible as vibration along a shared wooden rim, though at the edge of what a rear carrier would register in an open square.
