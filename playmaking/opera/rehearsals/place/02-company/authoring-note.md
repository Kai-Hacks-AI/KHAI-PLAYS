# Authoring Note: How the Two Places Differ

Both Places draw directly on the two spatial seeds in the *Mother's Painting* material—the cleared upstairs flat and the propped-open building entrance—while staging the same unresolved starting condition: the movers are gone, Mother's flat is cleared, and the painting rests unclaimed on neutral ground before four siblings who each want to take it home.

1. **Enclosed memory-room vs. porous public threshold:** `place-mothers-flat.md` seals the siblings inside an echoing interior that still holds the physical referents of their memories (the real kitchen window Ben sat beside, the bare hook Ada used, the room Cora knew from Mother's photograph, the skirting board exposing the frame Dan never mended). The room testifies silently, making departure feel like locking Mother away. `place-building-entrance.md` locks those referents upstairs out of sight and wedges the scene open to street air, passersby, and exhaust—detaching the painting from the window it depicts so anyone who wants it for personal reasons must speak that attachment out loud in semi-public view.
2. **Stillness vs. momentum:** In `place-mothers-flat.md`, stillness is the default; nothing moves until someone lifts the canvas from the skirting board or answers the buzzer, and stripping away boxes and tools leaves Ada without tasks to assign and Ben without items to trade for a "fair" split. In `place-building-entrance.md`, departure is already idling at the kerb; transport rules, weather on the split frame, and boot space hand Ada and Ben ready logistical cover, tempt Cora toward a casual kerbside offer, and sharpen Dan's suspicion that the car at the kerb means a decision is already in motion without him.
