# Mother's Flat

## Taxonomy

Upper-floor flat in Mother's apartment building.

## Owner

This rehearsal (*Mother's Painting*).

## Shown

Stripped rooms with bare floorboards scuffed where the movers carried the last box out. Uncurtained daylight falls through the kitchen window onto dusty skirting boards and a pale rectangle on the plaster wall around a single bare picture hook. Even quiet voices echo across the empty shell. Mother's painting—an oil of that same kitchen window from inside, its wooden frame open at one corner—leans against the skirting board. By the flat door, the wall-mounted entry intercom hangs silent until pressed from the street below.

## Holds

- Mother's painting, leaning unclaimed against the skirting board.
- The bare picture hook and pale mark on the wall where the painting last hung.
- The uncurtained kitchen window and deep sill—the exact view painted on the canvas.
- Empty cupboards, utility meters, the interior door lock, and the flat keys set on the kitchen counter for handover.
- The wall-mounted entry intercom and door-release button beside the exit.

## Offers

- Lift Mother's painting from the skirting board, inspect its split corner, or hold it back up against the pale mark on the wall.
- Stand at the real kitchen window or lean on its sill, looking between the glass and the painted room.
- Step into the adjoining bare kitchen or hallway to check meters, cupboards, or keys while remaining within earshot of every word.
- Answer the entry buzzer, press the release button for whoever waits downstairs, or leave the handset on its hook.

## Withheld

- **Chores, tools, or other keepsakes to hide behind:** Every box, screwdriver, and other object has left with the movers, and the canvas has no resale value. To claim fairness, stay useful, or promise repairs, someone must give up busying their hands or balancing an imaginary split and ask for the painting outright.
- **Taking the painting unseen or lightly:** The flat has one exit and echoing bare boards. To leave with the painting, someone must pick it up in front of the other three, carry it past the bare hook and kitchen window, and lock Mother's door for the last time—giving up the shelter of "still clearing the flat" and openly claiming the last trace of her home.
- **Unbroken family time:** Once the street buzzer rings, ignoring it stalls whoever waits below, while answering it gives up the siblings' last private minutes inside Mother's walls.
