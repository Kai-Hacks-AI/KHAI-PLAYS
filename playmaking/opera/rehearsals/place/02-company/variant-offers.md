# The Building Entrance

## Taxonomy

Ground-floor vestibule and kerbside threshold of Mother's apartment building.

## Owner

This rehearsal (*Mother's Painting*).

## Shown

The building's heavy front door stands wedged open onto the pavement, letting cold street air and traffic noise cut into a narrow tiled vestibule. Three paces away at the kerb, one car idles with exhaust drifting across the step and its boot still shut. Behind the siblings, the stairwell rises toward Mother's cleared, locked flat; along the wall sit brass letterboxes and the exterior buzzer panel. Mother's painting—an oil of her kitchen window from inside, its frame open at one corner—leans against the vestibule tiles just inside the doorframe, brought down last and set out of the street damp, not yet loaded.

## Holds

- Mother's painting, propped unclaimed against the inner vestibule wall just short of the doorstep.
- The heavy front door and the wooden wedge holding it open to the pavement.
- The narrow tiled passage between the foot of the stairs and the street step.
- The row of resident letterboxes, Mother's named slot, and the key-return box.
- The idling car at the kerb within three paces of the doorway, engine running and boot closed.

## Offers

- Step half-out onto the pavement by the idling car or stay inside the vestibule, keeping one foot in departure while still talking.
- Set Mother's painting flat onto a dry ledge behind the stairwell turn where all four siblings can stand clear of the doorway with their hands off the frame.
- Check the car boot, watch the street, or point to weather, boot space, and the running engine as practical reasons for who loads the canvas now.
- Drop the flat keys into the return slot or kick the wooden wedge free to let the heavy door swing shut.

## Withheld

- **Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:** With the door wedged open to street and stairwell, neighbors, passersby, or a waiting driver can hear any raised voice. To speak in private, someone must pull the wedge and stall the idling car—giving up the pretense of a brisk kerbside goodbye.
- **Mother's rooms as silent witness:** The cleared flat, bare wall hook, and real kitchen window are locked upstairs out of sight; in the lobby the painting looks like an awkward, damaged canvas with no resale value. To make a personal claim outweigh convenience, someone must give up logistical rules or sideways jokes and name their memory of Mother aloud in a drafty public doorway.
- **Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:** The boot is three steps away, yet the vestibule is narrow enough that one person's stance blocks the threshold. To put the painting in the car, someone must carry it past the other three into one vehicle—giving up any pretense that the handoff is neutral or reversible.
