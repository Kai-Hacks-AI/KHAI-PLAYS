# Performance: *Mother's Painting* in *Mother's Flat*

**Opening Conditions (Human Brief):**
Mother's flat has been cleared. The four siblings are in Mother's Flat with Mother's painting against the skirting board, and someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.

*(Initial blocking: Ada stands near the flat door and wall-mounted entry intercom; Ben stands near the bare kitchen doorway where the handover keys rest on the counter; Cora stands near the pale rectangle and bare picture hook on the plaster wall; Dan stands a step back from the dusty skirting board where Mother's painting leans with its frame open at one corner. The street entry buzzer rattles across the bare floorboards and stops.)*

---

**Turn 1 — Ada:**
Ada glances at the wall-mounted intercom without lifting the handset, folds her left cuff into a neat, hard square, and steps across the scuffed floorboards toward the skirting board. "Leave the buzzer a second—Ben, get a final reading off the kitchen meters before we hand those keys down, and Cora, check the hall cupboard's shut," she says evenly, stopping beside Dan with her eyes on the frame's split corner. "I'll carry the painting straight out to my car when I go down to let them in, before that loose corner catches on the stairwell."

**Turn 2 — Dan:**
Dan steps sideways across the scuffed floorboards to stand squarely between Ada and the skirting board, lifting his right heel and holding it off the wood as he sets his hand on the top rail of the frame, just clear of the open corner. "Nobody check the cupboard or the meters—Ada's just handing out fake chores so the painting's already halfway down the stairs before anyone gets a say," he says, his voice carrying cleanly across the bare plaster as he looks from Ben and Cora back to Ada. "Let's just say it out loud: all four of us stayed behind in an empty flat because we all want it. Mother asked me to fix that split corner months ago and I kept putting it off, and I'm asking you straight—let me take her painting home."

**Turn 3 — Ben:**
Ben leaves the handover keys untouched on the counter and steps from the kitchen doorway to the uncurtained window, resting his palm flat on the deep sill as he looks from the bare glass to the painted window under Dan's hand. "Take your hand off the rail," he says evenly, then catches his breath for a fraction of a second on—"Dan. Saying it out loud first doesn't give you a bigger right to it than Ada trying to hurry it down the stairs. There's nothing left in this flat to trade against it and nothing to price out, so if all four of us want it, we put it under one rule: we pass it home to home, a year apiece, the way Mother took it everywhere she lived, and nobody walks the first turn out that door until the other three sign off—after Cora's actually had her say."

*[By the flat door, the wall-mounted entry intercom buzzes a second time from the street below—two short, impatient bursts across the bare walls—and falls quiet again.]*

**Turn 4 — Cora:**
Cora lets out a quick laugh as the second buzz dies across the bare plaster, flicks the empty picture hook once with her thumb, and strolls from the pale mark on the wall to crouch beside the painting's split corner. "Oh, brilliant, Ben—a four-way custody treaty while whoever's downstairs wears their thumb out on the buzzer," she says brightly, tapping a brisk rhythm against her knee before reaching toward the open mitre, where her fingers go suddenly still a breath from the wood. "Mother had to send me a photo of this canvas when I first moved away just so I wouldn't lose the view, so do yourselves a favour: let me tuck the real thing under my arm right now while you hand down those keys—worst case, you three finally have an excuse to visit me and check I haven't pawned it."

**Turn 5 — Ada:**
Ada crosses to the pale rectangle on the plaster wall, steadies the bare picture hook Cora flicked with one finger, and folds her right cuff into a hard square to match her left before stepping to the kitchen counter and picking up the handover keys. "Fine, forget the meters—I'll deal with whoever's on the buzzer and hand these over myself, same as I sorted the movers," she says evenly from the bare doorway, looking across at the three of them. "I was the last one to hang that canvas on this hook for Mother, and once I lock up today there's nothing left here for me to look after. No yearly rota, Ben, and you're not tucking it under your arm while I close the flat, Cora—let me take her painting home tonight, and Dan can come round to mine on Sunday to mend that corner properly."

**Turn 6 — Ben:**
Ben looks down once at his palm on the deep sill, then pushes away from the uncurtained glass and crosses the scuffed floorboards to stop beside Dan and Cora in front of the canvas, keeping both hands open at his sides as he faces Ada in the doorway. "Picking up the keys and promising"—he catches his breath briefly on the name—"Dan a Sunday visit isn't settling anything, Ada; it's just buying one vote so two of us get shut out. Fine—scrap the rota. When I couldn't sleep, Mother used to sit with me right at that window until it got light, and once you lock up today the real view isn't ours anymore, so I'm asking the three of you straight: let me take her painting home tonight."

**Turn 7 — Cora:**
Cora grins crookedly, pushes up from beside the skirting board dusting her palms together, and strolls past the bare picture hook to the flat door. "Easy, Ada, I wasn't actually planning a sprint for the stairs—though Ben's lifelong rota lasting all of ninety seconds really has to be a record," she says lightly, tracing the edge of the intercom handset before her fingers go suddenly still beside the door-release button. "Look at the three of you, though: Ada's already rationing out Sunday visiting hours, Ben won't stand seeing it on her wall, and Dan still hasn't taken his hand off the wood—none of you living round here will ever stomach walking into one of the others' kitchens and seeing Mother's window hung up. Pack it off out of town with me tonight as neutral ground; nobody left here gets outranked on Sunday, I don't mind giving house room to a split frame, and the second you say yes I'll press this button and let whoever's downstairs right up."

**Turn 8 — Dan:**
Dan crouches to slide his other palm under the split corner of the frame, lifts Mother's painting clear of the dusty skirting board—pressing the open mitre together beneath his thumb—and straightens beside the pale rectangle on the plaster wall, lifting his right heel and holding it off the scuffed boards. "Take your finger away from that button, Cora, and stop talking about me over my head like I've already left the room—I'm not a Sunday repair job at Ada's and I'm not a spare vote for Ben," he says, holding the canvas steady in both hands in plain sight of the three of them. "Just say what we're all dancing round: the minute Ada hands down those keys, Mother's flat stops forcing us into the same room, and nobody trusts the rest of us to bother showing up anymore unless we've got her window on our wall. Hiding it out of town with a split frame is just calling it quits—let me take her painting home tonight, fix the corner I kept telling her I'd do next visit, and hang it in my flat so you three actually walk through my door on a Sunday for once."

**Turn 9 — Ada:**
Keys in hand, Ada crosses from the kitchen doorway to the flat door, lifts the intercom handset from beside Cora's still fingers, speaks evenly into it—"Wait by the street door, please, I'm bringing the keys down in two minutes"—and sets it firmly back on its hook. Pressing the folded square of her cuff hard against her wrist, she steps up to Dan beside the pale mark on the plaster wall and rests her free hand steadily on the intact side rail of the frame. "Leave the button alone, Cora—nobody's coming up here, and this canvas isn't disappearing out of town," she says, looking straight across the painted window at Dan with a brief glance to Ben. "When I hung this on that hook for Mother the last time, I had to take the weight carefully in both hands myself because that corner you kept putting off was already splitting, Dan. In two minutes I hand these keys down and my job for this family is finished—I've carried all the rest of it without asking for a thing, Ben, and I am not walking out of Mother's flat tonight holding nothing but a stranger's keys. Put it in my hands, Dan, and let me take her painting home."

**Turn 10 — Dan:**
Without pulling the frame away from Ada's hand on the intact rail, Dan cups the split corner firmly from underneath and presses the back of the canvas flat into the pale rectangle on the plaster wall just below the bare picture hook, holding its weight steady with his right heel lifted clear of the scuffed boards. "That corner was splitting on my watch, Ada—I kept telling myself there'd always be another Saturday here to mend it, and instead of telling me to get round here and fix it, you hung it yourself in silence and saved the bill for today," he says, looking straight at her across the painted window before glancing to Ben and Cora. "And listen to what you just said: 'my job for this family is finished.' Say it plain—none of us wants to admit that without Mother's walls, you're ready to clock out, Cora's ready to bolt, and Ben thinks we need a rule just to stay in touch. Your other hand's full of a stranger's keys, Ada; if I let go of this split corner right now, the frame gives way between us. I'm not putting Mother's window in your hands as severance pay so you can be done with us—I'm still asking the three of you to let me take it home tonight and mend what I owed her, and until the four of us settle this to each other's faces, I'm not letting go of my corner."

# Director's Note

### 1. What I Varied & Directed (Staging Choices vs. Authored Play)
- **Isolated Briefing & Blocking:** Each sibling ran in a separate read-only sub-context containing only the shared premise, the Piece, `place.md`, `promptbook.md`, their own Persona row, and their single private memory sentence. As a staging choice left open by the brief, I placed each sibling near a distinct feature of `place.md` when the buzzer sounded (Ada by the intercom/door, Ben by the bare kitchen doorway and handover keys, Cora by the pale wall mark and hook, Dan near the painting on the skirting board) and cued Ada first so the buzzer tested her task-assigning habit immediately.
- **Observable Action & Turn Routing:** Performers were directed to play strictly observable speech and physical action without internal monologue. Turn order (Ada → Dan → Ben → Cora → Ada → Ben → Cora → Dan → Ada → Dan) followed `promptbook.md`'s rule (*"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"*), routing each cue to whichever sibling's active claim-strategy had just been undercut or physically challenged.
- **Environmental Pressure:** After three turns of the siblings ignoring the street buzzer, I introduced one external beat fully accommodated by `place.md`—a brief second double-buzz from the street intercom before Turn 4—keeping whoever waited downstairs outside the fiction while tightening the pressure on the room. For Turn 10, I cued Dan that this was the closing beat within the two-minute window Ada had just set on the intercom, leaving the choice itself (and what remained unresolved) entirely to the performer.

### 2. What Happened
- **First pass (Turns 1–4) — Habits as cover:** Ada ignored the buzzer, assigned fake meter and cupboard checks, and tried to carry the painting down as routine stairwell care (Turn 1). Dan stepped between Ada and the skirting board, exposed the fake chores, confessed putting off fixing the split corner, and asked for the painting outright (Turn 2). Ben stepped to the real kitchen windowsill and tried to neutralize both claims under a "fair" one-year-apiece rota modeled on Mother's moves, sparing himself a personal ask (Turn 3). After the second buzz, Cora laughed off Ben's "custody treaty," flicked the bare hook, shared Mother's mailed photograph, and offered sideways to tuck the painting under her arm as an excuse for them to visit her (Turn 4).
- **Second pass (Turns 5–10) — Cover stripped away:** Ada dropped the chores, picked up the handover keys, named hanging the canvas last, rejected both Ben's rota and Cora's exit, and tried to absorb Dan's claim into a Sunday repair visit at her house (Turn 5). With his rota dead, Ben stepped off the windowsill, dropped the rule, named sitting sleepless with Mother at that window, and asked for the painting himself (Turn 6). Refused once, Cora covered by claiming she "wasn't actually planning a sprint," moved her still fingers beside the door-release button, and pitched taking the split-framed canvas out of town as "neutral ground" in trade for buzzing the caller up (Turn 7). Dan lifted the painting in both hands, cupping the split mitre, and named the unspoken fear that without Mother's flat none of them trusted the others to show up anymore (Turn 8). Ada answered the intercom to buy two final minutes, laid her free hand on the intact rail opposite Dan's, and refused to walk out holding only a stranger's keys (Turn 9). Dan pressed the canvas flat into its old pale rectangle beneath the bare hook—Ada's hand on one rail, his hand holding the split corner—refusing to give it up as "severance pay" while the two-minute clock ran (Turn 10).

### 3. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exacted Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown:** The scuffed bare boards, echoing plaster shell, uncurtained daylight, pale wall mark, and visibly split frame left nowhere to murmur privately or disguise physical intent—every Tell (folded cuffs, caught breath, still fingers, lifted heel) and every step toward the skirting board registered across the whole room.
- **Holds:** Every held fixture anchored a concrete dramatic move: the painting on the skirting board (guarded then lifted by Dan), the real uncurtained window and deep sill (where Ben's private memory pulled him in Turn 3 before he had to leave the glass behind in Turn 6), the bare picture hook and pale mark (flicked by Cora, steadied by Ada, and filled again in Turn 10), the handover keys on the kitchen counter (which occupied one of Ada's hands from Turn 5 onward, physically preventing her from supporting both sides of the split frame alone in Turn 10), and the entry intercom/release button by the exit.
- **Offers:** All four authored affordances drove turning points: standing at the real windowsill comparing glass and canvas (Turn 3); stepping to the kitchen counter for the keys within earshot (Turn 5); leaving, threatening to press, and finally answering the intercom handset (Turns 1, 7, 9); and lifting the painting, cupping its open corner, and pressing it back against the pale mark on the wall (Turns 8 & 10).
- **Withheld:**
  - *Chores, tools, or other keepsakes to hide behind* bankrupted Ada's meter/cupboard pretext on arrival (Turns 1–2), kept Dan from repairing the mitre on the spot (forcing him to hold the open joint together by hand), and denied Ben any secondary items or cash value to balance out—forcing Ben to abandon his rule and ask nakedly (Turn 6).
  - *Taking the painting unseen or lightly* blocked both Ada's stairwell-care exit (Turn 1) and Cora's "tuck it under my arm" gambit (Turns 4 & 7); because the flat had one exit and echoing boards, claiming the canvas meant lifting it in front of the other three (Turn 8).
  - *Unbroken family time* exacted a price whether ignored or answered: stalling the caller triggered a second buzz and handed Cora leverage at the door-release button (Turns 4 & 7), while Ada answering the handset in Turn 9 spent their last uncounted minutes inside Mother's walls, locking the siblings into a two-minute deadline.

### 4. Stageability Note (`khai-director`)
No gaps in `company.md`, `place.md`, or `promptbook.md` impeded staging; the Piece's zero resale value + open corner and `place.md`'s Withheld constraints reinforced the four Personas cleanly. One distinction between Play mechanics and staging judgment: when a single turn undercuts two siblings' claims simultaneously (as Ada's Turn 5 simultaneously killed Ben's rota and refused Cora's offer), `promptbook.md`'s *"most affected"* rule relies on Director judgment to sequence who answers first; prioritizing the sibling whose structural defense had just been invalidated (Ben's rule in Turn 6, then Cora's distance in Turn 7) kept all four claims active to the final beat.
