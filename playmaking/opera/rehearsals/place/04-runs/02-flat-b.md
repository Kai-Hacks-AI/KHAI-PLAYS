# Staged Performance — *Mother's Painting* (`Mother's Flat`)

**Opening Conditions (Human Brief & Curtain-Rise Stage Picture)**
> "Mother's flat has been cleared. The four siblings are in Mother's Flat with Mother's painting against the skirting board, and someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer."
>
> *The movers have gone. Ada stands near the bare kitchen doorway beside the counter where the flat keys sit ready for handover, a couple of paces from the wall-mounted entry intercom by the flat door. Ben stands near the uncurtained kitchen window and its deep sill; Cora stands out on the scuffed bare floorboards facing the pale rectangle and bare picture hook on the plaster wall; Dan stands a step back from the skirting board where Mother's painting leans with its wooden frame open at one corner. Suddenly, the wall-mounted entry buzzer harsh-rings from the street below, echoing across the stripped flat, and stops.*

---

### Turn 1
**ADA**
*Turns her head toward the silent entry intercom by the door, leaves the handset on its hook, and touches the flat keys on the bare kitchen counter before stepping onto the scuffed floorboards toward the skirting board, holding one hand out toward the canvas.*

That'll be the agent downstairs for the handover. Ben, double-check the utility meters in the hall before I buzz them up, and Cora, make sure the kitchen cupboards are shut tight. Dan, hand me Mother's painting before the door opens—that split corner won't survive being knocked on the stairwell, so I'll lay it flat in my car and take it home with me once I've signed over the keys.

---

### Turn 2
**DAN**
*Steps in front of Ada's outstretched hand, lifts Mother's painting off the skirting board by its intact sides—careful of the open corner—and holds it against his chest, shifting his weight so his right heel lifts and stays an inch off the bare floorboards.*

Nobody needs to check empty cupboards, Ada, and you don't get to decide whose car this goes in like it was settled without the rest of us. The movers took the last box ten minutes ago—the only reason all four of us are still standing in an empty flat is because every one of us wants this painting. Mother asked me to fix this split corner and I kept putting it off, so I'm taking it home with me.

---

### Turn 3
**BEN**
*Lifts his hand from the deep kitchen window sill, leaves the hallway meters unchecked, and steps onto the bare floorboards between Ada and Dan, motioning flat-palmed toward the skirting board.*

The meters were read an hour ago, and pulling rank or grabbing it first doesn't make it yours. *He catches his breath slightly.* Dan, put it back down against the wall. There's one thing left in here, it has zero resale value to split, and all four of us want it—so before anybody answers that buzzer, we agree on one rule for everyone: a year in each of our homes, in turn. And since you and— *a brief catch of breath* —Ada both just tried taking it for good, Cora or I should take the first year so everybody trusts it'll actually get passed on.

---

### Turn 4
**CORA**
*Turns from the pale rectangle on the plaster wall, drumming her fingers lightly against her forearm as she steps toward Dan and Ben.*

Oh, brilliant—a four-way custody rota before the agent rings twice, and not even a biro left in the flat to write it down. *She tips an open hand toward the canvas in Dan's arms, and her fingers go suddenly still.* Tell you what, skip the schedule and let me take it back to mine, purely as a public service so the three of you don't wrestle on the stairs. Mum sent me a photo of it when I first moved away anyway, so I'm really only upgrading the snapshot—and if that open corner starts bothering me, Dan, you can visit on weekends.

---

*By the flat door, the wall-mounted entry buzzer sounds a second, longer ring from the street below, echoing across the bare floorboards, then cuts off.*

---

### Turn 5
**ADA**
*Drops her outstretched hand at the second ring of the buzzer, leaves the intercom unanswered, and folds her left shirt cuff neatly into a hard, tight square before stepping to the plaster wall and touching the bare picture hook inside the pale rectangle.*

All right—the meters are read, the cupboards are empty, and there's nothing left in here for me to organise for any of you. I'm the one who drove this hook into the wall and hung that canvas for Mother the day she moved in, with the corner already split because she couldn't bear looking at bare plaster while she waited on promises. I've sorted the movers, the paperwork, and the keys on that counter without asking for a thing, Ben, so don't tell me I'm disqualified from keeping the one piece of her flat I actually put up with my own hands. Dan, put it in my arms now, and I'll go press that buzzer and finish the handover myself.

---

### Turn 6
**BEN**
*Lets his hands fall to his sides, steps back to the uncurtained kitchen window, and sets his palm flat on the deep sill, looking between the bare glass and the canvas against Dan's chest.*

All right—forget the rota. There isn't a biro, nobody was ever going to pass it along after a year, and I only called it a rule so I wouldn't have to stand here and ask for it for myself. Keeping a tally of who did the paperwork doesn't make it yours either— *he catches his breath* —Ada. Mother carried that canvas into every flat she lived in, and on the nights I couldn't sleep, I used to sit right here at this window with her until morning. I'm asking for it outright now— *his breath catches again* —Dan, let me take it home.

---

### Turn 7
**CORA**
*Lets out a short laugh that echoes off the bare plaster, steps up to Dan, and taps one fingertip lightly against the open wooden corner of the frame in his arms.*

Charming—the minute you two drop the act, I don't even make the shortlist. You three had the actual room whenever you wanted it—Ada's hook in the plaster, Ben's window sill—and when I moved away, Mum posted me a photograph of that canvas just so we'd still be looking at the same window. *Her fingers go suddenly still against the split wood.* Tell you what, Dan—pass it over to me and consider yourself officially pardoned from ever gluing this corner; I'll take it home crooked just as she had it, and even press that buzzer for Ada on my way out.

---

### Turn 8
**DAN**
*Looks down at the split wooden corner, draws the canvas closer against his ribs to step clear of Cora's hand, and carries Mother's painting across the echoing floorboards past the pale mark on the wall to stop beside the flat door and the silent entry intercom. He leaves the handset on its hook, shifting his weight onto his left foot so his right heel lifts and holds an inch off the floor.*

Keep your pardon, Cora—I'm not going to glue this corner either. Ada's right: Mother waited on me while she was alive, and talking about fixing it today was just my excuse not to stand here and admit I want it. You're still hiding behind wisecracks instead of asking for yourself, Cora, and none of us is touching this buzzer because the second the agent walks in, three of us leave these walls with nothing left of her at all. I'm tired of things in this family getting settled like I don't count—I want it for myself, crooked frame and all, and I'm taking it home.

---

### Turn 9
**CORA**
*Lowers the hand Dan stepped away from, walks across the echoing floorboards past the pale mark on the wall, and steps directly between Dan and the interior lock of the flat door, leaving the intercom handset untouched. She lifts both empty hands slightly toward the canvas, tapping her thumb once against her palm before her fingers go suddenly still.*

All right, Dan—no wisecracks, and no sideways bargains. I make a joke out of asking because once I moved away, acting like it cost me nothing was the only way none of you could look me in the eye and tell me no. The three of you got to stay close—you had her kitchen, that window, and these walls right up to the end, while all I've had since I left is the photograph of that canvas she sent me. I'm asking for myself now, even if you turn me down to my face: don't walk out that door with it, Dan—let me take Mother's painting home.

---

### Turn 10
**DAN**
*Looks at Cora's still, empty hands between him and the lock, sets his right heel flat on the bare floorboards, and places the intact sides of Mother's painting into her held-out hands, drawing his fingers back clear of the split wooden corner. Empty-handed, he reaches to the wall intercom beside the door, presses the door-release button firmly for a long second, then lets his hand fall to his side—shifting his weight so his right heel lifts an inch off the floorboards again and stays there.*

The three of us had this room right up to the end, and I still let her look at a broken frame until she died. Giving it to you doesn't square me with Mum, Cora, and it doesn't make any of this fair—Ada, Ben, and I still walk out of here with nothing of hers left, and once you take that canvas away, it's gone from us for good. At least this time all three of you actually had to ask me. Take it home crooked just like she kept it; I've buzzed the street door open, and all that's left in here now is handing back the keys.

---

# Director's Note

### 1. What Was Directed or Varied (Staging Choices vs. Fixed Play)
- **Initial spatial blocking (staging choice):** Within the Human's fixed opening condition, each sibling was placed near the room fixture tied to their posture at rise—Ada by the kitchen counter keys and intercom, Ben at the uncurtained kitchen window and sill, Cora facing the pale plaster mark and bare hook, and Dan beside the painting against the skirting board—leaving who spoke or touched the canvas open from Turn 1 onward.
- **Cue order under the Promptbook (`promptbook.md`):** Turn routing followed *"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"* (`Ada -> Dan -> Ben -> Cora -> Ada -> Ben -> Cora -> Dan -> Cora -> Dan`).
- **Mid-scene pressure (staging choice within an open condition):** Because all four left the entry intercom untouched across Turns 1–4, a single environmental beat—a second, longer ring from the street below—was introduced before Turn 5 without introducing an offstage speaker into the room.

### 2. What Happened
- **First pass—shields up (Turns 1–4):** Ada tried to fold the painting into handover logistics (sending Ben to the meters, Cora to the cupboards, and telling Dan to pass the canvas for her car). Dan physically picked the painting up to his chest, exposed the shared pretext, and claimed it via his unkept promise to repair the frame. Ben tried to subordinate both unilateral grabs to a one-year-each rotation led by himself or Cora. Cora mocked the unwritten rota and bid sideways for the painting as a light "public service."
- **Second pass—shields stripped (Turns 5–9):** Pressured by the second ring and empty room, Ada dropped her organiser role, folded her cuff, touched the bare hook she had driven in, and exposed that she hung the split frame while Mother waited on Dan's promises. Ben abandoned his rotation at the window sill, admitted the rule saved him from asking for himself, recalled sitting at that window with Mother on sleepless nights, and asked Dan outright. After Cora's sideways "pardon" on the split corner (Turn 7) was rejected by Dan—who carried the canvas to the door, abandoned his repair excuse, and called out Cora's wisecracks (Turn 8)—Cora stepped between Dan and the interior door lock to ask directly at the risk of face-to-face refusal (Turn 9).
- **Resting point—costly and unresolved (Turn 10):** Having finally been counted directly by all three siblings, Dan placed the still-broken painting into Cora's hands and pressed the door-release button, explicitly refusing false closure: giving Cora the painting neither absolves his broken promise to Mother nor softens the loss for Ada, Ben, and himself, and his heel rises off the floorboards again as the street door is buzzed open.

### 3. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exacted Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown:** Scuffed bare floorboards, uncurtained daylight on the pale plaster rectangle, and echoing walls denied quiet side-bargains; Ben's caught breaths, Cora's solitary laugh, Dan's walk to the exit, and both buzzer rings registered across the whole room.
- **Holds:** Every sibling anchored their claim to one concrete fixture still held by the shell: Ada to the counter keys and the bare picture hook; Ben to the uncurtained window and deep sill mirrored in the oil canvas; Cora and Dan to the frame's open wooden corner, the interior door lock, and the entry intercom.
- **Offers:** The cast used the room's specific affordances—leaving the handset on its hook across nine turns to preserve private time, lifting the canvas from the skirting board, touching the bare hook inside the pale mark, setting a palm on the real sill while looking between the glass and the painted window, and finally pressing the door-release button.
- **Withheld:**
  - *Chores, tools, or other keepsakes to hide behind:* With movers having cleared every box, screwdriver, and biro—and the canvas carrying zero resale value—Ada's meter/cupboard assignments collapsed immediately, Ben's four-way rota had nothing to rest on, and Dan could neither fix the frame on the spot nor sustain his repair pretext, forcing each sibling to pay the cost of asking outright.
  - *Taking the painting unseen or lightly:* One exit and echoing floorboards stopped Dan from slipping out quietly (Turn 8) and kept Cora's sideways jokes from carrying the canvas (Turns 4 & 7); Cora had to physically block the lock and risk refusal (Turn 9), and claiming it openly left the other three visibly empty-handed.
  - *Unbroken family time:* Stalling the street buzzer provoked the second ring's urgency, while Dan pressing the door-release button in Turn 10 spent the siblings' last private minutes inside Mother's walls.

### 4. Stageability & Authored Material Assessment
- **No gaps requiring Play edits:** `company.md`, `place.md`, and `promptbook.md` staged cleanly as written without needing any change to the authored Play.
- **Distinguishing Play mechanics from staging choices:** Because the Piece is indivisible and `promptbook.md` routes the next turn to whoever's claim is most affected, any transfer between two siblings naturally leaves the other two freshly affected; rather than inventing a contrived four-way compromise, using the room's own threshold (`door-release button` + `flat keys` awaiting handover) as a staging boundary let the exchange reach a clean dramatic stop inside `Mother's Flat` while preserving the Play's unresolved family fracture.
