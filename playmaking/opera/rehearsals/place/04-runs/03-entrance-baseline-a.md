# Performance — *Mother's Painting* at *The Building Entrance*

**Human Brief (Opening Conditions):**
> "Mother's flat has been cleared and locked upstairs. The four siblings are at The Building Entrance with Mother's painting leaning against the inner vestibule wall and one car idling at the kerb. Stage the exchange here while I watch."

---

### Turn 1 — Ada
*Ada checks the tag on the flat keys once, drops them into the key-return box with a sharp clatter, and steps forward along the narrow tiled passage toward the doorframe.*

**ADA:** That's the meter read and the keys in. Dan, pull that wooden wedge once we're out so the door doesn't slam on the latch. Cora, mind your coat against that loose corner—the mitre's come right open. Ben, don't sit burning petrol on a single yellow; your boot's packed as it is, so I'll wrap the canvas in my scarf, get the frame glued properly at mine, and hang onto it.

### Turn 2 — Ben
*Ben stays planted on the threshold step between the vestibule and the pavement, glancing back once at his shut boot at the kerb before turning back inside, one hand resting against the open doorframe.*

**BEN:** My back seat's completely clear, *—he catches his breath slightly—* Ada, and you're not carrying an open mitre home in a scarf through this damp. Upstairs we did every single box by turns so nobody just claimed things by offering to fix them. If all four of us want it, the only fair rule is we rotate it a year each—and start with the one car actually sitting at the kerb to get it home flat tonight, instead of Cora or— *—his breath hitches again—* Dan watching it go under someone's arm on the pavement.

### Turn 3 — Cora
*Cora tucks her coat clear of the split corner with a small, theatrical bow, eases the canvas an inch back from the cold draught along the vestibule tiles, and taps the top edge lightly—before her fingers go suddenly still on the frame.*

**CORA:** Oh, brilliant, shared custody of a wonky kitchen window—we'll need a spreadsheet and handover witnesses at service stations next. Tell you what, save Ben a ticket on that single yellow and spare Ada's good scarf: let me take a turn being practical and lug it back on the train tonight. Empty carriage at this hour, lies dead flat across the table, and if you lot ever actually come visit me you can inspect the glue job yourselves.

### Turn 4 — Dan
*Dan steps off the bottom stair into the narrow tiled passage alongside Ada, reaches past Cora's sleeve to set his hand flat against the upright just above the split corner of the frame, and lifts his right heel, holding it an inch off the tiles.*

**DAN:** You can pull your own wedge, Ada—I didn't spend all day clearing bedrooms just to hold the door while you three carve up what's left. Drop the talk about damp and single yellows and train tables, all of you. The keys just went in that box, there's nothing else of hers left upstairs, and we all know Ben's annual rota dies the second a nail goes into somebody's wall. Take your hand off it, Cora—I'm wrapping it in my coat and fixing that corner at mine tonight.

### Turn 5 — Cora
*Cora lifts both hands clear of the frame in brisk, palms-out surrender, then hooks the wooden wedge loose from under the heavy front door with the toe of her boot so the door eases off its stop toward the threshold.*

**CORA:** Whoa—hands off, Danny, breathe, I was winding you up about the train table. Let's not give the whole street a free matinee over a cracked bit of hardboard. *She gestures airily toward the canvas between them, then stops, her fingers going suddenly still an inch above the painted window.* Though if you three are going to wrestle over glue and rotas in the hall, let me play Switzerland—Ben runs me five minutes to the station in that warm car of his, I babysit the thing out of reach till New Year, and you lot can come repossess it together if you don't trust me.

### Turn 6 — Ada
*Ada stoops to pick up the loose wooden wedge from the tiles, sets it squarely atop the brass letterboxes, and folds her left coat cuff over twice into a hard square. She steps back in beside the canvas, slipping her fingers under the bottom rail beneath the split corner.*

**ADA:** Nobody's taking it till New Year, Cora—and Ben, either switch that engine off or step inside and let the door shut so we aren't broadcasting to the street. Dan's right, there isn't another thing of hers left behind that lock. I'm not offering to glue the frame just to make myself useful. I was the last one to hang this for Mum—I put the hook in the plaster upstairs, hung it level for her the last time it went on a wall, and lifted it down an hour ago when the flat was bare. You three can get on to your cars and your trains; I've seen the rest of today through to the keys, and the painting comes home with me.

### Turn 7 — Ben
*Ben steps the three paces out to the kerb, pulls his driver's door open to twist the key out of the ignition, and shuts the car door as the engine cuts out. Stepping back into the vestibule, he draws the heavy front door closed onto its latch behind him, pockets his keys, and stands square across the narrow threshold facing the canvas.*

**BEN:** Engine's off. Nobody's broadcasting to the street, Cora's not getting a station run, and nobody's rushing for a yellow line. Putting a hook in the plaster doesn't make it yours by right, *he catches his breath slightly,* Ada. You think I pitched a rota just to sneak a nail into my wall? I was trying to give the four of us an even rule so none of us had to beg the others in a hallway. I used to sit up with Mum at that kitchen window half the night whenever I couldn't sleep. Forget my boot space—I'm asking for it straight now, and neither you nor *—his breath hitches—* Dan is carrying it past this door just because you've got your hands on the wood.

### Turn 8 — Dan
*Dan shifts closer to the wall, sets his other hand along the top rail to bracket the open mitre, and presses the split corner gently together; shifting his weight onto the ball of his foot, he lifts his right heel and holds it clear of the tiles.*

**DAN:** Right, so now it's an audition for who showed up best, is it? Ada put the hook in and took the canvas down an hour ago before anyone else got a look-in, Ben did night shifts by the kettle, and Cora's pitching custody till New Year over a "bit of hardboard" when her hand just froze dead in front of the glass. Well, I haven't got a story where I came through for her. Mum asked me to fix this frame, and I kept putting it off—kept telling her I'd bring a clamp round next weekend, right up till she was gone and Ada was locking the flat without asking us. I'm not trying to muscle past the door, Ben; I'm telling you straight why my hands are on the wood, so let go of the rail, Ada, and let me finish the one thing she asked me to do.

### Turn 9 — Cora
*Cora starts a quick, dry smile, then lets it drop. Stepping in beside the unbroken left upright of the frame between Ben at the latched door and Ada and Dan against the wall, she sets her palm on the wood; her fingers slide half an inch down the grain, then go suddenly still.*

**CORA:** All right, Danny, points to you—my hand froze. If I make a joke of it first, none of you gets to turn me down to my face in a hallway. When I first moved away, Mum sent me a photograph of this painting—not of her, just this kitchen window on the wall, because she took it to every flat she ever lived in. You three had the real room upstairs—the hooks in the plaster, the late nights by the glass, the weekends she asked you round—and all I've ever had of hers at mine is a snapshot. So I'm done joking and I'm not offering to babysit it till New Year: let go of the wood, Ada, Dan, and step off the latch, Ben, or tell me straight I'm going home on that train with nothing but the photo.

### Turn 10 — Ada
*Ada slides her fingers slowly out from under the bottom rail, leaving the frame resting against the vestibule tiles, and takes half a step back beside the brass letterboxes. Looking down once, she folds her right coat cuff over twice into a hard square to match her left, then lifts her eyes to the three of them.*

**ADA:** My hand's off the wood. I read the meter and dropped those keys in the box without asking because I thought sparing you three the bare rooms upstairs was my job—not so you'd turn round in the hall and tell me carrying the end of it means I've already had my share of her. Ease off that dry corner before the mitre splits further, Dan; Cora, leave the next train; Ben, stay on that latch. I'm done sorting us out, and I'm not stepping aside just to keep the peace—I still want the window I hung for her on my wall, so none of us carries it out of this doorway tonight until three of us say aloud we're letting it go.

# Director's Note

### 1. What Was Varied & Directed (Staging Choices vs. Authored Play)
- **Fixed Play & Strict Isolation:** `company.md`, `place.md`, and `promptbook.md` were treated as completely fixed. Each sibling ran in a separate read-only `research` sub-context receiving only the shared situation and Piece, `place.md`, `promptbook.md`, their own Persona row, and their own private memory sentence, seeing only observable physical actions and spoken lines as turns unfolded.
- **Staging Conditions Varied (Left Open by Author & Brief):** To prevent continuity collisions across isolated performers while activating the doorway's pressures, I established initial blocking in the vestibule: **Ben's** car was the one idling on a single yellow line three paces away with its boot shut (while Ada, Cora, and Dan were travelling separately), and **Ada** began beside the brass letterboxes still holding the flat keys, with Cora by the wedged doorframe beside the painting, Ben on the threshold step, and Dan at the foot of the stairs.
- **Promptbook Turn Routing:** Following `promptbook.md` (*"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"*), each turn was routed to whichever sibling's stake was most undercut (`Ada -> Ben -> Cora -> Dan -> Cora -> Ada -> Ben -> Dan -> Cora -> Ada`, 10 turns).

### 2. What Happened
- **Turns 1–5 (Logistical Pretexts & Sideways Shields):** Ada locked in the irreversibility of departure by dropping the flat keys into the return box, assigning Dan the door wedge, ruling out Ben's boot, and claiming the canvas to glue the corner (Turn 1). Ben countered from the threshold with an "even" annual rotation conveniently beginning in his dry car tonight (Turn 2). Cora joked past the tension to pitch taking it flat on her train table (Turn 3). Pushed aside by all three, Dan stepped down, gripped the frame above the split corner, punctured Ben's rota, and ordered Cora's hand off the wood (Turn 4)—prompting Cora to retreat into her Shadow ("I was winding you up"), kick the wooden wedge loose, and pitch a "Switzerland" station lift in Ben's car till New Year (Turn 5).
- **Turns 6–10 (Costly Claims Inside the Closed Vestibule):** Stripped of authority and claim, Ada stowed the wedge, gripped the bottom rail, abandoned her repair excuse, and named hanging the painting last for Mother (Turn 6). Forced off his impersonal rule, Ben walked out to switch off his engine, latched the heavy front door behind him, and asked directly—naming his sleepless nights with Mother by that window (Turn 7). With Ada and Ben holding moral high ground and Ben blocking the exit, Dan bracketed the split corner and confessed the failure behind his grip: he kept putting off Mother's request to fix the frame until she died (Turn 8). Called out by Dan for her frozen fingers, Cora dropped her humour, placed her palm on the opposite upright, and named the snapshot Mother sent when she moved away—turning the others' proximity to the real room upstairs against them (Turn 9). Finally, Ada withdrew her fingers from the bottom rail, refused to let carrying the burden erase her own desire for the painting, and settled the scene inside the latched doorway: nobody leaves with the canvas tonight until three of them say aloud they are letting it go (Turn 10).

### 3. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exacted Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown & Holds:** The narrow tiled vestibule, cold street draught, idling car three paces out, brass letterboxes/key-return box, locked stairwell, and open-cornered canvas anchored every beat. Once Ada dropped the keys into the return box (`Holds`), Mother's flat became permanently unreachable behind them, concentrating all remaining attachment onto the damaged frame leaning against the tiles.
- **Offers:** Performers organically used all four authored Offers: Ada dropped the keys into the return slot (Turn 1); Ben held the half-out threshold invoking damp, yellow-line pressure, and car space (Turn 2); Cora eased the frame out of the draught and hooked the wooden wedge free with her boot (Turns 3, 5); Dan and Ada physically steadied/held the open mitre corner (Turns 4, 6, 8).
- **Withheld:**
  - *Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:* As long as the door stood wedged open and the car idled, Cora could deflect ("free matinee for the street," Turn 5) and Ben could hide behind urgency. Speaking plainly in Turn 7 cost Ben the kerbside exit: he had to step out, kill his engine, and latch the heavy door shut, forfeiting any brisk departure or station run.
  - *Mother's rooms as silent witness:* Because the bare plaster hook and real kitchen window were locked upstairs out of sight, practical pretexts (scarf, back seat, train table, clamp) cancelled each other out by Turn 5. Outweighing convenience cost all four siblings their defensive Personas in a cold public hallway—Ada gave up mere usefulness (Turn 6), Ben gave up his "fair rule" to ask for himself (Turn 7), Dan confessed procrastination rather than superior care (Turn 8), and Cora risked direct refusal by naming her snapshot (Turn 9).
  - *Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:* Even with the car boot three steps away, the vestibule's narrowness meant Ben's single stance across the latched door physically barred passage (Turns 7–10) while three siblings' hands converged on the wood—keeping any "neutral" temporary loading completely out of reach and forcing Ada's honest, unresolved truce at rest in Turn 10.

### 4. Staging Friction vs. Authored Play
- **No Gap Requiring Play Revision:** The Play staged cleanly as written. Two distinctions between staging choice and authored structure stood out: specifying *whose* car idled at the kerb was a necessary Director's choice for multi-context continuity rather than a defect in the Play; meanwhile, `promptbook.md`'s single-rule routing (*"most affected makes the next move"*) naturally drives symmetric escalation when four Personas all want an indivisible Piece, making `place.md`'s narrow latched threshold essential for bringing the scene to a grounded dramatic rest without forcing a false resolution.
