# Performance — *Mother's Painting* at *The Building Entrance*

**Human Brief (Opening Conditions):**
> "Mother's flat has been cleared and locked upstairs. The four siblings are at The Building Entrance with Mother's painting leaning against the inner vestibule wall and one car idling at the kerb. Stage the exchange here while I watch."

---

**Ada:** *[Drops the flat keys through the brass return slot with a sharp clink, then steps down the narrow tiles toward the painting with one hand held out to take its sound side.]* "Upstairs is locked and signed off. Ben, pop the boot so you can run me and the canvas back to mine before the damp gets into that split corner—Cora, mind the bad mitre as we lift it past the door, and Dan, kick the wedge free once we're clear."

**Ben:** *[Keeps his hand inside his coat pocket over the key fob and shifts half a step inward on the tiles, blocking the narrow gap to the doorstep.]* "Hold on, Ada—we split every box upstairs four ways, we don't just wave the last piece out the door because the engine's running. The only fair rule when there's one thing left is it goes in the boot flat on a blanket right now, gets glued at a proper bench, and we rotate it a year each—starting with whoever's already driving it out of the rain."

**Dan:** *[Leaves the wooden wedge untouched, steps off the threshold onto the tiles beside the painting's split corner, and lifts one heel, holding it off the floor.]* "I'm not kicking the wedge for you, Ada, and nobody believes a canvas that goes into Ben's boot 'for year one' ever comes back out on a family rota. Drop the talk about street damp and proper workbenches—all four of us want the last thing left of hers in our own flat, including Cora standing guard over it without saying a word. Did you lot already count me out before those keys went through the slot, or do I get to say it's coming home with me so I can fix that corner myself?"

**Cora:** *[Gives a quick laugh, drumming her fingers along the top rail as she tilts the canvas upright against her own coat—just clear of Ada's outstretched hand—before her fingers go suddenly still near the split corner.]* "Standing guard—give me some credit, Dan, I was just waiting to see if Ben's four-year rota came with paperwork. Look, since Ben won't pop the boot without taking first turn and you two are about to pull that loose mitre apart in front of the whole street, let me do everyone a favour and lug the wonky thing onto the train myself. Call my wall neutral territory while you three argue over visiting rights—saves Ben's blanket, saves Ada's nerves, and nobody even has to kick the wedge."

**Ada:** *[Draws back her empty hand, folds her coat cuff into a hard square, then reaches past Ben to pull the wooden wedge free so the heavy front door swings shut against the street draught.]* "Funny how none of you needs me to manage a thing the second the flat is scrubbed out and the keys are through the slot. Put the base back down on the tiles, Cora—that split frame isn't rattling onto a train as a joke when I was the last one to hang it on the wall for her upstairs. And if you're worried about your car idling on the kerb, Ben, go switch the engine off and come back in, because nobody is slipping her painting out this door on a dodge."

**Cora:** *[Lowers the sound corner of the frame onto the toe of her own boot instead of the cold tiles, flashing a quick, crooked smile before her fingers go suddenly still along the split edge.]* "Careful shutting us in like that, Ada, or I'll think you're actually worried I'll make a bolt for the platform. Mother posted me a photo of this exact kitchen window the week I first moved away, so I've been making do with glossy paper while the rest of you had the real oil upstairs. Tell you what—let me take the actual canvas home tonight and I'll post you my old snapshot in the morning, fair swap, and Ben doesn't even have to waste petrol turning his engine off."

**Ben:** *[Pushes the heavy door open long enough to reach into his cab at the kerb and twist the engine dead, then steps back onto the vestibule tiles, lets the door latch shut behind him, and rests one steady hand on the side rail of the frame, catching his breath sharply on each name.]* "Cora—engine's off, and I'm dropping the rota. The rest of us didn't just leave that picture hanging idle upstairs; I used to sit with—with Mother at that kitchen window whenever I couldn't sleep, watching the dark thin out in the glass. I hid behind a rule because I didn't want to stand in the doorway and beg the three of you to let me take it home, so I'm asking straight now."

**Dan:** *[Closes his fingers over the split corner of the frame to press the open mitre shut, lifting one heel and holding it clear of the tiles.]* "That corner isn't loose from the stairs—Mother asked me to fix this frame for her, and I kept putting it off until she was gone and the keys went through the slot. Now all four of us have laid our piece of her on the canvas—Ada's wall hook, Cora's photo, Ben's nights at the glass, and the job I never showed up to do—and we're still dodging the one thing asking nicely doesn't fix: whoever carries it out leaves the other three on the pavement with nothing of hers left. If Ben glues this at his bench, I never make good on what she asked me, so I'm not letting go of this corner unless the three of you look at me right here and decide I'm the one who doesn't count."

**Ada:** *[Sets the wooden wedge atop Mother's brass letterbox, presses the folded square of her cuff flat, and slides her palm under the bottom rail to lift the sound corner's weight off Cora's boot.]* "Keep your snapshot, Cora, and don't tell me about that split mitre, Dan—I had to hold it together with my own hands the last time I hung this frame for Mother because you still hadn't come round to mend it. Now the rooms are empty and the keys are gone, none of you needs me for a single thing anymore, and I am not walking home to bare walls as well. Bring your glue to my flat to finish what you promised her, Dan, and Ben—either open that door to run me and the canvas home, or look me in the eye after I cleared everything upstairs and take my hand off this rail yourself."

**Ben:** *[Looks Ada straight in the eye without touching her wrist, lifts his hand from the frame to take the wooden wedge off Mother's letterbox, pulls the heavy door wide onto the wedge, clicks his fob so the car boot swings open at the kerb, and plants himself squarely in the narrow doorway, his breath catching sharply on the names.]* "Ada—I'm not prying your hand off that rail, and I won't stand here asking a second time when asking straight only got—got Cora crowded off the frame. Mother took that kitchen window into every flat she ever lived in, and the one fair rule left is nobody carries it out just by gripping the wood hardest. The boot's open at the kerb for one address and one trip, mine included, and this canvas doesn't cross the step until three of us let go and say the same name out loud."

# Director's Note

### 1. What Was Varied and Directed
- **Grounding open conditions (staging choice vs. authored Play):** Neither `company.md` nor the Human brief specified who held the flat keys or whose car was the single vehicle idling at the kerb. To prevent continuity collisions across four isolated performers while keeping the Play fixed, I set concrete initial positions inside `place.md`: Ada held the flat keys by the brass return box after locking up upstairs; Ben's car was the one idling three paces away at the kerb with its boot closed (while the others had no car at the step, giving Cora train travel); Cora stood inside beside the leaning canvas; and Dan stood on the wedged-open threshold.
- **Turn routing & pacing:** Ada was cued first, and every subsequent cue (`Ada → Ben → Dan → Cora → Ada → Cora → Ben → Dan → Ada → Ben`) followed `promptbook.md` (*"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"*). Performers received only observable moves and strict single-turn cues; on Turn 10 I directed Ben not to end on a floor-passing question so the exchange would come cleanly to rest at the 10-turn ceiling while leaving his substantive choice open. No structural gap in the authored Play blocked staging; the unresolved ending reflects the Personas' own colliding claims rather than missing material.

### 2. What Happened
The first half of the exchange (Turns 1–4) ran entirely on Personas' protective habits: Ada dropped the keys into the return slot and tried to briskly assign the loading so the painting went home to hers; Ben blocked the step and proposed a "fair" four-year rotation starting in his own boot; Dan refused the door wedge, named the avoided truth that all four wanted the painting, and claimed the frame repair for himself; and Cora tilted the canvas against her coat to pitch taking it onto the train as a light-hearted "favour." Once Cora physically took the canvas out of Ada's reach, the logistical cover broke (Turns 5–8): Ada pulled the door wedge and named hanging the painting last; Cora rested the frame on her boot and named Mother's posted photograph; Ben switched his idling car off, shut the door, dropped his rota, and confessed sitting sleepless with Mother at that window; and Dan clamped the split mitre and admitted he had put off Mother's request to fix it. Rather than softening the deadlock, those confessions sharpened it (Turns 9–10): Ada lifted the sound corner off Cora's boot and dared Ben either to drive the canvas to hers or physically take her hand off the rail, prompting Ben—seeing his rare direct plea overrun—to retreat to a harder procedural rule: wedging the door open again, popping the boot, and blocking the narrow threshold until three siblings voluntarily let go and name the fourth aloud.

### 3. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exact Costs, and Foreclosed Options
- **Shown & Holds:** The **key-return slot** allowed Ada (Turn 1) to make the upstairs lock-up irreversible on the very first beat, foreclosing any retreat back into the flat to reconsider or leave the canvas behind. The **open mitre corner** leaning against the vestibule tiles became the literal contact point where Cora's stilled fingers, Dan's delayed repair, and Ada's hand-hung memory collided. **Mother's brass letterbox** held the pulled wedge (Turn 9) until Ben reclaimed it (Turn 10), and **Ben's closed boot and pocketed fob** three paces from the **narrow tiled passage** gave Ben physical veto power over transport without giving him uncontested possession.
- **Offers:** Every initial evasion leaned on what the threshold offered—street damp and the running engine (Ada, Turn 1), boot space and weather protection (Ben, Turn 2), stepping off the threshold vs. kicking the wedge (Dan, Turn 3; Ada, Turn 5), and tilting or resting the frame on a boot to shield its split corner (Cora, Turns 4 & 6; Ada, Turn 9).
- **Withheld:**
  - *Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:* Because the wedged door and running engine kept them in public kerbside departure, Ada had to **pull the wooden wedge** (Turn 5) and Ben had to **step out and kill his engine** (Turn 7)—sacrificing the brisk-goodbye pretense—before Ben and Dan could speak their private grief aloud.
  - *Mother's rooms as silent witness:* With the cleared flat and real kitchen window locked out of sight behind the return slot, logistical rules (Ben's rota) and sideways jokes (Cora's "neutral territory" / snapshot swap) failed to carry weight; each sibling had to pay the personal cost of exposing their private memory of Mother in a cold tiled lobby—and Cora paid her Shadow's exact fear when her half-joking snapshot offer met Ada's flat refusal (*"Keep your snapshot, Cora"*).
  - *Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:* Because the vestibule was narrow enough for one stance to block the step and the boot was three paces away in a single car, **no one could load the painting neutrally**. That spatial bottleneck stopped Ada's Turn 1 brisk load-out, defeated Ben's Turn 2 "year one" boot storage, and enabled Ben's final Turn 10 move: opening both door and boot while planting his body in the choke point so neither grip strength nor convenience could carry the painting across the step without an explicit three-way surrender.
