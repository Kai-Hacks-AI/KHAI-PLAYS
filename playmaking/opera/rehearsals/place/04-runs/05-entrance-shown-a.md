# Performance: *Mother's Painting* at *The Building Entrance*

**Opening Conditions (Human Brief):**
> "Mother's flat has been cleared and locked upstairs. The four siblings are at The Building Entrance with Mother's painting leaning against the inner vestibule wall and one car idling at the kerb. Stage the exchange here while I watch."

---

**Turn 1**
**Ada:** *Stops at the foot of the stairs, checks the tag on the flat keys once, and drops them cleanly into the metal key-return box. She steps forward toward Mother's painting, eyeing the split corner of the frame and the rain blowing past the doorway.* "That's upstairs locked and signed off. Ben, have you still got that polythene sheet in your boot? Pass it in so I can wrap this frame properly for the train—and Dan, once I've got it covered, kick that wedge out so we aren't flooding the hall."

**Turn 2**
**Ben:** *Slips a hand into his coat pocket without pressing the fob, stepping half a pace closer to Mother's painting beside Cora and gesturing toward its open corner and the rain-wet step; his breath catches slightly on Ada's name.* "Hold on—Ada, we haven't agreed it's going on the train. That corner's already split; one knock on a wet platform and the frame's done for. There's four of us here and one piece left, so let's put a fair rule on it instead of someone just wrapping it up—a year each, in turn, starting tonight in a flat, dry boot three paces from the door while the engine's already warm."

**Turn 3**
**Dan:** *Leaves the wooden wedge untouched, planting himself squarely in the narrow gap between the painting and the wet doorstep, one heel lifted and held just off the tile.* "Nobody's kicking the wedge out and nobody's fetching polythene. Listen to yourselves—'wrap it for the train,' 'a year each starting in my boot.' The keys are in the box, the flat's bare, and whoever walks out this door with that canvas tonight is hanging it on their wall for good. All four of us want it—I want it at mine too, not settled over my head because Ben left his engine running—so drop the polite rota act, and Cora, say your piece instead of just standing guard over it."

**Turn 4**
**Cora:** *Lets out a quick, bright laugh, easing Mother's painting an inch back along the wall out of the draught; she taps a light rhythm on the side of the frame, then her fingers go suddenly still against the wood.* "Standing guard? Give over, Dan, somebody had to keep the rain off Mum's wonky paintwork while you three auditioned for a courtroom drama. Look, do yourselves a favour and let me take the poor busted thing back to mine—saves Ada wrestling a split frame onto a packed train, saves Ben playing museum courier, and once I've glued the corner I'll even post you all a photo so everybody gets the kitchen window."

**Turn 5**
**Ada:** *Folds her damp coat cuff over twice into a hard, neat square, steps closer to the inner wall beside Cora, and glances once toward the open pavement before speaking in a low, level voice.* "Don't talk about saving me a train ride, Cora—I've sorted the clearance, the meters, and the keys today without asking any of you to spare me the trouble. And glue won't hold that mitre. When I hung this on the hook upstairs for Mum the last time, I had to brace that split corner with my palm while she stood behind me telling me when it was level. Ben, switch your engine off so the whole street isn't listening—I'm not walking away from the one thing in that flat I put up on the wall for her."

**Turn 6**
**Ben:** *Pulls the car fob from his coat pocket and holds the button down until the idling engine at the kerb cuts out, leaving only the steady rain at the open door. From the same pocket he takes a folded parking receipt, tears it neatly into four strips—tearing the corner off one—and sets them beneath his thumb on the ledge of the brass letterboxes, his breath catching short as he says his brother's name.* "Engine's off. Nobody's rushing anything into a warm boot, and alright—Dan, no rota. Whoever takes it tonight hangs it for good. You carried the clearance and the meters today, Ada, I know you did, and you put it on that last hook upstairs—but Mum took that window into every flat she ever lived in. If we turn this into who can stand in an open doorway and bid their memories of her loudest, that's not fair on anyone who won't use her like that. Four strips, one torn short: we each draw blind right here, and whoever pulls it walks out with the painting."

**Turn 7**
**Cora:** *Looks down at the torn parking strips under Ben's thumb with a quick, dry laugh, reaching two fingers toward the ledge as if to draw before stopping an inch short of the paper, her fingers going suddenly still against the brass of Mother's letterbox.* "A Pay-and-Display raffle, Ben? Gorgeous—nothing says nobody's using Mum like gambling her kitchen window on two hours' parking. Leave me out of the lucky dip. Mum posted me a photograph of that canvas when I first moved away, and I've been staring at the paper version ever since—so tell you what, let me walk out past Dan with the real thing tonight as a straight upgrade, and whichever of you wants the snapshot can have it free of charge."

**Turn 8**
**Dan:** *Kicks the wooden wedge free so the heavy front door swings shut against the rain, then sets his back against the closed panel, lifting one heel and holding it off the damp tile.* "Nobody's walking out past me like I'm just here to hold the door, Cora, and keep your parking strips, Ben. You want to know why Ada had to brace that split corner with her palm? Because Mum asked me to fix that frame—more than once—and I kept telling her I'd sort it next visit until we ran out of visits. I want to take it home tonight and mend that mitre like I told her I would, not settle the last thing left of her on a blind draw. And it's not an open doorway anymore, Ben—the street can't hear a word, so drop the referee act and say out loud why you can't let it go either."

**Turn 9**
**Ben:** *Sweeps the four torn parking strips off the brass letterboxes into his palm, shoves them into his coat pocket, and steps closer to the propped canvas, glancing once at the painted kitchen window before looking straight at Dan against the closed door. He keeps both hands empty at his sides, his breath catching short on Cora's name.* "Alright—no parking strips. When I couldn't sleep, I used to sit with her at that window in the dark until it got light. She never made a fuss about it; she just sat there with me watching the glass until morning, and with upstairs cleared out there's nothing else left of that. So alright, I'll ask—Cora, Ada, and you straight out: I want it, and I'm asking to take her window home tonight. Now all four of us have said our piece behind a shut door, and there's still one canvas between four of us—so here's the rule: nobody lifts that frame from the wall until the other three say yes."

**Turn 10**
**Ada:** *Folds her other coat cuff over twice into the same hard, neat square, presses the crease flat between thumb and forefinger, and lets both hands hang empty at her sides, glancing once at the metal key-return box behind her before looking back to Ben.* "Alright, Ben—your rule holds. Nobody lifts that frame until three of us say yes, and with the keys in that slot behind me we can't leave her window propped in a public hall overnight. I heard you about sitting with her until morning, same as I heard Dan about the corner and Cora about the photograph. Once those keys went in the box, there's no more meters or forms for me to sort out for any of you—if I walk off to the station empty-handed tonight, nobody needs me ringing up tomorrow and I've got nothing left to carry. I put that frame on her last hook with her standing right behind me, so you don't have my yes yet either. I'm letting my train go; Ben, leave the car switched off, Cora, mind that split mitre, and Dan, stay on the door—nobody leaves this wall until we've seen the last job through and three of us can say yes."

# Director's Note

### 1. Staging Choices & Open Conditions Varied
Keeping `company.md`, `place.md`, and `promptbook.md` strictly fixed across four isolated performer sub-contexts, I specified two open conditions left undetermined by the Play and Human brief:
- **Ownership of the idling car & modes of departure:** I assigned the kerbside idling car (with key fob in pocket) to **Ben**, while **Ada**, **Cora**, and **Dan** were travelling home separately (e.g., by train). That gave Ben an immediate practical lever ("dry boot three paces from the door while the engine's already warm") without pre-loading the painting.
- **Initial vestibule blocking:** I positioned **Ada** at the foot of the stairs holding the flat keys beside the key-return box, **Cora** inside the vestibule beside the propped canvas, **Ben** near the stairs/letterboxes, and **Dan** on the wet outer tiles in the narrow gap between the painting and the wedged-open front door.
- **Turn routing (`promptbook.md`):** Following *"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move,"* I routed cues dynamically (`Ada -> Ben -> Dan -> Cora -> Ada -> Ben -> Cora -> Dan -> Ben -> Ada`) and reminded Ada at Turn 5 (after all three siblings blocked her train handoff) and Turn 10 (closing beat) of the live physical state without prescribing whether she should hold or yield.

### 2. What Happened
In Rounds 1–4, each sibling attempted to claim the painting without exposing private need: Ada dropped the keys into the return box and tried to wrap the canvas as her final clearance task; Ben countered with a year-by-year rotation rule starting in his dry boot; Dan blocked the doorway and exposed both pretexts as permanent possession; and Cora shifted the frame out of the draught with a sideways joke about gluing the corner and posting everyone a photo. Once logistics, rotation, and jokes deadlocked, each sibling had to pay the cost of naming their private memory—and Shadow—aloud: Ada named bracing the split corner for Mother (Turn 5) and later admitted that with the keys surrendered she had nothing left to carry for the family (Turn 10); Ben killed his engine and still tried one more protective rule—a four-strip parking-receipt draw on the letterboxes (Turn 6)—before Cora named Mother's posted photograph (Turn 7) and Dan kicked the wedge out, shut the door, confessed putting off Mother's requests to mend the split mitre, and forced Ben out from behind his referee role (Turn 8). Ben finally pocketed the strips, spoke of sitting sleepless with Mother at that kitchen window until dawn, asked directly for the painting, and set a unanimous-consent lock: *nobody lifts the frame until the other three say yes* (Turn 9). Ada accepted that rule, let her train go, withheld her own "yes," and assigned all four siblings to stay shut inside the vestibule until three of them can genuinely consent—bringing the scene to rest honestly without manufacturing a false winner.

### 3. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exact Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown:** The steady rain blowing across the outer tiles, the split frame corner, and the idling exhaust three paces away drove the opening practical pretexts: Ada asked for polythene from Ben's boot to brave the rain on the train (Turn 1), Ben invoked rain on a station platform threatening the split mitre (Turn 2), and Cora eased the canvas back along the wall out of the wet draught (Turn 4).
- **Holds:** Every held fixture anchored a concrete turn: **the key-return box** swallowed the flat keys in Turn 1 (making upstairs permanently unreachable and trapping the canvas overnight in a public hall by Turn 10); **the brass letterboxes and Mother's named slot** became Ben's makeshift lottery table in Turn 6 and the surface where Cora's fingers went still in Turn 7; **the narrow tiled passage** allowed Dan's single stance to physically bottleneck exit (Turns 3, 8); and **the heavy front door + wooden wedge + idling car** held the boundary between kerbside departure and locked-in reckoning.
- **Offers:** Each sibling used the Place's affordances—Ada dropping the keys (Turn 1), Ben pointing to weather, boot space, and the warm engine (Turn 2), Cora shifting and guarding the split frame along the wall (Turns 4, 10), and Dan kicking the wooden wedge free to shut the door (Turn 8).
- **Withheld:**
  1. *Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:* Kept intimate confession out of reach while the street listened; Ben even used the open doorway in Turn 6 as a reason *not* to "bid memories of her loudest." Privacy had to be purchased across two separate sacrifices of brisk departure: Ben cutting the engine with his fob (Turn 6) and Dan kicking out the wedge to shut the heavy door (Turn 8), which stripped Ben's last excuse.
  2. *Mother's rooms as silent witness:* Because the cleared flat and real kitchen window were locked upstairs (sealed when Ada dropped the keys in Turn 1), neither Ada's clearance labour, Ben's rotation/raffle rules, nor Cora's "snapshot upgrade" joke could carry weight until each sibling paid the cost of naming their private memory of Mother aloud in the bare hallway (Turns 5, 7, 8, 9).
  3. *Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:* With Dan's body filling the narrow threshold three steps from the closed boot, nobody could slip the painting into Ben's car or out to the station as a neutral or reversible handoff—forcing Ben and Ada's final unanimous-consent standoff inside the shut vestibule (Turns 9–10).

### 4. Authored Play Assessment
No gaps or contradictions in the authored material impeded staging; `company.md`, `place.md`, and `promptbook.md` interlocked cleanly, and leaving car ownership and initial doorway stances open gave the Director room to apply threshold pressure without altering the Play.
