# Performance — *Mother's Painting* at *The Building Entrance*

**Opening Conditions (Human Brief):**
> "Mother's flat has been cleared and locked upstairs. The four siblings are at The Building Entrance with Mother's painting leaning against the inner vestibule wall and one car idling at the kerb. Stage the exchange here while I watch."

---

**Ada:** *Drops the flat keys into the metal return box with a sharp clink, pulls Mother's named strip from the brass slot beside it, and steps forward into the narrow passage toward the leaning canvas.* "That's the keys in and the meter read—Dan, pull the wedge once we're clear so we aren't flooding the hall. Ben, pop the boot and run me past mine on your way out; that mitre's come right open at the top corner, so I'll take the frame home tonight and clamp it flat before the damp gets in the board." *She reaches past the edge of the doorframe to lift the painting by its sound side.*

**Dan:** *Steps squarely across the wet threshold between the leaning canvas and the open street door, leaving the wooden wedge untouched.* "Leave the wedge where it is, Ada—if that painting goes into Ben's boot for yours, none of us is ever seeing it on another wall again. Stop calling it a repair job so nobody has to say out loud that we all want to keep it. I'm not shutting the door behind you while you walk off with the one thing left from upstairs, and if that corner needs glueing, it's coming home with me."

**Ben:** *Turns back from the idling car onto the wet outer step beside Dan, taking his thumb off the key fob and sliding it deep into his coat pocket.* "Nobody's popping the boot until we put this under one rule for all four of us—Cora's standing right beside it and hasn't even had a word yet. None of us gets to keep the last thing out of that flat for good just by calling it a repair job or blocking the doorway. The only fair way with one canvas and four houses is a straight rotation, a year apiece, and we aren't shifting an open frame twice in the rain tonight—it rides flat in my boot once and takes its first year on my wall, then moves down the line."

**Cora:** *Tucks the dry tail of her scarf over the open top corner of the frame and taps the wooden edge lightly with her knuckles, grinning out toward Ben on the wet step.* "Oh, brilliant, a four-way custody rota—nothing says family harmony like drawing up a time-share for zero pounds' worth of peeling kitchen paint, Ben. Tell you what, before Ada breaks out the G-clamps or Dan camps on the doormat till Christmas, let me haul the awkward thing onto the train tonight so you three locals don't have to police each other's walls. Ben's already burning petrol at the kerb—drop me five minutes down at the station with it wrapped in my coat, and you can all tell yourselves I just saved you the storage."

**Ada:** *Pauses, folds her damp coat cuff into a hard square, then eases the tail of Cora's scarf off the open top corner and slides her other palm under the bottom rail to lift the painting's weight onto the dry toe of her boot.* "Keep your scarf for the platform, Cora—this isn't clutter you're saving us from storing, and I'm not putting it on a twelve-month rota like shared kit, Ben. You wanted it plain, Dan: I was the last one to hang this for Mum upstairs, holding it to the plaster till she called it level, and after boxing up everything else she owned and dropping the keys in that slot, I'm not walking home empty-handed. Ben, run Cora down for her train before you choke the whole stairwell with exhaust—I'll button my coat round the frame and get a cab on the corner myself, so step clear of the wedge, Dan."

**Cora:** *Catches the loose tail of her scarf to flick it lightly over her shoulder, lets out half a laugh that cuts off in the draught, and stops squarely in the narrow passage—her fingers going suddenly still against her collar as the grin drops from her face.* "The week I first moved away, Mum posted me a photograph of that painting so I'd still have her kitchen window in a flat none of you ever visited. You three had the real room and the Sunday tea right up till today while I had a four-by-six print curling on rented plaster, Ada, and I wasn't joking about taking it home tonight. Keep her brass nameplate and put your cab home on me if it squares the ledger, just set that canvas inside my coat before Ben's engine chokes the street."

**Ben:** *Steps three paces through the rain to reach inside the driver's door, twists the key to kill the idling engine, and steps back onto the wet threshold with the silent keys shut in his fist.* "The car's off—I'm not the station cab, and I only pushed a rota so I wouldn't have to stand in this doorway asking for it for myself." *He draws a sharp, caught breath as he says her name.* "Cora—it wasn't Sunday tea; when I couldn't sleep, I used to sit with Mum at that exact window until the street got light. I'm asking plain now to take that canvas home tonight, Dan, so stop hiding behind a pot of glue and either say out loud what's keeping you in the doorway or let me past to lift it."

**Dan:** *Stoops to pull the wooden wedge from under the heavy door and eases the door across until it rests almost shut against the street rain, facing back into the passage with the wet wedge in his fist and one heel lifted and held off the tile.* "You want it out loud, Ben—that top corner's only gaping open because Mum asked me three times before Christmas to bring glue round and fix it, and I kept telling her next weekend until the flat was locked. Ada dropped the keys in that box with your boot already spoken for, handing me the doorstop like the whole flat had been settled over my head. If anyone else takes that home tonight and clamps that mitre shut, you're wiping out the last thing she ever asked me to do, Ada—so set it off your boot and let me carry it home and finish it myself."

**Ada:** *Sets Mother's brass name-strip flat on the letterbox ledge, unbuttons her coat, and lifts the canvas off her boot in both hands, tucking its sound edge under her dry lapel as she steps right up to Cora and Dan in the dim passage.* "Keep your train fare, Cora—that brass strip doesn't hold Mum's kitchen, and you don't square a ledger with me like I was hired in to pack boxes. I didn't clear that flat over your head, Dan; I carried the week because nobody else came round with tape, and the minute the heavy work's finished you're already settling up with me on the step and going four separate ways. Bring your glue to mine on Sunday so I don't take away the job she asked of you, Dan, but I won't lie to get past the door—once I hang this canvas at mine it stays on my wall for good, so either let me out into the rain or unlock the boot for me, Ben."

**Ben:** *Steps half a pace inward onto the wet outer tiles beside the doorframe, pulls the heavy unwedged door the last inch onto its latch against the rain, and slides the unpressed car keys deep into his coat pocket.* "Neither, Ada—that open frame isn't going out into the wet, and my boot doesn't unlock for a deal only one of us made. Giving Dan an hour with glue on Sunday doesn't answer Cora's wall or the nights I sat awake at that window, so there's one rule left for this doorway: nobody carries her painting past the latch until all four of us agree whose house it goes to, even if we stand here till morning." *He settles his damp shoulder against the shut door with both hands empty at his sides, standing still in the dim hall with the rain muffled behind the wood.*

# Director's Note

### What Was Varied and Directed (Staging Choices vs. Fixed Play)
- **Fixed Play & Information Isolation:** `company.md`, `place.md`, and `promptbook.md` were treated as strictly fixed. Each sibling ran in a separate, isolated read-only `research` sub-context receiving only the shared situation and Piece, the full `place.md` and `promptbook.md`, their own Persona row, and their own single private memory sentence. Performers were instructed to produce only observable speech and physical action (reserving Tells for genuine Shadow pressure) and one turn per cue.
- **Opening Conditions Varied Within the Brief:** Within the Human's brief and the inventory already in `place.md`, I established starting spatial blocking and ownership of the threshold items left open by the text: Ada at the letterboxes/key-return box holding the keys from locking Mother's flat upstairs; Ben on the wet outer step with *his* car idling three paces out at the kerb (boot shut); Cora inside the narrow passage closest to the propped painting; and Dan beside the wedged-open front door.
- **Promptbook Routing:** Turn order followed `promptbook.md` (*"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"*): `Ada (1) → Dan (2) → Ben (3) → Cora (4) → Ada (5) → Cora (6) → Ben (7) → Dan (8) → Ada (9) → Ben (10)`.

### What Happened
- **Turns 1–4 (Claims Disguised as Threshold Logistics):** Ada dropped the flat keys into the return slot and reached for the canvas under the guise of clamping its open mitre out of the damp. That repair claim struck Dan's unspoken guilt and exclusion; Dan stepped across the doorway, called out that all four wanted it, and claimed the glueing without naming why. Treated by both as a kerbside courier, Ben pocketed his fob and proposed an annual four-house rotation that conveniently put the painting into his dry boot tonight for year one. Called in by Ben, Cora draped her scarf over the broken corner and joked sideways about hauling "zero pounds' worth of peeling kitchen paint" onto her train to save the "locals" storage.
- **Turns 5–8 (Paying the Cost of Plain Claims):** Refusals stripped away each cover in turn. Ada folded her cuff into a square, peeled Cora's scarf off the frame, lifted the canvas onto her boot, and named hanging it last for Mother. Refused flatly and dismissed toward the platform, Cora's fingers went still at her collar; dropping her grin, she named the photograph Mother posted when she first moved away and tried to trade Mother's brass name-strip and cab fare for the canvas. Stung by being cast as both station cabbie and casual "Sunday tea" local, Ben walked into the rain to shut off his idling engine, caught his breath on Cora's name, admitted his rotation rule was cover to avoid asking for himself, and named his sleepless nights with Mother at that window—challenging Dan directly. Dan stooped to pull the wooden wedge, eased the heavy door nearly shut against the rain with one heel held off the tile, and confessed putting off Mother's three pre-Christmas requests to glue that open mitre.
- **Turns 9–10 (Verdict Blocked at the Latch):** Ada set the brass name-strip back on the ledge, tucked the canvas under her coat, voiced her Shadow (carrying the week's burden only for the others to settle accounts on the step), offered Dan Sunday at hers with his glue while admitting openly that once hung on her wall it would stay for good, and pressed Ben either to unlock his boot or let her out into the rain. With neither Cora's claim nor his own answered by Ada's offer to Dan, Ben pulled the unwedged door onto its latch, pocketed the unpressed car keys, and set his shoulder against the closed door—keeping the damaged frame out of the rain and out of his boot until all four agreed.

### How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exacted Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown & Holds:** The steady rain, wet outer tiles, drifting exhaust, narrow passage, open-cornered frame, key-return slot, brass letterbox name-strip, wooden wedge, and single shut-boot car grounded every beat. Dropping the keys into the return box (Turn 1) made going back upstairs impossible; the open mitre drew Cora's scarf, Ada's lapel, and Dan's confession; and Mother's brass name-strip became an inadequate substitute Cora tried to trade (Turn 6) and Ada laid down on the ledge (Turn 9).
- **Offers:** Each sibling initially used the Place's *Offers* to advance a claim without owning it—dropping the keys to signal wrap-up (Turn 1), citing rain, damp board, and single-journey boot space (Turns 1, 3), shielding the damaged frame corner (Turns 4, 5), and pulling the wedge / latching the heavy door (Turns 8, 10).
- **Withheld:**
  - *Mother's rooms as silent witness:* Locked upstairs out of sight, the bare hook and real kitchen window could not testify for anyone; in the vestibule the canvas looked like damaged clutter ("zero pounds' worth of peeling kitchen paint"), forcing all four siblings (Turns 5–8) to abandon repair talk, rules, and jokes and pay the cost of naming private memories aloud in a cold hallway.
  - *Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:* As long as the car idled and the wedge held the door open to street spray and exhaust, the siblings maintained the pretense of imminent departure (boot, cab, train). Speaking honestly cost them that momentum: Ben had to step into the rain to kill the engine (Turn 7) and Dan had to pull the wedge (Turn 8), culminating in Ben latching the door shut (Turn 10).
  - *Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:* Three paces from a single boot across a passage narrow enough for one person's body to block, no handoff could pretend to be neutral or reversible. Dan's stance blocked the doorway in Turn 2; Ada had to drop neutrality in Turn 9 ("it stays on my wall for good"); and Ben latching the door with the keys pocketed in Turn 10 kept both a unilateral loading into his car and a walkout into the rain out of reach.
- **Authored Material vs. Staging Friction:** No gap in the Play required changing authored text. One minor point in `place.md` for the author's awareness: *Withheld* mentions both "a waiting driver" and "someone must pull the wedge and stall the idling car"; staging the idling car as Ben's reconciled those phrases cleanly without adding props, though if staged with a hired driver instead, killing the engine from inside the scene would work differently.
