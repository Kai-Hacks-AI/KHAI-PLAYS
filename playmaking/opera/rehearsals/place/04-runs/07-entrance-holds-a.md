# Performance: *Mother's Painting* at *The Building Entrance*

**Opening Conditions (Human):**
"Mother's flat has been cleared and locked upstairs. The four siblings are at The Building Entrance with Mother's painting leaning against the inner vestibule wall and one car idling at the kerb. Stage the exchange here while I watch."

---

### Turn 1 — Ada
*Ada steps off the bottom stair, drops the flat keys cleanly into the key-return box beside the brass letterboxes, and folds her left coat cuff into a hard square. She reaches down toward the folded mover's blanket and brown parcel tape near Cora's feet.*

**Ada:** "That's upstairs locked and handed back. Cora, pass me that blanket and the tape—I'll bind the open corner on the frame and walk the canvas two streets over to my car before the damp gets into it. Ben, don't sit burning petrol at the kerb; run Dan and Cora to the station and I'll message once it's home safe."

### Turn 2 — Cora
*Cora scoops up the folded mover's blanket and hooks the roll of brown parcel tape onto her wrist just ahead of Ada's hand, flashing a quick grin as she spins the tape once—before her fingers go suddenly still against the cardboard core.*

**Cora:** "Two streets in this drizzle and you'll look like a soggy art thief, Ada. Tell you what—since Ben's already playing station taxi, let me take the poor wonky thing on the train with me. My flat's got a working radiator and zero taste to ruin. Saves your coat cuff and clears your boot, Ben."

### Turn 3 — Ben
*Ben steps half-in from the pavement, glancing back at the exhaust curling past his shut boot before pointing toward the frame's open corner beside Dan.*

**Ben:** "Two streets in the drizzle or a crowded train will pull that loose corner right apart. Let's use one fair rule: if more than one of us wants the last piece, nobody claims it on the kerb—we rotate it a year each, starting with the dry boot running three steps away. Pass the blanket, Cora, and Dan—"—*his breath catches hard on the name*—"help me slide it flat in the back."

### Turn 4 — Dan
*Dan shifts sideways into the narrow doorway between Ben and the leaning canvas, resting two fingers lightly above the split mitre of the frame. His right heel lifts off the vestibule tile and stays suspended.*

**Dan:** "Oh, brilliant—Ada issues the marching orders, Cora cracks a joke, and Ben invents a bylaw where his boot magically goes first. Notice nobody asked me? Drop the damp-and-radiator act. We all want Mother's painting, and if it goes in your car, Ben, none of us sees it again. Leave the blanket, Cora. Nobody's loading anything until you actually count all four of us."

### Turn 5 — Ben
*Ben raises a palm, takes three paces to the kerb to switch off his idling engine, and pockets the keys. As the exhaust thins, he steps back to the threshold facing Dan.*

**Ben:** "Engine's off. Nobody's sneaking it into my boot. You want all four counted fairly? Tear four strips off Cora's tape, crease one underneath, and draw blind—youngest first. Whoever pulls it takes the painting tonight, whether that's you, Cora, or Ada—"—*his breath catches short on her name*—"so nobody's left in an open doorway pleading a case for her window."

### Turn 6 — Ada
*Ada stoops near the threshold to pull the wooden wedge free, letting the heavy door swing toward the latch against the street noise, then straightens and refolds her coat cuff into a tight, hard square.*

**Ada:** "Keep that tape whole, Cora—we aren't raffling Mother's things on a doormat. Somebody had to run today, Dan, same as when she moved in here and I was the last one to hang that canvas for her, steadying that split corner till it sat level. I hung it last, I locked up behind us, and I'm taking it home."

### Turn 7 — Dan
*In the sudden quiet behind the latched door, Dan slides his thumb carefully along the open gap at the corner of the frame, pressing the loose wood almost flush before letting it spring slightly apart again. His right heel lifts off the tile and hovers there.*

**Dan:** "You only had to steady that corner because I never fixed it, Ada. Every time I came over, Mum asked me to bring wood glue and a clamp, and every time I told her next weekend. Now the flat's locked and there isn't a next weekend. Let me take it home and mend it properly—just once, let me finish what she asked me to do."

### Turn 8 — Cora
*Cora gives a short, bright laugh in the quiet vestibule, picking at the tape's edge toward Ben—then her fingers go suddenly still on the roll. She steps closer to the leaning canvas, blanket draped over her arm.*

**Cora:** "Funny thing is, Dan, I wasn't joking. When I first moved away, Mum sent me a photograph of that painting—split corner and all—since I couldn't pop round on weekends. Tell you what: I'll post you lot the photo and take the real window on the train. Easy swap, yeah, Ben? Saves wasting good tape."

### Turn 9 — Ben
*Ben reaches halfway for Cora's tape, stops, and rests his fingers lightly on the mover's blanket over her arm instead, looking down at the painted window beside Dan.*

**Ben:** "Keep the photo, Cora—"—*his breath catches short on her name*—"and leave the tape. No fair rule divides one canvas by four. Whenever I couldn't sleep, I sat with Mum at that window till morning, and she took it to every flat she lived in. I'm not proposing a draw anymore. I'm asking you for myself—please let me take her window home."

### Turn 10 — Ada
*Ada sets the wooden wedge beneath the brass letterboxes and folds her right cuff into a hard square, looking at the split corner near Dan's thumb.*

**Ada:** "The keys are gone—past this door, none of you needs me to sort anything. Nobody takes it alone. Cora, wrap the canvas in that blanket. Ben, drive Dan home with it tonight to glue and clamp that corner for her. Next Sunday I'll bring wire and hang it level—Cora's wall first since she only had a photo, then yours, Ben, then Dan's, then mine."

---

# Director's Note

### What Was Varied & Directed
- **Fixed Play & Strict Performer Isolation:** Treated `company.md`, `place.md`, and `promptbook.md` as fixed. Each sibling ran in an isolated read-only sub-context given only the shared situation and Piece, `place.md`, `promptbook.md`, their own Persona row, and their own private memory sentence, plus strictly observable moves as they unfolded.
- **Staging Choices on Open Initial Conditions:** To keep departure leverage split across the Company at curtain-up without adding props or characters, I set four concrete starting positions inside `place.md`: Ada at the bottom of the stairs holding the flat keys by the key-return box (her own car parked two streets away); Ben's car as the single vehicle idling three paces away at the kerb with its boot shut (Cora and Dan having arrived without cars); Cora beside the folded mover's blanket and brown parcel tape at the foot of the stairs; and Dan just inside the wedged-open front door beside the leaning painting.
- **Turn Routing (`promptbook.md`):** Followed *"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"* (`Ada -> Cora -> Ben -> Dan -> Ben -> Ada -> Dan -> Cora -> Ben -> Ada`), instructing performers to play only observable action and speech and never narrate consent or movement for the others.

### What Happened
- **Turns 1–4 (Threshold Pretexts & Blockade):** Ada dropped the flat keys into the return slot and reached for the blanket to claim the canvas as one more errand; Cora intercepted the blanket and tape, pitching taking "the poor wonky thing" on the train as a joke; Ben invoked street damp and loose framing to propose a "fair" one-year rotation starting with his kerbside boot; and Dan stepped between Ben and the canvas, calling out all three maneuvers and refusing to let the painting leave until all four counted.
- **Turns 5–6 (Paying for Privacy, First Confession):** Unmasked on his boot rule, Ben walked three paces out to switch off his idling engine—yet still clung to a procedural shield (a blind four-strip parcel-tape draw so nobody had to "plead a case for her window"). Refusing to raffle Mother's keepsake, Ada pulled the wooden wedge to latch the heavy door against the street and staked her claim on having hung the painting last—unknowingly hitting Dan's secret wound by mentioning *"steadying that split corner."*
- **Turns 7–10 (Open Claims & Unresolved Threshold):** In the quiet behind the shut door, Dan confessed putting off Mother's request to glue and clamp the frame; Cora admitted she hadn't been joking and named the photograph Mother mailed when she moved away (still cushioning her vulnerability with a sideways swap); and Ben finally abandoned rules altogether to name his sleepless nights beside Mother at that window and ask for himself. Confronted by all three claims—and admitting that once the keys were gone none of them needed her—Ada could yield taking the canvas alone only by assigning one more structure in which she still carries the burden last (Dan mending it tonight via Ben's car; Ada bringing wire next Sunday to hang it for Cora, then Ben, then Dan, then herself).

### How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exacted Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown:** The cold drizzle/damp, traffic noise through the wedged door, locked stairwell behind them, idling exhaust three paces away, and split-cornered frame on the vestibule tiles fuelled every logistical pretext in Turns 1–3 while sealing off any retreat back into the flat.
- **Holds:** Concrete threshold objects governed physical leverage—the **key-return box** let Ada make locking up irreversible in Turn 1 (`"The keys are gone"` in Turn 10); the **folded mover's blanket and brown parcel tape** let Cora intercept Ada's wrap in Turn 2 and supplied the raw material for Ben's desperate lottery rule in Turn 5; the **narrow tiled passage** allowed Dan's single sideways shift in Turn 4 to physically halt any move toward the kerb.
- **Offers:** Performers leaned directly on the Place's affordances—dropping the keys in the return slot (Ada, Turn 1), citing weather/boot space/running engine (Ada, Cora, Ben, Turns 1–3), touching and testing the open frame corner (Dan, Turns 4 & 7), stepping out to the kerb and back into the threshold (Ben, Turns 3 & 5), and pulling the wooden wedge free to let the heavy door latch (Ada, Turn 6).
- **Withheld:**
  1. *Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:* Kept all four siblings performing brisk kerbside efficiency, jokes, or rules through Turn 4. Earning a space where grief could be spoken literally cost Ben switching off his idling car (Turn 5) and Ada pulling the wooden wedge to shut the front door (Turn 6)—surrendering the pretense of a quick kerbside goodbye.
  2. *Mother's rooms as silent witness:* With the bare wall hook and real kitchen window locked upstairs out of sight, convenience and rules could not be outweighed until each sibling paid their Persona's hardest cost by naming their memory aloud in a drafty lobby—Ada naming her last hanging (Turn 6), Dan naming his broken promise over the split frame (Turn 7), Cora admitting she wasn't joking about the mailed photo (Turn 8), and Ben dropping fair rules to ask for her window himself (Turn 9).
  3. *Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:* Kept a clean, neutral handoff out of reach right through Turn 10. Because carrying the canvas three steps past the other three into one car forces an irreversible verdict, the scene comes to rest with the heavy door still latched and the painting still leaning inside the vestibule—Ada has spoken a schedule to share and mend it, but Cora has not wrapped it, Dan has not stepped aside, and no one has yet carried it across the doorstep into Ben's shut boot.

### Play vs. Staging Friction
- **Staging choice vs. Play gap:** No gap in `company.md`, `place.md`, or `promptbook.md` required changing the authored material. One small ambiguity in `place.md` (*"neighbors, passersby, or a waiting driver can hear... stall the idling car"*) leaves open whether the idling car is driven by a hired driver or a sibling; assigning it as Ben's car left idling at the kerb (a staging choice) avoided introducing an unauthored fifth presence while preserving the exact physical cost of stepping out to kill the engine. Likewise, when a turn affected two claims at once under `promptbook.md` (such as Ada's Turn 6 vetoing Ben's draw on Cora's tape while striking Dan's split-corner memory), cueing the sibling whose private stake was most directly struck kept causality taut.
