# Performance: *Mother's Painting* at *The Building Entrance*

**Turn 1**
**ADA**
*[Stops at the foot of the stairs, drops the flat keys through the brass slot of the key-return box with a sharp clink, then bends to pick up the folded mover's blanket and the roll of brown parcel tape, pausing briefly to fold her left coat cuff into a neat, hard square before stepping into the narrow vestibule toward the canvas.]*
That's the top lock turned and the keys in—we're officially out. Cora, hold the good side steady a second while I get this blanket round the split corner so the damp doesn't get under the mitre. Ben, if you pop the boot and drop me at the end of my road on your way out, I won't have to knock the frame apart taking it on the Tube before I can glue and clamp it properly. Dan, once it's wrapped, kick that wedge out for the porter, would you?

**Turn 2**
**DAN**
*[Shifts squarely across the narrow threshold between the propped canvas and the street step, leaving the wooden wedge untouched while his right heel lifts off the tile and holds suspended an inch above the floor.]*
Hang on—did the three of you decide this upstairs while I was bringing it down, or is handing out little jobs with the parcel tape just how Ada claims the painting without asking? Leave the blanket off it for a second. Every one of us has been eyeing that kitchen window all morning, and Ben hasn't got his engine running at the kerb just to play courier to the end of your road. Besides, if the open mitre is the excuse, I've got wood glue and clamps waiting at mine—I can carry it wrapped on the train just as easily as you can.

**Turn 3**
**BEN**
*[Steps up from the damp pavement onto the outer lip of the doorstep, glancing back through the exhaust at his idling car before pulling the key fob from his coat pocket and holding it unpressed against his thumb, his breath catching short when he says Cora's name.]*
Nobody decided anything upstairs, Dan, and I didn't leave the engine running to play courier to the end of Ada's road either. Even before—Cora's had a word, neither of you is hauling an unglued frame through Tube barriers or onto a damp train platform just to prove a point. If more than one of us wants the window, we put it under one rule that's actually fair: nobody keeps it outright, we each take it for a year, and it starts at mine only because my boot's sitting flat and dry three steps away. Let's get the blanket taped round that split corner and slide it in the back before a warden comes round the kerb, and we can draw the order for the next three years tonight.

**Turn 4**
**CORA**
*[Slides the painting an inch back along the vestibule tiles out of reach of Ada's blanket, tapping the solid side of the frame lightly like a drum before her fingers go suddenly still against the wood.]*
Look at us—keys aren't even cold in the box and Ben's already writing a four-year custody rota so he gets first dibs on the boot. Honestly, it's a crooked bit of kitchen window worth about four quid, so if it saves a fistfight on the step and a parking ticket at the kerb, I don't mind playing Switzerland and taking the eyesore home to mine for the first stretch. My hall wall's still bare anyway, and Ben's practically begging for an excuse to drive me past the Tube. Go on, Ada, hand over the tape before the neighbours think we're dividing up a Rembrandt.

**Turn 5**
**ADA**
*[Slips the roll of parcel tape onto her wrist out of Cora's reach, presses her right coat cuff into a tight, hard square with her thumb, and kneels to spread the mover's blanket flat across the vestibule tiles right against the bottom edge of the frame.]*
Don't call it an eyesore, Cora—if it's worth four quid to you, take your hand off the wood and stop pretending you'd be doing us all a favour. I wasn't sneaking anything past you, Dan; after sorting the rooms and turning the lock upstairs, I'm asking for the one thing left that I actually put on the wall for Mother myself, holding that split corner together with both hands the last time it ever went up. Ben, go switch that engine off so we aren't hiding behind exhaust fumes and traffic wardens—it isn't going on a rota, and I'm taking it home.

**Turn 6**
**CORA**
*[Slips the toe of her shoe beneath the lower rail of the frame to keep it off Ada's spread blanket, starting an easy wave with her free hand before setting it on the wood too, her fingers going suddenly still.]*
Easy, Ada—I meant four quid to a dealer, not to me, so the hand's staying put. When I first moved away, Mother sent me a photograph of this painting on her wall, just that kitchen window from inside so I still had a piece of whichever flat she was in. You got to hang the real thing with her while I made do with gloss paper, so really I'm overdue a promotion—let me take the split corner off your hands tonight. Shift half a pace off that doorstep, Dan, and let me carry it out to flag a cab before Ada tapes the lot of us up.

**Turn 7**
**BEN**
*[Crosses the three paces of damp pavement to his car, opens the driver's door to twist the key out of the ignition so the engine cuts dead, then shuts the door and steps straight back onto the outer doorstep beside Dan with the keys gripped in his fist, his breath catching short on Ada's name.]*
There—the engine's off, so nobody's hiding behind exhaust fumes or a traffic warden, and nobody's flagging a cab past this step either. You and Cora aren't the only ones with a piece of her in that frame—Ada; whenever I couldn't sleep, I used to sit with Mother at that window in the quiet, so don't talk as if my flat isn't in the running too. If you two are killing the rota so one of us keeps it for good, then one straight rule still holds: that canvas doesn't cross this doorstep—not onto the Tube, not into a cab, and not into my boot—until Dan's put his claim on the table too and three of us agree who takes it home.

**Turn 8**
**DAN**
*[Kicks the wooden wedge loose from under the heavy door so it swings partway toward the jamb and narrows the draught from the pavement, touching one knuckle to the gapped corner of the frame while his right heel lifts off the tile and holds there.]*
Spare me the three-vote rule, Ben—that's just our polite family ritual for saying you let me speak before the three of you settle it over my head. You lot get stories about quiet nights and photographs, while the real reason Ada had to hold this corner together with both hands is that Mother asked me three times to fix the frame and I kept telling her I'd bring my clamps next weekend. Now the flat's locked upstairs, there isn't a next weekend, and if Ada takes it home and glues this mitre shut herself, the last thing Mother asked of me gets tidied away without me too. None of us is voting this canvas away because the second it leaves this lobby three of us walk off with nothing of hers left—so keep the tape off it, Ada, and let me take it home for good and mend it myself.

**Turn 9**
**ADA**
*[Slides the roll of parcel tape off her wrist and sets it down on the ledge beneath the brass letterboxes, folds her left coat cuff into a tight, hard square, then rises from her knees while keeping the edge of the mover's blanket pressed right against the toe of Cora's shoe.]*
I didn't hold that split corner together to tidy you out of it, Dan—Mother was standing there waiting to see her window up on the wall and your clamps weren't in the flat, and with the keys in that box there isn't a single room left upstairs for me to look after either. Keep the tape off it, then: I won't put a drop of glue near the wood, and you can bring your clamps to my kitchen table on Sunday and mend the mitre yourself the way she asked. Cora, you still have the photograph she sent you—slip your shoe out from under the rail so we can fold the dry blanket loose round the frame. Ben, you said three of us had to settle where it goes for good before it crosses that step, so unlock the boot: it belongs on my wall, and my door's open whenever any of you needs to sit by that window.

**Turn 10**
**CORA**
*[Gives a quick, light laugh without budging her shoe from under the lower rail where Ada's blanket presses against her toe, tapping the frame once before her fingers go suddenly still on the wood.]*
Listen to you, Ada—the keys haven't been in that box five minutes and you're already handing out Sunday shifts and visiting hours like the new landlady. Tell you what: if the photograph Mother sent me is just as good as the real thing, I'll pop mine in the post to you tonight free of charge—doesn't even need clamping—and Dan can bring his glue round to my table while I put the kettle on for Ben. Until three of us actually vote to swap oil paint for gloss paper, though, my shoe's staying right under this rail—so keep those keys in your fist, Ben, looks like nobody's driving Mother's window off this step just yet.

# Director's Note

### 1. What Was Varied & Directed (Staging Choices vs. Fixed Play)
- **Initial open conditions settled before curtain-up:** While keeping `company.md`, `place.md`, and `promptbook.md` strictly fixed, neither the Play nor the Human brief specified *whose* car was idling at the kerb, how the other three siblings were travelling, or each sibling's exact starting position in the vestibule. To prevent isolated performers from inventing contradictory logistics (e.g., two siblings claiming the car or assuming a hired cab), I set shared opening positions: **Ben's** car was the one idling on a kerbside restriction with its boot shut while **Ada**, **Cora**, and **Dan** had come by Tube/train; **Ada** started at the foot of the stairs holding the flat keys beside the key-return box and mover's blanket; **Cora** stood beside the propped canvas; and **Dan** stood in the narrow wedged-open doorway.
- **Cueing under `promptbook.md`:** Each turn was routed strictly by *"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"* (`Ada -> Dan -> Ben -> Cora -> Ada -> Cora -> Ben -> Dan -> Ada -> Cora`), instructing performers to play only observable action and speech without narrating hidden motives.

### 2. What Happened
- In **Turns 1–4**, all four siblings attempted to claim the canvas without exposing private need: Ada dropped the keys into the return box and tried to wrap the canvas into Ben's boot while assigning tasks; Dan blocked the doorway and offered his clamps; Ben proposed an annual rotation that conveniently began in his dry boot; and Cora slid the frame away from Ada's blanket, calling it a four-quid eyesore she could take home as "Switzerland."
- In **Turns 5–8**, those covers collapsed one by one. Stung by Cora's joke, Ada named hanging the split frame with Mother and challenged Ben to cut his engine. Cora wedged her shoe under the rail and confessed living off the mailed photograph. Ben walked out, killed the ignition, named sitting sleepless with Mother at that window, and imposed a new rule blocking the doorstep until Dan spoke and three agreed. Dan kicked the door wedge loose and confessed that the mitre was only split because he had put off Mother's request three times.
- In **Turns 9–10**, Ada set down the parcel tape and yielded the repair to Dan at her Sunday table and visiting access to Ben, while telling Cora she still had Mother's photograph. Refusing to trade oil paint for gloss paper—and protected behind her quick laugh—Cora kept her shoe wedged beneath the frame, leaving the exchange at a genuine, earned standstill on the threshold.

### 3. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exacted Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown & Holds:** The narrow tiled passage, the split-corner canvas just inside the doorframe, the folded mover's blanket and brown parcel tape, the brass key-return slot, the wooden wedge, and the single idling car three paces away gave every psychological move a physical handle. Once Ada dropped the keys into the return box (Turn 1), going back upstairs for a second keepsake or private room was permanently gone.
- **Offers:** Every sibling used the Place's affordances as tactical leverage—Ada reaching for the blanket and tape, Ben pointing to damp air, boot space, and kerb wardens, Cora sliding and foot-blocking the frame along the tiles, and Dan kicking the wedge free.
- **Withheld (the decisive pressure):**
  - *Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:* Kept earnest confession out of reach until **Ben** paid the cost of walking out to cut the idling engine dead (Turn 7) and **Dan** kicked the wooden wedge loose to swing the heavy door partway shut against the street (Turn 8), surrendering the pretense of a brisk kerbside goodbye.
  - *Mother's rooms as silent witness:* Because the bare hook and real kitchen window were locked out of sight upstairs, logistics (Ada's Tube excuse, Ben's dry-boot rota) and irony (Cora's "four quid eyesore") cancelled each other out in Turns 1–4; each sibling had to pay the cost of naming their private memory—or guilt—aloud in a cold public lobby (Turns 5–8) to keep a hand on the canvas.
  - *Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:* Kept any "neutral" or reversible handoff out of reach right through Turn 10. Because carrying the canvas three steps into one car past the other three in a narrow doorway forces an irreversible loser on three siblings, Dan's body in the doorway, Ben's unpressed fob, Ada's blanket at Cora's toe, and Cora's shoe under the rail physically deadlocked departure.

### 4. Staging Friction: Authored Ambiguity vs. Staging Choice
- The Play staged cleanly with no structural gap requiring revision. One productive ambiguity in `place.md`—which leaves open whether the idling car belongs to a sibling or a "waiting driver" (`Withheld` line 32)—required an explicit **staging choice** before Turn 1 so four isolated performers would not contradict one another about who controlled the ignition and boot. Assigning the car to Ben (while leaving the other three on public transport) integrated cleanly with the fixed text and heightened Ben's temptation to disguise possession as a fair logistical rule.
