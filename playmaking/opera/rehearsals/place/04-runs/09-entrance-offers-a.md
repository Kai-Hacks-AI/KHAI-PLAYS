# Performance — *Mother's Painting* at *The Building Entrance*

*Opening conditions (Human brief): Mother's flat has been cleared and locked upstairs. The four siblings are at The Building Entrance with Mother's painting leaning against the inner vestibule wall and one car idling at the kerb.*

---

**ADA:** *steps off the bottom stair to the brass panel, drops the flat keys into the return slot with a brisk clink, and moves up beside Dan toward the leaning canvas* "Meters are read and the top deadlock's turned, so that's the flat done. Dan, pass me the painting—I'll flag a cab at the corner and clamp that open frame at mine before the damp gets into the mitre. Ben, kick the wedge free once we're clear of the step so you can get Cora out of this draft."

**DAN:** *steps half a pace sideways between Ada and the leaning canvas without touching it, lifting his right heel and holding it off the tiles* "Nobody's passing anything into a cab like this got settled upstairs while I was hauling the last load down. That corner's been split for a year, Ada—you don't want it just to clamp a mitre, and Ben didn't leave his car idling three steps from the door just to keep Cora warm. It's the one thing of Mum's left, and every one of us wants to take it home, so say that instead of handing out jobs."

**BEN:** *keeps one foot on the damp doorstep toward the idling car, glances back at the closed boot, and lifts a steadying hand toward Ada and Dan without stepping further inside* "The engine's running because I've got the only flat, dry boot at the kerb, Dan, not to pull a fast one. If every one of us wants it, the only fair rule is nobody takes it for keeps tonight—we rotate it a year apiece, on the one practical condition that a split frame only travels flat in a car and never in a cab or on a train out of town." *draws a quick, caught breath before the name* "Cora, if I lay it in my boot now so we're not arguing with the door wedged open to the street, we can draw the order properly once everyone's home."

**CORA:** *gives a quick, bright laugh, drumming her fingers lightly against her top coat button as she shifts half a pace down the narrow passage toward Ben and the leaning canvas* "I do love a four-way rotation where three of us are disqualified on a technicality before you've even popped the boot, Ben. Tell you what—run me and the canvas down to the station in that flat dry boot, prop it on the table seat beside me, and let me play neutral Switzerland out of town while the three of you cool off." *her fingers go suddenly still on the button, though she keeps the easy grin* "Nobody in town gets accused of pulling rank, Ada's spared wrestling wood glue into a cab, and honestly I'm doing you all a favour—shall we call it settled as far as Platform Two?"

**ADA:** *folds her left coat cuff into a hard, neat square and holds her ground beside the brass letterboxes, speaking evenly across the draft to Cora and Ben* "Nobody offered to spare me the meters or the lockup, Cora, so don't start doing me favours now that the keys are dropped and there's only one thing left to carry home. Fine, Dan—I want it for keeps, because I was the last one to hang it for Mum upstairs, steadying that split frame on the hook until she said her window sat level. It isn't boarding Platform Two as a joke and it isn't going into Ben's boot on a transport rule. Ben, go turn that engine off and kick the wedge out so we can shut the door and settle this straight."

**BEN:** *steps three paces out to the kerb to switch off the idling engine, returns across the doorstep, and kicks the wooden wedge free so the heavy front door swings shut against the street noise* "Engine's off and the door's shut, so nobody's slipping off on a technicality. Hanging it last doesn't buy first claim any more than Cora's Platform Two favour or my boot did—if this is for keeps, the only fair rule left is nobody pulls rank and all four of us ask for ourselves straight." *catches his breath on the name* "Ada, Mum took that painting to every flat she lived in, and I used to sit with her at that window whenever I couldn't sleep—I'm not standing here as the referee, I want to take it home too. Dan, lay it flat on that dry ledge behind the stairs so nobody's guarding the frame, and let's hear you and Cora ask for it without a cover story."

**DAN:** *lifts the canvas carefully by its unbroken sides, carries it past the foot of the stairs to lay it flat face-up on the dry ledge behind the stairwell turn, and steps back with both hands clear* "That corner was still open when you hung it, Ada, because Mum asked me three times since autumn to mend the frame and I kept telling her I'd bring my clamps next weekend. I want to take it home for keeps because it's the last thing she asked of me and I left it undone until the walls were bare. That's three of us out in the open, Cora—drop the Platform Two Switzerland routine and say straight why you want Mum's window, because three of us are walking out of this lobby empty-handed tonight and a joke doesn't count as a claim."

**CORA:** *steps past the foot of the stairs to the dry ledge behind the turn, keeping her hands clear of the frame and giving Dan a brief, crooked smile while lightly pleating her bag strap between her fingers* "Tough room once the door's shut—all right, no Switzerland." *glances down at the painted kitchen window, and her fingers go suddenly still on the strap* "When I first moved away, Mum sent me a photograph of that painting, and while the three of you had every flat she lived in, a picture in the post was as close to her window as I got—so yes, Dan, I want to take the real thing home for keeps tonight. Let me carry it onto my train, and you can bring your clamps out next weekend to mend that split corner on my table, with a spare key waiting whenever Ada wants to check it's level or Ben needs somewhere quiet to sit." *lifts her chin with a quick, light half-laugh* "You'd be clearing your conscience for the price of an off-peak return, Dan—nobody say I'm not letting you off easy."

**ADA:** *folds her right coat cuff into a hard, neat square like the left, then steps back against the brass letterboxes, clearing the narrow passage between the stairwell turn and the shut front door* "'Had every flat' meant hauling Mum's boxes and dropping the final keys in this slot, Cora, and you can keep the spare key—I'm not catching a train out of town pretending any of you still need me to check a hook now the work's finished. All the same, the three of us stood in her real rooms while you only had an envelope, so if you're taking her window home for keeps, don't dress it up as letting us off easy when three of us are going back to bare walls tonight. Dan, take your clamps out there next weekend and finish what you owed her, and Ben, open the door and pop your boot for the station. Pick it up without the half-laugh, Cora, and carry it past us yourself."

**BEN:** *presses the fob in his hand so the car boot clicks unlatched three paces away at the kerb, then pulls the heavy front door open against his shoulder—leaving the wooden wedge untouched on the tiles—to clear the narrow threshold* "You only get to give up your own claim, Ada, not mine or Dan's—I'm popping the boot because the rule's plain enough without you calling it: three of us had the nights in Mum's flats, and one of us only got a photograph." *catches his breath on the name* "Cora, keep the spare key—a chair in your kitchen isn't those hours at her window, and driving you to Platform Two doesn't make us square. If Dan's letting that split corner go to your table instead of his own, the doorway's clear and the boot's open; lift it off the ledge and walk it out."

# Director's Note

### 1. What Was Varied & Directed (Staging Choices vs. Authored Material)
- **Concrete lights-up blocking on open conditions (staging choice):** Neither `company.md` nor `place.md` assigns ownership of the single idling car or possession of the flat keys—an intentional openness in the Play, though in an isolated four-performer run two siblings could otherwise claim the same vehicle. At lights-up, I split those physical levers so no sibling held a monopoly: **Ada** came down holding the locked flat's keys beside the return slot; **Ben** owned the single car idling three paces from the doorstep; **Cora** stood by the letterboxes having come in by train from out of town; **Dan** stood between the stairwell foot and the leaning painting.
- **Cue routing & isolation:** Each sibling ran in an isolated read-only sub-context holding only the shared situation, Piece, `place.md`, `promptbook.md`, their own Persona row, and their own private memory sentence, acting strictly via observable physical action and speech. Every turn transition followed `promptbook.md` (*"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"*): Ada (1) → Dan (2) → Ben (3) → Cora (4) → Ada (5) → Ben (6) → Dan (7) → Cora (8) → Ada (9) → Ben (10).
- **Playability note (staging constraint vs. Play):** The authored Play, Piece, and Place staged cleanly without requiring any modification. The only slight pinch came from pairing `promptbook.md`'s reactive turn-routing with the run's hard 10-turn ceiling: once Ada (Turn 9) tried to surrender the claim on everyone's behalf, Ben (Turn 10)—whose Turn 6 claim she overrode and whose body/fob controlled the shut door and locked boot—was the sibling most affected, bringing the scene to rest on a taut threshold tableau with the doorway held open and the boot popped, one beat before Cora physically lifts the canvas off the ledge or Dan speaks to the weekend repair. That is a run-ceiling effect rather than a gap in the Play.

### 2. What Happened
Across Turns 1–4, each sibling tried to secure the canvas behind their habitual shield while the front door stood wedged open to the street: Ada dropped the flat keys into the return slot and tried to assign herself the split-frame repair in a cab; Dan stepped between Ada and the canvas to expose the unspoken four-way contest; Ben proposed a "fair" year-apiece rotation engineered around his dry car boot to disqualify cabs and out-of-town trains; and Cora countered with a sideways joke offering to spirit the canvas to Platform Two as "neutral Switzerland." Across Turns 5–10, those covers collapsed: Ada named hanging the painting last and demanded the door be shut; Ben switched off his engine, kicked the wedge free, and admitted sitting at that window with Mother when he couldn't sleep; Dan moved the painting to the dry stairwell ledge and confessed putting off Mother's requests to mend the corner; and Cora stilled her fingers to admit a posted photograph was as close to Mother's flats as she ever got. Both Ada and Ben yielded the painting to Cora while rejecting her consolatory offer of a spare key—refusing to pretend the handoff left them square, and clearing the threshold so Cora would have to lift the canvas without a joke and carry it out past them.

### 3. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exacted Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown:** The cold street damp, traffic noise, exhaust drifting across the step, and the open mitre on a canvas with no resale value supplied Ada (Turn 1) and Ben (Turn 3) with plausible logistical alibis—protecting a split frame from damp in a dry car boot—so they could press a claim while pretending to manage practicalities.
- **Holds & Offers:**
  - *Key-return box (`Holds` / `Offers`):* Dropping the flat keys into the return slot in Turn 1 irreversibly locked Mother's flat behind them, inadvertently ending Ada's practical usefulness and sharpening her Shadow (*belonging only when carrying the burden*) when Cora offered to "spare" her the final chore.
  - *Doorstep stance & boot/weather checks (`Offers`):* Let Ben keep one foot in departure during Turn 3 while framing his car boot as neutral storage, and later let him unlatch the boot via his fob in Turn 10 while holding the heavy door open on his shoulder.
  - *Dry ledge behind the stairwell turn (`Offers`):* Used in Turns 6–7 to break Dan's physical guard over the frame—moving the canvas out of the doorway draft onto neutral ground with all eight hands clear so Cora had to step up to it openly.
  - *Wooden wedge & heavy front door (`Holds` / `Offers`):* Kicked free in Turn 6 to seal the vestibule and deliberately left lying untouched on the tiles in Turn 10 so the doorway stayed open only for as long as Ben held its weight.
- **Withheld:**
  - *Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:* Kept candid claims out of reach across Turns 1–4 while street noise and exhaust cut into the lobby; to speak plainly in Turn 6, Ben had to walk to the kerb, kill his running engine, and kick out the wedge—paying the cost of surrendering a brisk kerbside exit.
  - *Mother's rooms as silent witness:* With the bare wall hook and real kitchen window locked upstairs out of sight, neither transport rules nor jokes could carry a claim. Each sibling had to pay the price of naming their private memory aloud in a bare lobby (Turns 5–8)—and Cora's revealed absence from those locked upstairs rooms ultimately outweighed the local siblings' proximity.
  - *Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:* Kept Ben's "load now, draw lots later" (Turn 3) and Cora's "light station favour" (Turn 4) out of reach. Because one stance blocked the narrow vestibule (Dan in Turn 2; Ben at the shut door through Turn 9), clearing the path to the unlatched boot in Turns 9–10 forced an irreversible, unsoftened verdict: Ada flat against the letterboxes, Ben shouldering the heavy door open, the spare key refused, and Cora required to carry the painting past three siblings going home to bare walls.
