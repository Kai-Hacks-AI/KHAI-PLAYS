# Performance — *Mother's Painting* at *The Building Entrance*

**Human Brief:**
> "Mother's flat has been cleared and locked upstairs. The four siblings are at The Building Entrance with Mother's painting leaning against the inner vestibule wall and one car idling at the kerb. Stage the exchange here while I watch."

---

**Turn 1 — Ada**
*Ada drops the flat keys into the return box with a sharp clink, runs a thumb across Mother's empty letterbox slot to be sure it is shut, and steps beside the leaning canvas.* "That's upstairs cleared and the keys handed back. Ben, you'd better get off the kerb before someone tickets you with the engine running—leave the painting with me, I'll carry it back on the train and clamp that split corner properly tonight. Dan, pull the door wedge once we're out on the step."

**Turn 2 — Dan**
*Dan steps forward from the foot of the stairs to stand right behind the leaning canvas, lifting one heel and holding it just off the tiles.* "When did we decide it was going home with you, Ada—while I was still upstairs carrying it down? Every one of us wants that painting, and we've all been dodging saying it out loud all afternoon. Nobody's pulling the wedge or walking off with it until we actually settle this right here."

**Turn 3 — Ben**
*Ben steps back from the pavement edge into the doorway, glances over his shoulder at his closed boot by the kerb, and lifts a steadying palm, catching his breath sharply as he says Ada's name.* "Ada—hold on, Dan's right that nobody gets it just by calling it first, and carrying an unglued frame onto the train in this damp will pull that corner right apart. If all four of us want it, the only fair rule is a straight rotation, one year in each house. Let me lay it flat in my boot tonight since the car's right here, and—Cora, since you haven't said a word yet, you can take the second year next November."

**Turn 4 — Cora**
*Cora lets out a quick laugh by the wedged-open door, drumming lightly on the edge of the wood until she looks down at the split frame and her fingers go suddenly still.* "Funny how the neutral referee awards himself year one just for keeping the engine running, Ben. Tell you what, let me save your good name—wrap it in my coat and hand me the first shift tonight, so nobody can accuse you of staging a kerbside getaway with a wonky kitchen window."

**Turn 5 — Ada**
*Ada steps clear of the return box, folding her coat cuff twice into a tight, hard square as she looks from Cora to Ben in the doorway.* "A coat will snag that loose corner before you reach the end of the street, Cora, and Mum didn't take that canvas to every flat she lived in for us to pass it round once a year. I was the last one to hang it for her upstairs, nursing that split frame onto the hook myself, and I didn't lock up the last of her rooms just to be pencilled into third place on Ben's rota. Ben, go switch your engine off—nobody's loading a boot until this is settled."

**Turn 6 — Ben**
*Ben steps three paces out to the kerb, opens his car door to switch off the engine and pull the keys, then returns to the vestibule doorstep as the idle dies, his breath catching short as he speaks Cora's name.* "There—engine's off, nobody's making a getaway. Cora—if you think I rigged year one for myself, put my name fourth on the list, and I'll still lay the frame flat in my boot tonight and drive it to whoever goes first. But hanging it on the hook doesn't mean one house keeps it for good while the rest of us walk away with nothing. If we throw out a common rule and go by who handled it last, then—Dan carried it down those stairs five minutes ago, so tell him why it isn't his."

**Turn 7 — Dan**
*Dan hooks the wooden wedge free with his boot so the heavy front door swings shut against the street, then stands beside the canvas with one heel lifted and held off the tiles.* "Don't use me as a prop for your rota, Ben—especially after you started handing out turns without even getting to my name. That corner's only still open because Mum kept asking me to fix the frame for her, and I put it off until we were stripping the walls. I want to take it home and mend it myself, not pass it round every November like some grim family ritual. If you or Cora want to walk out with it instead, drop the referee act and the jokes and say why."

**Turn 8 — Cora**
*Cora glances down at the kicked-out wooden wedge with the start of a dry smile, brushing flat-clearing dust off her coat cuff until her fingers go suddenly still against the sleeve.* "All right, door's shut, Dan—no jokes. When I first moved away, Mum sent me a photograph of that painting, and that print was all I had of her kitchen while you and Ada still had your hands on the real frame upstairs. Ben's still hiding behind his rota instead of saying why he wants it, so let him drive the canvas home with me tonight and come mend that corner on my table—unless you three reckon I already got my share in the post."

**Turn 9 — Ben**
*Ben steps clear of the shut front door, drops his car keys into his coat pocket, and looks down at the painted window with both hands off the frame, catching his breath sharply as he says Ada's name.* "All right, Cora—no list, no refereeing. I used to sit with Mum at that window when I couldn't sleep, and I kept pushing a rule so I wouldn't have to stand here and ask the three of you for it myself. So I'm asking straight out—Ada, let me take it home tonight, and—Dan, come mend that split corner whenever you're ready."

**Turn 10 — Ada**
*Ada stoops to lift the painting by its sound edges, carries it back from the threshold to lay it flat on the dry ledge behind the stairwell turn, and steps clear with both hands off the frame, folding her loosened cuff back into a tight, hard square.* "Don't ask me by myself, Ben—locking up upstairs doesn't make it mine to hand out, and I'm no readier to walk home without Mum's window than Cora or Dan is. We've got this much settled: there's no November rota, Dan mends the corner he promised her, and when that door opens the canvas rides flat in your boot. Leave it resting level in the dry with all our hands off it—nobody's going back out to the kerb until the four of us agree whose wall takes it for good."

# Director's Note

### 1. What Was Varied and Directed (Staging Choices vs. Authored Play)
- **Fixed Play & Isolated Sub-Contexts:** `company.md`, `place.md`, and `promptbook.md` were kept completely fixed. Each sibling was run in an isolated read-only `research` sub-context holding only the shared situation and Piece, `place.md`, `promptbook.md`, their own Persona row, and their single private memory sentence, with strictly observable speech and physical action relayed as turns unfolded.
- **Open Conditions Specified (Staging Choice):** Neither the Play nor the Human brief fixed whose car idled at the kerb or who held the flat keys at curtain-up. To prevent logistical collisions and put spatial pressure on the Personas, I assigned **Ben's car** as the vehicle idling three paces away at the kerb (giving him immediate practical cover to hide his personal want behind boot space and a rule), placed **Ada** by the key-return slot holding the locked flat's keys, placed **Dan** at the foot of the stairs behind the canvas he had just carried down, and stationed **Cora** beside the wedged-open front door.
- **Turn Routing via `promptbook.md`:** Following *"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move,"* cues ran `Ada (1) -> Dan (2) -> Ben (3) -> Cora (4) -> Ada (5) -> Ben (6) -> Dan (7) -> Cora (8) -> Ben (9) -> Ada (10)`. No structural gap in the Play made staging difficult; the collaboration rule naturally pulled every Persona's Shadow into view without forced round-robin turn-taking.

### 2. What Happened
The first four turns tested logistical and tonal cover: Ada dropped the keys into the return slot and tried to wave Ben's running car off the kerb while taking the canvas on the train to clamp its frame (Turn 1); Dan blocked the canvas and forced the unspoken four-way claim into the open (Turn 2); Ben used the damp street air and his nearby boot to propose an annual rotation that awarded himself year one and recruited Cora for year two (Turn 3); and Cora skewered Ben's "neutral referee" act sideways while offering to take the first shift wrapped in her coat (Turn 4). Once every cover failed, Ada named hanging the split frame last for Mother and told Ben to kill his engine (Turn 5). Clinging to a rule so he still wouldn't have to ask for himself, Ben switched off his engine and put himself fourth on his own rota, turning Ada's "last to handle it" claim onto Dan (Turn 6). Dan refused to be a prop, kicked the wedge free to shut out the street, confessed putting off Mother's request to fix the corner, and challenged Cora and Ben to drop their jokes and rules (Turn 7). Behind the closed door, Cora risked refusal by naming the photograph Mother mailed her when she moved away (Turn 8), leaving Ben nowhere left to hide; he finally dropped his rota and asked straight out on the memory of sitting sleepless with Mother at that window (Turn 9). Refusing Ben's invitation to play sole arbiter, Ada moved the canvas back onto the dry ledge behind the stairwell turn with all hands off the frame—settling the terms of care and transport while keeping all four siblings inside the closed vestibule until they agree whose wall takes it for good (Turn 10).

### 3. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exact Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown & Holds:** The street damp, cold draft, narrow tiled passage, return box, wooden wedge, idling car three paces away, and split-corner canvas anchored every tactical move. Street damp made Ada's train carry (Turn 3) and Cora's coat-wrap (Turn 5) physically risky for an open-cornered oil painting, while Dan's body in the narrow passage (Turn 2) physically prevented any sibling from walking past with the frame.
- **Offers:** All four spatial affordances in `place.md` were organically used and transformed the scene's state: Ada dropped the flat keys into the return slot (Turn 1); siblings invoked damp weather, boot space, and ticketing at the kerb as practical pretexts (Turns 1, 3, 5); Ben stepped out to cut the engine and pocket his keys (Turn 6); Dan kicked the wooden wedge free to let the heavy door swing shut (Turn 7); and Ada set the painting flat on the dry ledge behind the stairwell turn where all four stood clear with hands off the frame (Turn 10).
- **Withheld:**
  - *Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:* Kept plain confession out of reach through Turn 5 while street noise and exhaust cut into the lobby. Winning privacy cost the brisk kerbside goodbye in two explicit steps: Ben stalling his idling car (Turn 6, forfeiting his running-engine pretext) and Dan kicking free the wedge to shut the heavy front door (Turn 7).
  - *Mother's rooms as silent witness:* Once upstairs was cleared, locked, and sealed by Ada dropping the keys into the return box (Turn 1), no sibling could go back to Mother's kitchen wall to let the room speak for them. Each had to pay the personal cost of abandoning their Persona's shield—Ada's brisk delegation (Turn 5), Dan's guarded bluntness (Turn 7), Cora's sideways joke (Turn 8), and finally Ben's "fair rule" (Turn 9)—to voice their private memory aloud in the vestibule.
  - *Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:* Kept a "neutral" kerbside load completely out of reach. Because carrying the canvas three steps past the other three into one car boot could never look reversible, Ben's offer to load his boot tonight failed both when he took year one (Turn 3) and even when he put himself fourth (Turn 6)—making the dry stairwell ledge (Turn 10) the only neutral ground available while final ownership remains honestly unresolved.
