# Performance: *Mother's Painting* at *The Building Entrance*

**Human Opening Brief:**
> "Mother's flat has been cleared and locked upstairs. The four siblings are at The Building Entrance with Mother's painting leaning against the inner vestibule wall and one car idling at the kerb. Stage the exchange here while I watch."

---

### Turn 1 — Ada
*Ada drops the flat keys into the brass return slot, folds her sleeve cuff into a hard square, and steps along the tiles toward the leaning canvas, unbuttoning her coat to wrap it.*

"Keys are in, and there's a warden working up the kerb toward your bumper, Ben, so don't sit idling there until you get a ticket. Cora, tilt the sound edge up to me—mind that open corner—so I can tuck it inside my coat for the train. That frame needs sorting properly before it goes near another wall, so I'll take charge of it tonight; Dan, kick the wedge free once we're over the step."

### Turn 2 — Cora
*Cora eases a half-step between the open coat and the wall, sliding the canvas an inch toward her own hip and tapping a light rhythm on the top rail before her fingers go suddenly still beside the open corner.*

"If you board the train with a canvas buttoned inside your front, Ada, people will think we've knocked over a very modest gallery. Save your good coat and let me lug the poor split thing back to mine on my lap tonight—call it my share of the clearing after you handled locking up. If it doesn't suit my wall, you can have first refusal, so let's get moving before that warden reaches Ben's bumper."

*(Director's observable kerb beat: Out on the pavement, the traffic warden has stopped two cars down from Ben's idling bumper, checking windscreens in the damp.)*

### Turn 3 — Ben
*Ben backs two paces to the kerb, clicks his car boot open above the drifting exhaust, glances at the warden two cars down, and turns back to the step, catching his breath sharply on each sibling's name.*

"Cora—Ada, neither of you should be squeezing a split frame onto an evening train in the damp. If more than one of us wants it, the only fair rule is that nobody claims it on the pavement: it rides flat in the one dry boot tonight, and all four of us draw for it or work out a rotation once we're home. Step clear of the doorway a second—Dan—so we can lay it in the back before that warden reaches my bumper."

### Turn 4 — Dan
*Dan stays planted square across the narrow threshold, nudges the wooden wedge tighter under the door with his toe instead of kicking it free, and lifts one heel, holding it off the floor.*

"Stop pretending this is about damp trains or coat linings—all four of us want that painting on our own wall because there's nothing else of hers left upstairs. And we all know what 'sorting a rotation once we're home' means in this family: that boot shuts, Ben drives off, and where it lives gets settled later without me in the room. Nobody's carrying it past this step into a car I'm not riding in—if I'm taking it home tonight to fix that open corner myself, or one of you is, we settle it right here while all four of us are standing at the door."

*(Director's observable kerb beat: At the kerb, the traffic warden reaches the car directly behind Ben's open boot and looks up at the running exhaust; a passerby on the pavement glances into the wedged-open doorway as Dan's voice carries.)*

### Turn 5 — Ben
*Ben reaches through his driver's window to switch off the ignition—letting the exhaust die in front of the watching warden—then steps back to the foot of the doorway, lowering his voice, his breath catching short each time he lands on a name.*

"Keep your voice down with the door wedged open to the street, —Dan; the engine's off, he can write the ticket, and nobody's driving away behind your back. You're right that there's nothing else of hers left upstairs, which is why trading 'first refusal' for the train isn't any fairer than me shutting my boot. If we're settling it on this step, put all four of us under one rule right now: three months a year in each house for that window, we draw right here for first turn, and you can ride in my front seat so it never leaves your sight. Are —Ada and —Cora agreeing to equal shares, or is someone asking the other three to walk away with nothing?"

*(Director's observable kerb beat: Out at the kerb, the traffic warden steps up to Ben's rear plate and begins keying details into a handheld machine.)*

### Turn 6 — Ada
*Ada refolds her coat cuff into a tighter, harder square at her wrist, steps close to the vestibule tiles to set her palm steady on the frame's sound corner opposite Cora, and lowers her voice against the street draught.*

"Mother took this window to every flat she lived in so it stayed put on one wall, Ben, not parcelled out every three months with a split corner until it comes apart. I was the last one to hang it for her upstairs, and now those keys are gone into the slot, it belongs up at mine so there's still one room I'm keeping for the three of you to come back to. Cora, take your fingers off the rail so I can lift this side cleanly—and Dan, pull that wedge in if we're finishing this inside."

### Turn 7 — Cora
*Cora leaves her hand planted on the top rail near the split corner, angling her shoulder toward the doorstep with a quick, bright half-laugh before her fingers go suddenly still against the wood.*

"Do we need to book ahead for visiting hours at yours, Ada, or just buzz up from the pavement? Mother sent me a photograph of this painting when I first moved away, and I've spent long enough making do with a paper copy of her kitchen window while the real one hung here. Tell you what—let me take the frame home on my train tonight, I'll square Ben's ticket on my way past the kerb and cover Dan's fare to come fix this open corner at mine, and you're welcome to the snapshot if your wall feels bare."

*(Director's observable kerb beat: Out at the kerb, the traffic warden slips a printed penalty notice under Ben's windscreen wiper and continues up the pavement, leaving Ben's silent car with its boot standing open to the damp air.)*

### Turn 8 — Ben
*Ben glances once at the penalty notice under his wiper, leaves it untouched in the damp, and steps onto the outer edge of the threshold directly in front of Dan, gripping the doorjamb as his breath catches short on each name.*

"Keep your money, —Cora; I didn't take a ticket just to stand at the kerb as your driver. I pushed for equal turns so I wouldn't have to ask for the whole thing against the rest of you, —Ada, but I used to sit with her at that window whenever I couldn't sleep, and if it stays on one wall, I'm asking to take it home to mine tonight. Both of you take your hands off opposite corners before that open joint gives way, and —Dan, either say why it's yours or let me across the step."

### Turn 9 — Dan
*Dan stoops to yank the wooden wedge free from under the door, steps one pace inward to cup his palm beneath the frame's open corner between Ada and Cora, and lifts his heel, holding it off the floor.*

"That corner's only open because Mother asked me to fix this frame, and I kept putting it off until we were locking up an empty flat. Look at us—two of you gripping opposite ends of a split frame and dressing it up as visiting hours or spare snapshots, when every one of us is really asking the other three to go home with nothing. I'm not taking train fare to mend it on Cora's wall or buzzing up at Ada's like an afterthought; I want to take it home tonight and finally fix the joint she asked me to fix. Step inside before the heavy door swings shut, Ben, and if one of you is still taking it out of my hands, tell me to my face why my wall stays bare."

*(Director's observable doorway beat: With the wedge pulled free, the heavy front door begins swinging slowly toward the jamb where Ben stands on the outer step, cutting down the street noise in the narrow vestibule where Ada's palm, Cora's fingers, and Dan's cupped hand all rest on the frame.)*

### Turn 10 — Ada
*Ada lifts her palm slowly off the sound corner of the frame so she is no longer pulling against the split joint, folds her other cuff into the same tight, hard square at her wrist, and braces her shoulder against the heavy door's inner edge an inch short of the jamb, blocking the street draught while leaving space at the step.*

"I didn't know she'd asked you to fix that corner, Dan, and if she left that job to you, I won't take the repair out of your hands just because the keys are gone and I've nothing else of hers left to look after. Still, I can't tell you to your face why your wall stays bare any more than Cora or Ben can, and I don't know how to board that train tonight knowing I'll never hang her window again myself. Step inside off the kerb, Ben, so I can let this door shut out the street—nobody lifts the canvas from that wall until the four of us work out what we can actually live with."

# Director's Note

### 1. What Was Directed / Varied vs. Fixed in the Play
- **Kept strictly fixed:** All four Personas, private memories, the Piece, *The Building Entrance* (`place.md`), and the Promptbook's turn-routing rule (*"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"*), staged across four isolated read-only performer sub-contexts receiving only their scoped brief and observable actions.
- **Open conditions set by the Director at lights-up (staging choices, not Play gaps):** Because the Human brief and `place.md` leave open *whose* car is idling at the kerb, *who* holds the flat keys, and *where* each sibling stands in the doorway—facts four isolated performers would otherwise invent inconsistently—I fixed the initial blocking before Turn 1: **Ada** at the brass letterboxes/key-return slot with the flat keys; **Ben** as owner of the one idling car at the kerb (the other three returning by train); **Cora** inside the vestibule beside the leaning canvas; **Dan** standing directly in the narrow doorway threshold beside the wooden wedge; and the traffic warden from `place.md` working slowly up the kerb. Performers were also directed to output strictly observable action and speech (no interior monologue).
- **Playability / staging friction:** None required altering the Play. The one coordination hazard in multi-performer isolation—siblings speaking past physical facts like car ownership—was cleanly solved by explicit opening blocking.

### 2. What Happened
- Routing strictly by **most-affected claim** (`Ada -> Cora -> Ben -> Dan -> Ben -> Ada -> Cora -> Ben -> Dan -> Ada`), the siblings first tried to take the painting without asking for it personally: Ada claimed custodial duty over the broken frame while dropping the keys in the return slot (Turn 1); Cora stepped between Ada's open coat and the canvas with a sideways joke and a "first refusal" bargain for the train (Turn 2); Ben popped his dry boot and proposed a "fair" deferred rule (Turn 3).
- Dan blocked the narrow threshold and blew up the logistical pretenses (Turn 4). Cornered between Dan's block and the warden, Ben killed his engine and proposed an immediate four-way three-month rotation (Turn 5). That forced each sibling out of cover in sequence: Ada rejected splitting up the frame and named hanging it last for Mother (Turn 6); Cora kept her hand on the rail, named the photograph Mother sent when she moved away, and offered cash for Ben's ticket and Dan's train fare (Turn 7); stung at being treated like a paid driver after taking the ticket, Ben abandoned his "fair rule" and admitted sitting with Mother at that window when he couldn't sleep (Turn 8); and Dan pulled the door wedge, cupped the split corner, and confessed he kept putting off Mother's request to fix that joint (Turn 9).
- In Turn 10, Ada released her pull on the sound corner—ceding the repair Mother left to Dan without falsely resolving whose wall keeps the painting permanently—and braced the heavy door an inch from the jamb for Ben to step inside off the street before anyone lifts the canvas.

### 3. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exacted Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Shown & Holds:**
  - *Key-return box & locked stairwell:* Dropping the keys into the return slot in Turn 1 made the upstairs clearance irreversible, raising the stakes on the painting as the last physical vessel of Mother's rooms.
  - *Split-corner canvas against vestibule tiles + narrow passage:* Allowed Cora (Turn 2), Ada (Turn 6), and Dan (Turn 9) to place competing hands on opposite corners until physically pulling further risked breaking the open joint (Turns 8, 10), while the narrow threshold let Dan's single stance bar the path to the kerb (Turns 4–8).
- **Offers:**
  - *Weather, train vs. dry car boot, and running engine:* Handed Ada, Cora, and Ben plausible logistical alibis (coat lining, lap on the train, dry flat boot) to mask personal grief as practical care in Turns 1–3.
  - *Wooden door-wedge:* Served first as Ada's errand command (Turn 1), then Dan's toe-brace keeping the threshold open on his terms (Turn 4), and finally the physical lever Dan pulled (Turn 9) and Ada caught with her shoulder (Turn 10) to move the family off the public kerb.
- **Withheld:**
  - *Loading the painting without forcing a verdict (warden + narrow threshold):* Kept Ben's "load now, decide later" escape hatch out of reach. Dan's block on the step plus the approaching warden forced Ben to switch off his ignition in Turn 5 and eat an actual parking penalty notice by Turn 8—paying the exact cost of stalling his kerbside departure without winning automatic custody.
  - *Mother's rooms as silent witness:* Because the cleared kitchen and bare hook were locked out of sight upstairs, neither Ada's managerial authority, Cora's light jokes, nor Ben's rotation rule carried weight on the lobby tiles; each sibling had to pay the cost of naming their private memory of Mother aloud in a cold doorway (Turns 6, 7, 8, 9)—and Ben hiding behind a rule first cost him being treated by Cora as a mere driver whose ticket could be reimbursed (Turn 7).
  - *Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:* A passersby's glance and street noise forced hushed voices (Turns 5–6) and kept private reckoning out of reach until Dan pulled the wooden wedge (Turn 9) and Ada held the heavy door nearly shut while inviting Ben off the pavement beside his ticketed, open-booted car (Turn 10)—surrendering the brisk kerbside goodbye altogether.
