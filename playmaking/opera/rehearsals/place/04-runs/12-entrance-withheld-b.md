# Performance: *Mother's Painting* at The Building Entrance

**Opening Conditions (Human Brief & Curtain-Up Blocking):**  
Mother's flat has been cleared and locked upstairs. At The Building Entrance, the heavy front door stands wedged open onto the pavement; Mother's painting leans unclaimed against the inner vestibule tiles just inside the doorframe; Ben's car is the one car idling three paces away at the kerb with its boot still shut (Cora is travelling back by train and Dan came without a car). Ada reaches the foot of the stairs holding Mother's flat keys by the brass letterboxes and key-return box; Cora stands near the propped canvas; Dan stands beside the wedged-open front door; Ben stands on the doorstep between vestibule and pavement.

---

**Turn 1 — Ada**  
*Ada steps off the bottom stair, drops Mother's flat keys cleanly into the brass return box, and moves beside the propped canvas, folding her coat cuff into a neat, hard square before setting a steadying hand above the frame's open corner.*

That's the flat locked and signed off. Ben, pop your boot before the damp gets into this mitre—you can run me and the canvas round to mine on your way out, since I've got the wood glue and clamps to set the corner right. Dan, kick that wedge loose once I've got it lifted, and Cora, mind your coat hem against the paint.

**Turn 2 — Ben**  
*Ben keeps his hand off the key fob in his coat pocket and shifts squarely into the narrow doorframe between the propped canvas and the kerb, catching his breath sharply on his sister's name.*

Hold on—Ada. We didn't divide three rooms fairly upstairs just to settle the last piece by who owns wood clamps. Before my boot opens, we put this under one rule for all four of us: either we draw lots right here on the step, or we set a strict rotation, a year apiece. Cora and Dan get the exact same shot at it before anyone takes it home to glue up, and if a warden comes up the kerb before we've settled that, it only goes flat in my boot so nobody jumps the line.

**Turn 3 — Cora**  
*Cora hitches her coat hem clear with an easy grin, unloops her wool scarf as if to wrap the canvas, and lets her fingers go suddenly still against the weave.*

Oh, please don't turn Mum's wonky kitchen window into a time-share with a spreadsheet, Ben—I'll save you the parking ticket and Ada the carpentry homework and just tuck it under my arm onto the 4:15. Honestly, a split corner matches every bit of furniture in my place anyway, and you two have already got actual cars full of the good china to keep you busy. Go on, let the Prodigal Daughter lug the awkward bit on the train for a laugh—saves everyone fighting on the pavement, doesn't it?

**Turn 4 — Dan**  
*Dan leaves the wooden wedge untouched under the heavy door, shifts inward beside the propped canvas, and lifts his back heel, holding it an inch off the tiles.*

I'm not the doorman, Ada, and Cora's already carving this up with "you two" like I'm not even standing on the mat. Can we drop the act with the wood clamps and the spreadsheets and the wonky-furniture routine? Those keys just went in the box, the flat's bare, and all four of us want that canvas because it's the only bit of Mum's place left. Nobody's tucking it onto the 4:15 or sliding it into Ben's boot "just for the damp"—I want it at mine, and I'll fix that corner myself.

*(Outside on the pavement beyond Ben's idling exhaust, a passerby glances into the wedged-open vestibule at the raised voice, and four cars down the street a traffic warden turns the corner and starts checking windscreens along the kerb.)*

**Turn 5 — Ada**  
*Ada refolds her cuff tighter into a hard square, glances past Ben down the pavement, and lowers her voice while keeping one steady palm on the frame beside Dan.*

Keep your voice off the pavement, Dan—and fine, forget the clamps. I was the last one to hang this for Mum when she moved into this flat, taking the weight of that split frame in my hands while she stood behind me telling me when it sat level. I've cleared the rooms, squared the agent, and dropped those keys in the slot so none of you had to carry it, and I'm not handing the last piece of her walls over to a commuter train or a yearly rota. There's a warden working up the kerb, Ben, so either kill your engine and pull that door wedge while we settle this properly, or drive round the block empty—because it isn't going in your boot first.

*(The warden moves to two cars away along the kerb, looking toward the plume of exhaust from Ben's car.)*

**Turn 6 — Ben**  
*Ben steps three paces to the kerb, opens his car door to twist the ignition off in plain view of the warden, then steps back inside and kicks the wooden wedge free so the heavy front door swings shut, his breath catching audibly as he says his sister's name.*

Let the warden write the ticket—I'm not driving round the block empty. Squaring the keys doesn't make this yours—Ada; in every flat Mum carried that painting into, I sat up with her at that kitchen window on the nights I couldn't sleep. I wanted a rotation so none of the four of us got shut out cold, because if we're throwing out rules and asking for ourselves, then I'm asking to take it home too. Now the door's shut and the jokes are done, say straight out what you're actually asking for, Cora.

*(With the heavy front door shut and Ben's engine dead, the traffic noise drops to a dull hum in the narrow tiled vestibule; outside on the kerb, the warden slips a yellow ticket under Ben's windscreen wiper and walks on.)*

**Turn 7 — Cora**  
*Cora half-lifts her scarf with the start of a dry smile, then lowers her hand to the bare side of the frame opposite Ada, her fingers going suddenly still on the wood.*

All right, no routine—I'm asking to take it home with me, straight out. I wasn't here levelling hooks with Ada or sitting up nights in the kitchen like Ben; when I first moved away, Mum sent me a photograph of this painting. That's how she kept me in the room with her, and now those keys are in the box, this canvas is the only real piece of her flat left. You wanted the act dropped, Dan—well, mine's gone, so before anyone lifts this off the tiles, tell me straight whether I'm actually in the running or just making up the numbers.

**Turn 8 — Dan**  
*Dan sets two fingers on the open split at the corner of the frame just beneath Ada's palm, lifts one heel, and holds it off the tiles.*

You're in the running, Cora—all four of us are, and trading memories on the doormat isn't going to make three of us politely disappear. That corner's only open because Mum kept asking me to fix the frame, and I kept putting it off until there weren't any visits left. Nobody asked you to drop those keys alone as a down payment on keeping her to yourself, Ada, and you're not clamping shut the one job she asked me to do. Let me take it home tonight to mend that corner properly, and once it's set, it goes to Cora's next and round all four of us—unless you want to look us in the eye right now and say the rest of us don't count.

**Turn 9 — Ada**  
*Ada looks down at Dan's fingers beneath hers, lifts her palm clear of the split corner, and folds her other cuff into a matching hard square.*

When I hung it for her here, that corner was already open—I didn't know she was saving the mend for you, Dan. Of course the three of you count; there's just no flat left to look after once we leave this step, so if I'm taking my hand off the frame tonight, nobody posts it in bubble wrap when their turn is up. You take it home now to set the mitre properly, then it goes to Cora, then Ben, then me—a year apiece, carried through the next front door by hand. Cora, get your scarf round that split corner before we let the damp back in, and Ben—since you took the ticket for all four of us—say straight out if your boot's open to driving Dan and the canvas first.

**Turn 10 — Ben**  
*Ben takes the key fob from his coat pocket, presses the release to unlatch his boot outside at the kerb, and rests his other hand on the heavy door's inner latch, his breath catching once as he says his brother's name.*

The boot's open for you—Dan. That's one rule for all four of us, and I can wait my year after Cora's if it gets that split mended the way Mum asked. Hand to hand at the next front door is part of the deal, Ada, so none of us disappears now those keys are in the box. Get your scarf round that corner, Cora—I'll drop you for the 4:15 on my way to run Dan and the window home whenever you're ready for the door.

# Director's Note

### 1. What Was Varied & Directed (Staging Choices vs. Authored Play)
- **Concrete ownership of the open logistics (Staging Choice):** Neither `company.md` nor the Human brief specifies *whose* car is idling at the kerb or who holds the flat keys at curtain-up. To keep four isolated performers sharing one unambiguous physical reality, I assigned **Ben** the single idling car at the kerb (with Cora heading to a train and Dan without a car) and gave **Ada** the flat keys at the foot of the stairs. That immediately sharpened each sibling's authored Action without altering the Play: Ada could open by trying to finish the job and commandeer Ben's boot; Ben had a dry boot right behind him to hide behind a "fair" fallback rule; Cora had to pitch carrying an oil canvas onto a train; Dan stood empty-handed at the wedge.
- **Cue routing via the Promptbook:** Every turn handoff strictly followed `promptbook.md` (*"When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move"*): Ada (1) commandeered Ben's boot -> Ben (2) blocked the doorway with a rotation/boot fallback that squeezed out out-of-town Cora -> Cora (3) tried a sideways 4:15 train joke addressing "you two" -> Dan (4) blew up all three pretexts and claimed the broken corner Ada was holding -> Ada (5) named her memory and forced Ben's hand on the idling car -> Ben (6) shut off the car, kicked the wedge, confessed, and challenged Cora -> Cora (7) dropped her joke, named the photograph, and put Dan on the spot -> Dan (8) confessed putting off the frame repair beneath Ada's palm -> Ada (9) yielded the mend and first turn to Dan while binding the four to hand-to-hand delivery and asking Ben for his boot -> Ben (10) popped the boot for Dan and brought the scene to rest at the latch.
- **External pressure accommodated by the Place (Staging Choice):** After Turn 4, once Dan raised his voice with the door still wedged open, I introduced two observable street beats straight out of `place.md`—a passerby glancing into the open doorway and the traffic warden working up the kerb to ticket Ben's car.
- **Playability / Authored Material Assessment:** No gap in `company.md`, `place.md`, or `promptbook.md` required changing the Play. One small staging friction worth noting for the author: `place.md` mentions *"neighbors, passersby, or a waiting driver"* in `Withheld` #1 alongside *"either drive the car off empty or let the painting into its boot now"* in `Withheld` #3; specifying in staging that the idling car belonged to one of the siblings (rather than a hired cab) kept `Withheld` #3 playable inside the four-sibling cast without inventing a fifth character.

### 2. How `place.md` Shaped Choices, Exacted Costs, and Kept Options Out of Reach
- **Holds & Offers — The key-return box and locked stairwell:** On Turn 1, Ada used `Offers` (*"Drop the flat keys into the return slot"*) immediately. Once the keys dropped into the brass return box (`Holds`), retreating upstairs to grieve or sort things out in private was permanently out of reach; as Dan (Turn 4), Cora (Turn 7), and Ada (Turn 9: *"there's just no flat left to look after once we leave this step"*) all registered, the threshold became the last room the family shared.
- **Shown & Holds — Narrow tiled passage, propped open-corner canvas, and closed car boot:** Because the vestibule was narrow enough for one stance to block the threshold (`Withheld` #3), Ben could physically bar Ada's exit on Turn 2 simply by standing in the doorframe with his hand off his key fob. Inside, the propped painting's split corner became the literal contact point where Ada's palm (Turn 1), Cora's still fingers (Turn 7), and Dan's two fingers (Turn 8) met—and where Cora's scarf (`Offers`: protecting the open corner from damp/draughts) shifted from a sideways grab (Turn 3) into shared care (Turns 9–10).
- **Withheld #1 — Privacy to grieve or argue unseen:** Dan's raised voice on Turn 4 drew a passerby's glance through the wedged-open doorway, prompting Ada to hush him (Turn 5) and demand Ben pull the wedge. On Turn 6, Ben paid the exact price `Withheld` #1 stipulates: he twisted off his ignition and kicked the wooden wedge free so the heavy door swung shut, giving up the pretense of a brisk kerbside goodbye so they could speak plainly inside.
- **Withheld #2 — Mother's rooms as silent witness:** With the bare wall hook and real kitchen window locked upstairs out of sight, logistical pretexts (Ada's wood clamps, Ben's spreadsheet/lots, Cora's wonky-furniture joke) all collapsed under Dan's Turn 4 challenge. To make a real claim in a drafty lobby, each sibling had to pay `Withheld` #2's cost and speak their private memory aloud: Ada hanging the frame level for Mother (Turn 5), Ben sitting sleepless at that window (Turn 6), Cora receiving the photograph after moving away (Turn 7), and Dan confessing he put off fixing the corner until visits ran out (Turn 8).
- **Withheld #3 — Loading the painting without forcing a verdict:** The approaching traffic warden made Ben's "neutral" fallback (*"it only goes flat in my boot so nobody jumps the line"*) untenable. Forced to choose between driving off empty or forfeiting his leverage, Ben killed the engine in plain sight of the warden and ate the parking ticket (Turn 6)—turning his car boot from a tactical claim into genuine transport he could freely unlatch for Dan on Turn 10.
