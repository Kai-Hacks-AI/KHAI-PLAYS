## [Part 1: Dusk on the Lock Wall](../02-company/initial/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md)

**Place:** [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/initial/place-lock-wall.md)  
**Pieces & Process in Play:** [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md), [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/initial/piece-orchard-barge.md), [Drawing the Chamber](../02-company/initial/process-chamber-draw.md)

*At curtain: Cold October dusk drops fast over [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/initial/place-lock-wall.md) under an active municipal drought restriction. Both upper and lower tarred oak mitre gates stand shut; [Drawing the Chamber](../02-company/initial/process-chamber-draw.md) has not started. Flat black water in the upper pound rests barely half an inch above the forty-two-inch red enamel stripe on [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md). [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/initial/piece-orchard-barge.md) lies snubbed nose-in at the upper lay-by on the last scheduled passage before night lock-close, riding low to her rubbing strake beneath tarped stacks of ripe autumn pears. [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md) stands beside the shut upper gate balance beam in her faded oilskin vest, her brass sluice windlass key clipped at her hip and a lit hand lantern set near [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md). [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md) steps onto the masonry coping from his foredeck with his bow check-line in hand.*

### Turn 1 — [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md)

*Bram steps from the iron foredeck onto the granite coping with the damp hemp bow check-line in both hands, drops a figure-eight turn over the upper lay-by bollard, and pulls the slack taut until the barge's stem settles snug against the timber fender. He looks down the coping toward the lantern beside [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md), then across the shut mitre gates to the brass windlass key clipped at Lena's hip.*

**BRAM:** "Six minutes inside dusk close, Lena. Made the three-mile pound from Lock Three without losing a turn on the screw."

*He reaches back across the gunwale, hooks two calloused fingers under the tied corner of the forward tarpaulin, and lifts a hand's-breadth of canvas off the top slatted crate—damp wood dark at the joints, russet pears packed stem-up and glistening in the cold air—before letting the canvas drop flat again.*

**BRAM:** "Hundred and eighty crates for the five-o'clock wholesale bell down at Basin Wharf. Take the bow line up to the gate bollard now, or do you want me hanging back off the wash while you crack the land-side paddle?"

### Turn 2 — [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md)

*Lena leaves the brass windlass key clipped at her belt, stoops to lift the lit hand lantern by its wire bail, and steps two paces along the coping to the upper wing wall. She lowers the lantern over the edge until the yellow beam falls square across the chipped white porcelain of [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md)—lighting the bright red forty-two-inch band just under the flat black surface—then shifts the beam briefly across the water to the barge's iron hull, riding right down to her rubbing strake, before setting the light steady on the gauge again.*

**LENA:** "Leave the check-line on the lay-by bollard, Bram. Step up here and call the figure on the porcelain for me first."

*She rests her free palm flat on the cold oak of the shut upper gate beam, keeping the lantern angled down at the wet enamel stripe.*

**LENA:** "October tenth drought order holds this pound at forty-two inches through the midnight town intake. Full chamber draw off this level pulls the pound three-quarters of an inch to an inch—puts me under the red before the gates even swing. Read the mark in front of you, and tell me what draft you're carrying under that strake."

### Turn 3 — [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md)

*Bram leaves the check-line made fast on the lay-by bollard, walks four paces up the granite coping to the upper wing wall, and leans out beside Lena's lantern to squint down at the wet porcelain.*

*His eyes flick once across the black water toward the dark midships waterline under his barge's rubbing strake, and his chalk-dry fingers curl tight as he shoves his hand into his coat pocket. He speaks a beat too briskly.*

**BRAM:** "Forty-two and a half on the porcelain. Full half-inch over your red paint. And she's trimmed level—three-foot-five, three-foot-six at most, same October load Lock Three passed down to you at four-fifteen."

*He draws his hand back out of his pocket and raps two knuckles flat against the tarred oak of the upper gate beam.*

**BRAM:** "Lock Three's wash is still coming down the reach behind me—you'll pick back a quarter-inch of feeder creep long before the town opens that pipe at midnight. Keep these gates shut on me till morning and those pears sweat soft in the middle tiers before sunup; I miss the five-o'clock bell at Basin Wharf, and I'll have to write a refused passage inside published hours across your ledger. Crack one land-side paddle slow on the ratchet and lock me down clean—or tell me straight what water you're willing to put in that chamber."

### Turn 4 — [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md)

*Lena sets the lantern base down on the edge of the granite coping directly above [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md), keeping its beam pinned on the wet forty-two-inch stripe. She looks out across the black water to the barge's midships hull where the surface laps flush against the iron rubbing strake, then looks back at Bram.*

*Her right thumb hooks onto the notched brass ward of the windlass key clipped at her belt, rubbing slowly across the cut brass until her knuckle whitens.*

**LENA:** "Bailiff doesn't wait till midnight, Bram. Wall phone inside rings twenty minutes after close, and he takes the mark off that porcelain long before Lock Three's wash reaches my wing wall. And three-foot-six on your hull leaves two dry fingers under that rubbing strake—right now it's awash."

*She unclips the brass windlass key, steps to the land-side upper sluice post, and slides the square socket onto the iron spindle without turning it. She sets her left hand flat on the iron pawl.*

**LENA:** "I won't uncover that red stripe tonight. You've got two ways before close. Leave her snubbed at the lay-by till five a.m. under the October tenth drought hold, and we ink a hold line. Or I crack this land-side paddle three teeth on the ratchet and spend only the half-inch sitting above forty-two. Second the water kisses the top of the red paint, I knock this pawl off and drop the paddle short. If you're truly three-foot-six, you'll clear the upper masonry sill on a checked draw. If you're deeper than you declared, your keel takes the stone and you still sign three-foot-six in my ledger. Call it."

### Turn 5 — [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md)

*Bram's eyes flick once toward the dark midships waterline lapping flush along his barge's rubbing strake; his chalk-dry fingers curl tight into his coat pocket, and he answers a beat too briskly.*

**BRAM:** "Diesel tank's topped aft and the canvas has the evening damp in it—she'll carry three-foot-six clear once she's moving. Sitting here till five a.m. leaves me three locks short of Basin Wharf when the wholesale bell rings and growers' bills due by noon."

*He pulls his hand out of his pocket, strides back to the granite lay-by bollard, and whips the figure-eight turn of the bow check-line free of the stone. Gathering two wet coils into his left hand, he steps back up beside the upper gate balance beam and leans his weight into the hemp until the barge's iron stem eases forward within a foot of the mitre post.*

**BRAM:** "Crack your three teeth, Lena. Spend the full half-inch down to the top lip of that red paint, and don't knock your pawl off a hair early. Minute there's slack on the mitre, I'll push my side beam open and haul her across the sill by hand—we'll settle the ink inside once the gates shut behind her."

*(End of [Part 1](../02-company/initial/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) on the `Triggers` commitment to a checked shallow/partial draw via [Drawing the Chamber](../02-company/initial/process-chamber-draw.md).)*

## 20-Minute `Triggers` Handoff

*Twenty minutes elapse on [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/initial/place-lock-wall.md) as Part 1's operational commitment settles into physical fact:*

1. **Checked Sluice Draw:** [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md) cranked the land-side upper gate paddle three ratchet teeth while watching [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md) under her hand lantern, then knocked off the iron pawl and dropped the paddle shut short of full equalization the instant the upper pound kissed the top lip of the forty-two-inch red enamel stripe.
2. **Sill Scrape & Strained Squeeze:** On thin slack at the mitre post, the upper gates were leaned open and [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md) hauled [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/initial/piece-orchard-barge.md) forward by hand—until halfway across the dressed granite upper sill, her deep-trimmed iron keel (`3'10"`, four inches deeper than declared) ground hard into the stone with a long, grating scrape and hung fast midships. Straining the lines against the chamber bollards—while a shallow lip of upper-pound surge spilled through the cracked gates—finally dragged her stern clear into the chamber so Lena could swing the upper mitre gates shut tight behind her.
3. **Physical State at Part 2 Curtain:**
   - **[Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/initial/piece-orchard-barge.md):** Floating penned inside Lock Four's chamber between the shut upper and lower mitre gates—past the upper sill after rubbing stone, waiting only on the lower paddles and dusk sign-off to drop into the lower reach toward the morning market.
   - **[The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md):** Visible through the lock-house casement under the lantern, the upper pound sits shaved right down on the forty-two-inch hairline—flush with the top edge of the red enamel band on calm water, uncovering a wet thread of red paint (`41 15/16"`–`42"` hairline) whenever night wind troughs the wall, with five hours still remaining until midnight.

## [Part 2: The Dusk Gauge Call](../02-company/initial/plot-2-the-dusk-gauge-call.md)

**Place:** [Lock Four House](../02-company/initial/place-lock-house.md)  
**Pieces & Process in Play:** [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/initial/piece-wall-phone.md), [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md), [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md), [The Dusk Gauge Check](../02-company/initial/process-dusk-gauge-call.md)

*At curtain: Inside the cramped eight-foot whitewashed brick watch room of [Lock Four House](../02-company/initial/place-lock-house.md), lit by a single brass wick lamp. [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md) stands between the casement window overlooking [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md) and [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/initial/piece-wall-phone.md). Three paces away just inside the door, [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md) stands beside the sloped pine desk where [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md) lies open with steel nib pen, inkpot, and blotter. Before pen touches paper, the twin brass bells at the crown of [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/initial/piece-wall-phone.md) cut sharply into the quiet to initiate [The Dusk Gauge Check](../02-company/initial/process-dusk-gauge-call.md).*

> **NIGHT WATER-BAILIFF *(on the district wire as the receiver lifts)*:** "District water office—bailiff on the dusk roll-call. Lock Four: give me your upper-pound inches and your closing passage."

### Turn 1 — [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md)

*At the first sharp ring of the twin brass bells, Lena glances through the narrow casement window toward the lantern above [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md), where wind rippling the dark pound bares a wet thread of the forty-two-inch red band. On the second ring, her right thumb hooks onto the notched brass ward of the windlass key at her belt and rubs across the cut brass until her knuckle whitens.*

*She lets go of the key, lifts the black bakelite receiver from its hook, holds it to her ear for the bailiff's call, and turns her eyes straight onto Bram three paces away as she speaks into the mouthpiece.*

**LENA:** "Lock Four, Lena on duty. Upper pound forty-two inches on the mark, upper gates shut tight. One motor-barge inside the chamber on a checked dusk draw—holding on the lower paddles for ledger sign-off."

> **NIGHT WATER-BAILIFF *(faintly through the earpiece)*:** "Forty-two even, logged; hold your upper gates shut till midnight." *(Click of the district line.)*

*She waits a beat with the receiver at her ear, cradles it back onto the metal fork, and steps to the sloped pine desk. She squares the open double page of [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md) across the wood between herself and Bram, picks up the steel nib pen, dips the point once into the inkpot, and holds the wet nib poised over the blank closing row.*

**LENA:** "You heard the figure I just put on the district wire. Look out that casement at the red thread showing under the ripple—that's the pound surge we lost dragging your hull off my granite sill. Three-foot-six never touches that stone. State the actual mark on the glass and the true draft under your keel before I ink this line, Bram. I'm entering forty-two inches, checked draw, sill contact over declared draft at skipper's risk—and this key stays on my belt till your signature's beside mine."

### Turn 2 — [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md)

*Bram steps once to the narrow casement window and looks out at the lantern beam over [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md), then shifts his gaze down toward the dark chamber coping. His chalk-dry fingers curl tight inside the mouth of his coat pocket, and when he turns back to the pine desk he answers a beat too briskly.*

**BRAM:** "Forty-two on the flat, forty-one and fifteen-sixteenths when the wind troughs it—wet thread of red paint showing under your lamp. Same way you heard me rub stone, I heard you give the bailiff forty-two even on the wire. That kept the district office off both our backs tonight."

*He draws his hand from his pocket and plants his calloused palm flat on the open page of [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md) right beside the skipper's signature column, looking straight at the inked nib in Lena's fingers.*

**BRAM:** "True draft's three-foot-ten midships. Upland growers heard dock talk at noon about tighter stops coming down the flight and put thirty extra crates aboard on consignment credit. I miss the five-o'clock bell at Basin Wharf, I default on their note. But I'm not signing 'over declared draft' under a red drought stamp so the board can pull my carrier ticket Monday and bill my boat for fifty years of chips on your 1928 sill—or start asking why Lock Four logged a grounding on forty-two flat."

*He taps one scarred knuckle on the blank `draw type` and `declared draft` columns.*

**BRAM:** "Your upper gates are mitred dry—not a drop leaking out of that pound—and lifting the lower paddles takes zero water off your mark. Ink forty-two inches, three-foot-ten draft, checked dusk draw—hull clear at vessel's own risk, no claim on Lock Four. Leave 'over declared' and 'sill contact' out of the book, and I'll sign beside you right now so we can drop the lower paddles and clear your chamber."

### Turn 3 — [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md)

*Lena holds the inked nib still above the page for a beat, eyes on Bram's hand resting beside the signature column. Her left thumb hooks onto the notched brass ward of the windlass key at her belt, rubbing hard across the brass teeth until her knuckle whitens.*

*She releases the key, plants her left palm flat on the center gutter of [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md), and sets the steel nib to the closing row. Moving deliberately from column to column across the double page, she inks `17:54` for the hour, enters the barge in the vessel column, writes `3 ft. 10 in.` under declared draft, writes `Checked dusk draw to 42 in. — keel rubbed upper sill on short head; vessel & cargo at skipper's sole risk, no claim on Lock Four` across draw type, enters `42 in.` under upper-gauge inches, and signs her name in the lockkeeper's column.*

*She rolls the blotter once across the fresh ink, turns the open foolscap book square toward Bram on the sloped pine desk, and lays the steel nib pen beside the blank skipper's counter-signature space.*

**LENA:** "I left 'over declared' out of it. Three-foot-ten goes straight in the draft column, so the board has no grounds to pull your carrier ticket Monday, and forty-two flat matches the district wire."

*She unclips the brass windlass key from her belt and holds it in her right hand, two paces from the door latch.*

**LENA:** "What I won't write in my book is 'hull clear' after sixty feet of riveted iron ground across my upper sill. If a plate weeps between here and Basin Wharf, or the engineer sees black iron rubbed on that granite, this line keeps it off my ticket. Put your name in the skipper's column—clean, or with your own note beside it—and I'll step outside now and lift both lower paddles."

### Turn 4 — [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md)

*Bram leans over the sloped pine desk under the brass wick lamp, his eyes tracking across the inked columns from left to right. As his gaze crosses the `draw type` entry, his chalk-dry left fingers curl tight into his coat pocket; he speaks a beat too briskly.*

**BRAM:** "Half-inch iron shoe under her keel—she'll stay dry in the bilge down to Basin Wharf. Fair enough leaving 'over declared' off the sheet."

*He picks up the steel nib pen with his right hand, dips the point once into the inkpot, steadies the foolscap edge with his wrist, and writes clearly across the skipper's counter-signature space: `Risk accepted on hull & cargo only; upper gates shut tight — Bram`. He rolls the blotter firm across the fresh ink, sets the pen down beside the inkpot, and steps a pace back beside the desk, leaving the way to the door latch clear.*

**BRAM:** "Keeps my rivets off your ticket and your 1928 stonework off mine. Come Tuesday working back up empty, I'll show you two dry fingers clear under the strake. Let's get those lower paddles up—I've still got three locks to clear before the five-o'clock bell."

### Turn 5 — [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md)

*Lena steps back to the sloped pine desk and leans over [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md) under the brass wick lamp, reading across Bram's qualification and signature.*

*Setting the brass windlass key down on the desk rail, she picks up the steel nib pen, puts her initials cleanly beside the end of Bram's note, and rolls the blotter once over the mark. She wipes the steel nib dry on the blotter cloth, sets the pen in its groove, drops the stopper into the inkpot, and leaves the open foolscap ledger flat under the lamp.*

**LENA:** "Line stands."

*She picks up the brass windlass key in a firm grip around its shaft, glances once through the narrow casement window toward the lantern burning above the forty-two-inch band on [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md), then lifts the iron door latch and pulls the lock-house door open to the dark coping.*

**LENA:** "Get your stern line onto the lower check bollard. I'll crack the land-side paddle first so the chamber drop doesn't kick your rudder into the mitre—Lock Five's running thin on the tail reach too. Come Tuesday, bring her up with those two dry fingers showing under the strake, or I'll still have the lantern on the porcelain."

***Curtain.***

# Director’s Note

## (a) `Triggers` Handoff and Carried-Forward `Stakes`

- **`Triggers` Handoff Operation:** [Part 1](../02-company/initial/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) ran five brisk turns completely unsteered and cut immediately on the [ENACTS.md](../02-company/initial/ENACTS.md) `Triggers` commitment once [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md) set her brass windlass key onto the land-side sluice spindle offering a three-tooth checked draw to the `42"` lip (Turn 4) and [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md) cast off from the lay-by bollard, hauled his stem to the mitre post, and committed to taking that checked draw while deferring ledger wording inside (Turn 5). - **`Stakes` Carried Forward into [Part 2](../02-company/initial/plot-2-the-dusk-gauge-call.md):** Both Part 1 gambles converted directly into evidentiary leverage inside the eight-foot room of [Lock Four House](../02-company/initial/place-lock-house.md). Because the barge lay penned inside the chamber waiting on the lower paddles (requiring zero further upper-pound water, only Lena's windlass key), Lena held physical gate control over Bram's morning market run while facing a hairline gauge reading on [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/initial/piece-wall-phone.md) that could cost her post. Once Lena shaded the ripple-trough reading to `"forty-two inches on the mark"` on the bailiff's wire within Bram's hearing, mutual exposure forced a real trade on [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md): Bram disclosed his true `3'10"` draft and thirty extra consignment crates; Lena dropped `'over declared draft'` (sparing his carrier ticket) while refusing to write `'hull clear'`; and Bram counter-signed with a targeted qualification (`Risk accepted on hull & cargo only; upper gates shut tight — Bram`), which Lena initialed before stepping out to lift the lower paddles.

## (c) Extra Direction Supplied vs. Authored Play / Reference Seams

### Staging Choices Made by the Director (Within Open Play Space)
1. **Part 1 Opening Turn Assignment:** [Part 1](../02-company/initial/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) (`## Stage`) places Bram in motion stepping from foredeck to coping with his check-line while Lena stands at the balance beam; cueing Bram first let his arrival pressure trigger Lena's signature lantern-on-gauge `Action` directly in Turn 2.
2. **Shallow-Draw Physical Resolution:** [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/initial/PROMPTBOOK.md) (`## Knowledge`) allows a checked draw to leave *"either a strained squeeze into the chamber or a stalled hull."* Because Bram threw hand-hauling leverage onto the mitre slack at `3'10"` draft (`4"` over `3'6"`), staging a **midships sill hang and scrape followed by a strained squeeze into the chamber** kept both Plans genuinely contested in Part 2 (lower paddles still ahead, hairline ripple dip on the gauge) rather than settling passage failure before the ledger scene opened.
3. **Night Water-Bailiff Wire Handling:** Supplied one terse incoming roll-call prompt and a single seven-word log-off click, keeping the bailiff strictly an offstage institutional audit per [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/initial/PROMPTBOOK.md) (`## Agent`).
4. **Ratifying Beat in Part 2 (Turn 5):** Because Bram introduced a substantive liability carve-out in Turn 4 (`hull & cargo only`), giving Lena a fifth closing beat allowed the ledger state to settle by mutual ratification (her initials) rather than an unanswered last-second qualification.

### Overlaps, Gaps, and Frictions Across the Authored Company Files
