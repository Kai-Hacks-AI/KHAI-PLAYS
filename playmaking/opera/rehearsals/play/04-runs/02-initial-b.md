## [Part 1: Dusk on the Lock Wall](../02-company/initial/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md)

### Curtain Up — [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/initial/place-lock-wall.md)

*Cold October dusk dropping fast on [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/initial/place-lock-wall.md) under an active municipal drought restriction. Both upper and lower tarred oak mitre gates stand shut tight; [Drawing the Chamber](../02-company/initial/process-chamber-draw.md) has not started. Flat black water in the upper pound rests barely half an inch above the forty-two-inch red enamel stripe on [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md) (`42½"`), lit by a hand lantern set near the upper wing wall. [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md) stands beside the shut upper gate balance beam in her faded oilskin vest, her brass sluice windlass key clipped at her belt. [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/initial/piece-orchard-barge.md) lies snubbed nose-in at the upper lay-by on the final scheduled passage before night lock-close, riding low to her rubbing strake beneath tarped stacks of ripe autumn pears. [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md) steps onto the masonry coping from the foredeck with his bow check-line in hand.*

---

### Turn 1 — Bram

*Bram steps from the low foredeck onto the granite coping with the damp hemp bow line, throws two quick turns round the upper lay-by bollard, and snubs the heavy iron hull steady against the stone. He glances once toward the lantern over the Red Intake Gauge, then reaches back to hook two fingers under the stiff canvas tarp at the forward stack, lifting the corner an inch or two so the slatted orchard crates and damp newsprint liners show in the grey light.*

**BRAM:** Six-ten by the post clock at Three, Lena. Last scheduled slot before you pin up for the night. Give us a paddle on the upper gate and I'll have her down in the reach before dark settles—these russets are sweating under the cloth already, and the wholesale buyers clear the quay by five.

---

### Turn 2 — Lena

*Lena steps off the balance beam to the upper wing wall, lifts the hand lantern by its wire bail, and angles the lens straight down onto the chipped white porcelain of the Red Intake Gauge so the light cuts across the black water and the wet red band beneath it. Her eyes shift once to the barge's iron bow where the water laps right up against the rubbing strake, then back to Bram.*

**LENA:** You're inside the clock, Bram. Nobody's arguing six-ten. Step over here and read me the inches off that staff before you ask for a paddle—and tell me what draft you're drawing tonight with your strake near awash.

---

### Turn 3 — Bram

*Bram drops the tarp corner back over the crates and walks three paces down the coping to the upper wing wall. He leans over the iron rail to squint down into the lantern light on the chipped porcelain staff. At her question about his draft, his eyes flick once toward the dark midships waterline under his rubbing strake, and his chalk-dry right hand slips into his coat pocket, fingers curling tight inside the wool before he speaks a beat too briskly.*

**BRAM:** Staff reads forty-two and a half—half an inch clear water over town paint, inside published hours. And she's drawing three-seven, near enough; wet canvas and forward stowage always pull her nose down an inch. Crack one gate paddle four teeth, Lena. Bring the chamber up easy off the back-water from Three, float me across the sill, and drop your pawls the minute my stern clears the mitre. If you pen me up here till daylight, that whole hold goes soft in its own heat and I've got nothing to pay the valley carts with tomorrow.

---

### Turn 4 — Lena

*Lena sets the lantern down on the stone coping beside the gauge so its beam stays fixed across the wet red stripe. She leaves the windlass key clipped at her hip, her right thumb hooking onto the notched brass ward and rubbing the cut edge until the knuckle whitens.*

**LENA:** Three pinned his gates at six, Bram—there's no back-water coming down this pound tonight. A full chamber pulls three-quarters of an inch and puts me under the forty-two mark before the bailiff rings my wire at half-past. And don't tell me three-seven off wet canvas when your midships strake is kissing black water; you're down near three-eleven. Even four teeth knocked off at the lip of the paint leaves you short over the stone sill and leaves me nothing for gate seepage till midnight. Run your stern line to the lay-by bollard—upper gates stay shut till morning.

---

### Turn 5 — Bram

*Bram's eyes flick hard to the black water lapping his midships strake; his fist knots tight inside his coat pocket before he pulls it free, steps back to the lay-by bollard, and eases half a turn off his bow line instead of making fast. He holds the wet hemp taut toward the shut upper mitre, looking straight from the brass key at Lena's hip to her face.*

**BRAM:** All right—she's three-ten and a half. Upland carts met me at noon with thirty extra boxes on credit, and if they rot against your wall tonight I default the lot by Saturday. Look at your porcelain, Lena: forty-two and a half is legal open water at six-ten, not under-mark. Give me that half-inch down to the top lip of the red paint—crack one paddle, knock the pawl off right on forty-two, and I'll heave twenty forward crates aft to lift her nose and haul her across the sill on my own rope and my own hull risk in your book. Keep that key on your belt with legal water on the staff, and I'm walking inside to ink a perishable-freight refusal against Lock Four on your dusk line. Put the key on the rack or call me refused.

---

### Turn 6 — Lena

*Lena's thumb presses hard against the notched brass ward at her belt until the knuckle blanches, then drops clean away from her hip, leaving the windlass key clipped shut. She checks the iron pawl seated fast in the upper sluice rack, leaves the lantern burning on the stone beside the Red Intake Gauge, and turns back to Bram.*

**LENA:** Heaving twenty crates aft lifts your nose by pushing your sternpost down to four feet, Bram—you'd hang dead across my upper sill with the mitres jammed open, and spending that half-inch now leaves me bare to gate seepage long before midnight. Three-ten and a half doesn't pass on forty-two and a half. Make your bow and stern fast on the lay-by bollards for the night, then step inside—the book's open under the October tenth stamp, and we'll ink the refusal line straight before the half-past bell.

*She turns from the balance beam, climbs the three stone steps to Lock Four House, and pushes the door open onto the lamp-lit desk.*

*(End of [Part 1](../02-company/initial/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) — `Triggers` cut on commitment to hold [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/initial/piece-orchard-barge.md) above the shut gates overnight.)*

## [Part 2: The Dusk Gauge Call](../02-company/initial/plot-2-the-dusk-gauge-call.md)

### Curtain Up — [Lock Four House](../02-company/initial/place-lock-house.md)

*Inside [Lock Four House](../02-company/initial/place-lock-house.md), a cramped whitewashed brick watch room eight feet across lit by a single brass wick lamp. [Lena](../02-company/initial/persona-lena.md) stands between the casement overlooking [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/initial/piece-intake-gauge.md) and the ringing [Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/initial/piece-wall-phone.md). Three paces away, [Bram](../02-company/initial/persona-bram.md) stands just inside the door beside the sloped pine desk where [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md) lies open under the red October 10 drought-order stamp beside steel nib pen, inkpot, and blotter.*

*As Lena lifts the receiver on the second ring, the night water-bailiff's clipped prompt comes down the district wire:*
> **NIGHT WATER-BAILIFF *(over the wire)*:** District water office—bailiff on the dusk roll. Give me Lock Four's upper-staff inches and your last-passage status.

---

### Turn 1 — Lena

*On the second ring of the twin brass bells, Lena glances once through the narrow casement window toward the lantern burning beside the Red Intake Gauge, then lifts the heavy bakelite receiver from its hook.*

**LENA *(into the mouthpiece)*:** Lock Four, Lena speaking. Upper staff stands at forty-two and a half inches—half an inch clear above the red band. Gates shut, pawls seated, no evening draw. One down-bound hull moored at the upper lay-by: Bram's orchard barge, arrived six-ten inside the timetable, drawing three-ten and a half. Held above the gates overnight under the October tenth order—full chamber would pull the pound a quarter-inch under the forty-two mark. *(Pauses a beat, listening.)* Six-thirty. Understood.

*She cradles the receiver back onto the brass switch-hook, steps to the sloped pine desk, squares the open Lock Four Ledger under the wick lamp beneath the red October tenth stamp, and uncorks the inkpot without lifting the steel nib pen. She looks across the desk at Bram.*

**LENA:** Bailiff's got forty-two and a half and an overnight drought hold on the district sheet. Same columns go down here—six-ten arrival, three-ten and a half declared draft, no draw under the October tenth stamp, forty-two and a half at lock-close. Tell me straight before I put ink on the page whether you're counter-signing clean or putting a protest under it.

---

### Turn 2 — Bram

*At "three-ten and a half declared draft," Bram's eyes flick once through the casement glass toward the dark lay-by outside, and his chalk-dry fingers curl tight inside his coat pocket. A half-beat later he pulls his hand out, steps up to the sloped pine desk under the wick lamp, and plants his calloused palm flat on the foolscap beside the empty skipper's signature column.*

**BRAM:** Nothing clean goes under my name on that line, Lena. I gave you three-ten and a half between two canal hands on the coping, and you read it straight onto the bailiff's wire. You said it yourself into that mouthpiece—a full chamber pulls three-quarters of an inch off the pound whether a boat draws three-six or four feet. Write three-ten and a half down as my declared draft beside a hold, and the growers' factor and the navigation clerk both read it one way: skipper came down over-trim and stalled his own fruit. If you want my ink under that red stamp, you write *three-seven declared, keeper's sight three-ten and a half*, you write *passage refused at six-ten inside hours—pound short for full chamber, not held for hull draft*, and I'm still writing *signed under protest—perishable fruit cargo* beside my name so I've got paper when the valley carts come collecting Saturday.

---

### Turn 3 — Lena

*Lena looks down at Bram's palm flat on the open foolscap. For a second her right thumb hooks onto the notched brass ward at her belt, rubbing it once; then she lets go of the key, lifts the steel nib pen cleanly from the desk, and dips it in the inkpot.*

**LENA:** Bailiff asks why a boat inside the timetable is tied up on forty-two and a half inches of water, he gets the waterline sitting outside my window. I won't put a lie in the draft box, and I won't write *not held for hull draft* when three-ten and a half is why nobody could warp you across that sill on a short paddle either. What I'll give you is the order they actually closed us on.

*Holding the page steady with her left hand under the red October tenth stamp, she writes steadily across the ruled columns—nib scratching loud in the small room—signs her name in the lockkeeper's box, presses the blotter once over the wet line, and turns the heavy ledger half-round toward Bram, laying the pen beside the empty counter-signature space.*

**LENA:** Six-ten arrival, inside hours. Draft column reads *three-seven given, three-ten and a half on strake*. Draw column reads *No draw—held above gates under October tenth order; forty-two and a half pound short for full chamber above forty-two mark*. That tells your factor the town pipe shut the chamber, not late running, and it matches the district wire word for word. Write *under protest—perishable fruit* beside your name if you need paper for Saturday, or leave the box blank. I'm not altering the line.

---

### Turn 4 — Bram

*Bram leans over the sloped pine desk under the wick lamp, his eyes tracking across Lena's blotted columns. When his gaze hits "three-ten and a half on strake," his dry left fingers curl tight inside his coat pocket; a beat too briskly, he reaches out with his right hand, lifts the steel nib pen, and dips it once in the inkpot.*

**BRAM:** Nobody put a gauging rod on my hull tonight, Lena—just lantern light off dark water. That goes down beside my name too.

*Bracing the foolscap corner with his thumb, he writes in hard, cramped strokes across the counter-signature space, signs his name beneath it, presses the blotter once with the heel of his hand, and sets the pen back beside the inkpot, turning the book square to her again.*

**BRAM:** Reads *Bram — signed under protest, perishable orchard cargo; strake unrod-measured; held 6:10 PM on pound order*. That keeps the navigation clerk off my licence and gives me something to lay in front of the upland growers Saturday when this load fetches cannery pennies instead of market price. I'm going out to prop battens under my tarps so the stack gets night air. Have your key at the rack at six sharp if overnight seepage gives you the inch—we've still got two more weeks of October runs up this flight.

---

### Turn 5 — Lena

*Lena leans once over the foolscap to read Bram's cramped line under the wick lamp. Leaving the pen untouched, she wipes the steel nib dry on the blotter edge, presses the cork tight into the inkpot, and closes the heavy cloth cover of the Lock Four Ledger, her palm resting flat on the boards for a beat before she glances through the casement toward the lantern out on the coping.*

**LENA:** Nobody put a rod on her—your line stands as inked. Town pipe shuts off at midnight; if the feeder puts the inch back on that porcelain by six, my key's on the landward rack when the clock strikes. Leave the wall lantern burning beside the staff while you prop your tarps—and next trip down from the carts, Bram, show dry iron under that strake before you clear Three.

***Curtain.***

## (c) Director Interventions vs. Authored Play & Packet Seams

### Director Staging Choices
- **Opening speaker in [Part 1](../02-company/initial/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md):** [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/initial/PROMPTBOOK.md) specifies turn flow *"by the scene"* without naming the first speaker. Because [Part 1](../02-company/initial/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) (`## Stage`) places Bram stepping onto the coping from his foredeck with bow check-line in hand against shut gates, the Director cued Bram first (`Turn 1`), establishing natural alternation across `6` turns (`Bram → Lena → Bram → Lena → Bram → Lena`) until Lena's key-on-belt refusal settled the cut.
- **Offstage bailiff wire prompt in [Part 2](../02-company/initial/plot-2-the-dusk-gauge-call.md):** To honor [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/initial/PROMPTBOOK.md)'s rule against turning the night water-bailiff into a third stage protagonist while keeping within `4–5` cast turns, the Director embedded a single 19-word incoming prompt (*"District water office—bailiff on the dusk roll. Give me Lock Four's upper-staff inches and your last-passage status."*) directly inside Lena's Part 2 opening cue so the entire telephone exchange resolved inside `Turn 1` (`5` cast turns total in Part 2).
- **Closing confirmation beat (`Part 2, Turn 5`):** Because Bram introduced a new evidentiary qualification (`strake unrod-measured`) in `Turn 4` and turned the open ledger back to Lena, the Director ran `Turn 5` so Lena could visibly accept or contest that rider before corking the inkpot and closing the book, cleanly satisfying [ENACTS.md](../02-company/initial/ENACTS.md)'s mutual ledger-settlement Trigger.

### Play & Reference Seams Noticed During Staging
1. **Cast-packet omission of Plot `Cue`/`Stage` and Promptbook rules:** Each isolated performer packet (`cast-lena`, `cast-bram`) contained 11 files (`Persona`, own `Plan`, shared `Places`, `Pieces`, `Processes`, and `Pitch`) and omitted [ENACTS.md](../02-company/initial/ENACTS.md), [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/initial/PROMPTBOOK.md), [Part 1](../02-company/initial/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md), and [Part 2](../02-company/initial/plot-2-the-dusk-gauge-call.md). While withholding the Plot files protects each Persona's private `Shadow` summarized in `Plot ## Tension`, it leaves performers without the authored `## Stage` blocking, turn-count ceiling, or observable-only acting constraint unless the Director relays those rules in turn cues. Splitting public Plot `Cue`/`Stage` from `Tension` (or providing a cast-facing call sheet) would let isolated performers see stage geometry directly.
2. **Draft-measurement rule on a `No draw` hold in [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md):** [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/initial/piece-lock-ledger.md) (`## Apparent`) includes a `declared draft` column, and [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/initial/place-lock-wall.md) (`## Holds`) lists no hull-gauging rod. In performance, the cast turned that silence into sharp working-waterway realism—Lena cited visual strake immersion (`3'10½" on strake`) and Bram countered with `strake unrod-measured`—but if district drought rules formally distinguish declared manifest draft from keeper's rod measurement, stating whether Lock Four holds a gauging rod would clarify whether Lena's visual figure carries official weight.
3. **Turn-count compression (`4–5` turns vs. three-way physical bargaining):** [ENACTS.md](../02-company/initial/ENACTS.md) and [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/initial/PROMPTBOOK.md) target *"roughly 4–5 cast turns"* per Part. When unsteered performers genuinely test staff inches, conceal and expose hidden draft, debate partial-draw trim physics, and commit the windlass key, `4` turns is slightly compressed; `5–6` turns (`6` in Part 1, `5` in Part 2) proved the natural dramatic span without rushing either performer's turn.
4. **Relative skill reference outside isolated packets:** Line 9 of [khai-director/SKILL.md](../../../skills/khai-director/SKILL.md) links [Pitch](../../../skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch.md) via `../khai-playwright/`, which resolves in the full `skills/` tree while an isolated run folder carries only the Play's concrete Pitch file ([Working-Waterway Realism](../02-company/initial/pitch-working-waterway-realism.md)).
