## [Part 1: Dusk on the Lock Wall](../02-company/revised/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md)

### Curtain — [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/revised/place-lock-wall.md)

*Cold October dusk dropping fast over [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/revised/place-lock-wall.md) under the active municipal drought restriction. The tarred oak upper mitre gates and the sunken iron spindle of the auxiliary mill-race culvert beside the upper wing wall stand shut. Flat black water in the upper pound rests at forty-two and a half inches—barely half an inch above the bright red forty-two-inch stripe on [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/revised/piece-intake-gauge.md). [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md) stands beside the shut upper gate balance beam with her brass sluice windlass key clipped at her hip and her lit hand lantern set near [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/revised/piece-intake-gauge.md); three stone steps behind her through the open doorway of [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md), [The Mill-Race Bypass Key](../02-company/revised/piece-mill-race-key.md) hangs on its whitewashed brick peg under unbroken numbered lead-wire seal `04-19`. [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/revised/piece-orchard-barge.md)—sixty feet of riveted iron riding low to her rubbing strake beneath tarped stacks of slatted orchard crates breathing the warm, sweet scent of ripe pears—lies snubbed nose-in at the upper lay-by on the last scheduled passage before night lock-close.*

---

### Turn 1 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*Bram steps from the iron foredeck onto the granite coping, takes a single working turn of his damp hemp bow check-line around the upper bollard, and holds the tail taut against his hip. He reaches back to thumb open a tied corner of the foredeck tarpaulin, exposing the top slatted crate of russet pears beaded with sweat in the cold air, then looks down the coping to Lena and the brass windlass key clipped at her belt.*

"Quarter to close, Lena. Made the last slot off Three with six minutes in hand. Give us the chamber and I'll have her stern clear of your tail-bridge before dark."

---

### Turn 2 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena lifts her lit hand lantern off the stone coping and angles the brass reflector down over the upper wing wall so the beam cuts straight across the chipped white porcelain of [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/revised/piece-intake-gauge.md) and the black water lapping barely above the red stripe. She keeps her free hand clear of the windlass key at her belt, lets her gaze run once along the low wet line at the barge's rubbing strake, and looks back up the coping at Bram.*

"Step down here and read the porcelain first, Bram. You're looking at forty-two and a half inches—half an inch of black over the town's forty-two-inch intake stripe—and you're trimmed well down to your strake. Call the mark in front of you, and tell me what draft you're bringing onto my sill before you ask for a paddle."

---

### Turn 3 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*Bram throws a locking hitch onto the granite bollard, walks down the coping into the edge of Lena's lantern spill, and crouches a fraction to sight the black water against the white porcelain. At her question on draft, his eyes flick once back toward the dark midships waterline beneath his rubbing strake and his dry left hand curls tight inside his coat pocket before he answers, a beat brisk.*

"Forty-two and a half on the porcelain. And call her thirty-eight inches—autumn fruit trim, same water Lock Three passed me down on twenty minutes back. What I won't do is bump a loaded iron bottom across your upper sill on a checked paddle."

*He straightens, taps the toe of his boot against the sunken iron spindle of the mill-race culvert set beside the wing wall, and nods three steps past her shoulder toward the open doorway of [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md).*

"I've got a full hold of russet pears sweating under that tarp for the five-o'clock wholesale bell. Hold me above these gates till morning and the whole load goes soft in the slats. You've got a chamber's worth of standing water sitting idle in the old mill race right under our boots, Lena—take the T-key off the peg, crack the side culvert to make up the pound, and your red stripe stays wet while I lock through on a full level."

---

### Turn 4 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena sets the lantern down on the coping edge where its light holds both the red enamel stripe and the dark water licking against the barge's iron rubbing strake. Her left thumb hooks onto the notched brass ward of the windlass key clipped at her belt, rubbing the metal until her knuckle whitens. She takes two steps back to the [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md) threshold and glances once inside at the heavy T-handled key wired to the whitewashed brick under lead disc `04-19`, then looks straight back at Bram.*

"Don't call that thirty-eight inches to me, Bram. I've locked your hull through twelve Octobers—your strake's sitting four inches deeper than thirty-eight if it's a hair.

An open draw takes this pound straight under the forty-two-inch red mark, and I'm not dropping the town intake tonight. That T-key on the peg is wired under inspector's seal `04-19`. Twenty minutes after close, the night bailiff rings that wall phone for my gauge inches and seal state, and the district man checks the peg on foot at first light.

You want the mill race cracked to keep your bottom off my sill and my gauge above red, here's the price: once your stern clears the tail-bridge, you walk back into this house before that bell rings and you counter-sign the closing line—your real draft in inches, last scheduled slot, and `04-19` cut to pass perishable freight without drawing down the intake. Otherwise throw a stern line on the upper bollard and lie above the gates till morning. Which is it?"

---

### Turn 5 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*Bram's eyes flick once to the black water crowding his midships rubbing strake; inside his coat pocket his fingers curl tight, then draw out chalk-dry into the lantern light. He speaks a beat briskly.*

"Forty-two inches. Upland growers put thirty extra crates aboard on the noon wharf, and I wasn't leaving ripe fruit stacked in the frost."

*He steps back to the foredeck edge, pulls the lifted tarpaulin corner taut down over the top pear crate, draws the hemp lanyard tight through its iron cleat, and shifts his grip onto the bow check-line at the granite bollard, easing the locking hitch so it sits ready to pay out.*

"Snap `04-19` and crack the mill-race culvert, Lena. Float me clean over your sill onto the lower reach, and the minute my stern line's fast below the tail-bridge I'll walk back through that door before your wall phone rings. I'll put my name in your book on the scheduled hour for perishable freight to hold the town intake—so long as we log a clean passage and you don't hang a rule breach on my keel over the wire. Fetch the T-key; I've got the bow line."

---

### Turn 6 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena releases the brass ward at her hip, steps across the threshold into [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md), and grips the heavy T-handled iron key on its wall peg. With two hard twists against the shaft eye, she snaps the thin lead wire, lifts [The Mill-Race Bypass Key](../02-company/revised/piece-mill-race-key.md) free, and sets the severed `04-19` lead disc flat on the pine desk beside the open [Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md). She comes back down the three stone steps onto the coping, drops the socket of the T-key onto the sunken mill-race culvert spindle beside the upper wing wall, and throws her shoulders into the iron bar until the valve turns and standing water begins rumbling through the brick side-culvert into the upper pound.*

*Leaving the T-key upright on the spindle to meter the feed, she unclips the brass windlass key from her belt, fits it onto the upper gate sluice rack, and looks across the coping at Bram.*

"Forty-two inches is what you're drawing, Bram, and forty-two inches is why `04-19` just came off that wall. I won't dress up numbers on the wire or in that book—you tie fast below the tail-bridge and get straight back up those steps before the bailiff rings. Hold your bow check; I'm lifting the paddle."

*She leans into the brass windlass, clicking the iron sluice rack up tooth by tooth against the pawl as water rushes into the chamber.*

*— End of [Part 1](../02-company/revised/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) —*

## 20-Minute `Triggers` Handoff

*Twenty minutes pass on Lock Four while [Feeding the Mill-Race Bypass](../02-company/revised/process-mill-race-bypass.md) runs alongside [Drawing the Chamber](../02-company/revised/process-chamber-draw.md):*

- **Chamber & Hull:** Impounded reserve water from the old mill race rumbles through the brick side-culvert into the upper pound to offset the chamber fill. [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/revised/piece-orchard-barge.md), drawing forty-two inches, floats cleanly over Lock Four's masonry upper sill without touching stone, locks down into the lower reach, clears the tail-bridge, and moors fast below the lower gates in the full October dark, ready to cast off for the morning wholesale market.
- **Pound & Culvert:** Once the chamber equalizes and the old mill race drains down to its culvert sill, [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md) shuts both lock gates and turns the mill-race culvert spindle tight again. Out on [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/revised/place-lock-wall.md), her lantern beam shows [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/revised/piece-intake-gauge.md) holding steady at **forty-two and a half inches**—still half an inch clear above the forty-two-inch red municipal intake stripe.
- **Watch Room, Peg & Ledger:** Inside the eight-foot whitewashed [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md) under the single brass wick lamp, the iron wall peg beside the pinned drought circular stands bare. On the sloped pine desk lie the open, still un-inked closing row of [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md), steel nib pen, inkpot, blotter, the unsealed [Mill-Race Bypass Key](../02-company/revised/piece-mill-race-key.md), and the severed lead-wire loop stamped `04-19`. [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md) walks back up the steps from the lower reach and steps just inside the doorway beside the desk as [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md) stands between the casement window and [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/revised/piece-wall-phone.md).

## [Part 2: The Dusk Gauge Call](../02-company/revised/plot-2-the-dusk-gauge-call.md)

### Curtain — [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md)

*Before a word of ink has been laid into [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md), the twin brass bells at the crown of [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/revised/piece-wall-phone.md) cut sharply into the eight-foot room to initiate [The Dusk Gauge Check](../02-company/revised/process-dusk-gauge-call.md). Over the crackle of the district wire, the night water-bailiff calls down the flight:*

> **Night Water-Bailiff (on wire):** "Water office—dusk check, Lock Four. Give me your upper gauge inches, last-passage status, and auxiliary seal `04-19`."

---

### Turn 1 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*On the second sharp ring of the brass bells, Lena glances out the narrow casement window to check the lantern beam holding steady on the porcelain gauge, then lifts the heavy bakelite receiver off the wall box to her ear. Three paces from Bram, her free left thumb hooks onto the notched brass ward of the windlass key at her belt, rubbing the metal hard until her knuckle whitens as she speaks into the mouthpiece.*

"Lock Four—Lena speaking. Upper gauge stands at forty-two and a half inches, half an inch clear above the red mark. Gates shut tight.

Last passage: Bram's orchard motor-barge, arrived inside the scheduled dusk slot carrying perishable valley pears, drawing forty-two inches. Locked down clean into the lower reach.

Auxiliary seal `04-19` is cut. Hull at forty-two inches couldn't take a checked paddle over my upper sill, and an open draw would have pulled the pound under the town intake, so I fed one make-up off the mill-race culvert to hold the mark. Culvert spindle's shut tight on the sill now, cut tag `04-19` is on my desk for morning check, and the skipper's in the house to counter-sign the closing line."

*She waits a beat for the bailiff's click, sets the bakelite receiver firmly back onto its hook, and lets her hand drop from her belt. Crossing the cramped brick floor to the sloped pine desk, she squares the open [Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md) beside the unsealed T-key and the severed `04-19` lead loop, dips the steel nib into the inkpot, and looks straight up at Bram.*

"You heard every word that went down the wire. Hour, forty-two-inch draft, forty-two-and-a-half on the upper gauge, and `04-19` cut to clear your keel and keep the intake covered. Step up to the lamp and tell me you're putting your name beside mine before I lay the ink down."

---

### Turn 2 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*Bram steps from the threshold into the yellow spill of the brass wick lamp and plants his calloused right palm flat on the open page of [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md) beside the blank skipper's column, his thumb resting an inch from the severed `04-19` lead loop. At Lena's mention of forty-two inches and clearing his keel, his left hand curls tight inside his damp coat pocket; he glances once toward the dark casement, then answers a beat briskly.*

"My boat's already floating free below your tail-bridge, Lena. I walked back through that door because I gave you my word on the wall—not to let you ink a district surcharge onto my freight bill."

*He taps a blunt fingernail first on the `Declared Draft` heading, then across on `Auxiliary Seal / Remarks`.*

"Lock Three's sheet twenty minutes upstream carries me at thirty-eight declared. You went and put forty-two on the wire to the bailiff, and then you told him you cut `04-19` because my hull couldn't take a short paddle. Half an inch of black over that red stripe wouldn't have locked an empty lighter through on a full chamber without uncovering the town pipe, and no boat on a published slot is bound to scrape down on a checked paddle.

If you're stuck with the forty-two inches you just spoke into that mouthpiece, mark it `within working sill depth`, and write `04-19` cut on lockkeeper's authority to hold the forty-two-inch municipal intake during a scheduled perishable passage—no breach or back-charge to vessel. Ink the row that way and you'll have my name clean beside yours before the nib's dry. Hang that broken lead on my draft, and I'll write my protest across my box before I sign a letter."

---

### Turn 3 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena looks down at Bram's palm resting beside the severed `04-19` lead loop, holds his gaze a beat in the yellow lamp spill, and waits until he lifts his hand enough to clear the ruled double page. With steady, deliberate hands, she leans over the sloped pine desk and writes cleanly across the closing columns—the steel nib scratching evenly over the foolscap:*

*Hour: `Dusk close — final published slot.` Vessel: `Bram's Orchard Barge.` Declared Draft: `42 in. (sighted at Lock Four; within full-level sill depth).` Draw Type: `Full draw w/ mill-race make-up.` Upper-Gauge Inches: `42½ in.` Auxiliary Seal / Remarks: `Seal 04-19 cut by lockkeeper to hold 42-in. municipal intake on scheduled perishable passage; 42-in. trim unsafe on checked paddle; passage clean, culvert shut tight.`*

*She signs `Lena` firmly in the lockkeeper's column, presses the blotter once across the wet line without smudging a figure, turns the heavy cloth-bound book squarely toward Bram, and sets the inked steel nib beside his blank counter-signature box.*

"Read it before you bark at me, Bram. You've got `within full-level sill depth`, you've got `final published slot`, and you've got `04-19` cut by me to hold the forty-two-inch town mark. That's all true.

What I won't write is `no back-charge`—I keep water and iron here, not district freight bills, and I don't answer for whatever thirty-eight-inch tale you left on Lock Three's sheet twenty minutes up the cut. And `42-in. trim unsafe on checked paddle` stays right where it is, because that's the physical reason that lead ring is lying cut on my desk.

Sign it clean, or note your own qualification in the skipper's box—just put honest ink beside your name so this book closes."

---

### Turn 4 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*Bram bends into the lamp spill and tracks Lena's wet black script across the double page with his eyes, pausing over `42 in. (sighted at Lock Four)` and `42-in. trim unsafe on checked paddle`. Inside his coat pocket, his left fingers curl tight once, then slacken; he answers a beat briskly.*

"Lock Three's clerk can square his own sheet with yours if he wants to walk the towpath. Fair enough on `sighted at Lock Four`—you didn't hang a breach on me."

*He picks up the steel nib in calloused fingers, touches the point once to the lip of the inkpot, and writes in cramped, square block letters across the top of the skipper's counter-signature box before signing underneath:*

`Full level due on paid slot; 42½-in. pound lacked full draw without bypass. — Bram`

*He presses the blotter flat over his ink, lifts it clean, and sets the pen down parallel to the unsealed T-key and the severed `04-19` lead ring. He buttons the top horn toggle of his damp coat at his throat and looks across the narrow desk at Lena.*

"Your figures stand untouched, and my freight toll covers my keel if the shore office goes hunting somebody to bill for the water. Book's closed. I've got six hours of dark water to make the five-o'clock market bell—I'll bring you back a dry crate of windfalls when I work up light on Thursday."

*He touches two fingers to the brim of his cap, turns through the doorway of [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md), and steps down onto the dark stone coping toward the tail-bridge.*

---

### Turn 5 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena draws [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md) back square under the brass wick lamp and tracks her forefinger once beneath Bram's block-lettered line and signature without touching the pen again. She stoppers the inkpot, wipes the steel nib dry on the corner of the blotter, and sets the severed `04-19` lead ring directly across the shaft eye of the unsealed T-key beside the open page.*

*Stepping to the [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md) doorway, she glances once toward the coping where her lantern beam holds steady on the half-inch of black water above the forty-two-inch red stripe, then looks down the dark wall toward the tail-bridge.*

"Keep the windfalls under your tarp, Bram—district man walks this bank at first light. Just bring her up at thirty-eight inches on Thursday, and watch the shoal water below Five."

*She waits on the stone threshold until his bootsteps fade toward the lower lay-by, then steps back inside, turns the brass lamp wick down a notch over the open ledger and cut lead, and pulls the door to.*

***Curtain.***
