## [Part 1: Dusk on the Lock Wall](../02-company/revised/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md)

**Place:** [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/revised/place-lock-wall.md) (three stone steps outside [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md))

*At Curtain: Cold October dusk drops fast over [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/revised/place-lock-wall.md) under an active municipal drought restriction holding the upper pound at or above forty-two inches until midnight. The tarred oak upper mitre gates, iron sluice paddles, and sunken mill-race culvert spindle beside the upper wing wall stand shut. Still black water in the upper pound licks at forty-two and a half inches (`42½"`)—barely half an inch above the bright red enamel stripe on [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/revised/piece-intake-gauge.md). Three stone steps back inside lamp-lit [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md), [The Mill-Race Bypass Key](../02-company/revised/piece-mill-race-key.md) hangs wired to its brick peg under unbroken numbered lead seal `04-19`. [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/revised/piece-orchard-barge.md) lies snubbed nose-in at the upper lay-by on the final scheduled passage before night lock-close, riding low to her rubbing strake under tarped stacks of slatted pear crates. [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md) stands beside the shut upper gate balance beam with her brass sluice windlass key clipped at her hip and a lit hand lantern set near [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/revised/piece-intake-gauge.md). [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md) steps onto the masonry coping from the foredeck with his bow check-line in hand.*

### Turn 1 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*Bram takes a single turn with the bow check-line around the granite bollard, snubs the iron bow tight to the lay-by stone, and steps back to peel up a corner of the foredeck tarpaulin. Condensation beads along the slatted crate wood underneath.*

"Six minutes inside dusk close, Lena. Cleared Lock Three on the published card."

*He sets his boot on the coping edge, gaze moving from the shut upper sluice rack to the lantern light over the white porcelain staff.*

"Full deck of valley russets sweating under that canvas—due on the wholesale quay at five tomorrow morning. Lift the upper paddles and I'll have my stern clear of your lower gates before the light's gone."

### Turn 2 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena lifts the hand lantern by its wire bail, steps to the upper wing wall, and sets the beam square on the white porcelain staff. Flat black water rests at forty-two and a half—barely half an inch above the wet red enamel band. She holds the light steady there, then shifts her gaze down to the iron hull riding low to her rubbing strake.*

"Clock isn't the trouble, Bram. Read the mark on the porcelain out loud before you ask me to put iron on that rack."

*Her left hand stays clear of the brass windlass key clipped at her hip.*

"Full chamber draw pulls three-quarters of an inch off this pound. Drops me under forty-two before the bailiff rings at twenty past—and you're trimmed deep to your strake."

### Turn 3 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*Bram looks into the lantern light on the porcelain. At her word on his strake, his eyes flick once to the dark midships waterline and his chalk-dry fingers curl tight inside his coat pocket; he answers a beat too briskly.*

"Forty-two and a half. She rides fine—provided I get full sill water, not a pinched draw over stone."

*He taps his boot against the sunken mill-race spindle beside the wing wall, then nods toward the lit doorway where the T-key hangs wired.*

"You've got a full chamber sitting in the old mill culvert, Lena. Crack `04-19`, feed the pound as we draw, and your red band stays covered when the bailiff rings. Pin me till daylight and these crates heat to rot."

### Turn 4 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena sets the lantern on the coping beside the gauge. Her gaze cuts to the water crowding the barge's midships strake, then back toward the lit doorway where `04-19` hangs wired. At her hip, her thumb hooks onto the notched brass ward of her windlass key, rubbing until the knuckle whitens.*

"You're loaded past drought trim, Bram. That's why you won't chance the sill."

*She stills her thumb, standing square between the sluice rack and the house steps.*

"Breaking `04-19` brings morning inspection onto my desk. State your true draft now, tie up below, and co-sign the broken seal and draft line when the bailiff calls—I'll pull that key. Balk at the ink, and `04-19` stays wired: you sit till six, or take forty-two flat over stone."

### Turn 5 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*Bram's eyes flick once toward the dark midships waterline; inside his coat pocket his chalk-dry fingers curl tight before he answers a beat too briskly.*

"Four inches past my drought mark. Took thirty extra crates off the upland growers at noon."

*He pulls his hand from his pocket and takes a working grip on the damp bow check-line beside the bollard.*

"Fetch `04-19` and turn that mill culvert, Lena. Put me down on full sill water, I'll snub short below the tail gates and walk straight inside to co-sign the seal and draft line—logged as scheduled perishable cargo, not an unauthorized draw. Get the key; I've got the bow line."

### Turn 6 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena turns up the three stone steps into the lamp-lit house, grips the T-handled iron key, and twists until lead wire `04-19` snaps. She drops the severed lead disc onto the desk blotter beside the open ledger and steps back out onto the coping. Fitting the iron socket onto the sunken culvert spindle beside the wing wall, she leans her weight into two hard turns; water rumbles low through the brick side-culvert into the upper pound.*

"Scheduled perishable slot goes in the book—right beside four inches over drought trim and `04-19` pulled to hold forty-two."

*She unclips the brass windlass key from her hip and fits it to the upper sluice rack.*

"Ease your bow fender. I'm lifting the land-side paddle."

*(End of [Part 1](../02-company/revised/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) — commitment triggered on bypass-fed full draw via [Feeding the Mill-Race Bypass](../02-company/revised/process-mill-race-bypass.md) paired with [Drawing the Chamber](../02-company/revised/process-chamber-draw.md).)*

## Twenty-Minute `Triggers` Handoff

*Twenty minutes pass after dusk lock-close. Outside in the dark, [Feeding the Mill-Race Bypass](../02-company/revised/process-mill-race-bypass.md) ran alongside [Drawing the Chamber](../02-company/revised/process-chamber-draw.md) to replace the chamber draw volume and was metered shut again once the chamber equalized, leaving the old mill race drained down to its culvert sill. [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/revised/piece-orchard-barge.md) cleared the upper masonry sill on full water, locked down cleanly, and now lies snubbed in the lower reach below the shut tail gates, ready to run down the flight toward the five-o'clock wholesale market once the dusk record is settled. Outside the casement window, the hand lantern illuminates [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/revised/piece-intake-gauge.md): black water in the upper pound holds steady at forty-two and a half inches (`42½"`), safely half an inch above the forty-two-inch red municipal mark.*

*Inside [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md), the iron wall peg beside the pinned drought circular stands empty. On the sloped pine desk under the brass wick lamp, [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md) lies open and still unsigned on tonight's closing row beside the steel nib pen, inkpot, blotter, the heavy unsealed [Mill-Race Bypass Key](../02-company/revised/piece-mill-race-key.md), and the severed lead wire bearing stamped disc `04-19`.*

## [Part 2: The Dusk Gauge Call](../02-company/revised/plot-2-the-dusk-gauge-call.md)

**Place:** [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md)

*At Curtain: [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md) stands in the eight-foot whitewashed room between the narrow casement window overlooking [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/revised/piece-intake-gauge.md) and [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/revised/piece-wall-phone.md). [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md) stands three paces away just inside the doorway beside the sloped desk and open [Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md). Before ink touches paper, the twin brass bells on [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/revised/piece-wall-phone.md) cut sharply into the quiet room to initiate [The Dusk Gauge Check](../02-company/revised/process-dusk-gauge-call.md).*

*As Lena lifts the heavy corded receiver off its hook, the night water-bailiff's clipped voice comes down the district wire:*

> **NIGHT WATER-BAILIFF** *(over the wire)*: "Night bailiff, district office—Lock Four dusk check. Give me your upper-pound inches, last passage status, and mill-race seal `04-19`."

### Turn 1 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena checks the lantern-lit porcelain staff through the casement glass, then speaks into the bakelite mouthpiece, eyes cutting across the tight room to Bram.*

"Lock Four. Upper pound's steady at forty-two and a half—half an inch above the red stripe, gates and mill culvert shut. Worked one downbound orchard barge six minutes inside dusk close. Pulled seal `04-19` for one metered bypass feed to clear a deep perishable load without dropping the town mark. Severed tag's on my desk; skipper's co-signing now. Lock Four out."

*She cradles the receiver on its brass hook, steps to the sloped desk, squares the open ledger beside stamped disc `04-19`, and dips the steel nib.*

"Vessel name and your full draft in inches for the third column, Bram."

### Turn 2 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*At "full draft in inches," Bram's chalk-dry fingers curl tight inside his coat pocket; he answers a beat too briskly, then steps to the desk and plants his calloused palm flat beside the skipper's signature column.*

"You kept it straight on the wire. Vessel's *Orchard*."

*His thumb touches the blotter beside severed disc `04-19`.*

"Ink thirty-four inches—drought card. Any full draw off forty-two and a half needed `04-19` to keep your red band covered. Put thirty-eight in that column and the district clerk pulls my flight permit Monday before I've paid the growers. Write thirty-four, scheduled perishable feed holding forty-two and a half, and I'll sign right now."

### Turn 3 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena's left thumb hooks the notched brass ward at her belt, rubbing until the knuckle whitens. She meets Bram's eyes across the desk.*

"Kept your draft off the wire, Bram. Won't fake it in ink. Left a dip unlogged two weeks back—engineer warned one bad line costs my ticket and this cottage."

*She inks the closing row steadily: `17:54`, `Orchard`, `38"`, `Full / bypass`, `42½"`, `04-19 pulled—scheduled perishable clearance, declared at wall, zero pound loss`. She signs her column and turns the pen toward his palm.*

"At thirty-four the inspector asks why `04-19` snapped, and Lock Five sees your strake tonight anyway. Sign clean, qualify it, or walk."

### Turn 4 — [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)

*At her word on Lock Five and his strake, Bram's left hand curls tight inside his coat pocket; he answers a beat too briskly.*

"Didn't know they had your cottage on warning."

*He takes the steel nib, dips once, and signs `Bram` square in the skipper's column, bracketing beneath it: `Loaded noon; zero pound draw.` He rolls the blotter over the wet line beside severed disc `04-19` and sets the pen in its groove.*

"Gives me an argument with the clerk Monday without crossing a figure of yours. Inspector pushes tomorrow, you point straight to my ink. See you coming up light Wednesday, Lena."

*He touches his cap brim and pulls open the door toward the lower reach.*

### Turn 5 — [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md)

*Lena lets her thumb drop from the brass key at her belt. She checks the blotted row once, stoppers the inkpot, and sets severed lead disc `04-19` square on the open page beside the heavy T-key under the lamp.*

"Mind the gravel bar below Five's tail bridge on that trim, Bram."

*She steps to the casement glass, looking out to the lantern beam where black water holds steady half an inch above the red enamel stripe.*

"Lock'll be open Wednesday. Clear run to the quay."

***Curtain.***

# Director’s Note

## (a) `Triggers` Handoff and Carried `Stakes`

- **Part 1 -> Part 2 `Triggers` Mechanics:** In [Part 1](../02-company/revised/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md), neither performer had access to the other's private file, yet [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md) read [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md)'s low rubbing strake, his refusal of a pinched sill draw, and his physical `Tell` on Turn 3 to deduce that he was loaded past drought trim. She made breaking numbered seal `04-19` on [The Mill-Race Bypass Key](../02-company/revised/piece-mill-race-key.md) contingent on Bram declaring his true draft and promising to co-sign both the broken seal and draft line. When Bram confessed his four-inch overload on Turn 5 (`"Took thirty extra crates off the upland growers at noon"`) and Lena physically snapped `04-19`, opened the mill-race culvert spindle ([Feeding the Mill-Race Bypass](../02-company/revised/process-mill-race-bypass.md)), and set her windlass key to lift the land-side paddle ([Drawing the Chamber](../02-company/revised/process-chamber-draw.md)) on Turn 6, [ENACTS.md](../02-company/revised/ENACTS.md)'s Part 1 closing trigger fired cleanly.
- **Twenty-Minute Physical Accounting:** Following [ENACTS.md](../02-company/revised/ENACTS.md) (`## Triggers`) and [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/revised/PROMPTBOOK.md) (`## Knowledge` — *Bypass-fed full draw*), the transition settled four irreversible physical facts before the telephone bell opened [Part 2](../02-company/revised/plot-2-the-dusk-gauge-call.md): (1) [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/revised/piece-orchard-barge.md) cleared the sill on full water into the lower reach; (2) [The Red Intake Gauge](../02-company/revised/piece-intake-gauge.md) held steady at `42½"` (`½"` above the municipal intake mark); (3) the mill-race reserve drained down to its culvert sill and was metered shut; and (4) the unsealed key and severed disc `04-19` lay beside the still-unsigned [Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md) inside [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md).
- **Carried `Stakes` in Part 2:** Solving the water-and-cargo problem in Part 1 sharpened rather than dissolved the conflict in Part 2 because the two characters entered [Lock Four House](../02-company/revised/place-lock-house.md) with an unresolved split over the third column (`declared draft`). On the wall, Bram had pledged to co-sign `"logged as scheduled perishable cargo, not an unauthorized draw"`, whereas Lena had answered `"right beside four inches over drought trim"`. Inside the house, Lena protected Bram on [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/revised/piece-wall-phone.md) by reporting `"a deep perishable load"` without citing his `38"` overload on the district wire, which emboldened Bram to ask for his `34"` drought-card draft in the ledger so the clerk would not pull his flight permit Monday. That collided head-on with Lena's `Shadow` (the engineer's warning over her unlogged dip two weeks earlier): logging `34"` would leave her unable to justify to the morning inspector why she broke `04-19` instead of running a checked draw, and Lock Five would see Bram's strake down-flight anyway. Their final settlement—`38"` inked honestly alongside `04-19 pulled—scheduled perishable clearance, declared at wall, zero pound loss`, co-signed by Bram with the bracketed qualification `Loaded noon; zero pound draw.`—left both standing behind a single defensible physical record while leaving Bram to argue permit mercy with the district clerk on Monday after paying his growers.

## (c) Director’s Staging Choices vs. Play & Reference Seams

### Director Staging Choices (within fixed Play constraints)

1. **Part 1 opening turn assignment:** [Part 1](../02-company/revised/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) places both Personas on the coping at curtain and [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/revised/PROMPTBOOK.md) governs turn order *by the scene* without naming the first speaker. I cued [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md) to initiate Turn 1 on stepping ashore with his bow check-line so his timetable claim immediately pressed [Lena](../02-company/revised/persona-lena.md) at the gauge.
2. **Using Turn 6 in Part 1 to confirm physical commitment:** When Bram revealed his `+4"` overload on Turn 5 while qualifying how the ledger entry should read (`"logged as scheduled perishable cargo, not an unauthorized draw"`), I ran a sixth turn (within the `6`-turn ceiling) rather than cutting after Turn 5. That allowed Lena to react to his newly disclosed `Shadow` and physically snap `04-19` herself rather than having the Director assume her consent during the handoff.
3. **Bundling the incoming bailiff prompt into Part 2's curtain cue:** Per [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/revised/PROMPTBOOK.md), I supplied one terse incoming prompt from the night water-bailiff over [The Lock-House Wall Phone](../02-company/revised/piece-wall-phone.md) at Part 2 curtain so Lena could answer and deliver her spoken report on Turn 1 without burning extra turns on telephone salutations or turning the bailiff into a third stage protagonist.

### Play & Reference Seams Observed

1. **Plot `## Tension` privacy vs. shared `## Cue` / `## Stage` coordinates:** Both [Part 1](../02-company/revised/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) and [Part 2](../02-company/revised/plot-2-the-dusk-gauge-call.md) summarize *both* Personas' private `Shadows` inside `## Tension`. Consequently, isolated performer packets must omit the `Plot` files to preserve `Shadow` secrecy—which also withholds each Plot's `## Cue`, `## Stage` blocking, and `4–5` turn pacing note unless the Director relays those non-secret stage coordinates explicitly in turn prompts. Keeping `## Tension` pitched to observable structural stakes (or separating private `Shadow` notes into `PROMPTBOOK.md` / `Persona` files) would let performers read the `Plot` cues directly without leaking secrets.
2. **Exact cut-point of breaking seal `04-19` (Part 1 close vs. 20-minute transition):** [ENACTS.md](../02-company/revised/ENACTS.md) (`## Triggers`) and [Part 1](../02-company/revised/plot-1-dusk-on-the-lock-wall.md) (`## Stage`) instruct the scene to cut *the moment* Lena and Bram commit to one of the four courses, and [PROMPTBOOK.md](../02-company/revised/PROMPTBOOK.md) (`## Knowledge`) narrates `"Seal 04-19 was snapped"` inside the between-Parts transition; meanwhile, [Feeding the Mill-Race Bypass](../02-company/revised/process-mill-race-bypass.md) (`## Initiated by`) and [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/revised/place-lock-wall.md) (`## Offers`) treat stepping three paces into the house to snap `04-19` and turning the wing-wall spindle as tactile onstage business. Having Lena snap `04-19` and open the spindle as the physical clincher of Part 1's final turn reconciled both cleanly while leaving the twenty-minute equalization and lock-down to the handoff.
3. **Unspecified numeric baseline for drought draft and sill depth:** [The Lock Four Ledger](../02-company/revised/piece-lock-ledger.md) requires `"declared draft"` in inches, whereas [Bram](../02-company/revised/persona-bram.md) and [Bram's Orchard Barge](../02-company/revised/piece-orchard-barge.md) define his trim only relatively (`"four inches deeper than his normal drought trim"`), and neither [Lock Four Wall](../02-company/revised/place-lock-wall.md) nor [Drawing the Chamber](../02-company/revised/process-chamber-draw.md) specifies a numeric drought-card draft or chamber-draw inch drop. Here, Lena supplied `"three-quarters of an inch"` for an unassisted chamber draw and Bram supplied `"thirty-four inches—drought card"` (`38"` actual), which aligned seamlessly across turns; anchoring canonical inch figures in the `Piece` or `Place` files would prevent independent performers from stating incompatible numbers simultaneously.
