# Fare on the Bare Plowlands

## Taxonomy

Opening Plot of [*The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse*](play.md), tuned by default to [Table Pride and Friendly Irony](pitch-fable-table.md).

## Owner

*The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse* (`source-based` rehearsal Company).

## Cue

On [The Bare Plowlands and Hedgerow](place-bare-plowlands.md), [The Country Mouse](persona-country-mouse.md) holds [Host of the Repast](position-host.md) after inviting his intimate friend [The Town Mouse](persona-town-mouse.md) to visit; [Wheat-Stocks and Hedgerow Roots](piece-country-fare.md) are already pulled and set out on bare soil, and every gritty chew invites comparison between country labour and town ease.

## Action

[The Country Mouse](persona-country-mouse.md) works to honour an old friend with the best store his hedgerow yields while keeping his dignity on home ground; [The Town Mouse](persona-town-mouse.md) tastes the dry fare, launches [Convert the Country Friend to Town Luxury](plan-share-the-horn-of-plenty.md) by calling this "the life of the ants," and presses his friend to come back with him right now for an ample share of the town house's "horn of plenty." Their efforts collide over whether plain safety is a life worth defending or a drudgery a true friend should rescue him from.

## Stage

Both Personas begin together beside the hedgerow scrape on [The Bare Plowlands and Hedgerow](place-bare-plowlands.md) with [Wheat-Stocks and Hedgerow Roots](piece-country-fare.md) between them; no human approaches. Fixed condition: the meal here is coarse but completely unthreatened. How vigorously [The Country Mouse](persona-country-mouse.md) defends his fare, what promises [The Town Mouse](persona-town-mouse.md) makes about his house, and on what terms the invitation is accepted remain open to play.

## Tension

[The Country Mouse](persona-country-mouse.md) privately measures his rough roots through his sleek friend's eyes and feels a sting of provincial smallness (`Shadow`), while [The Town Mouse](persona-town-mouse.md) sincerely believes his own boast of "every luxury" (`Shadow`) and leaves out—without quite admitting it to himself—what happens in his house whenever a door latch lifts.
