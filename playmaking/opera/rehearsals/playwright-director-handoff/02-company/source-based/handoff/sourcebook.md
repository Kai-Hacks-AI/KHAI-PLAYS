# Sourcebook — *The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse*

## Line of Work

This Sourcebook documents the Playwright's adaptation of Aesop's *The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse* (George Fyler Townsend translation, Project Gutenberg eBook `#21`) into a complete, playable KHAI Play ([`play.md`](play.md)) and linked Company in `handoff/`. It explains six core design decisions:

1. Dividing the single-paragraph fable into three sequential Plots ([`plot-01-bare-plowlands.md`](plot-01-bare-plowlands.md), [`plot-02-horn-of-plenty.md`](plot-02-horn-of-plenty.md), [`plot-03-second-footstep.md`](plot-03-second-footstep.md)) so persuasion, interrupted conversion, and famished reckoning each turn on a distinct live choice.
2. Authoring one transferable Position ([`position-host.md`](position-host.md)) that passes from the Country Mouse in Plot 1 to the Town Mouse in Plots 2–3.
3. Staging the action across two contrasting Places ([`place-bare-plowlands.md`](place-bare-plowlands.md) and [`place-town-larder.md`](place-town-larder.md)), folding the narrow refuge hole and cupboard threshold into the town room rather than splitting them into separate scenes.
4. Concentrating prop handling on two load-bearing Pieces ([`piece-country-fare.md`](piece-country-fare.md) and [`piece-basket-cheese.md`](piece-basket-cheese.md)) while holding the remaining six town provisions inside [`place-town-larder.md`](place-town-larder.md).
5. Representing the two unseen humans as an environmental Process ([`process-household-intrusion.md`](process-household-intrusion.md)) rather than onstage Personas, paired with Town Mouse's active campaign ([`plan-share-the-horn-of-plenty.md`](plan-share-the-horn-of-plenty.md)).
6. Pairing a Play-wide social-comedy Pitch ([`pitch-fable-table.md`](pitch-fable-table.md)) with a Plot 3 shift to floor-level dread ([`pitch-footstep-dread.md`](pitch-footstep-dread.md)).

## Restrictions

- **Directly supported by the eBook `#21` text:**
  - The two mice begin as “intimate friend[s],” with Country Mouse inviting Town Mouse first (`S1`).
  - Country fare consists specifically of “wheat-stocks and roots pulled up from the hedgerow” eaten on “the bare plowlands” (`S2`).
  - Town Mouse contrasts “the life of the ants” with his house as “the horn of plenty,” claiming “every luxury” and offering “an ample share of my dainties”; Country Mouse is “easily persuaded” and travels to town with him (`S2`–`S3`).
  - Town Mouse lays out seven named foods in order—“bread, barley, beans, dried figs, honey, raisins, and, last of all, brought a dainty piece of cheese from a basket”—prompting Country Mouse's warm delight and lamentation over his “own hard fate” (`S4`–`S5`).
  - Two distinct interruptions abort the meal before Country Mouse can eat his fill: first, “someone opened the door,” sending both mice “squeaking, as fast as they could, to a hole so narrow that two could only find room in it by squeezing” (`S6`); second, after they have “scarcely begun their repast again,” “someone else entered to take something out of a cupboard,” leaving them “more frightened than before” as they run away and hide (`S7`).
  - Country Mouse reaches the end “almost famished” beside a “dainty a feast” and states his preference for bare plowlands and hedgerow roots where he can live “in safety, and without fear”; eBook `#21` appends no external narrator moral (`S8`).
- **Added or inferred theatrical adaptations (requiring rehearsal verification):**
  - Psychological blind spots (`Shadow`) and involuntary physical signs (`Tell`) on [`persona-country-mouse.md`](persona-country-mouse.md) and [`persona-town-mouse.md`](persona-town-mouse.md) are inferred from their contrasting reactions to plainness, luxury, and routine household alarm.
  - Object histories (`Yearbook`) on [`piece-country-fare.md`](piece-country-fare.md) and [`piece-basket-cheese.md`](piece-basket-cheese.md) extrapolate how each host gathered the food immediately prior to the guest's arrival.
  - Treating the town spread, basket, cupboard, door, and narrow wainscot hole as one contiguous playable room in [`place-town-larder.md`](place-town-larder.md) infers domestic proximity from the mice's split-second dashes between repast and cover (`S6`–`S7`).
  - Leaving Plot 1 persuasion, Plot 2 squeeze-hole friction, and Plot 3 departure open to live performer choice adapts prose past-tense certainty into playable KHAI tension; Director rehearsal must verify that the authored pressures reliably invite—without forcing—the fable's arc.
- **Boundaries for future authors and Directors:**
  - **No imported predators or props from rival versions:** Do not add cats, mastiffs, mousetraps, velvet carpets, or named stewards found in Horace, Babrius, or La Fontaine; Townsend's eBook `#21` threat is strictly ordinary human doorway and cupboard traffic (`S6`–`S7`).
  - **No detached moralizer:** Keep evaluative claims inside character action and speech rather than adding an external Aesopic maxim absent from eBook `#21`.
  - **Preserve mouse-level scale:** Keep the two humans offstage as cues driven by [`process-household-intrusion.md`](process-household-intrusion.md) rather than speaking roles.
  - **Repository coverage note:** Because this Sourcebook was authored inside an isolated rehearsal workspace, shared House collections outside the run directory were not inspected for overlapping adaptations.
