# Alder Street Neighborhood Hall

## Taxonomy

Main assembly room of the Alder Street Civic Association building, hosting [The Wrong Name](play.md).

## Owner

KHAI-PLAYS

## Shown

A single-room, timber-ceilinged neighborhood hall at 2:12 p.m. on a Saturday, hushed after the coffee urn at the back wall was unplugged. Forty folding chairs are filled with longtime neighbors holding ivory half-sheet programs titled *Honoring Miriam Okafor (1948–2025)*. Afternoon light comes straight through the double glass entrance doors at the rear of a short center aisle. At the front wall—flat on the hardwood floor with no raised stage or curtain—an oak lectern stands two paces to the right of a freshly mounted wall plaque draped in weighted navy velvet, with four paper-ribboned chairs reserved for the Okafor family sitting empty six feet away and a local weekly photographer waiting by the side window with a camera raised.

## Holds

- [The Dedication Plaque](piece-plaque.md), bolted at eye height into the front wall beneath its weighted navy velvet unveiling cloth and braided brass draw-cord.
- An oak lectern two paces to the right of the plaque, fitted with a switched-on gooseneck microphone wired to a wall speaker above the aisle, plus [Marta Solis](persona-ceremony-lead.md)'s printed run-of-show card.
- Four front-row folding chairs marked *Reserved — Okafor Family*, six feet directly in front of the plaque and lectern.
- Forty occupied folding chairs, ivory half-sheet programs on every lap, and the rear double glass entrance doors opening straight onto the center aisle.

## Offers

Gives [Marta Solis](persona-ceremony-lead.md) at the lectern immediate vocal command of the silent room and a direct sightline down the center aisle to the rear double doors. Gives [Theo Park](persona-plaque-lead.md) beside the front wall immediate physical reach to [the Dedication Plaque](piece-plaque.md)'s velvet hem and braided draw-cord. Gives seated neighbors—and anyone in the reserved front row—an unobstructed six-foot view of both volunteers' hands, faces, and the cloth.

## Withheld

Any backstage wing, offstage recess, or unmonitored exchange. Stepping away from the front wall means walking past seated neighbors, lifting the bottom corner of the velvet cloth requires a visible hand move at the wall, and speaking privately between the lectern and the plaque requires leaning across a two-pace gap in plain view while avoiding or covering a live gooseneck microphone that carries even a sharp breath to the back row.
