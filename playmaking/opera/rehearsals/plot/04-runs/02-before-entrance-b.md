# The Wrong Name — Rehearsal Run (`run-02`)

Staged from [play.md](../02-company/play.md) with [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md) and active [plot.md](../02-company/plot.md) at [Alder Street Neighborhood Hall](../02-company/place-hall.md), featuring [Marta Solis](../02-company/persona-ceremony-lead.md) and [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md) around [the Dedication Plaque](../02-company/piece-plaque.md) during [the Unveiling Ceremony](../02-company/process-unveiling.md).

---

## Performance

### Opening (2:12 p.m.)

Forty neighbors sit hushed in folding chairs inside [Alder Street Neighborhood Hall](../02-company/place-hall.md), holding ivory half-sheet programs titled *Honoring Miriam Okafor (1948–2025)*. At the oak lectern, [Marta Solis](../02-company/persona-ceremony-lead.md) stands behind the live gooseneck microphone on the closing lines of her brief welcome, with the press photographer poised by the side window ahead of a hard 2:20 p.m. departure, a youth group booked for 2:30 p.m., and four paper-ribboned front-row chairs reserved for the Okafor family sitting empty six feet from the front wall. Two paces to Marta's left, [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md) stands beside [the Dedication Plaque](../02-company/piece-plaque.md), draped smooth beneath its weighted navy velvet cloth and braided brass draw-cord. Neither volunteer has seen the bare bronze face since casting.

### Event 1 — Discovery Cue

- **Public Stage View (seen by Marta and the hall):** Beside [the Dedication Plaque](../02-company/piece-plaque.md), [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md) reaches down and angles the weighted bottom corner of the navy velvet cloth an inch off the wall, glancing underneath for a second as if checking that no foundry packing tape or cord snag remains, before letting the velvet settle flat over the plaque again. From the lectern and the chairs, only that brief hand check is visible; the bronze face stays hidden.
- **Private Visual View (seen ONLY by [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md)):** Behind the lifted corner of the navy velvet, the 1.5-inch raised polished-brass capitals across the top header line catch the light: **`MARIAM OKAFOR`**—not **`MIRIAM OKAFOR`**.

---

### Beat 1

#### Turn 1 — [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md)

Theo's fingers stay pressed against the settled hem of the navy velvet for half a second longer than a routine check before sliding up to wrap around the braided brass draw-cord, pinning it flat against the wall rather than taking slack to pull. His shoulders lock rigid; his right thumb and forefinger pinch tight along the seam of his right jacket pocket. Closing half a pace into the narrow gap toward the left side of the oak lectern, he fixes his gaze squarely on Marta's profile, gives one tight, unmistakable shake of his head, and murmurs **off-mic** just loud enough to cross the step between them without carrying to the chairs:

> "Marta—hold the cue. Don't uncover it."

*(In the hushed hall behind the empty reserved front row, forty seated neighbors watch the lectern and the draped wall; by the side window, the weekly's photographer checks his watch and lifts his camera slightly.)*

#### Turn 2 — [Marta Solis](../02-company/persona-ceremony-lead.md)

Marta's gaze flicks for half a second to Theo's hand pinning the braided cord; she taps the top edge of her printed run-of-show card twice against the oak lectern rim—sending a faint double-thump through the wall speaker—then lifts a composed, warm smile out over the forty seated neighbors.

**Into the live microphone** *(even, unhurried cadence)*:

> "Forty households along Alder Street came together to place Miriam's tribute permanently on this wall, right beside the floor where she set out the Saturday bread crates for decades."

As the line finishes, she cups her right palm firmly over the head of the gooseneck microphone, takes a half-step to the left edge of the lectern with her run-of-show card tucked flat against her wrist, meets Theo's eyes squarely, and murmurs **off-mic** in a low, brisk voice:

> "Theo, the camera leaves at two-twenty—if there's tape on the corner, leave it. What's wrong?"

---

### Event 2 — Entrance Cue

At the rear of [Alder Street Neighborhood Hall](../02-company/place-hall.md), the double glass doors ease open. Miriam's widower Samuel Okafor, leaning lightly on his walking cane, and her daughter Tomi Okafor, carrying white chrysanthemums and a folded printed program, walk quietly down the center aisle as seated neighbors turn with warm nods, taking two of the reserved front-row chairs six feet directly in front of [the Dedication Plaque](../02-company/piece-plaque.md).

*(Seated six feet from the draped plaque and lectern, Samuel rests both hands atop his cane and looks up expectantly toward the front wall while Tomi settles the white chrysanthemums across her lap beside her printed program.)*

---

### Open Volunteer Exchange

#### Turn 3 — [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md)

Theo dips his chin in a brief, solemn nod toward Samuel and Tomi as they take their seats six feet away, his face drained of color and his left knuckles white around the braided brass draw-cord against the wall. With his right thumb and forefinger still pinching hard along the seam of his jacket pocket, he leans half an inch closer to the left edge of the lectern, keeping his eyes low and his lips barely moving so his breath-thin **off-mic whisper** won't carry the six feet to the front row:

> "It's her name, Marta—they cast M-A-R-I-A-M across the top. I approved the rush proof on my phone by myself. We can't drop this cloth in front of them."

*(Six feet away in the front row, Samuel Okafor rests both hands atop his walking cane and looks up patiently toward the lectern; beside him, Tomi Okafor's gaze shifts from Marta's palm cupped over the microphone to Theo's hand gripping the draw-cord against the wall. Across the silent room, forty neighbors sit with programs open on their laps, and by the side window the weekly's photographer checks his watch and lifts his camera toward the plaque.)*

#### Turn 4 — [Marta Solis](../02-company/persona-ceremony-lead.md)

Marta goes rigid for half a second, her left fingers whitening against the edge of her printed run-of-show card. Keeping her right palm pressed snug over the head of the gooseneck microphone and her lips barely moving, she breathes back a tight, barely audible **off-mic whisper** across the step toward Theo:

> "We recast it Monday—we're not halting this room in front of Samuel. Pull it on cue."

She lifts her palm cleanly off the microphone, squares her card with two quick taps of its top edge against the oak lectern rim—sending another faint double-thump through the wall speaker—and opens her shoulders toward the reserved front row, meeting Samuel and Tomi's eyes six feet away with steady, unhurried warmth.

**Into the live microphone** *(gentle, measured cadence)*:

> "Samuel, Tomi—welcome. Every Saturday morning in this hall still runs on the steadiness and quiet generosity Miriam Okafor built here with her own hands. On behalf of forty Alder Street households and every neighbor in this room, we are proud to dedicate the Miriam Okafor Community Pantry Wall."

Marta steps half a pace back from the live microphone, angles her run-of-show card toward Theo beside the draped wall, and extends an open left hand toward the braided brass draw-cord, holding a composed smile.

*(Six feet away in the front row, Samuel Okafor inclines his head warmly and shifts his grip higher on his walking cane to look up at the navy velvet cloth; beside him, Tomi lifts her eyes to Theo's hand on the braided cord. Across the hushed hall, forty neighbors look to the draped plaque, and by the side window the press photographer braces his elbows with his lens aimed squarely at the cloth and his finger on the shutter.)*

#### Turn 5 — [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md)

For a full second after Marta's hand cue, Theo does not pull. Instead, his left hand shifts an inch inward to grip both the braided brass draw-cord and the right border of the navy velvet together, holding the cloth shut against the wall. His right thumb and forefinger press hard along the seam of his right jacket pocket; his shoulders lock rigid, and he swallows visibly before turning his gaze squarely down to Samuel and Tomi six feet away.

**Aloud to Samuel, Tomi, and the hushed hall** *(voice tight and slightly unsteady, carrying clearly from beside the lectern)*:

> "Mr. Okafor, Tomi—I'm so sorry, I have to stop before I pull this cord. I checked under the corner a minute ago, and her first name was cast with an *A* instead of an *I*—it reads *Mariam*. Eleven days ago I approved the foundry's rush proof by myself without a second check, so this is nobody's mistake in this hall but mine, and I'm ordering the corrected bronze myself first thing Monday—I just couldn't drop this cloth in front of you and the camera without telling you first."

*(A quiet intake of breath moves across the forty folding chairs as several neighbors glance down at the printed programs on their laps and back up to the draped wall. Six feet away in the front row, Tomi Okafor's hand moves across to rest lightly on Samuel's coat sleeve while Samuel sits very still atop his cane, looking from Theo's hand holding the navy velvet shut over to Marta beside the live oak lectern. By the side window, the press photographer lowers the camera half an inch from his eye, waiting.)*

#### Turn 6 — [Marta Solis](../02-company/persona-ceremony-lead.md)

Marta's extended left hand hangs still for a beat, then lowers slowly to her side as the composed smile fades from her face. She places her printed run-of-show card silently flat on the oak lectern, gives Theo a brief, steady nod, and turns her shoulders squarely toward Samuel and Tomi six feet away.

**Into the live microphone** *(quiet, level, unhurried)*:

> "Samuel, Tomi—as Events Chair, my eyes should have been on that proof alongside Theo's, and the Civic Association shares this mistake with him. The corrected bronze with Miriam's name spelled properly will be ordered first thing Monday."

She glances once toward the photographer by the side window, then looks straight back down to Samuel and Tomi, resting both hands lightly on the front edge of the oak lectern.

**Into the live microphone** *(gentle, clear)*:

> "We won't uncover the wrong spelling for a camera unless the two of you want to see the bronze today. With your permission, we'll keep the velvet closed until her true name is on this wall, read Miriam's dedication aloud from our programs together right now, and take today's photograph right here at the front row with you, her flowers, and the neighbors who loved her—whatever the two of you prefer."

---

### Immediate Consequence

Across [Alder Street Neighborhood Hall](../02-company/place-hall.md), the weighted navy velvet cloth stays flat and un-pulled over [the Dedication Plaque](../02-company/piece-plaque.md), sealing **`MARIAM OKAFOR`** out of sight behind [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md)'s slackening grip on the braided brass draw-cord. Six feet from the wall, Tomi Okafor's jaw tightens as she looks down at *Honoring Miriam Okafor (1948–2025)* on her folded ivory program and then up at the dark, still-covered rectangle above Theo's shoulder. Beside her, Samuel Okafor sits silent for a long count, both hands folded over the handle of his cane, before giving one slow, measured nod toward the front wall.

"Leave the cloth on it today, Theo," Samuel says quietly into the hush, clear enough for the front rows to hear. "Wait until it carries her name."

Tomi rises halfway from her chair—not stepping toward the plaque, but laying the white chrysanthemums across the empty reserved chair directly beneath the veiled bronze—then sits back beside her father and opens her printed program to the dedication text. Across the forty folding chairs, neighbors lift their half-sheet programs with a low, sober rustle, trading quiet glances toward Theo's pale profile beside the un-pulled cord and [Marta Solis](../02-company/persona-ceremony-lead.md)'s still run-of-show card on the lectern. By the side window, ahead of his 2:20 p.m. departure, the weekly's photographer steps out of his unveiling angle without a shot of the bronze and frames Samuel, Tomi, and the white chrysanthemums in the front row while the centerpiece of [the Unveiling Ceremony](../02-company/process-unveiling.md) hangs dark and unopened on the wall behind them.

**Curtain.**

# Director’s Note

### 1. Epistemic Timeline, Shifting Costs, and Stage Trajectory

- **Opening (2:12 p.m.):** Neither volunteer knew [the Dedication Plaque](../02-company/piece-plaque.md) carried a typo. [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md) privately carried his solo 4:45 p.m. phone proof approval from eleven days prior and his 1:45 p.m. kraft-paper mounting shortcut; [Marta Solis](../02-company/persona-ceremony-lead.md) privately carried her morning phone promise to Samuel Okafor that [the Unveiling Ceremony](../02-company/process-unveiling.md) would remain brief, steady, and free of spectacle.
- **Event 1 (`First` in [plot.md](../02-company/plot.md) — Discovery Cue) and Beat 1 (Turns 1–2):** Because the **Discovery Cue** landed *before* the Okafor family entered, Theo saw **`MARIAM OKAFOR`** while the four front-row chairs six feet away were still empty. That timing made a low off-mic warning across the two-pace gap briefly affordable without reaching the family's ears, though forty seated neighbors and a live gooseneck microphone still constrained him to a half-step lean, a pinned draw-cord, and a short murmur (*"Marta—hold the cue. Don't uncover it"*). Marta, holding only the **Public Stage View**, did not know about the misspelling; filtering Theo's hesitation through her Shadow as a minor snag or stage fright ahead of the 2:20 p.m. press cutoff, she bridged on-mic for a beat and cupped the microphone head to ask off-mic *"What's wrong?"*—consuming their sole family-free exchange on diagnosis rather than joint planning.
- **Event 2 (`After one beat` in [plot.md](../02-company/plot.md) — Entrance Cue) and Open Exchange (Turns 3–6):** Samuel and Tomi Okafor walking down the center aisle into the front row six feet from [the Dedication Plaque](../02-company/piece-plaque.md) immediately raised the acoustic and emotional stakes: any whisper above a tight breath would now reach them, and any ceremony halt would unfold directly in front of Miriam's widower and daughter.
  - **Turn 3:** Theo used the close lectern-edge proximity already established on Turns 1–2 to breathe the casting error (`M-A-R-I-A-M`) and his solo rush proof approval to Marta, equalizing knowledge between the volunteers while the room remained unaware.
  - **Turn 4:** Now knowing the truth, Marta weighed a public halt in front of Samuel against a temporary unveil + Monday recast, ordered Theo off-mic (*"Pull it on cue"*), welcomed Samuel and Tomi into the live microphone, and extended her hand to cue the cord pull. That public hand-off made quiet delay impossible for Theo: pulling the cord would expose **`MARIAM OKAFOR`** to Samuel, Tomi, forty donors, and the press lens; refusing would break Marta's live ceremony in public view.
  - **Turn 5:** Theo gripped both the draw-cord and velvet border shut against the wall and confessed aloud to Samuel, Tomi, and the hall—naming the *Mariam* casting error, taking sole personal responsibility for bypassing the second proof check, and refusing to drop the cloth unannounced.
  - **Turn 6 & Immediate Consequence:** With the halt public and irreversible, Marta abandoned her run-of-show momentum, stepped beside Theo on the live microphone to share institutional responsibility as Events Chair, and offered Samuel and Tomi the choice of keeping the velvet closed while reading from the printed programs and reframing the 2:20 p.m. photograph. Samuel accepted keeping the plaque covered until it bore Miriam's right name, while Tomi's placement of the flowers on the empty chair and the unopened velvet behind the photographer's reframed shot preserved the tangible public cost of the failed unveiling.

### 2. Directorial Mediation and Context Isolation

- **Strict epistemic separation:** [Marta Solis](../02-company/persona-ceremony-lead.md) and [Theo Park](../02-company/persona-plaque-lead.md) ran in isolated performer contexts reading only their own cast files, with zero mention of **`MARIAM OKAFOR`** supplied to Theo until the **Discovery Cue** fired and only the **Public Stage View** supplied to Marta until Theo whispered the truth onstage in Turn 3. Internal monologue was disallowed across every turn.
- **Turn hand-offs and room pressure:** Following [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md), Turn 1 went to Theo after Event 1 (**Discovery Cue**). After Event 2 (**Entrance Cue**), Turn 3 went to Theo because Marta had ended Turn 2 cupped over the microphone asking him *"What's wrong?"* at the exact moment Samuel and Tomi took their seats six feet away. Between turns, mediation was restricted to observable room geometry (the photographer checking his watch and raising/lowering his viewfinder, forty neighbors watching, Tomi's gaze tracking Marta's covered mic and Theo's hand on the cord) without steering either volunteer's tactical or ethical decision.

### 3. Staging Choices vs. Strains in the Authored Material

- **Staging choices made by the Director (within the fixed Play):**
  1. *Assigning Turn 3 to Theo after the Entrance Cue:* [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md) directs Turn 3 after an Entrance Cue to *"whichever volunteer is most directly put on the spot."* While an arriving family naturally pulls the emcee's welcome, Marta's direct off-mic question closing Turn 2 put immediate conversational pressure on Theo first, allowing Turn 3 (Theo's whispered confession) and Turn 4 (Marta's public welcome + unveil cue) to interlock cleanly.
  2. *Running Turn 6 before calling Immediate Consequence:* Although Theo's Turn 5 public confession settled that he was holding the cloth shut, [Marta Solis](../02-company/persona-ceremony-lead.md) was still standing mid-cue beside a live microphone as Ceremony Lead; giving Marta Turn 6 (`6` turns total, within the `4–6` turn envelope) allowed both volunteers to complete their onstage reckoning before Samuel and Tomi answered.
- **Strains in the authored material (returned as observations; Play kept fixed):**
  1. *Optics of the Discovery Cue ([play.md](../02-company/play.md) and [piece-plaque.md](../02-company/piece-plaque.md)):* The fixed **Discovery Cue** has Theo angle the *weighted bottom corner* of an 18-by-24-inch eye-height plaque *"an inch off the wall, glancing underneath for a second"* to spot 1.5-inch capitals across the *top header line*. Because the cue is delivered verbatim as a given stage perception, performers accepted it cleanly, but physically sighting top-border lettering nearly eighteen inches up behind flush, weighted velvet through a one-inch bottom gap is optically tight.
  2. *Acoustic margin between the two-pace gap and six-foot front row ([place-hall.md](../02-company/place-hall.md) and [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md)):* [place-hall.md](../02-company/place-hall.md) separates the lectern and plaque by two paces while [promptbook.md](../02-company/promptbook.md) specifies that once Samuel and Tomi sit six feet away, *"anything louder than a tight breath reaches them as well."* Delivering multi-sentence information in a *"tight breath"* across two full paces would strain audibility unless the volunteers have already closed the physical gap; in this cue order (`Discovery` then `Entrance`), Turns 1–2 naturally drew both volunteers half a step into the gap before the family sat, making Turn 3's unheard breath-whisper physically plausible onstage.
