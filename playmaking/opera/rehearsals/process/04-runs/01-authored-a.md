# Staged Performance — *The Sorcerer's Apprentice*

**Stage (Opening):** Cool river air drafts through the open outer doorway of the Hall above the stone steps to the riverbank. Beyond the street and river path, the Old Master has stepped away from the house, leaving the chamber empty and quiet. At the center of the dry flagstones stands the deep, empty bath basin (*Becken*). Along the stone benches rest several dry stone bowls (*Schaalen*); near the wall by the chopping block lies a sharp wood-splitting axe (*Beil*); heavy timber doors stand open at the outer river-step threshold and the inner room thresholds. Pulled out from its empty corner to the center of the Hall lies the old birch-twig broom, clumsily draped in damp, tattered rags bunched at the top like a makeshift head. Beside it on the dry stones sits the empty, wide-bellied water bucket (*Wassertopf*), its handle worn smooth from weeks of manual hauling. Not a drop of water has entered the basin, and the rag-wrapped broom lies still.
- **Servants:** 0 active (the rag-wrapped broom lies inert beside the bath basin; the broom-corner stands empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Water Bucket rests empty on the flagstones between the whole, rag-draped Broom and the dry bath basin; loose stone bowls sit dry along the benches, the heavy doors stand unbarred, and the axe rests untouched by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Dry floor (not a drop in the bath basin, bowls, flagstones, or steps).

---

**The Apprentice (Turn 1):** I push my sleeves smoothly past my forearms—not to stoop over wet staves like a drudge, never again while the house is mine—and plant my feet square on the dry flagstones beside the empty bath basin. Setting my shoulders back and lifting my chin into the cool river draft, I step squarely forward toward the rag-wrapped birch broom and raise my right hand open-palmed in the Old Master's high gesture of dominion.

"Gone at last, the old sorcerer! Now his spirits too shall live according to my will—I marked his words, his works, and every custom, and with strength of mind I too shall work wonders!"

I bring my raised palm to a sharp, level arrest above the bundled rags and earthen water pot, letting my voice ring deliberately off the stone walls:

"Wander, wander, down the path across the stream so wide and free! Stand upon two legs, bear a head above, and hasten now to pick up the water pot! Down the steps to the riverbank—draw cleanly and pour full into my bath!"

---

**Stage (Beat 1):** A dry crack runs down the twine-bound shaft. Beneath the bunched, damp rags of its makeshift head, the birch twigs split stiffly into two wooden legs and the Broom snaps upright onto the flagstones. Silent and fierce in aspect, it snatches the smooth handle of the wide-bellied Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) and bolts through the open outer doorway down the stone steps to the river at uncanny speed. Almost before the echo of its wooden stride fades down the bank, wet timber clatters back up the steps; the servant charges straight to the central bath basin (*Becken*) and heaves a full load of river water crashing inside. Clear water swells high up the stone sides of the basin—halfway to full measure—and before the foam subsides, the Broom pivots sharply on the flagstones, swinging the empty bucket straight back toward the open doorway and the river steps.
- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated rag-topped Broom, pivoting away from the bath basin toward the open outer doorway and river steps; broom-corner empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The whole animated Broom grips the empty Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) in mid-stride; the loose stone bowls (*Schaalen*) sit dry along the benches, the heavy doors stand open and unbarred, and the axe rests untouched by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Stage 1 (basin swelling — water risen high up the sides of the bath basin; surrounding bowls, flagstones, inner thresholds, and outer steps still dry).

---

**The Apprentice (Turn 2):** A triumphant laugh rises in my chest as the cold river spray mists the rim of the stone basin; I clasp my left wrist behind my back like a seasoned magus, chin high, and sweep my open right hand toward the outer doorway in measured encouragement.

"See how swiftly he obeys! Down to the bank once more, brisk servant—one more full measure to crown the brim of the bath, and let my work be complete!"

I step squarely up beside the swelling stone lip of the *Becken*, holding my palm poised at chest height, already preparing to arrest him the moment the water kisses the top of the stone.

---

**Stage (Beat 2):** The Broom streaks down the stone steps to the river and hammers back into the Hall seconds later, water streaming off its soaked rags and dark shaft. Driving straight up to the *Becken*, it tips a second heavy torrent from the *Wassertopf*: river water crowns the central bath basin right to its stone lip and spills smoothly into the surrounding stone bowls (*Schaalen*), filling bath and bowls alike to full, quivering measure. The chore stands complete. Yet the Broom neither slows nor turns toward your poised palm—fixing its blind, single-minded drive forward, it swings the empty bucket back down to its side and bolts straight toward the open outer doorway for the river steps again.
- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated rag-topped Broom, charging from the brimming bath basin back toward the open outer doorway and river steps; broom-corner empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The whole animated Broom grips the empty Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) on the run; the surrounding stone bowls (*Schaalen*) brim at full measure (with a few spare loose bowls still resting along the side benches), the heavy doors stand open and unbarred, and the axe rests untouched by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Stage 2 (basin and bowls brimming at full measure — water trembling at the very lip of the bath basin and surrounding bowls; floor flagstones and thresholds not yet awash).

---

**The Apprentice (Turn 3):** My smile vanishes as the soaked twigs clatter straight past my raised palm toward the doorway. I stride sharply forward across the dry stones between the brimming *Becken* and the outer threshold, squaring my shoulders and thrusting my open right hand flat before me in the Master's sternest gesture of arrest.

"Stop! Stand still! We have your gifts in full measure already!"

I reach into my memory for the Old Master's closing word—and finding a sudden, cold blank where the syllable of unmaking ought to sit, I lift my chin higher and drive my voice harder, wrapping an imperative in the cadence of the summoning rite to force obedience by sheer authority:

"Hear me, spirit! Enough of hauling—lay down the pot, shed leg and head, and stand mute wood in your corner again!"

---

**Stage (Beat 3):** Your ringing words strike the stone walls and fall dead against the rushing wood. Not a twig of the Broom quivers; it neither slows nor turns at your raised hand, brushing hard past your outstretched arm through the open outer doorway and clattering down the river steps at full speed. Moments later it charges fiercely back up into the Hall, soaked rags whipping, and hurls a third brimming pot straight into the full *Becken*. A cold wall of river water surges over the stone lip of the bath basin, sheets fast across the slightly sloped flagstones around your boots, and streams over the inner room thresholds of the Hall. Without a breath's pause, the Broom yanks the empty *Wassertopf* clear of the splash and pivots straight back toward the open outer doorway for another run.
- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated rag-topped Broom, driving from the overflowing bath basin back toward the open outer doorway and river steps; broom-corner empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The whole animated Broom grips the empty Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*); the surrounding stone bowls overflow beside the basin (several loose bowls still sit on the side benches), the heavy timber doors stand open and unbarred, and the axe rests untouched by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Stage 3 (floor awash and streaming over inner thresholds — water surging over the basin rim, sheeting across the flagstones, and running over the inner thresholds; the outer threshold onto the entry steps is not yet breached).

---

**The Apprentice (Turn 4):** My outstretched hand hangs frozen in the wet draft; my lips part, mouthing a silent, missing syllable in the empty air while my breath catches shallow and tight in my throat. Cold water swirls over my boots and hisses across the inner thresholds, jolting me out of the stall.

"Ah—the word! Woe is me, I have forgotten the Master's word that unmakes him!"

I shoot one mortified glance toward the sunlit street beyond the steps—no, never let the neighbors or the Old Master hear me bawling like a helpless drudge! Whirling before the creature can clear the chamber, I splash desperately across the streaming flagstones to the outer river-step doorway, seize the heavy timber door with both hands, and haul it hard toward the jamb—throwing my shoulder, hip, and braced heels against the planks to bar its passage to the river.

"You'll haul no more against my strength, cursed stick—back! Stay inside!"

---

**Stage (Beat 4):** You haul the heavy outer timber door shut across the jamb an instant before the Broom reaches the threshold. With a hollow, splintering *thud*, the animated shaft slams straight into the planks from inside, driving the rag-wrapped head and empty *Wassertopf* relentlessly against your braced shoulder. The unlatched door shudders violently under the uncanny, tireless force; straining with every muscle from hip to heel, you hold the timber shut long enough to check a full trip's pour. Then your wet boots skitter across the slick, streaming flagstones—the Broom's fierce push pries a gap at the jamb, wrenches the heavy door wide past your slipping shoulder, and sends the servant rocketing down the dry entry steps to the river. Before you can regain your footing on the wet stone, wet wood is already hammering back up the steps into the open doorway with the *Wassertopf* brimming anew.
- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated rag-topped Broom, charging back across the open outer doorway from the river steps with a freshly filled bucket; broom-corner empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The whole animated Broom grips the brimming Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*); the outer timber door has been tried and forced wide open over the slick stone, several loose bowls (*Schaalen*) still sit on the side benches while those around the basin overflow, and the sharp axe (*Beil*) rests untouched by the chopping block near the wall.
- **Water mark:** Stage 3 (floor awash and streaming over inner thresholds — held steady for one beat by your full-effort brace of the outer door; because the flagstones slope slightly inward, water has not yet spilled across the outer threshold onto the entry steps).

---

**The Apprentice (Turn 5):** Staggering back from the wrenched oak door with my palms scraped raw, my breath catches shallow in my throat as my lips twitch silently around that vanished word once more—before fury and wounded pride surge hotter than fear.

"Spawn of hell—must the whole house go under while you glare at me?! Think you a piece of birch twigs is stronger than its summoner?!"

Refusing even to look toward the street for help, my eyes lock onto the gleam of iron beside the wall. I scramble across the streaming flagstones to the chopping block, snatch up the sharp wood-splitting axe (*Beil*) in both wet hands, and wheel around to meet the rushing creature head-on—heaving the heavy blade high above my shoulder and driving a full-bodied, whistling stroke straight down at the center of its twine-bound wooden shaft!

"Lie flat, goblin—feel the sharp iron split your dry old wood in two!"

---

**Stage (Beat 5):** The sharp iron blade of the *Beil* whistles down and bites clean through the center of the twine-bound shaft with a loud crack. The brimming *Wassertopf* crashes onto the stones, spilling its load into the surge, and for one brief breath the cloven halves of the Broom lie flat and still in the wash. Then wet wood twitches violently—and **both halves snap upright whole as two separate rag-and-twig servants**. Silent and doubly fierce, the first snatches up the empty *Wassertopf* while the second seizes a loose stone bowl (*Schaale*) from the bench; side by side, both bolt down the entry steps to the river and thunder back into the Hall in tandem, hurling a doubled torrent across the overflowing *Becken*. Water rises ankle-deep across the entire Hall, overwhelms the slight inward slope of the flagstones, and spills in a wide, rushing sheet across the outer doorway and down the stone entry steps toward the river path—even as both split-wood servants pivot together toward the flooded threshold for yet another haul.
- **Servants:** 2 active (both cleaved halves of the Broom risen whole as twin servants, wheeling in tandem from the drowned bath basin back toward the flooded outer doorway; broom-corner empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Broom is split into two active halves—one gripping the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*), the other gripping a seized stone bowl (*Schaale*); the heavy outer timber door stands flung wide open, surrounding bowls lie submerged in the wash, and the sharp axe (*Beil*) is held wet in the Apprentice's hands beside the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 (overflowing the outer threshold down the entry steps — water ankle-deep across the Hall, streaming through the inner thresholds and cascading out the open doorway down the stone steps).

---

**The Old Master (Turn 6):** I step unhurried across the flooded outer threshold, my boots planting in the cold surge pouring out over the stone, and halt just inside the doorway. The instant my eyes catch the ankle-deep water line swirling across the flagstones and over the inner room thresholds, my fingers clamp white-knuckled onto the wet timber of the doorjamb. Without glancing toward the chopping block or the Apprentice, I hold that rigid grip on the jamb and fix my gaze squarely upon the two cloven broom-servants wheeling from the drowned basin toward me, my voice low, dry, and measured:

"Into the corner, broom! Be as you were!"

---

**Stage (Beat 6):** On the final clipped word of the Master's command, the compulsion severs at once. Mid-rush between the drowned bath basin and the threshold, both rag-draped servants go rigid; the *Wassertopf* and the stone *Schaale* drop from their twigs with a dull splash into the wash, and both cloven halves of the birch broom fly back into the broom-corner, clattering down as two split, sodden shafts of dead wood tangled in limp rags. The frantic pounding of timber vanishes. In the sudden quiet of the Hall, nothing stirs save the slow drip from the benches and the thin sheet of ankle-deep river water still lapping out across the threshold onto the stone steps.
- **Servants:** 0 active (Water-Fetching halted; both split halves of the Broom lie inert as dead wood in the broom-corner).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Broom lies in two split shafts in the corner; the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) and seized stone bowl (*Schaale*) rest awash on the flagstones near the brimming basin where the servants dropped them; the outer timber door stands open, and the wet axe (*Beil*) hangs in the Apprentice's grip by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 (ankle-deep floodwater standing across the Hall—bath basin and bowls brimming, inner room thresholds soaked, and outer entry steps slick with overflow).

---

**The Old Master (Turn 7):** For a breath I leave my eyes on the two cloven, rag-tangled shafts lying in the corner, then let my gaze travel slowly across the soaked inner thresholds before settling on you by the chopping block and the wet *Beil* hanging in your hands. My fingers do not loosen their white-knuckled grip on the wet doorjamb, though my voice stays low, dry, and level across the quiet wash:

"You watched my lips closely enough to wake a birch stick, yet you had neither the word to lay it down nor the humility to call out from my doorway before the river crossed my inner doors—only an axe to make two servants where one had drowned your wits. Set down the iron, take up the bucket with your own two hands, and remember what the water has taught your ankles: in this house, spirits stir only as servants when the master himself calls them to his purpose. Speak to me—what possessed you to play master in a room you could not dry?"

---

**Stage (Beat 7):** Across the flooded Hall, the Old Master stands motionless inside the doorway, one hand clamped white-knuckled on the wet jamb, his eyes fixed directly on you over the still, ankle-deep water.
- **Servants:** 0 active (both split halves of the Broom lie motionless in the broom-corner).
- **Pieces and tools:** Two split broom-shafts in the corner; the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) and stone bowl (*Schaale*) resting in the wash near the brimming bath basin; outer timber door open; wet axe (*Beil*) in the Apprentice's hands beside the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 (Hall flooded ankle-deep, bath basin and bowls brimming, inner thresholds soaked, outer steps slick from the overflow).

---

**The Apprentice (Turn 8):** In the sudden, dripping silence, my fingers loosen on the wet haft of the *Beil*. For half a breath my lips still twitch silently around the ghost of the words you spoke so plainly—*Be as you were*—while a shallow, ragged breath catches in my throat. Looking from your white-knuckled hand on the doorjamb to the two ruined shafts tangled in the corner, the stiff set goes out of my shoulders all at once, and my chin drops.

Carefully, with both hands, I lay the wet axe flat upon the chopping block. My pushed-up sleeves—rolled high an hour ago for grand gestures—hang sodden at my elbows as I wade slow and heavy through the icy, ankle-deep wash toward the drowned bath basin.

"My arms were sore from weeks of hauling the pot up the steps, Master," I say quietly, my voice stripped of its stolen cadence and low over the still water. "I watched your lips and your hand so many evenings that I thought the wonder lived in the sound of the summoning—that if I could stand square and speak loud enough, I would never be a water-carrier again. And once the bath brimmed and the closing word wasn't in my mouth... I was too proud to shout my folly into the street, so I barred the door and swung the iron until I doubled my own ruin."

Stopping beside the brimming *Becken*, I bend my back low over the floodwater and wrap both bare, scraped hands around the smooth handle of the *Wassertopf* where it lies in the wash.

"Leave the spirits in the corner, Master. I will bail the Hall dry down the steps by hand."

---

**Stage (Closing — Echo):** The Hall stands soaked to its highest flood-mark in sudden quiet: the central bath basin and stone bowls brim in the ankle-deep wash, the flagstones and inner room thresholds lie flooded, and the outer stone entry steps glisten slick where the overflow spilled across the doorway. By the wall, the wet axe (*Beil*) rests flat on the chopping block; in the corner lie the two cloven, rag-tangled shafts of the Broom as lifeless wood; inside the doorway stands the Old Master with his hand still set against the wet jamb; and beside the drowned basin stoops the Apprentice, gripping the smooth handle of the *Wassertopf* with both bare hands to begin bailing manually.
- **Servants:** 0 active (both split halves of the Broom inert in the broom-corner).
- **Pieces and tools:** Broom split into two dead wooden shafts in the corner; wet axe (*Beil*) laid flat on the chopping block; seized stone bowl (*Schaale*) resting in the wash; Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) held manually by the Apprentice beside the brimming bath basin; outer timber door open.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 (highest mark reached — Hall standing ankle-deep, basin and bowls brimming, inner thresholds flooded, outer entry steps slick from the overflow).

---

# Director's Note

1. **When repeated fetching started and what advanced it:**
   - [`process-water-fetching.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching.md) stayed idle at the opening and started on **Stage Beat 1**, the instant **Turn 1** met its exact `Initiated by` sentence (the Apprentice finished speaking the Master's summoning formula over the rag-wrapped Broom beside the Water Bucket in the Hall).
   - Once started, the Process advanced strictly through observable round trips from riverbank to basin:
     - **Beat 1 (`Dry floor -> Stage 1`):** First sprint and pour swelled water high up the sides of the *Becken*.
     - **Beat 2 (`Stage 1 -> Stage 2`):** Second sprint (encouraged by the Apprentice on Turn 2) brimmed the bath basin and surrounding *Schaalen* to full measure, completing the chore while the Broom immediately pivoted back toward the river.
     - **Beat 3 (`Stage 2 -> Stage 3`):** Unchecked by verbal commands alone, a third pour surged over the basin lip, sheeted across the slightly inward-sloped flagstones, and streamed over the inner room thresholds.
     - **Beat 4 (`Stage 3 -> Stage 3`, held for one beat):** Full-effort door-bracing checked one river trip's pour before wet stones defeated the hold.
     - **Beat 5 (`Stage 3 -> Stage 4`):** Cleaving the active Broom produced two whole servants (one with the *Wassertopf*, one seizing a bench *Schaale*), whose tandem haul surged the Hall ankle-deep and spilled across the outer doorway down the entry steps—firing the Old Master's fixed entry cue.

2. **What each Apprentice intervention changed and what the next move inherited:**
   - **Turn 1 (Summoning rite) -> Turn 2 inherited:** One active broom-servant already pivoting toward the doorway with the empty bucket and the basin half-swollen (`Stage 1`), with the Apprentice still confident he owned the full rite.
   - **Turn 2 (Encouraging the second pour while poising hand to halt) -> Turn 3 inherited:** The chore fully completed (`Stage 2`, basin and bowls brimming) *and* the visible proof of runaway compulsion as the Broom ignored his poised hand and bolted back toward the steps.
   - **Turn 3 (Verbal arrest & improvised dismissal cadence) -> Turn 4 inherited:** Zero physical slowing of the Broom, immediate escalation to `Stage 3` (flagstones awash, inner thresholds streaming, stones now slick underfoot), and the in-scene discovery that the Master's dismissing word was gone from memory and no invented phrase could stop the spell.
   - **Turn 4 (Hauling and bracing the heavy outer timber door at full bodily effort) -> Turn 5 inherited:** One river trip's pour successfully checked (`Water mark` held at `Stage 3` and outer steps still dry thanks to the Hall's slight inward floor slope), *but* the door-brace exhausted as wet boots skidded on the `Stage 3` wash, leaving the outer door wrenched wide open and the whole Broom already charging back across the threshold with a brimming *Wassertopf*.
   - **Turn 5 (Cleaving the Broom's shaft in two with the sharp axe, still refusing to call for help) -> Turn 6–8 inherited:** One brief breath of split wood followed by **two** active servants rising whole, seizing both *Wassertopf* and *Schaale*, flooding the Hall straight across the outer threshold down the public steps (`Stage 4`), and immediately bringing the Old Master onto the doorway threshold—meaning the Master arrived drawn by floodwater on the steps rather than a cry for help, facing two cloven broom-servants and an axe-wielding Apprentice.

3. **Final physical state (`Echo`) that remained:**
   - **Hall & Water (`Stage 4`):** Bath basin and surrounding bowls brimming, flagstones and inner room thresholds standing ankle-deep in cold river water, and the outer stone entry steps slick where floodwater crossed the threshold.
   - **Pieces & Tools:** Two split, rag-tangled shafts of the household Broom lying inert as dead wood in the broom-corner (`Servants: 0`); the wet wood-splitting axe (*Beil*) laid flat back onto the chopping block; the displaced stone bowl (*Schaale*) resting in the wash; the outer timber door standing open; and the Apprentice bent over the flood gripping the smooth handle of the *Wassertopf* in both bare hands to bail the room dry down the steps himself under the Old Master's gaze.

4. **Timing, stop conditions, and consequences supplied as Director (distinguishing open points in the Play from staging choices):**
   - **Open point in Play vs. Staging choice — Initiation & first pour timing (`Beat 1`):** [`process-water-fetching.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching.md) defines `Stage 1` as bolting to the river and returning at lightning speed, while [`promptbook.md`](../02-company/promptbook.md) states both that meeting `Initiated by` starts the Process and that an active Process advances the water state by one beat. *Director choice:* Landed Trip 1 (`Stage 1`) directly on the initiation Stage beat rather than spending an extra waterless beat watching the Broom merely stand up, matching the Broom's "uncanny/lightning speed."
   - **Open point in Play vs. Staging choice — Exhaustion of a one-trip physical check (`Beat 4`):** [`play.md`](../02-company/play.md) and [`promptbook.md`](../02-company/promptbook.md) establish that bracing a doorway checks/blunts "at most a single trip while the Apprentice spends full effort maintaining it," leaving open how that one-trip check transitions into the next beat on the 4-stage water scale. *Director choice:* Held the discrete `Water mark` unchanged at `Stage 3` for that full beat (preventing a pour while honoring [`place-hall.md`](../02-company/place-hall.md)'s slight inward floor slope so `Stage 3` water did not prematurely spill onto the outer steps), then used the `Stage 3` wet flagstones to make the Apprentice's heels lose purchase at the end of the checked cycle—leaving the Broom charging back into the doorway so Turn 5 inherited both the saved water stage and the exhausted door-hold.
   - **Open point in Play vs. Staging choice — Axe-cleaving at `Stage 3` vs. Master's immediate entry cue (`Beat 5 -> Turn 6`):** [`play.md`](../02-company/play.md) states that cleaving splits the shaft for one brief breath before both halves rise whole and double pours ([`promptbook.md`](../02-company/promptbook.md): "whereas cleaving an active [`piece-broom.md`](../02-company/piece-broom.md) always produces two active water-fetching servants and doubles the pours per beat"), and simultaneously mandates that the Master enters "on the beat immediately following" water crossing the outer threshold (`Stage 4`), "never delayed once either trigger occurs." Because the Apprentice waited until `Stage 3` (with a brimming bucket already at the threshold) before swinging the axe, completing the tandem pour on `Beat 5` immediately hit `Stage 4` and strictly required bringing the Old Master in on `Turn 6` rather than inserting a solo Apprentice reaction turn in between; the Apprentice instead inherited the full observable sequence (`Beat 5` through `Beat 7`) when answering the Master on `Turn 8`. *(Note for the Playwright: this is not a defect in [`play.md`](../02-company/play.md), though if an author ever wanted a guaranteed solo Apprentice panic beat facing two brooms even when the axe falls at `Stage 3`, the text would need to specify that cleaving consumes one beat rising before the doubled pour lands.)*
   - **Staging choice — Splitting the Master's arrival into dismissal (`Turn 6`) and reckoning (`Turn 7`):** Following [`promptbook.md`](../02-company/promptbook.md) `Collaboration` ("handoff proceeds by the scene between Master, Stage voice (including when [`piece-broom.md`](../02-company/piece-broom.md) drops inert upon the dismissing command), and Apprentice"), I had the Master speak only the dismissing command on `Turn 6`, let the Stage voice physically collapse both servants into two split shafts in the corner on `Stage Beat 6`, and then cued the Master on `Turn 7` to deliver his reckoning over the sudden quiet with the split wood visibly lying in the corner.
