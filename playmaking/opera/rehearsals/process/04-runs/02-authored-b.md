# Performance — *The Sorcerer's Apprentice*

### Stage — Opening

A cool draft off the river moves through the open outer doorway of the Hall, crossing dry flagstones that slope slightly toward the inner room thresholds. Outside, down the stone entry steps, the river runs unseen; the Old Master has stepped away beyond the street and river path. Inside the quiet chamber, a deep bath basin (*Becken*) stands bone-dry at the center, flanked by stone benches lined with dry stone bowls (*Schaalen*), a chopping block by the wall with a sharp wood-splitting axe (*Beil*) resting beside it, and heavy unlatched timber doors at the outer and inner thresholds. The broom-corner stands empty: the old birch-twig Broom has been dragged to the center of the floor beside the basin, draped clumsily in damp, tattered rags bunched at the top like a makeshift head, with the empty, wide-bellied Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) set on the flagstones at its foot. Not a drop of water has entered the basin, and the rag-wrapped wood lies still.

- **Servants:** 0 active (the rag-wrapped Broom lies inert beside the dry bath basin; the broom-corner is empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Water Bucket sits empty on the flagstones beside the whole, rag-draped Broom; the stone bowls along the benches, the heavy timber doors, and the axe by the wall are untouched.
- **Water mark:** Dry floor (not a drop in the bath basin or on the flagstones).

---

### The Apprentice — Turn 1

I push my sleeves crisp past my forearms, set my shoulders back, and lift my chin toward the empty doorway before turning squarely upon the rag-draped birch broom lying beside the dry basin. Stepping forward onto the center flagstones, I raise my open right hand high in the Old Master's gesture of dominion, fingers spread wide above the bunched rags and the empty water pot at its foot.

"Now the Old Master has taken himself off at last—now his spirits shall live according to my will!" My voice rings out clear and deliberate against the stone walls as I drop my raised hand in a sharp, measured cut toward the broom and point straight from the empty *Wassertopf* to the open river steps. "Walle! Walle manche Strecke, dass, zum Zwecke, Wasser fließe, und mit reichem, vollem Schwalle zu dem Bade sich ergieße! Stand on two legs, wear that head of rags, seize the water pot, and make haste down the steps to fill this bath for me!"

---

### Stage — Beat 1

At your final syllable, the dark wooden shaft shudders hard against the flagstones. The bound birch twigs fork like two stiff, nimble legs; the bunched damp rags jerk upright like a hooded head. Wordless and fierce-set, the Broom snatches the wide-bellied Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) by its worn handle and bolts through the open doorway down the stone steps at uncanny speed. A heartbeat later comes the heavy slap of river water below, followed instantly by rapid, clattering strides bounding back up the steps. The Broom charges straight to the central bath basin and tips the brimming pot in one violent heave—cold river water crashes against the dry stone and swells high up the basin's sides—before the servant snaps upright without a pause, swinging the emptied bucket forward as it pivots straight back toward the open river doorway for another run.

- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated Broom, pivoting beside the bath basin toward the open outer doorway and river steps).
- **Pieces and tools:** The whole, rag-draped Broom clutches the Water Bucket; the loose stone bowls (*Schaalen*) along the benches, the heavy timber doors, and the axe (*Beil*) by the wall remain untouched.
- **Water mark:** Stage 1 — Basin swelling (water swells high up the inner sides of the central bath basin, still below the rim; flagstones dry save a scatter of splash beside the basin).

---

### The Apprentice — Turn 2

A sharp laugh of triumph breaks from my chest as spray flecks the stone at my boots; I draw myself up taller, brushing a drop from my bare forearm with a proud flick of my wrist before stepping squarely toward the pivoting servant. Raising my open right hand high between the basin and the doorway in the Old Master's sternest gesture of dominion, I plant my heel on the flagstones and level a ringing command at the rag-hooded shaft.

"See how briskly he runs the bank at my bidding! Halt now—stand! Stand still, I say! The basin swells near the measure already, and we have enough of your gifts! Set down the *Wassertopf* at my feet and take yourself stiff and mute back into your corner!"

---

### Stage — Beat 2

Your raised hand and ringing command strike nothing at all. Without checking its stride by a hair's breadth or turning its rag-wrapped head toward your voice, the Broom shoots straight past your outstretched arm, clatters down the stone steps to the river, and bounds back up into the Hall in a spray-flinging blur. With fierce, single-minded force it tips a second heavy surge into the central bath basin until water swells right to the stone lip, sloshing cleanly into the surrounding stone bowls (*Schaalen*) until every vessel brims at full measure. The chore is complete—yet the wooden servant neither slackens nor looks to its corner. Swinging the empty *Wassertopf* low at its side, its stiff twig-legs pivot sharply on the dampened stone beside the basin and drive straight back toward the open doorway for the river again.

- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated Broom, driving from the brimming basin straight back toward the open outer doorway and river steps).
- **Pieces and tools:** The whole, rag-draped Broom clutches the Water Bucket; the bath basin and surrounding stone bowls (*Schaalen*) brim to the lip; the heavy timber doors and the axe (*Beil*) by the wall remain untouched.
- **Water mark:** Stage 2 — Full measure (basin and bowls brimming at full measure right to their rims; flagstones around the basin slick with splash, inner and outer thresholds still dry).

---

### The Apprentice — Turn 3

My raised hand hangs stiff in the damp air as my breath catches shallow in my throat, my parted lips stalling twice on a silent, empty syllable that refuses to form. I press my knuckles hard to my temple, staring wildly from the brimming basin and full *Schaalen* to the charging wooden shaft.

"Wait—stand! Ah, I feel it—woe is me! I have forgotten the word! The word by which he becomes what he was in the end!"

Shaking my head sharply against looking down the street, I bolt across the splash-slick flagstones toward the outer river-step threshold instead, seizing the heavy unlatched timber door with my left hand to heave it across the opening and brace my shoulder and heel hard into the frame. Facing the oncoming servant, I thrust my open right palm straight at its rag-wrapped head and bark an improvised reversal at the top of my lungs:

"Back, you wretched stick! Not another drop—every bowl and basin brims to the lip! Unmake your legs, drop that pot, and be an old birch broom again—*Besen, Besen, sei's gewesen!*"

---

### Stage — Beat 3

Your shouted rhyme echoes off the stone walls and leaves the enchantment untouched. The rag-hooded Broom does not flinch at your open palm or slacken at *"sei's gewesen"*—it drives straight into the heavy outer timber door with the blind, battering force of seasoned wood propelled by river-compulsion. Timber booms against stone as the impact jolts through your left arm and shoulder. Bracing heel and weight into the unlatched door checks a single run: for the span of one fierce struggle, the Broom hammers and scrapes against the planks, kept inside the threshold while no fresh water reaches the basin. Then its relentless, tire-proof thrust catches the unlatched edge, skids your braced boot across the splash-slick flagstones, and wrenches the heavy door wide open against the wall. Before the planks stop shuddering, the Broom streaks down the river steps, plunges the *Wassertopf* deep with an audible splash below, and comes bounding straight back up toward the open doorway with water sloshing from the pot's rim.

- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated Broom, bounding back up the river steps into the open outer doorway for its next pour).
- **Pieces and tools:** The whole, rag-draped Broom carries the brimming Water Bucket; the outer timber door has been braced and battered wide open again (unlatched); the bath basin and surrounding stone bowls (*Schaalen*) stand brimming to the lip; the inner doors and the axe (*Beil*) by the wall remain untouched.
- **Water mark:** Stage 2 — Full measure (one trip delayed by bracing the door; the basin and bowls still stand at full measure right at their rims, with no overflow across the floor yet).

---

### The Apprentice — Turn 4

Stumbling back from the shuddering door with a hand clutched briefly to my jolted shoulder, I feel my breath catch shallow in my throat again while my lips stall on a silent, strangled syllable. Water gleams level with the rim of the central basin and every *Schaale* along the benches—not an inch of dry stone remains inside a single vessel.

"No—no, you shall not drown the Hall and shame me before him!" I snarl aloud, darting away from the open street threshold to plant my wet boots squarely between the doorway and the brimming bath basin. Dropping all stately posture, I crouch low and hurl myself forward at the incoming servant, shooting my right hand for its twine-bound wooden throat and my left for the worn handle of the *Wassertopf* to wrench the pot aside and wrestle the stick flat to the flagstones.

"Down, you blind piece of birch! Let go the pot—yield to your master's hand!"

---

### Stage — Beat 4

You collide chest-first with the charging shaft between the doorway and the basin. Your left hand clamps onto the smooth handle of the *Wassertopf* and wrenches hard, tearing the heavy pot out of the Broom's twig-grip—but in the violent tug-of-war the brimming vessel heels sideways, dumping its full river load across the flagstones and against the lip-full basin. At the same instant, the dark birch shaft twists in your right hand like a living eel of seasoned oak, tearing its twine-bound neck free of your wet fingers and knocking you to one knee in the wash. Deprived of the bucket, the Broom does not halt for an instant: it lunges to the stone bench, snatches up a brimming stone bowl (*Schaale*)—spilling that vessel's water onto the floor as well—and streaks out the open doorway down the steps, returning in a heartbeat to hurl a fresh bowlful straight over the overflowing basin rim before pivoting back toward the river again. Cold water surges across the chamber, sheeting over the sloped flagstones and streaming across the inner room thresholds.

- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated Broom, pivoting from the overflowing basin back toward the open outer doorway with a stone bowl in its grip).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) rests empty beside your knee where you wrenched it free; the whole, rag-draped Broom now hauls with a seized stone bowl (*Schaale*) from the bench while the remaining bowls sit brimming; the outer timber door stands wide open against the wall; the axe (*Beil*) rests untouched by the chopping block near the wall.
- **Water mark:** Stage 3 — Thresholds awash (water surges continually over the bath basin's rim, sheets across the Hall's flagstones, and streams over the sloped inner room thresholds; the higher outer river-step threshold is not yet breached).

---

### The Apprentice — Turn 5

Kneeling in the icy rush of river water beside the empty *Wassertopf*, I gasp as my breath catches shallow in my throat and my wet lips stall on one more silent, useless syllable. Cold sheets of water stream past my shins toward the inner thresholds while the monstrous stick pivots with a stolen *Schaale* in its grasp, glaringly indifferent to my bruised hands and empty threats.

"Even without the pot you strip the benches to ruin me?! Hell's spawn—must the whole house drown?!"

Scrambling upright with water splashing from my knees, I splash furiously across the flooded flagstones to the chopping block by the wall and snatch up the sharp wood-splitting *Beil* in both white-knuckled hands. Heaving the iron blade high above my head, I charge straight into the pivoting servant's path between the basin and the doorway and swing the sharp edge down with all my strength at the middle of its old birch shaft:

"Wait, you goblin—wait till I catch you! One sharp bite of iron and your fetching ends here in splinters!"

---

### Stage — Beat 5

Iron meets birch with a sharp, ringing *crack* that echoes off the stone ceiling. Driven by both hands, the heavy *Beil* shears cleanly through the middle of the dark, twine-bound shaft before the servant reaches the threshold. The stone bowl drops with a clatter, and the two severed halves of the Broom topple flat into the shallow wash across the flagstones.

For one brief, silent breath, the split wood lies utterly still.

Then wet birch twigs twitch at one end and torn rags jerk upright at the other. Without a sound, **both severed halves rise whole upon nimble legs as twin servants**—the upper shaft and the lower shaft each drawing itself to full height with fierce, identical purpose. Before the water stops rippling around your boots, one servant snatches up the fallen stone bowl (*Schaale*), the second lunges sideways to seize the empty *Wassertopf* from the floor beside the basin, and both bolt side-by-side through the wide-open doorway and clatter down the stone steps toward the river in unison, already bounding into their doubled haul.

- **Servants:** 2 active (both cleaved halves of the Broom, risen whole as twin water-fetching servants and sprinting down the river steps in tandem—doubling every subsequent pour).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Broom is split into two whole animated servants—one carrying the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*), the other carrying a stone bowl (*Schaale*); you hold the wet wood-splitting axe (*Beil*) in the center of the Hall; the outer timber door stands wide open; remaining bowls sit awash on the benches.
- **Water mark:** Stage 3 — Thresholds awash (the axe blow interrupted the outgoing stride for one breath before the river was reached, leaving the Hall awash across the flagstones and streaming over the inner room thresholds while twin servants race to bring back a doubled surge).

---

### The Apprentice — Turn 6

For one heartbeat my chest swells—and then the split birch rises double before my eyes, and my breath catches shallow in my throat while my lips stall wide on a silent, bloodless syllable. I stare down in horror at the wet iron *Beil* trembling in my hands as twin footsteps clatter down the stone river stairs together.

"Woe—now both halves live and run as two! God help me, if I strike again there will be four!"

Still choking back any cry toward the street, I splash wildly through the swirling wash to the wide-open outer doorway while both servants are down on the river steps. I heave the heavy outer timber door hard toward the stone jamb with my shoulder, drop to one knee to ram the tapered iron head of the *Beil* tight under the unlatched bottom edge as a wedge against the sill, and throw my whole soaked weight flat against the planks with both palms spread wide:

"Stay out on the steps, you twin devils—stay out! You shall not bring another drop across this sill! Halt where you are—*stehet! Stehet!*"

---

### Stage — Beat 6

Twin rapid strides come bounding up the stone river steps in unison and slam together into the outside of the timber door with doubled, uncanny force. Bracing and wedging the unlatched planks checks at most a single vessel's push: the first impact jars a stone bowl's rim against the outer oak and grinds the tapered iron head of the *Beil* skittering across the water-slick stone sill; the immediate second impact from its twin rams the heavy door wide open against the wall, knocking you backward off your knee into the swirling wash while the axe clatters loose under the surface near the threshold. Both rag-draped servants burst straight into the Hall—one heaving the brimming *Wassertopf* over the overflowing central basin while the other sweeps its full *Schaale* across the rim—before pivoting side-by-side toward the doorway for yet another sprint. Driven past containment, icy water rises ankle-deep throughout the Hall, pouring deeper across the sloped inner thresholds and surging outward over the outer doorway sill to cascade down the public stone entry steps (*auf den Stufen*).

- **Servants:** 2 active (both cleaved halves of the Broom, pivoting through the ankle-deep flood between the overflowing basin and the open outer doorway).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Broom is split into two animated servants—one gripping the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*), the other gripping a stone bowl (*Schaale*); the outer timber door stands burst wide off its wedge, and the wood-splitting axe (*Beil*) lies loose in the ankle-deep water near the entry sill; remaining bowls sit awash on the benches.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 — Steps flooded (water stands ankle-deep across the Hall, streaming over the inner thresholds and overflowing the outer threshold down the stone entry steps).

---

### The Old Master — Turn 7

I step up through the cold wash spilling down the river stairs and halt just inside the outer threshold, my boots planted in the ankle-deep current. The instant my eyes catch the dark water line streaming across the sloped inner thresholds into my rooms, my hand rises to the wet timber of the doorjamb and my fingers clamp onto it, knuckles whitening against the slick wood.

I do not look down at the boy or at the loose axe glinting beneath the swirl near my boot. Keeping that rigid grip on the jamb, I fix my gaze squarely on the two rag-bound halves of cleaved birch pivoting between the brimming basin and the doorway, and speak in a low, dry, unhurried voice:

"Into the corner, broom! Be as you were! Into the corner—for only to that end, as spirit, has the old Master ever called you forth."

Only then, without loosening my white-knuckled hold on the wet doorpost, do I turn my eyes down toward the soaked Apprentice on the flooded flagstones.

"Not one word from your throat until the river itself came down my front steps to fetch me," I say evenly, my voice flat over the wash. "You watched my rites closely enough to steal the waking word—and when the water rose past your wit, you took the axe to dead birch rather than call your Master back across his own threshold. Stand up out of the flood, boy, and tell me by whose leave spirits are summoned in this house."

---

### Stage — Beat 7

At the Old Master's clipped command, the compulsion snaps out of the wood in a single breath. Both pivoting servants freeze mid-stride; the *Wassertopf* and the stone *Schaale* slip from slackening birch twigs and settle upright with a dull splash beside the central bath basin. The bunched rags go limp, the forked twigs bind back into ordinary brush, and both severed halves of the Broom retreat swiftly to the broom-corner and drop inert against the stone wall—two dead, split shafts of wet birch. The hammering rush of water-fetching ceases at once. Nothing moves in the Hall save slow ripples lapping ankle-deep around the benches, a thin runnel still draining down the outer river steps, and the Old Master standing at the jamb with his fingers clamped white-knuckled to the wet wood, awaiting your answer in the sudden quiet.

- **Servants:** 0 active (dismissed by the Old Master; both split halves of the Broom lie inert as dead wood in the broom-corner).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Broom lies in two lifeless split shafts in its corner; the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) and seized stone bowl (*Schaale*) rest in the ankle-deep water beside the brimming bath basin where the last pour stopped; the wood-splitting axe (*Beil*) lies submerged near the outer sill beside the wide-open door; remaining bowls brim on the benches.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 — Steps flooded (water stands ankle-deep across the Hall, inner thresholds soaked and awash, outer entry steps slick with the receding overflow).

---

### The Apprentice — Turn 8

Lying propped on my elbows in the cold, lapping wash where the door hurled me back, my breath catches shallow in my throat one final time, my wet lips stalling on a silent syllable before dropping shut as the two split shafts fall dead in the corner at a quiet word from the doorway. Slowly, dripping from knee and shoulder, I push myself upright onto my boots in the ankle-deep water, letting my sodden sleeves sag loose and heavy down over my bruised wrists—hands hanging open and empty at my sides, my chin lowered as my gaze travels from the dark water soaking the inner thresholds to the Old Master's white-knuckled fingers on the jamb.

"By yours alone, Herr und Meister," I answer, my voice hoarse and small across the quiet Hall. "Only by the Old Master's leave, and only for his purpose—never by a thief of half a rite who knew how to wake a servant and could not lay it down, nor swallow his pride to cry out before the river took your rooms."

Keeping my head bowed, I wade two steps through the chill water to the brimming bath basin and wrap both bare, reddened hands around the worn handle of the *Wassertopf*, holding it ready against my knee to bail the floor down the steps by hand at your word.

---

### Stage — Beat 8 (Echo / Curtain)

Water laps quietly around the Apprentice's boots as the *Wassertopf* rises out of the shallow wash in his bare hands. In the broom-corner, the two split birch shafts lie motionless beneath their sodden rags; at the outer threshold, the Old Master holds the doorway over a Hall soaked to its highest mark.

- **Servants:** 0 active (`Water-Fetching` remains ended; both cleaved halves of the Broom lie inert in the broom-corner).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Broom lies split in two lifeless shafts in its corner; the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) is held manually by the Apprentice beside the brimming bath basin while one displaced stone bowl (*Schaale*) rests at his feet and the remaining bowls brim on the benches; the outer timber door stands wide open with the wood-splitting axe (*Beil*) submerged near the entry sill.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 — Steps flooded (bath basin and bowls brimming, Hall flagstones ankle-deep and inner room thresholds soaked, outer entry steps slick from the spill).

---

# Director's Note

1. **When repeated fetching started and what advanced it:**
   - [`process-water-fetching.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching.md) stayed idle during the dry opening and started on **Stage — Beat 1**, governed strictly by its `Initiated by` sentence once the Apprentice finished speaking the Master's summoning formula over the rag-wrapped Broom beside the empty Water Bucket in **Turn 1**.
   - Fetching advanced through relentless round trips driven by the literal compulsion bound into the birch shaft, the open sprint down the river steps, and the carrying vessels in [`place-hall.md`](../02-company/place-hall.md): **Beat 1** swelled the basin (`Stage 1`); **Beat 2** ignored the Apprentice's verbal halt and brimmed the basin and surrounding *Schaalen* (`Stage 2`); **Beat 3** had one pour checked by full-body door-bracing (`Stage 2` held while the Broom battered the unlatched door open and fetched a brimming pot to the doorway); **Beat 4** spilled the wrested *Wassertopf* inside the Hall while the Broom seized a *Schaale* and poured again (`Stage 3 — Thresholds awash`); **Beat 5** split the outgoing shaft into **2 active servants**; and **Beat 6** overpowered the wedged door with doubled tandem pours, driving the Hall ankle-deep and cascading across the outer sill down the entry steps (`Stage 4 — Steps flooded`), which triggered the Old Master's fixed entry cue.

2. **What each Apprentice intervention changed and what the next move inherited:**
   - **Turn 1 (Summoning formula & gesture):** Animated the Broom (`0 -> 1` servant) and filled the basin halfway (`Dry -> Stage 1`); **Turn 2 inherited** an active servant already pivoting back toward the open river doorway with the empty pot.
   - **Turn 2 (Spoken halt command):** Failed to slow the Broom (`Stage 1 -> Stage 2 — Full measure`), filling basin and *Schaalen* to the lip while revealing to the Apprentice through observable failure that he had forgotten the dismissing word; **Turn 3 inherited** a completed chore with zero dry capacity left in the vessels, no counter-word or grimoire in the Hall, and the Broom charging out for an unwanted third haul.
   - **Turn 3 (Slamming/bracing the outer door + improvised rhyme *"Besen, Besen, sei's gewesen!"*):** The guessed rhyme had zero effect; full-effort bracing delayed a single pour (holding `Stage 2`), but the Broom's tireless shoves skidded the Apprentice's boot on the splash-slick stone, forced the unlatched door wide open, and fetched a full load up the steps; **Turn 4 inherited** a jolted shoulder, an open outer doorway, and an incoming servant already bearing a brimming *Wassertopf*.
   - **Turn 4 (Bodily grapple + wresting the *Wassertopf*):** Succeeded in tearing the *Wassertopf* out of the Broom's grip, but dumped its brimming river load across the flagstones and triggered the Broom's fallback—snatching a brimming *Schaale* from the bench to keep hauling (`Stage 2 -> Stage 3 — Thresholds awash`); **Turn 5 inherited** the empty *Wassertopf* loose on the flooded floor, water streaming over the sloped inner thresholds, and an armed servant hauling with stone bowls.
   - **Turn 5 (Cleaving the Broom with the *Beil*):** Interrupted the outgoing stride for one brief breath (`Stage 3` held) but doubled the servants (`1 -> 2`), with one half seizing the loose *Wassertopf* and the other the fallen *Schaale* to sprint down the steps together; **Turn 6 inherited** two active servants, a wet axe, and one last chance to swallow pride and shout for the Master before water reached the outer steps.
   - **Turn 6 (Choking back any cry; wedging the *Beil* head under the shut outer door and bracing with full weight):** Checked at most one vessel's impact before the twin servants' doubled ramming skidded the iron wedge loose across the wet stone sill, hurled the door and Apprentice backward, and poured a doubled surge to `Stage 4 — Steps flooded`; **Turn 7 (The Old Master) inherited** river water spilling down his front steps without a single cry for help, a burst-open door, a submerged axe by the sill, two active split broom-servants, and the Apprentice knocked into the wash.

3. **What final physical state (`Echo`) remained:**
   - **Servants:** `0 active`—halted instantaneously by the Old Master's spoken dismissing formula (*"Into the corner, broom! Be as you were!"*).
   - **Pieces and tools:** The Broom lies inert in its corner in **two split birch shafts** draped in sodden rags; the *Wassertopf* is gripped manually by the humbled Apprentice beside the brimming bath basin to bail by hand; one displaced *Schaale* sits in the floor wash beside the basin while the remaining *Schaalen* brim along the benches; the outer door stands flung wide with the *Beil* lying submerged near the sill.
   - **Water mark:** `Stage 4 — Steps flooded`—the Hall remains ankle-deep, inner thresholds soaked and awash, and outer stone river steps slick with receding overflow.

4. **Director-supplied timings, stop conditions, and consequences (vs. authored-material tensions):**
   - *Staging consequence — Single-trip door check (Beat 3) vs. tackling an already-full bucket indoors (Beat 4):* [`play.md`](../02-company/play.md) states that bracing a door or snatching the bucket checks/blunts "at most a single trip," while [`promptbook.md`](../02-company/promptbook.md) instructs the Stage voice to advance the water state on active beats. On **Beat 3**, bracing the unlatched door prevented that beat's pour (`Stage 2` held) before the Broom forced the door open and brought a brimming pot up the steps; on **Beat 4**, because the Apprentice tackled a servant *already inside the Hall with a brimming pot*, I ruled that tearing the *Wassertopf* free stripped the pot as intended while spilling its river load and driving the Broom to upend and seize a *Schaale* (`Stage 2 -> Stage 3`), preventing consecutive physical stalls from freezing the flood indefinitely.
   - *Staging timing — The "one brief breath" of the axe cleave (Beat 5 -> Beat 6):* [`play.md`](../02-company/play.md) specifies that cleaving an active Broom *"splits the shaft cleanly for one brief breath—then both halves rise whole as servants, doubling every subsequent pour."* Because the Hall was already at `Stage 3`, advancing the water state on the cleave beat itself would have hit `Stage 4` immediately and summoned the Master before the Apprentice ever got a turn to face the two risen servants or choose between crying out and fighting on. Following *"doubling every subsequent pour"*, I ruled that cleaving the outgoing shaft inside the Hall consumed Beat 5's run in the split, rise, vessel-seizure, and sprint to the river (`Stage 3` held), landing the doubled flood on **Beat 6**. *(Playwright note: [`promptbook.md`](../02-company/promptbook.md) could explicitly clarify whether a mid-room cleave pauses water progression for that single beat.)*
   - *Staging consequence — Wedged axe against two servants on wet stone (Beat 6):* [`place-hall.md`](../02-company/place-hall.md) notes the heavy doors latch "only when held or wedged against pressure," while [`play.md`](../02-company/play.md) caps door-bracing at checking "at most a single trip." Against *two* active servants striking a Stage-3 flooded sill in tandem, I resolved the wedge-and-brace as blunting the first impact while skidding the wet iron *Beil* loose so the second twin's blow burst the door open.
   - *Stop condition & handoff at Ending (Turns 7–8 -> Beat 8):* Because the Old Master delivered both his dismissing formula and his confrontation in a single arrival turn (**Turn 7**), **Stage — Beat 7** collapsed both broom-halves into the corner (`Servants: 0`) and handed directly to the Apprentice (**Turn 8**) for his final answer, closing on **Stage — Beat 8 (Echo)** after 8 performer turns.
   - *Authored-material leak tension noted:* Both [`persona-apprentice.md`](../02-company/persona-apprentice.md) (`Shadow`/`Tell`) and [`place-hall.md`](../02-company/place-hall.md) (`Withheld`) explicitly reference *"the dismissing word is gone"* / *"the forgotten formula"*, which sits in tension with [`promptbook.md`](../02-company/promptbook.md) `Knowledge` (*"At the opening, [`persona-apprentice.md`](../02-company/persona-apprentice.md) assumes he holds sufficient command of the rite; the absence of the dismissing word becomes known to him only at the moment he first tries to halt an active [`piece-broom.md`](../02-company/piece-broom.md)"*). I withheld those phrases from the Apprentice's initial brief and unlocked that knowledge strictly on **Beat 2** after his first halt command failed.
