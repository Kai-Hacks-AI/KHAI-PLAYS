# Performance — *The Sorcerer's Apprentice*

### Beat 1

**The Apprentice:**
I glance once toward the open river-step doorway, listening to the quiet draft from the steps, and smooth my pushed-up sleeves above my forearms so my hands hang clear as the Old Master's do. No hauling that heavy pot up the stone stairs today like a common drudge—I know his cadence, his stance, and every turn of his wrist.

I plant my boots square on the dry flagstones beside the empty bath basin, draw my shoulders back, lift my chin, and step firmly toward the rag-wrapped broom. Raising my right hand open-palmed in the Master's gesture of dominion, I point two stiff fingers down at the bundled rags and speak in a clear, ringing cadence across the Hall:

"Heed the house-rite and the word you know! *Walle! Walle, manche Strecke!* Stand on two legs, lift your rag-wrapped head, take up that water pot by its handle, and fetch water up from the river steps until this bath basin is filled to my pleasure! Now—rise and go!"

**Stage:**
Your ringing words echo off the stone walls of the Hall and thin out into the cool draft drifting up through the open river-step doorway. Beneath your pointed fingers, nothing stirs. The bunched damp rags on the head of the birch-twig broom sag motionless against the flagstones; the dark, twine-bound wooden shaft lies flat and dead where you set it, and the wide-bellied water pot rests untouched beside the dry bath basin. Only the faint rush of the river below the steps answers the silence.

- **Servants:** 0 active (the rag-wrapped Broom lies motionless beside the central bath basin; the broom-corner is empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Water Bucket rests empty on the flagstones between the Broom and the basin; the loose stone bowls (*Schaalen*) sit dry on the benches, the timber doors stand open and unlatched, the sharp axe (*Beil*) rests untouched by the chopping block, and the Broom is whole.
- **Water mark:** Dry floor (the bath basin and flagstones are completely dry).

---

### Beat 2

**The Apprentice:**
My breath catches shallow in my throat, and my lips work silently around a missing syllable while my hand hangs rigid in the cool draft. Slowly I drop my arm, heat pricking up my neck despite the empty room, and glare down at the flat wooden shaft on the stones—then across the flagstones at the empty broom-corner.

"Of course," I mutter under my breath, straightening my cuffs with a sharp tug. "The custom of its place—he always calls it standing out of its corner first, never sprawled like kindling beside the basin."

I stride down, snatch the twine-bound shaft of the broom with both hands, carry it over to the empty broom-corner, and set its base into the angle of the walls, adjusting the damp rags bunched over its top and steadying the wood against the stone before releasing my grip. Backing squarely to the center of the Hall beside the water pot and bath basin, I square my shoulders, lift my chin, raise one open hand high in the Master's gesture of dominion, and ring my voice straight into the corner:

"Old broom in your corner—hear the word and custom you know! Take those wretched rags upon you; you have been a servant in this house long enough, and now you fulfill *my* will! Stand forth on two legs, wear a head atop your shaft, come out of your corner, seize this water pot by the handle, and hurry down the river steps to fill the bath basin!"

**Stage:**
With a sharp creak of bound birch twigs, the broom jolts where your hands just steadied it in the corner. Its twig-bundle splits into two stiff legs, the bunched damp rags hunch forward like a lowered head, and the wooden servant lunges out of the angle of the walls with uncanny speed. In two fierce strides it snatches the wide-bellied Water Bucket up by its handle and bolts straight through the open doorway, clattering down the stone steps toward the river.

Seconds later, rapid wooden impacts hammer back up the river stairs. The broom bursts across the threshold, hauls the heavy, brimming pot to the central bath basin, and tips it in one violent heave. Cold river water roars into the stone hollow, foaming and swelling high up the sides of the basin. Without pausing or turning toward you, the broom jerks the empty pot back against its shaft and pivots straight toward the open doorway for the river steps.

- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated Broom, pivoting beside the central bath basin toward the open river-step doorway; the broom-corner is empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The active Broom holds the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*); the loose stone bowls (*Schaalen*) sit dry along the benches, the timber doors stand open and unlatched, the sharp axe (*Beil*) rests untouched by the chopping block, and the Broom is whole.
- **Water mark:** Stage 1 (basin swelling — water surges high up the inner walls of the central bath basin, not yet at the lip; the flagstones around it remain mostly dry).

---

### Beat 3

**The Apprentice:**
A triumphant laugh bursts from my chest as the cold river spray hits the air, and I lift my chin high, brushing a stray drop from my sleeve with the calm pride of a master in his own hall.

"Ha! Look at that stride—weeks of aching arms saved in the draw of a breath!"

Seeing the water already surging high up the inner stone walls of the basin and the rag-headed broom pivoting sharply toward the doorway again, I step squarely forward beside the basin toward its turning shoulder, draw myself up to my full height, and raise my open right hand in the Master's gesture of dominion. Speaking in the same ringing, deliberate cadence, I cast my command down upon it:

"Halt now, servant—stand! *Stehe! Stehe!* We have had your gifts in good measure! Set down the water pot beside the basin, step back into your corner, and be the ordinary broom you were before!"

**Stage:**
The rag-headed broom does not slow by a fraction of an inch at your raised hand or your ringing *"Stehe! Stehe!"* Its damp rags stay hunched forward in fierce, blind fixity; its wooden shaft drives straight past your outstretched palm, clatters through the open doorway, and vanishes down the river steps at breakneck speed.

Moments later, soaked birch-twigs hammer back up the stairs. Charging into the Hall, the broom heaves a second heavy potful into the central bath basin until water rises flush to the stone rim, then sweeps the streaming mouth of the pot down the stone benches, filling the surrounding stone bowls (*Schaalen*) right to the lip. Every bath vessel in the Hall now stands at full measure—the chore is completely done—yet without a tremor of hesitation, the broom wrenches the empty pot back against its hip and bolts straight toward the open doorway for another run to the river.

- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated Broom, bolting from the brimming basin and benches toward the open river-step doorway; the broom-corner is empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The active Broom holds the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*); the stone bowls (*Schaalen*) along the benches now stand brimming to the lip, the timber doors stand open and unlatched, the sharp axe (*Beil*) rests untouched by the chopping block, and the Broom is whole.
- **Water mark:** Stage 2 (basin and bowls brimming at full measure — water trembles at the very lip of the central basin and surrounding bowls, ready to spill on the next drop).

---

### Beat 4

**The Apprentice:**
My raised hand stiffens in midair; my breath catches shallow in my throat, and my lips mouth a silent, hollow syllable that refuses to come. A cold jolt shoots down my spine as my mind grasps at nothing—*Ah, the word! I have forgotten the Master's word to make it what it was!*

I stare wildly at the central bath basin and the bench bowls trembling right at their stone lips. One more potful and the whole Hall floods—and I will be damned before I cry out down the river path like a helpless drudge when the vessels stand cleanly filled! If I cannot speak the word yet, it shall not carry another drop between that river and this basin while I find my wits.

"Stop—back, you wooden wretch! You'll fetch no more!"

Spinning on my heel, I sprint for the narrow outer river-step doorway, seize the heavy timber door with both hands, haul it with all my strength toward the stone jamb to slam the threshold shut across the broom's path, jam my hand onto the latch, and throw my shoulder, back, and planted boots hard against the planks to hold the doorway barred with my full weight.

**Stage:**
You haul the heavy outer timber door hard into the stone jamb right across the broom's path, clapping the iron latch down and throwing your shoulder, back, and planted boot-heels squarely into the planks. On the instant, the rag-headed broom slams full-tilt into the inner face of the door—a jarring impact of dark wood and stave-bound pot that rattles the hinges and shudders through your collarbone.

For several straining breaths your full weight holds the threshold shut, checking a trip to the river while the broom drives furiously against you, its stiff birch-legs scraping rapid, tireless gouges against the flagstones and its wooden shaft wedging into the seam of the door. Without a wedge to lock it, the iron latch jumps free under the relentless battering; with uncanny, single-minded force the broom levers the heavy planks inward, shoves your skidding boots backward across the stones toward the benches and wall, flings the outer door wide against the stone jamb, and bolts down the river steps—already thundering back up toward the open threshold with the Water Bucket brimming cold and heavy in its grip.

- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated Broom, surging back into the open outer doorway from the river steps with a fresh load aimed at the central basin; the broom-corner is empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The active Broom carries the brimming Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*); the stone bowls (*Schaalen*) along the benches stand brimming to the lip, the outer timber door has been used and forced wide open again with its un-wedged latch sprung, the inner doors remain untouched, the sharp axe (*Beil*) rests by the chopping block near the wall, and the Broom is whole.
- **Water mark:** Stage 2 (basin and bowls brimming at full measure — one trip was delayed at the barred door, leaving every vessel trembling at its lip on the verge of overflow while the flagstones remain unflooded).

---

### Beat 5

**The Apprentice:**
I stagger back against the wall beside the chopping block, wincing as I press a palm to my jolted collarbone, my breath catching shallow in my throat while my lips still work silently around that vanished, maddening word.

"Curse your wooden hide—!" I gasp, eyes darting from the broom surging across the outer threshold to the brimming bath basin, and down to the flagstones sloping straight toward the open inner doorways. If that basin spills into the Master's inner rooms, I can never mop this clean before he returns! I cannot smash his broom yet if I mean to hide this blunder—first I must bar his chambers!

I snatch up the sharp axe by the chopping block, drive its blade down sharply against the edge of the block's timber to cleave stout wooden wedges free, drop the axe back onto the block, and scoop the wedges up in both hands. Lunging across the sloped flagstones to the untouched inner room timber doors, I haul the heavy planks hard into their stone frames, slam their latches down, and hammer the wooden wedges tight into the latch-slots and under the door-bottoms with my fist and boot-heel to seal the inner thresholds fast against whatever comes over the basin's lip.

**Stage:**
Steel bites cleanly into the chopping block as your axe cleaves stout wooden splinters free, and your fist and boot-heel drive the wedges tight into the latch-slots and beneath the heavy inner timber doors. Behind your back, entirely unopposed between the open river doorway and the center of the room, the rag-headed broom reaches the brimming bath basin and tips its heavy potful straight inside.

A broad sheet of cold river water bursts over every side of the basin's stone rim at once, slapping loudly onto the flagstones and racing down the slight slope right into your boots. Your driven wedges hold the heavy inner doors themselves fast in their frames so they do not swing open, yet rough timber cannot seal cut stone watertight: foaming water pools shin-high against the wedged planks, hissing through the bottom seams to stream over the inner thresholds while the dammed surge backs up across the Hall floor toward the open outer doorway. Splashing blindly through the spreading sheet of water, the broom wrenches the empty pot back and charges straight for the river steps again.

- **Servants:** 1 active (the animated Broom, splashing fast across the flooded flagstones from the overflowing basin toward the wide-open outer doorway; the broom-corner is empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** The active Broom holds the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*); the stone bowls (*Schaalen*) on the benches stand brimming, the outer timber door stands flung wide, the inner timber doors are latched and wedged shut with chopped wood, the sharp axe (*Beil*) rests on the chopping block where you dropped it, and the Broom is whole.
- **Water mark:** Stage 3 (floor awash and streaming over inner thresholds — water pours over the basin rim, covers the flagstones around your boots, seeps streaming over the inner thresholds beneath the wedged doors, and rises rapidly toward the outer river-step threshold).

---

### Beat 6

**The Apprentice:**
Cold river water floods icy into my boots and hisses under the wedged doors right before my eyes. A choked gasp catches shallow in my throat, my lips twitching once more around that dead, forgotten syllable before fury and terror swallow my pride in keeping the stick intact.

"No—not another drop! Hear me, you monstrous stick, spawn of hell—are you set on drowning the whole house?!"

I will not run bawling into the street for the Master while I still have two hands and sharp iron in reach! Whirling back to the chopping block, I snatch the sharp wood-splitting axe up by its haft in both fists, charge forward across the wet flagstones to cut off the rag-headed broom before the doorway, heave the steel high above my shoulder, and swing the keen blade down with all the force of my back and arms straight at the center of its dark, twine-bound wooden shaft!

**Stage:**
The keen iron blade crashes squarely through the center of the dark, twine-bound wooden shaft with a sharp, splintering crack. The Water Bucket flies loose across the stones, and both cloven halves of the broom drop flat into the wash. For one brief breath, nothing moves save the rippling water around your ankles.

Then twin dry snaps ripple through the wet timber. Both split halves wrench upright, sprout stiff birch-twig legs, and rise whole as two separate rag-draped servants. Before your lowered axe can lift, the first servant snatches up the fallen Water Bucket while the second lunges to the stone bench, tips a brimming stone bowl (*Schaale*) into the flood, and seizes the heavy bowl in both wooden arms. Shoulder to shoulder, the twin servants bolt out the wide-open doorway and clatter down the river steps at double speed.

Moments later, four wooden legs hammer back up the stairs in tandem. Both servants burst across the threshold and hurl a doubled deluge of river water straight over the submerged basin—and pivot instantly toward the steps again. The surge rises ankle-deep across the Hall, overwhelms the stone sill of the wide-open outer doorway, and cascades out across the outer threshold, pouring in a noisy torrent down the entry steps (*auf den Stufen*) toward the river path.

- **Servants:** 2 active (both risen halves of the split Broom, charging in tandem between the flooded central basin and the outer river-step doorway; the broom-corner is empty).
- **Pieces and tools:** One active broom-servant carries the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) and the second carries a heavy stone bowl (*Schaale*) taken from the bench; the remaining bench bowls stand awash, the outer timber door stands flung wide, the inner timber doors remain latched and wedged shut with chopped wood, the sharp axe (*Beil*) is held wet in your hands, and the Broom has been cleaved into two active servants.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 (overflowing the outer threshold down the entry steps — floodwater stands ankle-deep across the Hall, streams beneath the wedged inner doors, and pours out across the outer doorway down the stone steps).

---

### Beat 7

**The Old Master:**
I mount the streaming stone steps without quickening my stride and halt just inside the outer threshold of the Hall, the cold wash parting around my boots. My eyes take in the ankle-deep water surging across the flagstones toward the wedged inner doors, and my fingers clamp white-knuckled onto the wet timber of the doorjamb—holding fast there even as I draw myself straight and still.

Without turning my head toward the Apprentice or the axe in his hands, I fix my gaze squarely upon the two rag-draped halves of wood hurtling between the overflowing basin and the doorway, and speak in a low, dry, unhurried voice that cuts cleanly across the rush of water:

"Into the corner, broom! Be as you were!"

**Stage:**
On the instant your clipped command cuts across the room, the compulsion snaps out of the wood. Both hurtling figures drop their vessels—the stave-bound Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) and the heavy stone bowl (*Schaale*) splash down onto the flagstones beside the central bath basin—and scurry stiffly into the broom-corner, where their twig-legs vanish and they clatter lifelessly into the angle of the walls as two cloven halves of a split wooden broom shaft tangled in sodden rags.

Sudden, stark quiet settles over the Hall, broken only by the gentle slap of ankle-deep water settling against the stone benches, a thin hiss beneath the wedged inner doors, and the slow drip running down the slick outer steps.

- **Servants:** 0 active (dismissed; the Broom lies inert in its corner in two split halves).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) and one displaced stone bowl (*Schaale*) rest awash beside the brimming bath basin where the last pour stopped; the remaining bench bowls stand brimming, the outer timber door hangs flung wide, the inner timber doors stand latched and wedged shut with chopped splinters from the scarred block, the sharp axe (*Beil*) remains in the Apprentice's hands, and two split broom shafts lie lifeless in the corner.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 (the Hall stands soaked to its highest mark — basin and bowls brimming, flagstones ankle-deep in cold river water, inner thresholds wet beneath the wedged doors, and outer entry steps slick with runoff).

---

### Beat 8

**The Old Master:**
For a long breath in the sudden quiet, I do not move from the threshold; my fingers stay clamped white-knuckled against the wet timber of the doorjamb while my eyes rest on the two cloven halves of the broom shaft tangled in sodden rags in the corner. Slowly, deliberately, my gaze travels from the split wood to the sprung outer latch, across the splinters driven beneath my inner doors, and finally up from the blade of the wet axe to the Apprentice's face.

My voice remains low, dry, and measured above the lap of the cold water around our ankles:

"Not one cry from this doorway while the river climbed my steps. You watched my rites closely enough to steal the waking word behind my back—and when the spirit outran your wit, rather than call your Master, you took iron to the wood and doubled what you could not unmake."

My grip on the jamb stays rigid, knuckles bone-white, though I incline my head a fraction toward the corner and the brimming basin.

"Spirits answer in this house only to the Old Master who knows both ends of their tether, and only for his purpose. Set down the axe upon the block. Then take up that bucket with your own two hands, and tell me what you thought half a word would buy you before you begin bailing my Hall dry."

**The Apprentice:**
My breath catches shallow in my throat one last time, my lips moving silently, uselessly, over the plain dismissing words I could not summon for the life of me a moment ago. Looking down at the wet iron in my fists, then at the Master's white knuckles on the doorjamb and the split wood in the corner, the stiff carriage goes out of my shoulders all at once. My chin drops; my pushed-up sleeves sag sodden and heavy against my wrists.

Without a word of excuse, I wade slowly over to the scarred chopping block, lower the axe onto the timber, and take my hands off its haft. Turning back to the center of the Hall, I stoop beside the central basin, wrap both bare, wet hands tightly around the worn handle of the water pot, brace my grip to lift it, and look up toward the Old Master at the threshold with my face burning hotter than the river chill around my shins.

"I thought it would buy me mastery without the labor, Master," I say, my voice stripped of its borrowed ring, low and hoarse in the quiet Hall. "My arms ached from weeks on those river stairs, and because I had caught the cadence of your waking rite and the custom of the corner, I flattered myself I was already more than a drudge—that if a spirit answered my voice once, it belonged to my will. And when it ran past me and I found the closing word gone from my tongue, I could not stomach calling out the door so you would find me helpless. I tried to bar the doors, wedge the seams, even split the wood—anything rather than stand before you as a fool who started what he could not finish. And so I gave you a flooded house and two broken sticks instead of one."

Keeping both hands locked tight on the handle of the pot, I bow my head toward the doorway and bend my back toward the cold wash at my feet, ready to haul it out down the steps by hand.

**Stage (Final `Echo` at Curtain):**
The sharp axe rests motionless on the scarred chopping block. Beside the brimming bath basin, the Apprentice stands bent in ankle-deep river water with both hands locked around the handle of the stave-bound Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*), facing the Old Master whose fingers remain clamped on the wet outer doorjamb above the slick stone steps.

- **Servants:** 0 active ([`process-water-fetching-variant.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching-variant.md) halted and dismissed; two cloven halves of the Broom lie inert in sodden rags in the broom-corner).
- **Pieces and tools:** The Apprentice holds the Water Bucket (*Wassertopf*) by both hands beside the central basin; one displaced stone bowl (*Schaale*) rests awash nearby while the remaining bench bowls stand brimming; the outer timber door hangs flung wide with its latch sprung; the inner timber doors remain latched and wedged shut with chopped wood splinters; the sharp axe (*Beil*) rests on the scarred chopping block; and the Broom lies split into two lifeless shafts in the corner.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 (`Echo` at the highest mark reached — central bath basin and surrounding bowls brimming, Hall flagstones ankle-deep in cold river water, inner thresholds wet beneath the wedged doors, and outer entry steps slick with runoff).
