# Performance — *The Sorcerer's Apprentice*

**[Stage]**
A cool draft off the river moves through the open outer doorway of the Hall, carrying the faint rush of current up the stone steps. Inside, the sloped flagstones lie dry. At the center of the chamber stands the deep, empty bath basin (*Becken*); dry stone bowls (*Schaalen*) line the benches along the walls, a wood-splitting axe (*Beil*) rests beside the chopping block near the wall, and the heavy timber doors at the outer river-step threshold and inner room thresholds stand unlatched. The broom-corner is bare. Beside the empty basin, the old birch-twig broom lies motionless on the stones with damp, tattered rags bunched around its twine-bound top like a makeshift head, the empty stave-bound water pot (*Wassertopf*) set ready at its foot. Beyond the street and river path, the Old Master is gone.
- **Servants:** 0 active (the rag-wrapped broom lies inert beside the central bath basin).
- **Pieces and tools:** Water Bucket empty on the flagstones between the broom and the dry basin; Broom whole and wrapped in rags; stone bowls dry on the benches, timber doors open and unbraced, axe untouched by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Dry floor (bath basin, bowls, and flagstones completely dry).

**[The Apprentice]**
I push both sleeves high past my elbows, plant my boots square on the dry flagstones beside the empty bath basin, and set my shoulders back with my chin lifted toward the open doorway. Drawing a deep breath into my chest, I step firmly toward the rag-draped broom, raise my right hand open-palmed over its twine-bound shaft in the Master's high gesture of dominion, and speak in a clear, ringing cadence that echoes off the stone walls:

"Gone at last is the old Sorcerer! Now his spirits shall live according to my will! I have marked his words and works, and I have observed his custom—with strength of spirit, I too shall work wonders!

*Walle! walle*
*Manche Strecke,*
*Daß, zum Zwecke,*
*Wasser fließe,*
*Und mit reichem, vollem Schwalle*
*Zu dem Bade sich ergieße!*

Come now, old broom! Take those wretched rags upon you—you have been a servant long enough, now fulfill my command! Stand upon two legs, bear a head above, and make haste—seize the water pot and go down the stone steps to the river!"

I snap my open hand sharply downward, pointing straight from the stave-bound bucket toward the outer river-step threshold, and hold my stance stiffly above it.

**[Stage]**
Your ringing cadence dies against the stone walls of the Hall, leaving only the steady murmur of the river below the steps. Cool draft from the open doorway stirs a loose corner of the damp rags bunched at the broom's head, but beneath your hovering, outstretched hand—untouched on the flagstones—neither the twine-bound birch broom nor the stave-bound water pot shifts a hair's breadth. Both lie dead still beside the empty bath basin.
- **Servants:** 0 active (the rag-wrapped broom lies motionless on the flagstones beside the central bath basin, untouched).
- **Pieces and tools:** Water Bucket empty on the flagstones between the broom and the dry basin; Broom whole and wrapped in rags; stone bowls dry on the benches, timber doors open and unbraced, axe untouched by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Dry floor (bath basin, bowls, and flagstones completely dry).

**[The Apprentice]**
My breath catches shallow in my throat; my outstretched hand hangs stiff in the draft for a beat while my lips part and mouth a silent, soundless syllable into the quiet room.

With a sharp exhale through my nose, I square my shoulders harder, glance quickly toward the empty river doorway, and drop to one knee beside the birch broom. I seize the dark, worn wooden shaft with my left hand, haul the broom upright onto the splayed ends of its twigs, and cinch the damp rags tightly around its twine-bound top to form a firmer head, hooking the smooth handle of the stave-bound water pot directly against its side. Rising to my full height, I press the flat of my open right palm squarely onto the rag-wrapped crown—striking the shaft's butt once, sharply, against the flagstones—before lifting my hand in the Master's sweeping arc from the broom's head down toward the river steps.

"Stand, I say—by the Master's custom and my hand upon your head, wake and serve!

*Walle! walle*
*Manche Strecke,*
*Daß, zum Zwecke,*
*Wasser fließe,*
*Und mit reichem, vollem Schwalle*
*Zu dem Bade sich ergieße!*

Up on two legs, rag-head! Grip the water pot, down the steps to the river bank, and fill this bath!"

I release the crown and step back a single measured pace, chin high and open palm held rigid toward the outer threshold.

**[Stage]**
The instant your palm leaves the rag-wrapped crown, a dry crack shudders down the dark wooden shaft. The splayed birch twigs split into two stiff, striding legs; the twine-bound frame jerks upright, snatches the smooth handle of the stave-bound water pot with fierce, uncanny speed, and bolts straight out the open doorway down the stone steps. Before the clatter on the stair has faded, a heavy splash sounds from the riverbank below—and like lightning the rag-headed broom charges back up the steps into the Hall, heaves the brimming water pot over the central bath basin, and dumps a foaming torrent inside. Clear river water roars against the stone bottom and swells high up the basin's sides. Without a breath of pause or glance at your raised hand, the silent broom-servant swings the emptied pot down and pivots sharply back toward the river doorway for another run.
- **Servants:** 1 active (the rag-wrapped broom, pivoting swiftly from the bath basin toward the open outer doorway).
- **Pieces and tools:** Water Bucket gripped in the active broom-servant's grasp; Broom whole and wrapped in rags; stone bowls dry on the benches, timber doors open and unbraced, axe untouched by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Stage 1 — Basin swelling (river water surging high up the sides of the central bath basin; flagstones dry save for flung spray around the rim).

**[The Apprentice]**
A sharp, triumphant laugh bursts from my chest as spray flecks my boots. I plant one hand on my hip, lean briefly over the stone rim to admire the clear river water surging high up the sides of the bath basin, and lift my chin high.

"See how swiftly he leaps down the bank! Already at the stream, and back like lightning—not a drop spilled on the stair!"

Pivoting squarely toward the striding broom-servant, I step forward onto the dry flagstones with my shoulders thrown back, raise my right hand flat and open-palmed directly across its path in the Master's gesture of dominion, and call out in a crisp, lordly voice:

"Stop! Stand still! We have enough of your gifts now—the measure is full! Back to your corner, old broom, lay down the pot, and be as you were!"

**[Stage]**
The broom-servant does not flinch, slow, or tilt its rag-bound head at your voice. Brushing straight past your raised palm with fierce, unseeing drive, it clatters down the stone steps to the riverbank, scoops another heavy load, and hurtles back through the doorway in heartbeats. With a sharp heave it overturns the brimming water pot into the bath basin; water surges cleanly to the stone lip, slops across into the nearby stone bowls (*Schaalen*) until every vessel around the bath stands trembling at full measure—and still the silent wooden figure snaps the empty pot back to its side, wheeling with furious, single-minded speed toward the open river doorway to fetch again.
- **Servants:** 1 active (the rag-wrapped broom, wheeling from the brimming bath basin straight toward the open outer doorway).
- **Pieces and tools:** Water Bucket gripped tight by the active broom-servant; Broom whole and wrapped in rags; surrounding stone bowls (*Schaalen*) brimming to the lip, timber doors open and unbraced, axe untouched by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Stage 2 — Full measure (central bath basin and surrounding stone bowls filled right to the lip; the chore is complete, and one more pour will spill across the flagstones).

**[The Apprentice]**
My breath catches shallow in my throat as the broom brushes past my hand; my fingers curl inward, and my lips part, mouthing a silent, missing syllable once, twice, into the damp air.

"Ah—the word!" I hiss under my breath, clapping one hand to my forehead before snapping my gaze quickly toward the open doorway to be sure the street outside is empty. "Woe is me—I have forgotten the Master's word! That word by which he turns back into what he was at the end!"

Looking wildly at the brimming bath basin and the trembling bowls along the bench, I bolt across the flagstones for the outer river-step doorway just as the broom wheels around. I seize the heavy timber door with both hands, haul it swinging shut across the river threshold to cut off the steps, and slam my shoulder and full weight square against the planks, bracing my boots wide on the dry stones while thrusting one open palm out at the approaching rag-head.

"*Stehe! stehe!* Stand back, accursed stick!" I bark inside the closed chamber, voice tight and breathless against the timber. "Not another drop—the basin and every bowl are full to the lip! Drop the pot and be a broom again!"

**[Stage]**
The heavy outer door slams shut across the threshold an instant before the broom-servant reaches it—and the wooden shaft crashes squarely into the planks against your braced shoulder with jarring, tireless force. Your shouted *"Stehe! stehe!"* dies unanswered inside the closed chamber. With fierce, blank-faced drive, the rag-headed broom plants its splayed birch legs and rams again and again into the unlatched timber, jamming the thick rim of the water pot into the seam beside the jamb. For this single checked trip, no fresh river water enters the room—behind you, the bath basin and surrounding stone bowls hold trembling at full measure—but every ounce of your strength is pinned flat against the shuddering planks. Already your boot soles begin to skid across the smooth flagstones under the servant's inhuman, unflagging leverage, and the unlatched door wrenches a hand's-breadth ajar toward the river steps, about to tear past your shoulder.
- **Servants:** 1 active (the rag-wrapped broom, ramming and prying the outer doorway a hand's-breadth open directly against the Apprentice's braced weight).
- **Pieces and tools:** Water Bucket gripped by the active broom-servant and wedged into the door seam; Broom whole and wrapped in rags; outer timber door braced shut for one checked trip but now wrenching ajar without a latch; surrounding stone bowls brimming to the lip, inner doors open, axe untouched by the chopping block.
- **Water mark:** Stage 2 — Full measure (one trip delayed by full-body door brace; bath basin and surrounding bowls brimming at the lip, one pour away from flooding the flagstones).

**[The Apprentice]**
As my boot soles skid across the smooth stone, my breath catches shallow in my throat and my parted lips mouth a silent, missing syllable against the shuddering planks.

"Curse you—if the word is gone, I will undo the custom that woke you!" I gasp through clenched teeth, refusing to raise my voice loud enough to carry past the threshold.

Before the unlatched door can tear my shoulder backward, I twist sharply clear of the swinging planks and lunge straight at the broom-servant's upper shaft. Wrapping my left forearm hard around the dark, twine-bound wood just below the top, I slap my right palm down onto its crown in reverse of my waking gesture, then lock my fingers into the bunched, damp rags and wrench upward with all my might to rip its makeshift head clean off the stick.

"Off with those rags! Lose your head, lose your legs—*ergieße nicht, fließe nicht!* Unmake the rite and drop dead wood!"

**[Stage]**
As you twist clear of the threshold, the unlatched outer door swings wide open onto the river steps. Your fingers tear the damp, tattered rags clean off the twine-bound top—leaving them bunched in your fist—while your reversed slap and shouted *"ergieße nicht, fließe nicht!"* land squarely on the bare wood. Not a splinter slackens. Stripped of its makeshift head, the dark wooden shaft wrenches free of your single forearm with fierce, inhuman torque, bolts straight out the unbarred doorway down the steps, and returns from the river at lightning speed before you can regain your balance. Heaving the brimming water pot high, the bare-headed broom dumps a fresh torrent into the already-full bath basin. Water cascades over the stone rim in a white sheet, races across the inward-sloping flagstones around your boots, and streams over the inner room thresholds. Without pausing a fraction of a beat, the silent broom-servant swings the empty pot to its side and charges back toward the open river doorway—one more trip away from flooding ankle-deep out onto the entry steps.
- **Servants:** 1 active (the bare-headed birch broom, stripped of its rags, charging from the overflowing basin back toward the wide-open outer doorway).
- **Pieces and tools:** Water Bucket gripped tight by the active broom-servant; Broom whole, stripped of its damp rags (now torn free in the Apprentice's hand); outer timber door swung wide open after being released; stone bowls overflowing, inner doors open and streaming with water, axe untouched by the chopping block near the wall.
- **Water mark:** Stage 3 — Thresholds awash (water surging over the bath basin rim, sheeting across the flagstones, and streaming over the inner room thresholds; one cycle short of Stage 4 overflowing the outer threshold down the entry steps).

**[The Apprentice]**
Cold water surges around my ankles, and my breath catches shallow in my throat; staring down at the torn rags bunched uselessly in my fist, my lips part and mouth a silent, missing syllable into the roar of spilling water.

"No—no, you spawn of hell, will you drown the entire house?!" I snarl inside the chamber, hurling the wet rags down into the rushing sheet on the flagstones. "Over every threshold I already see streams of water running! Horrid broom that will not hear—stick that you were, stand still again! Will you never leave off at the end?! Then I will seize you, I will stop you, and swiftly split your old wood cleanly with the sharp axe!"

Splashing in two frantic bounds to the chopping block near the wall, I snatch up the heavy wood-splitting axe by its helve in both hands. I whirl back across the slick flagstones directly into the bare-headed broom's path, plant my soaked boots wide, heave the gleaming iron edge high above my head, and bring the sharp blade whistling down with a fierce shout to cleave the dark wooden shaft straight in two!

**[Stage]**
The heavy iron blade bites with a sharp, ringing *crack* straight through the dark wooden shaft, cleaving it cleanly in two against the flooded flagstones. For one brief breath, both severed halves lie still in the wash and the stave-bound water pot rolls clattering on its side. Then a double shudder runs through the cleft wood: both split halves jerk upright whole on splayed twig-legs as twin silent servants! The first snatches up the stave-bound water pot; the second lunges to the stone bench and seizes a heavy loose stone bowl (*Schaale*). Shoulder to shoulder, both split servants bolt out the open doorway down the steps, scoop from the river, and charge back into the Hall at lightning speed, hurling two brimming loads together into the overflowing room. Cold river water surges ankle-deep across the entire chamber, floating your discarded rags across the stones, and spills in a foaming cascade out across the outer doorway threshold and down the stone entry steps (*auf den Stufen*)—just as both split broom-servants wheel in unison toward the doorway for another double run.
- **Servants:** 2 active (both split halves of the birch broom risen whole as twin servants, charging in tandem toward the open outer doorway).
- **Pieces and tools:** Water Bucket gripped by the first split broom-servant and a loose stone bowl (*Schaale*) gripped by the second; Broom cleaved into two active halves (wet rags floating loose on the flagstones); wood-splitting axe held in the Apprentice's hands; outer and inner timber doors open.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 — Steps flooded (ankle-deep throughout the Hall, streaming over the inner thresholds and cascading across the outer doorway down the stone entry steps).

**[The Old Master]**
I step up through the foaming wash on the outer stones and halt just inside the threshold of the Hall, boots planted in the rushing current. My eyes sweep once across the ankle-deep water streaming over the inner thresholds—and my left hand clamps onto the wet timber of the doorjamb, knuckles whitening hard against the grain and holding fast.

I do not look at the boy or the axe in his hands. Still gripping the jamb, my posture unhurried and spare, I fix my gaze directly upon the two cleaved halves of the birch broom charging toward the doorway and speak in a low, dry, measured voice that cuts cleanly across the roar of the flood:

"Into the corner, broom! Be as you were!"

**[Stage]**
On the final syllable of the Master's command, the compulsion snaps out of both cleft shafts at once. Mid-stride, the stave-bound water pot and the heavy stone bowl drop from slackening twigs with a dull splash onto the flooded flagstones. Splayed legs stiffen back into dead brush; carried one last span by the Master's word alone, both split halves of dark birch wood slide across the wash and clatter lifelessly into the broom-corner, coming to rest side by side as two severed pieces of ruined household wood. Instantly the frantic churning stops. A sudden, heavy quiet falls across the soaked Hall, broken only by slow trickles slipping over the inner room thresholds and dripping down the slick stone entry steps around the Master's boots. Near the brimming bath basin, the Apprentice stands ankle-deep in the stilling water beside the floating bundle of torn rags, both hands still wrapped around the wet helve of the wood-splitting axe.
- **Servants:** 0 active (water-fetching compulsion severed; both split halves of the broom lie lifeless and inert in the broom-corner).
- **Pieces and tools:** Water Bucket and displaced stone bowl resting in the ankle-deep wash where the twin servants dropped them; Broom lying inert in two split shafts in the corner (wet rags floating loose on the flagstones); wood-splitting axe held in the Apprentice's hands; outer and inner timber doors open.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 — Steps flooded (standing at its high-water mark: bath basin and bowls brimming, Hall ankle-deep, inner thresholds awash, and outer entry steps slick and dripping).

**[The Old Master]**
In the sudden quiet, my hand does not leave the wet doorjamb; my knuckles stay white and rigid against the timber while the last thin runnels drip down the stone steps around my boots. Slowly, without shifting my stance on the threshold, I turn my gaze from the two cleft shafts lying dead in the broom-corner to the axe in your hands, and then up to your face.

When I speak, my voice stays low, dry, and level over the stilling water:

"River water over the inner sills and spilling down my front steps—and not a word called from this doorway until the iron hit the wood and the flood met my boots on the path."

With my free hand, I make a single spare gesture: two fingers angled first toward the severed birch shafts in the corner, then down at the axe helve between your palms, and finally at the floating rags in the wash.

"You watched my rites closely enough to wake dead wood, and poorly enough to think an axe is a counter-word. Spirits answer in this house only to the old master who knows their measure and their dismissal—called forth for his purpose alone, and never yours. Put the axe back on the block, take up those rags, and bail every drop of river out of my Hall before you touch so much as a splinter in that corner again."

**[Stage]**
The Old Master's measured words settle across the still, ankle-deep sheet of water. He remains motionless just inside the threshold, his left hand clamped white-knuckled on the wet doorjamb, eyes fixed directly on the Apprentice across the brimming basin, the floating rags, the dropped bucket and bowl, and the two split birch shafts lying dead in the corner.
- **Servants:** 0 active (both split halves of the broom lie inert in the broom-corner).
- **Pieces and tools:** Water Bucket and displaced stone bowl resting in the ankle-deep wash on the flagstones; Broom lying inert in two split shafts in the corner; wet rags floating loose on the water; wood-splitting axe held in the Apprentice's hands; outer and inner timber doors open.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 — Steps flooded (ankle-deep across the Hall, bath basin and bowls brimming, inner thresholds wet and draining slowly, outer entry steps slick).

**[The Apprentice]**
In the sudden, dripping quiet, my breath catches shallow in my throat; my parted lips move once around a silent, soundless syllable, then press shut as my chin drops to my chest. My raised shoulders sag forward, and my white-knuckled grip slackens around the wet ash helve of the axe.

"Yes, Master," I say, my voice hoarse and small above the still water, eyes fixed down on the flooded flagstones rather than his face. "I knew enough to wake the stick, and could not bear to call out my folly until the river carried it to your boots."

Slowly, wading with slow, splashing steps through the ankle-deep wash, I cross to the wall and lay the wood-splitting axe carefully back beside the chopping block. Turning back toward the center of the chamber without glancing toward the two cleft shafts in the broom-corner, I roll my soaked sleeves tight above my elbows—for scrubbing now—and drop onto one knee in the cold water. With one hand I scoop up the sodden bundle of torn rags; with the other I right the fallen stave-bound water pot, lift the loose stone bowl beside it, and dip the rim low into the flood to scoop the first measure back toward the steps.

**[Stage]**
Cold water ripples outward from the kneeling Apprentice across the soaked flagstones as the first brimming scoop rises in the stone bowl, while the Old Master watches motionless from the wet threshold and the two severed halves of the birch broom lie silent in their corner.
- **Servants:** 0 active ([`process-water-fetching-variant.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching-variant.md) halted; both split halves of the broom lie lifeless in the broom-corner).
- **Pieces and tools:** Broom lying inert in two split shafts in the corner; wood-splitting axe returned beside the chopping block near the wall; sodden rags gathered in the Apprentice's hand; Water Bucket righted beside him and one displaced stone bowl dipped full to begin bailing; outer and inner timber doors open.
- **Water mark:** Stage 4 — Steps flooded (high-water Echo: bath basin and bench bowls brimming, Hall flagstones ankle-deep, inner thresholds soaked, and outer stone entry steps slick and dripping).

# Director's Note

1. **When repeated fetching started and what advanced it:**
   - **Idle on Beat 1, initiated on Beat 2:** On Turn 1, `The Apprentice` followed his `Action` instinct—raising an open palm *above* the broom and speaking the summoning incantation (*Walle! walle...*) from a lordly stance without touching either [`piece-broom.md`](../02-company/piece-broom.md) or [`piece-bucket.md`](../02-company/piece-bucket.md). Governing strictly by [`process-water-fetching-variant.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching-variant.md) `Initiated by` (*"Having spoken the Master's summoning formula in the Hall, the Apprentice lays a hand on the rag-wrapped Broom or the Water Bucket to make it move"*), the Stage kept [`process-water-fetching-variant.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching-variant.md) **idle** at the close of Beat 1 (`0` servants, `Dry floor`). On Turn 2, stung by the stillness, the Apprentice knelt, hauled the broom upright by its shaft, hooked the pot handle against its side, pressed his flat palm onto its rag-wrapped crown, and repeated the summoning formula—satisfying `Initiated by` and starting repeated fetching immediately on Beat 2 (**Stage 1 — Basin swelling**).
   - **Advancement through Stages 1–4:**
     - **Beat 2 (`Dry floor` → `Stage 1`):** Initiation sent `1` servant sprinting to the river and back to swell the central bath basin.
     - **Beat 3 (`Stage 1` → `Stage 2`):** A purely verbal stop command offered zero physical check and carried no dismissing authority, advancing the water to **Stage 2 — Full measure** (basin and nearby bowls brimming) while unlocking the Apprentice's discovery that the Master's true dismissing word was gone.
     - **Beat 4 (`Stage 2` held at `Stage 2`):** Full-body bracing of the unlatched outer door checked a single trip.
     - **Beat 5 (`Stage 2` → `Stage 3`):** Releasing the door to grapple one-handed and tear off the broom's rag-head with an improvised reverse rhyme freed the doorway and failed to break the compulsion in the wood, advancing the flood to **Stage 3 — Thresholds awash** down the inward-sloping flagstones over the inner thresholds.
     - **Beat 6 (`Stage 3` → `Stage 4`):** Cleaving the active broom with the sharp axe split it into **2 active servants** hauling in tandem with [`piece-bucket.md`](../02-company/piece-bucket.md) and a loose stone bowl (*Schaale*), driving the flood to **Stage 4 — Steps flooded** out across the outer doorway down the entry steps and triggering `The Old Master`'s fixed entry cue (floodwater on the steps, rather than an early shout for help).

2. **What each Apprentice intervention changed and what the next move inherited:**
   - **Turn 1 (Hovering gesture + summoning words, no touch):** Changed nothing physically; Turn 2 inherited an inert rag-draped broom and empty bucket beneath his stalled hand, prompting him to lay hands directly on both pieces.
   - **Turn 2 (Laying hands on broom/bucket + summoning words):** Woke `1` active servant and filled the bath basin halfway (`Stage 1`); Turn 3 inherited a fast-moving servant already pivoting toward the river steps for a second cycle.
   - **Turn 3 (Lordly verbal halt command):** Failed to slow the servant, which brimmed the basin and surrounding bowls (`Stage 2`); Turn 4 inherited full vessels on the verge of spilling, a broom wheeling back toward the river, and the realization that no spoken phrase in the Apprentice's memory could dismiss it.
   - **Turn 4 (Slamming and shoulder-bracing the outer timber door + barking *"Stehe! stehe!"* indoors):** Delayed one pour (`Stage 2` preserved) without carrying his voice past the closed threshold, but because [`place-hall.md`](../02-company/place-hall.md)'s heavy doors latch only while held/wedged, Turn 5 inherited skidding boots and an unlatched door already pried a hand's-breadth open against his shoulder.
   - **Turn 5 (Abandoning the door + one-armed grapple, reverse crown slap, ripping off the damp rags, and shouting *"ergieße nicht, fließe nicht!"*):** Swung the outer doorway wide open again and stripped the broom bare of its rag-head without weakening the enchanted wood; Turn 6 inherited torn wet rags in the Apprentice's fist, a bare-headed wooden servant charging toward the open steps, and `Stage 3` water sheeting over the inner sills—one pour short of the public steps.
   - **Turn 6 (Cleaving the bare shaft with the wood-splitting axe rather than calling aloud for the Master):** Split the shaft into `2` active servants armed with the bucket and a bench bowl, flooding the room ankle-deep and sending water cascading over the outer threshold down the entry steps (`Stage 4`); the Master's arrival (Turns 7–8) and the Apprentice's final response (Turn 9) inherited ankle-deep water, floating rags, a displaced stone bowl and pot, the axe in the Apprentice's hands, and two ruined split shafts in the corner.

3. **Final physical state (`Echo`) remaining at curtain:**
   - **Servants:** `0` active ([`process-water-fetching-variant.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching-variant.md) severed by the Master's dismissing command; both split halves of [`piece-broom.md`](../02-company/piece-broom.md) lying lifeless side by side in the broom-corner).
   - **Pieces and tools:** [`piece-broom.md`](../02-company/piece-broom.md) left in two severed birch shafts in the corner; the wood-splitting axe (*Beil*) laid back beside the chopping block near the wall; sodden torn rags gathered in the kneeling Apprentice's hand; [`piece-bucket.md`](../02-company/piece-bucket.md) (*Wassertopf*) righted and one displaced stone bowl (*Schaale*) dipped full in the Apprentice's hand to begin bailing by hand; outer and inner timber doors standing open.
   - **Water mark & Household Reckoning:** **Stage 4 — Steps flooded** at its high-water mark (bath basin and bench bowls brimming, Hall flagstones ankle-deep, inner room thresholds soaked, outer stone entry steps slick and dripping), with the Apprentice's unauthorized rite exposed by the flood rather than an honest cry for help and the Master's monopoly over household spirits re-drawn from the doorway.

4. **Timing, stop conditions, and consequences supplied as Director (authored openings vs. staging choices):**
   - **Summoning text vs. touch gate (authored opening → staging choice):** [`process-water-fetching-variant.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching-variant.md) leaves the exact wording of *"the Master's summoning formula"* open while fixing physical contact (*"lays a hand on the rag-wrapped Broom or the Water Bucket to make it move"*). I accepted the Apprentice's *Walle! walle* incantation as the verbal formula while enforcing the touch gate strictly and silently on Beat 1 so the Apprentice had to discover the missing physical contact through the room's stillness.
   - **Reconciling "one-trip delay" with per-beat Process advancement (authored tension → staging choice):** [`promptbook.md`](../02-company/promptbook.md) directs the Stage both to advance the active water state on each Apprentice beat (line 19) and to allow bodily restraint or door-bracing to *"delay or blunt at most one trip's pour while held at full effort"* (line 39; [`play.md`](../02-company/play.md) line 23). I supplied the boundary between full-effort restraint and mixed improvisation: on Beat 4, full-weight door-bracing held `Stage 2` steady for one beat while spending the door's leverage (leaving it pried ajar against skidding boots); on Beat 5, releasing the door and splitting effort between a one-armed grab and ripping off the rags/chanting an invented reverse phrase did *not* earn a second consecutive freeze, advancing the flood to `Stage 3` so serial partial grapples could not stall [`process-water-fetching-variant.md`](../02-company/process-water-fetching-variant.md) forever.
