# Before the Notary's Bell

*A Play in One Scene*

## The Situation

It is two minutes before nine in the morning in the tiled outer waiting room of the Harbour Notary's Office in Altona. Cold rain streaks a high frosted window. On the wall beside an iron coal-stove, a pendulum regulator clock ticks toward the hour. Above the heavy inner door to **Room 3** hangs a brass **session bell** wired to the Notary's desk.

Two estranged siblings, **Maren Voss** and **Ivo Voss**, sit across a narrow oak table. Yesterday they buried their father, **Anselm Voss**, a master maker of marine chronometers. For two exhausting days through the Notary's clerk, they hammered out a **Final Mutual Settlement and Waiver** to divide a precarious estate without court audit:

- **Maren** takes sole title to the Altona chronometer workshop, its tools, and its Admiralty maintenance warrant.
- **Ivo** takes **four hundred marks** in liquid bank drafts—the exact sum needed to buy his navigator's share aboard the survey brig *Kestrel*, which sails on today's afternoon ebb.
- **Clause 4 (Clean Slate):** Both waive all prior claims, private debts, and unsubmitted family papers; any sealed private writing not entered before signing must be burned unread in the waiting-room stove before they step through the door.

When the **nine o'clock session bell** rings above Room 3, the clerk opens the inner door for immediate signature. Neither sibling has room to stall: if either refuses to sign at the bell or demands an accounting, Guild probate law places the entire estate into two-year Chancery sequestration. On the first of next month the workshop lease will lapse out from under Maren, and today's tide will carry the *Kestrel* out to sea without Ivo.

Moments before curtain-up, the night watchman clearing Anselm's bench delivered a heavy cream envelope found taped beneath the drawer of Anselm's dividing engine, logged at the outer desk in both their names, and set it on the oak table beside a brass paper-knife. Its red shellac seal is unbroken. Written across the face in Anselm's steady copperplate are three lines:

> *For Maren and Ivo together.*
> *Open this only if you both dare know the true account before the Notary's bell.*
> *Once you sign your peace, give this unread to the stove.*

Once the session bell rings, the window to open the letter closes: an unbroken letter must either go unread into the stove so they can sign Clause 4, or be impounded unread if the settlement collapses.

## Roles

### Maren Voss (38)
Master bench-hand of the Voss workshop. Twelve years ago, she stayed at the bench when Ivo left; for the last four years she ran every repair, payroll, and supplier account while Anselm's health failed.

- **How she carries herself:** Unsentimental, tired to the bone, exact with words and numbers, protective of work she kept alive with her own hands. She resents that Ivo vanished for six years and still walks out with nearly all the estate's cash.
- **Why she is drawn to open the letter:** A fortnight before dying, Anselm gripped her wrist and said, *"The ledger between you and your brother has never been straight."* She suspects Anselm finally put in writing why Ivo forfeited part of his claim—or left a final instruction she feels cowardly burning unread just to buy a quiet morning.
- **Why she fears opening it (private to Maren):** Eight months ago, when Anselm's surgeon fees and brass bills came due together, Maren secretly pawned the shop's prized **No. 14 Exhibition Chronometer** with old Lukas Brandt to keep the doors open. She has not yet redeemed it, though it remains listed on today's shop inventory. She fears Anselm found the pawn ticket under his bench; if the letter exposes the missing chronometer, Ivo can accuse her of falsifying the estate schedule and blow up the settlement—or strip her of the shop.

### Ivo Voss (33)
Hydrographic surveyor and coastal mate. Six years ago he walked out of Altona after a bitter public rupture with Anselm and did not set foot in the house again until the burial.

- **How he carries himself:** Salt-weathered, guarded, restless in closed rooms, quick to hear judgment in his sister's voice, hungry to get his bank draft signed and get clear of this town by afternoon.
- **Why he is drawn to open the letter:** Six years ago, Anselm threw him out before two Guild wardens after the master arc on an Admiralty quadrant was found miscut by three minutes of arc, blaming Ivo's carelessness. Ivo swore he set the vernier right, though six years of exile have left a splinter of doubt even in him. Burning the last words his father ever wrote—without knowing whether Anselm recanted or cursed him—feels like carrying the accusation to his grave.
- **Why he fears opening it (private to Ivo):** Two winters ago, laid up with marsh fever in Lisbon without a berth, Ivo wrote secretly to Anselm begging for fifty marks and promised in writing never to ask another pfennig from the Altona estate if Anselm wired it. Anselm wired the money and never told Maren. Ivo fears Anselm enclosed or cited that pledge; if Maren reads that Ivo already signed away his inheritance for fifty marks, she will never hand over four hundred today, and the *Kestrel* sails without him.

## What Is Fixed at the Opening

1. **The Room and Props:** Only Maren and Ivo are in the waiting room. On the oak table lie the **sealed envelope** (red shellac wax intact) and a **brass paper-knife**. Beside the wall stands the **iron coal-stove** (with a slotted ash-door showing live coals) and the **ticking regulator clock**. Above the closed door to Room 3 hangs the **brass session bell**.
2. **Shared History and Stakes:** Both siblings know the terms of the Settlement and Waiver, the consequences of Chancery sequestration if either balks at the bell, the public quarrel over the miscut Admiralty quadrant six years ago, and the three-line inscription on the front of the envelope.
3. **Private Secrets and Blindness:** Maren alone knows about the pawned No. 14 Chronometer. Ivo alone knows about the fifty marks in Lisbon and the promise attached to it. **Neither sibling knows what is written inside the sealed envelope** unless and until the red wax is broken before the bell.
4. **Why Neither Can Open or Burn It Unchallenged:** Because the watchman logged the enclosure in both names and they sit arm's length apart across a narrow table, any unconsented move to slice the wax or feed the envelope to the coals happens in plain sight—giving the other sibling an immediate chance to stay their hand, strike terms, or let it happen.
5. **Adjustable Opening Conditions (set before curtain-up, or played with defaults):**
   - **Where the letter starts:** On the table between them *(default)*, in Maren's hand, or in Ivo's hand.
   - **How close the clock stands to nine:** Tight, standard *(default)*, or extended countdown to the session bell.
   - **Initial tilt:** Unfixed *(default)*, or a specified starting lean for either sibling toward opening or leaving the wax unbroken.

## What the Performers Can Decide

- **What to reveal or conceal:** Whether to confess their own secret (the pawned chronometer or the Lisbon draft) to build trust or bargain, keep it hidden, bluff, or probe what the other is hiding.
- **What pact to offer:** Whether to propose conditions before touching the wax (for instance, swearing to sign regardless of what Anselm wrote, trading assurances, or daring the other to take responsibility for breaking the seal).
- **The fate of the letter before the bell:**
  - **Open it** before the session bell rings—and face what Anselm actually wrote in the moments left before the door opens.
  - **Burn it unread** in the iron coal-stove before the bell, locking in ignorance by choice.
  - **Hold the standoff** until the session bell cuts off the chance to open it.
- **The threshold choice at the bell:** Once the brass bell rings and Room 3 opens, whether each sibling walks inside to sign the peace (and, if the letter is still sealed on the table, drops it unread into the stove as Clause 4 requires) or refuses the settlement and accepts ruin.

## The Scene

### Opening Image
Rain rattles against the high glass. The wall regulator clicks steadily toward nine. Between Maren and Ivo—or in the hand set at curtain-up—lies Anselm Voss's sealed cream envelope beside the brass paper-knife, coals glowing dull red behind the slot of the iron stove. Neither has spoken since the watchman closed the outer door.

### Course of Action and Stage Cues
1. **Reckoning Across the Table:** Maren and Ivo confront the envelope, each other, and the narrowing minutes before nine. Physical handling of the envelope, the brass paper-knife, and distance from the stove or inner door are live action in the room.
2. **The Warning Chime:** Shortly before nine, the wall regulator gives a crisp mechanical click and strikes **one dry warning chime**—signaling that the Notary's session bell is moments away.
3. **Irreversible Acts Before the Bell:**
   - **If the red wax seal is broken before the bell:** The heavy cream sheet unfolds, and both siblings read Anselm's words on the spot (see **The Sealed Letter** below). The wax cannot be mended; whatever time remains before the bell belongs to the fallout.
   - **If the unopened envelope is pushed through the stove's coal-slot:** Dry paper catches at once on the embers and curls to ash unread. The letter is gone forever, bringing on the Notary's bell.
4. **The Notary's Bell (Climax and Curtain):**
   Sharp and metallic, the **brass session bell** rings above the lintel. The latch of Room 3 clicks open; warm lamplight spills across the wet tiles as the clerk's voice calls from within: *"Voss estate—Room Three."*
   - If the seal has **not** been broken when the bell sounds, it can no longer be opened.
   - At the open threshold, Maren and Ivo each make one final move—disposing of the letter (into the coals, into a coat pocket, or left on the oak table) and either stepping into Room 3 to sign or turning toward the street door.

*Blackout.*

## The Sealed Letter

*(Strictly withheld at curtain-up. Revealed on stage only if and when the red shellac seal is broken before the Notary's bell rings.)*

> *Maren. Ivo.*
>
> *If you broke my wax, neither of you trusted the peace you made without me. Good. Better an ugly truth in the anteroom than a clean lie in front of the Notary.*
>
> *First, clear your heads of small terrors:*
>
> *Ivo — I burned your Lisbon letter the week I wired the fifty marks. A father keeping his son alive in a foreign fever-ward is not an estate advance. You owe your sister nothing for Lisbon.*
>
> *Maren — Lukas Brandt came to me in January as an old friend should. I redeemed the No. 14 Exhibition Chronometer myself out of my burial tin three weeks ago. It sits in the Notary's strongbox right now, free and clear, to be handed to you once the deed is signed. You kept my house warm when I could no longer see the escapement. Stop hiding your hands.*
>
> *Now the heavy weight, which is mine:*
>
> *Six years ago, Ivo did not miscut the Admiralty quadrant. My left eye clouded over on the vernier scale the night before inspection, and when the wardens spotted the three-minute error, I let my son take blame for a ruined arc rather than confess that Anselm Voss was going blind and watch the Guild strip this shop of its Admiralty warrant.*
>
> *Maren kept a blind man's bench standing without knowing why her brother hated the house. Ivo carried an exile he never earned so the roof over you both would hold.*
>
> *Sign the settlement. Let Maren have the shop she saved, and let Ivo have the sea I drove him to. Or tear the deed in two if you still prefer your grievances to each other. The bell will not wait on dead men.*
>
> *— A. V.*
