# Mother's Painting — combined Promptbook, run B

Revision `a0cc75f506171cea52d6349b3e9fe9430b755650`

**Human.** The Human sets the opening conditions once, then watches from outside the scene.

**Collaboration.** When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move.

## Performance

Opening and transition cues are in block quotes. The text below each cue is the verbatim performer output in order.

### The flat

> The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.

*Ada folds her cuff into a hard square.* “Leave the buzzer. I’ll take the painting. I was the last one to hang it for her.”

*Dan lifts one heel and holds it there.* “You’ve decided, then. I told Mum I’d fix that corner. I’m taking it home.”

*Ada crouches and puts her palm under the loose corner.* “You can mend it at mine. I’ve already cleared a place for it.”

*Dan sets his heel down.* “I said I’d take it home. You clearing a space doesn’t make it yours.”

### The entrance

> The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. The four siblings are at the entrance. The painting is still upstairs against the skirting board in the flat and no one has taken it.

*Ada takes her hand off the propped door.* “All right, Dan. Come back up with me. Show me how you want to carry it with that corner loose.”

*Dan puts one foot on the bottom stair.* “I’ll take the weight. You keep that corner from opening. We can argue about whose car when we get back down.”

*Ben catches his breath on Dan’s name.* “Dan, bring it down. Before it goes in a car, each of us gets a turn to say why we want it.”

*Cora’s fingers go still around her keys.* “Mine’s the car with the big boot, if that’s any help. Mum sent me a photo of it when I moved out. I wouldn’t mind having the full-size one for a change.”

*Ben looks up the stairs.* “Put me down for a turn, too. When I couldn’t sleep, Mum used to sit with me at that window.”

## Director notes

I read the pinned Director skill and the pinned company README. I used the assigned Promptbook verbatim. The Human supplied the opening conditions once and remained outside the action. The entrance used the fixture's second opening and the state established in the flat. I staged the siblings at the entrance between scenes. Ada's hand under the frame never lifted it and nobody else moved or acquired it. Its location and the open corner carried over, as did Ada's and Dan's public claims, Ada's reported place for the painting, and Dan's publicly stated promise to fix the corner. The buzzer was unanswered and its source stayed unknown. No car was assigned by the opening. Cora later introduced her own car in dialogue without establishing that it was the idling one.

### Performer contexts

Four separate performers began with empty inherited histories. Each got the shared premise, the whole Piece, the two Promptbook instructions as quoted above, and only their own Persona and private memory. The common acting directions were to use no tools or outside sources, return one brief in-character action and/or line, and write only their own sibling's response. Cues said whether it was their first or next move. I gave only the opening and observable action or dialogue with each cue. Ben and Cora were present in the fiction in the flat and received its public transcript at their first cues.

| Performer | Private brief | Observable material supplied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ada | Eldest, steady and dependable. Assigns the work and gets on with her own part. Feels she belongs only when she carries the burden. Folds her cuff into a hard square. She last hung the painting for Mother. | Flat opening for her first move. Dan's first move on return. The entrance opening, painting location and custody, and Dan's last flat move at the transition. |
| Dan | Youngest, blunt about family rituals. Says what everyone else is avoiding. A decision made in his absence feels like proof he does not count. Lifts his heel and holds it off the floor. He kept putting off Mother's request to fix the frame. | Flat opening and Ada's first move. Ada's second move on return. The entrance opening, painting location and custody, and Ada's first entrance move at the transition. |
| Ben | Fair and practical. Puts a difficult choice under a common rule. Calls a rule fair when it saves him from asking for something himself. Catches his breath on ordinary names. He sat with Mother at that window when he could not sleep. | Both openings, the entire flat scene, the carried painting state, and Ada's and Dan's entrance moves for his first cue. Cora's entrance move on return. |
| Cora | Light and quick to joke. Makes an offer sideways as if it costs her nothing. Would rather be taken for joking than risk refusal. Her fingers go suddenly still. Mother sent her a photograph of the painting when she first moved away. | Both openings, the entire flat scene, the carried painting state, and all three entrance moves before her cue. |

The actors retained their own original contexts on every return. No performer was evicted or recast. The Director disclosed others' memories only as spoken in the scene. Dan disclosed the repair promise and kept his delay private.

### Handoffs and rests

| After | Cue or rest | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat opening | Ada | I used the eldest's authored tendency to take charge to start, where no claim-affecting move yet governed succession. |
| Ada claims it because she last hung it | Dan | The proposed unilateral decision affected all three siblings. I chose Dan because her rationale privileged prior care, and his private connection to the unfinished repair made that basis press hardest on his claim. His private reason stayed with him. |
| Dan says he will take it home | Ada | He directly displaced Ada's asserted destination and challenged the way she decided. |
| Ada proposes repair at her home | Dan | Her proposal incorporated his work while placing the painting in her custody. |
| Dan rejects Ada's planned destination | Rest at the flat. Ada gets the next move at the entrance. | Dan directly disputed Ada's rationale. I cut while that challenge still stood, keeping her next move across the scene change. |
| Ada asks Dan to show her how he wants to carry it | Dan | The invitation gave him a say in handling the painting after she had assigned the repair to her home. |
| Dan proposes that he and Ada carry it and settle the car later | Ben | The two carriers would gain practical influence before Ben or Cora had spoken. The move affected them both. I cued Ben because the decision about which car could stand in for a decision about the claim, a pressure on his role of putting choices under a common rule. |
| Ben proposes that each sibling say why they want it before it goes into a car | Cora | The proposal explicitly gave Cora a chance to state a claim while the other two had already argued for theirs. Ben had just asserted his place in the decision by offering the rule. |
| Cora offers her car and asks for the painting | Ben | She both used his proposed procedure and offered a destination before the others had taken it up. That put his terms and his own still-unspoken claim in play. |
| Ben states his interest and gives his reason | Rest at the entrance | Each sibling had voiced a claim. Nobody had brought down the painting, agreed who would take it, or agreed to Ben's procedure. I ended on those unresolved choices. |

The material leaves the initial cue, ties in whose claim is most affected, and the moment to rest open. I made those staging judgments. Ben's talk of turns was his own proposal inside the scene. I did not schedule a round or use the buzzer or car to impose an outcome.
