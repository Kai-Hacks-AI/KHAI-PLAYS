# *Mother's Painting* — Two-Scene Performance

## Scene 1: *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Ada:** *Steps across the bare floorboards toward the skirting board, stooping to gather a loose sheet of packing paper beside the painting.* Ben, get the buzzer—that'll be the agent for the meter readings. Dan, check the airing cupboard one last time while I wrap this up for my boot; the mitre on this top corner's still split and it won't survive rattling loose.

**Ben:** *Crosses to the wall handset by the front door, thumbs the entry buzzer to let them in, and turns back toward Ada with an open palm.* Hold on, Ada—don't wrap that yet. Everything else went by the list we agreed, and since none of us put a name on the frame beforehand, the only fair way is tearing four strips off that paper and drawing for it before it goes near anyone's boot.

**Cora:** *Strolls over with her keys swinging around one forefinger, grinning between Ben and Ada.* Oh, spare us a tombola in an empty hallway, Ben—neither of you actually wants a wonky kitchen window shedding gilt on your upholstery. Tell you what, I'll save the peace, chuck it on my back seat, and buy everyone a round with the nonexistent fortune it's worth.

**Dan:** *Stays planted instead of moving toward the airing cupboard, stepping squarely up to the skirting board and looking between the three of them.* Nobody's checking the airing cupboard, Ada, and nobody's tearing up packing paper or chucking it on Cora's back seat like it's a chore. All four of us have been circling this wall all afternoon waiting for the movers to clear out because every single one of us wants it—and I'm taking it home and clamping that corner properly.

**Ada:** *Straightens up with the packing paper still in one hand, sets it down beside the frame, and folds her left cuff into a neat, hard square.* That corner's been open through three moves, Dan—if you glue and clamp old pine now you'll warp the canvas, which is why you always have to take the weight on two picture hooks from the back wire instead of the top rail. *Looks evenly from Dan to Ben and Cora.* Somebody's coming up the stairs right now for the final handover, and I'm the one signing the inventory and keeping the probate file together—leave the frame with me where it'll be hung properly, and let's get the keys ready.

**Ben:** *Steps in to pick up the sheet of packing paper Ada set beside the skirting board, smoothing its crease flat between both hands.* Signing the meters doesn't give the executor dibs on something with zero probate value, *—catches his breath briefly—* Ada, and nobody settles a split just by staking the loudest claim either, Dan. When the handover's already on the stairs, the only fair rule is that nobody takes ownership today: it goes into neutral keeping with someone who hasn't claimed it for themselves until we agree a proper rota, so I'll wrap it and hold it flat at mine tonight.

**Cora:** *Lets out a bright laugh and nudges the edge of Ben's packing paper with one knuckle.* "Neutral keeping" in your hallway is still your hallway, Switzerland—you're not fooling anyone with the sacrificial custodian act. Look, let me cart it back to mine for now so none of you three have to glare at it over Sunday lunch, and call me whenever you've drafted the custody treaty.

**Dan:** *Crouches beside the skirting board and sets one hand firmly on the unbroken side of the frame, looking up at the three of them.* Stop talking about rotas and Sunday lunches—once Ada hands over the keys, there isn't a Sunday lunch left to go to, and whoever carries this down the stairs keeps it for good. You don't know half as much as you think about why that corner's still open, Ada, and it isn't going into Ben's hallway or anybody else's boot; it's coming home with me.

*(No footsteps have come up the stairs since Ben buzzed the street door.)*

**Ada:** *Glances toward the silent landing behind Ben, then steps closer to the skirting board and rests her fingers lightly over the top rail above the split mitre.* Nobody's come up the stairs, Ben—the street latch sticks in the damp; go down and prop it before whoever rang drives off. *Looks straight down at Dan.* And don't tell me what I don't know about this frame, Dan—I was the one standing on a kitchen chair putting the two brass hooks in that plaster behind you the day she moved in here, while the rest of you were still parking, and I'm not handing over the keys just to walk out empty-handed.

**Ben:** *Crouches just short of Dan to spread the packing paper flat on the floorboards, motioning firmly toward Ada's fingers on the top rail.* I'm not walking down two flights alone while two of you have your hands on a split frame, Ada, and putting brass hooks up years ago doesn't invent a new rule after the van's gone. Both of you let go of the bare pine before that corner gives way—we fold the paper round it right here, all four of us go down together to catch the street latch, and I'll carry it down flat until the keys are signed over and we draw for it properly.

## Scene 2: *The entrance*

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.*

*Carried forward from Scene 1: The empty flat upstairs is shut; all four siblings have come down the two flights together so no one was left alone with the painting. Ada still holds the flat keys. Ben holds the painting loosely folded in the sheet of packing paper just inside the propped-open entrance. No one has agreed to Ben's draw-lots rule or surrendered their claim to the painting.*

**Cora:** *Leans against the doorframe by the propped-open door, glancing out at the car idling at the kerb before reaching lightly for the parcel in Ben's hands.* Whoever's burning diesel out there clearly isn't staying for your raffle, Ben, and we look like four bailiffs hovering in the lobby. Tell you what—pass me the wonky window right now and you three can split the whole box sitting in my boot; saves me unpacking at the other end, and Ada gets to march out to the kerb looking official.

**Dan:** *Steps squarely in front of Cora's reaching hand, eyeing the parcel in Ben's grip before jerking his chin toward the idling car at the kerb.* For a "wonky window" you pretend not to care about, Cora, you just offered your whole box for it—because we all know the stuff in our boots doesn't mean a thing. And Ada won't even walk five yards to that car with the keys because none of us trusts the other three alone in this doorway for ten seconds. Hand it over, Ben—I've got the clamps at home to put that split corner right, and once it's settled Ada can go sign the flat away.

**Ada:** *Pauses with the flat keys pressed in her palm, folds her right cuff into a hard square, and steps beside Ben to slide her free hand firmly under the bottom edge of the wrapped frame.* Cora, walk the five yards to the kerb and check whether that's even the letting agent or a minicab for next door. I've spent six weeks handling the hospital, the clearance, and every signature in this building while the rest of you turned up for the last hour, Dan—I'm not marching out to hand over the keys like hired help while you three divvy up the only thing of hers left to look after. Let go of the top rail, Ben; you're pinching right on the split mitre, and it's going flat in my boot before I sign anything away.

**Ben:** *Shifts both hands cleanly off the top rail onto the two solid side stiles, keeping the split corner clear without yielding any of the weight to Ada's hand underneath.* My grip's on the sound wood, Ada, and keeping score of signatures doesn't give you first pick any more than Cora's boot box or Dan's clamps. Nobody gets to invent a private claim just by pressing hardest at the door. Cora, tear four uneven strips off the spare edge of this paper right now—longest strip takes it home for good, and then the keys can go out to the kerb with it settled fairly.

**Cora:** *Pinches the spare corner of Ben's packing paper as if to tear it, then her fingers go suddenly still against the brown edge before she drops her hand with a quick, bright laugh.* Relax, Dan, my boot box is four chipped mugs and a toaster—I was trying to dump my recycling on you, not confess a grand passion. Save the paper, Ben: let Ada go deal with the kerb before they bill the estate for waiting, and I'll gladly cart the crooked thing back out of town with me and send you three a photograph of it on the wall.

*(Outside at the kerb, the idling car gives a single short tap of its horn.)*

**Dan:** *Steps onto the threshold between them and the propped-open door, his right heel lifting off the tiles and staying held off the floor as he puts a flat palm out toward the parcel between Ben and Ada.* Nobody rang me while the two of you spent six weeks drawing up lists and leaving Cora four chipped mugs, Ada—you only wanted me here for the last hour to shift boxes and stay quiet while you decided what counts without me. Cora couldn't even bring herself to tear your paper strip, Ben, because none of us is gambling the last piece of Mother's flat on a raffle while that horn's going at the kerb. Walk out and hand over the keys, Ada, and pass me the frame—she asked me to mend that split corner, and I'm taking it home tonight to finally put it right.

**Ben:** *Draws the upper edge of the wrapped frame slightly back from Dan's outstretched palm while keeping both hands locked on the solid side stiles above Ada's grip.* Nobody shut you out of the lists, *—catches his breath short—* Dan, and she hung that window with the corner open in every flat she lived in—you don't get to claim it on a promise nobody else heard, any more than I'm asking to keep it for knowing that sill at three in the morning. If none of us will risk a draw while that car's honking at the kerb, then nobody takes sole ownership: we put a three-month rota on the back of the handover slip when Ada signs at the window, and it goes flat in my boot for the first stretch where nobody's clamping the pine or tossing it in with chipped mugs.

**Ada:** *Slips the keyring onto her little finger so she can bring both hands firmly onto the lower stiles of the wrapped frame below Ben's grip.* Cora, step out to the pavement and tell whoever just tapped that horn to wait sixty seconds—nobody's signing the flat away while we're deadlocked in the doorway. I didn't keep the last six weeks off your plate to shut you out, Dan; I carried it myself so the rest of you wouldn't have to, the same way I hung this frame for her the last time upstairs with that corner left open before a single box was unpacked. And we all know a three-month rota starting in your boot ends on your wall, Ben—let go of the stiles so I can lay it flat in mine, and then I'll walk straight to the kerb and finish the handover.

**Cora:** *Stays planted inside the doorway instead of stepping out to the pavement, tapping one finger lightly on the brown paper right between Ben's grip and Ada's.* I'm not going out there to mime at a windscreen while you two pull it in half like a Christmas cracker, Ada—and flatter yourself less, Dan, I only spared Ben's paper so he wouldn't sulk when he drew short. If neither of you trusts the other with the first leg of Ben's rota, dump the opening stint on me and my four chipped mugs: let me chuck it on my back seat right now so the keys can go to the kerb, and if I'm not sick of staring at a draughty sill by Christmas you three can drive out and repossess it.

**Dan:** *Steps in off the threshold and cups one hand just outside the loose brown paper around the split top corner without pulling, looking flatly from Ben to Ada.* "Carrying it yourself" is still deciding without me, Ada—and she didn't hang it broken because she liked the gap, Ben; that corner was still waiting on my clamps. Look at the pair of you with four hands locked on dry pine while that car idles at the kerb, pretending we'll swap walls every three months or drive out to Cora's at Christmas when we all know nothing brings us back into one room once those keys leave Ada's finger. Let go of the stiles before you pull the last piece of her flat in half—let me take it home, and go finish the handover.

# Director's Note

### What I Varied or Directed
- **Isolated Performer Direction:** Each sibling ran in a separate read-only sub-context given only the shared premise, Piece, scene openings, their own Persona row, and their own private memory sentence. I directed performers to play strictly observable physical action and concise speech (no internal narration), letting their Shadows, physical Tells, and private memories emerge only when pressed rather than announcing them upfront.
- **Responsive Cueing:** Instead of a mechanical round-robin, I cued whichever sibling had just been directly ordered, exposed, or physically crowded, while balancing the floor (each sibling took 5 turns across the 20-turn run; Cora opened Scene 2 after watching the three-way grip form at the end of Scene 1).
- **Open Conditions & Pressure:** Keeping the authored Play fixed, I kept whoever rang the entry buzzer and whoever waited in the idling car offstage so no unauthored fifth persona entered the space, and supplied two minimal external pressures accommodated by the openings:
  1. Before Scene 1 Turn 9, noting that *no footsteps had come up the stairs* since Ben buzzed the street door in Turn 2 (prompting Ada to deduce a sticking street latch and push the group downstairs).
  2. Before Scene 2 Turn 6, a single short tap of the horn from the idling car at the kerb to tighten the threshold deadlock.

### What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **In *The flat*:** Ada immediately folded claiming the painting into assigning the final chores; Ben blocked her with a "fair" four-strip paper draw; Cora deflected both with a sideways offer to toss the "wonky kitchen window" onto her back seat; and Dan blew up all three pretexts by naming that every one of them had been circling the wall all afternoon. Pressed by Dan, Ada revealed hanging the painting on two brass hooks and refused to leave empty-handed after carrying the clearance, while Ben shifted to "neutral keeping" and spread the packing paper so all four would go downstairs together rather than leave two siblings gripping a split frame upstairs.
- **Carried into *The entrance*:** Mother's flat shut upstairs; the flat keys in Ada's palm; the painting loosely folded in packing paper in Ben's hands just inside the propped-open front door with all three siblings beside him because none trusted the others alone with it for ten seconds; Ben's draw unaccepted; and Cora's, Dan's, and Ben's private memories still unspoken.
- **In *The entrance*:** Cora raised her sideways bid to trading away her entire boot box, which Dan immediately exposed. Ordered by Ben to tear the lottery strips, Cora's fingers froze on the paper—her Tell—before she laughed it off and offered sideways to send them a photograph of it on her wall out of town. Under the horn tap at the kerb, Dan's heel lifted and stayed off the floor as he accused Ada and Ben of shutting him out for six weeks and admitted Mother had asked him to mend the split corner. With his raffle dead, Ben caught his breath and let slip "knowing that sill at three in the morning" while retreating into a three-month rota starting in his own boot. The Play closed in a four-way physical knot at the open doorway: Ben gripping the upper stiles, Ada with the keyring on her little finger gripping the lower stiles, Cora tapping the paper between them, and Dan cupping a hand protectively outside the split corner without pulling—naming that once the keys leave Ada's finger, no rota will ever bring them back into one room.

### Supplied Material & Promptbook Assessment
- **Promptbook (`promptbook.md`):** Empty (0 bytes); it supplied no additional rules and had no occasion to constrain or alter staging.
- **Where `company.md` Helped:** The Piece design (*frame open at one corner*, *no resale value*, *Load Bearing: nothing left in the flat to carry private memories*) interlocked tightly with the four asymmetric memories and Shadows. Every sibling's private memory attached to a concrete facet of the same object (hanging wire/hooks, split mitre waiting on clamps, 3 a.m. kitchen window sill, mailed photograph), allowing subtext and Tells to surface organically without melodrama.
- **Staging Friction (Play Gap vs. Staging Choice):**
  - *Offstage buzzer/car identity (open condition handled via staging choice):* When Ben buzzed the street door in Turn 2 without speaking into the intercom, Ada verbally assumed someone was climbing the stairs. Clarifying via a minimal stage note that no footsteps came up prevented performers from inventing contradictory offstage visitors and linked Scene 1's buzzer cleanly to Scene 2's propped door and idling car.
  - *Scene-break physical transition (structural friction in sequential staging):* Scene 1 hit its natural exit proposal on Turn 10 with Ben proposing to fold the paper around the painting and carry it downstairs together before Ada or Dan could verbally consent or refuse. Carrying forward the physical descent into Scene 2 while explicitly preserving that *no one had agreed to Ben's rule or yielded their claim* worked cleanly as a Director continuity bridge, though in isolated turn-by-turn staging any scene transition immediately following a multi-character physical proposal requires explicit state framing from the Director so performers share one exact physical reality at the top of Scene 2.
