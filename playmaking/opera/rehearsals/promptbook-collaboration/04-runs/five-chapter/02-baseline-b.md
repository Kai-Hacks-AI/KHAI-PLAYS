# Performance: *Mother's Painting*

## Scene 1 — *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Ada:** *Crosses to the skirting board with a folded dust sheet under her arm, glancing once toward the entryphone by the door.* Ben, get the buzzer—that'll be the landlord's agent for the meter readings. Dan, check the bathroom cupboard one last time while I wrap this up and get it down to my boot.

**Ben:** *Stops by the door with his hand resting on the entryphone without pressing the release, looking straight at the dust sheet under her arm.* Hang on—*he catches his breath slightly*—Ada, don't wrap that yet. Nothing else went out to anyone's boot without all four of us putting it on the list first, so before I buzz the agent up, we settle this one under the same rule as everything else.

**Cora:** *Steps between Ada and the skirting board, twirling a loose roll of masking tape around two fingers with an easy grin.* Oh, relax, you two—we don't need a parliamentary committee for a wonky frame held together by prayer. Tell you what, Ada, save your boot space and let me lug it home on the train; you can have my share of the good saucepans, or I'll buy dinner before we all stop speaking to each other.

**Dan:** *Stays planted where he is, lifting his right heel off the floorboards and holding it there as he looks straight at Ada.* I checked the bathroom twenty minutes ago, Ada—don't send me out of the room so you can walk off with it. Everybody's been stepping around that painting all afternoon pretending we forgot it, and all four of us want it. And Cora, if you drag it onto the train with that corner split open, the glass'll slide straight out before you hit the platform—I'm taking it home in the van and clamping the frame properly.

*The entry buzzer rings a second time, longer and sharper.*

**Ada:** *Stops short of Cora, sets the dust sheet over her forearm, and folds her left cuff into a neat, hard square.* There isn't any glass in it, Dan—it's oil on board, and that top corner's been open since I hung it on the chimney breast for her the day she moved in here. I haven't put my name against the saucepans, or the dresser, or a single box that went down those stairs today, because I was busy getting this flat cleared on time. Ben, press the release and tell him two minutes on the landing, because I'm not auctioning Mother's kitchen window on the doorstep while the agent's standing in the street.

**Ben:** *Presses the entryphone release, speaks briskly into the handset—"Second floor, give us two minutes on the landing"—and clicks it back into the cradle before pulling a folded envelope from his pocket and tearing it into four even strips.* Passing on the saucepans was your own choice, Ada, it doesn't buy you automatic rights to the last thing in the flat, and—*his breath catches slightly*—Cora isn't taking bare oil board with an open corner onto a train. If all four of us want it, we put it under one fair rule: a year in each house, and we draw right now off the mantelpiece for who takes it home tonight.

**Cora:** *Her fingers go suddenly still on the roll of masking tape for a beat before she tosses it lightly into her other palm and laughs.* God, listen to us—custody swaps and paper straws like it's a prize greyhound, when I'm perfectly happy to spring for a cab if the train offends everyone's conservation standards. Tell you what, Ben, save the envelope: lend me Ada's dust sheet so I can take the first turn tonight, and the three of you can draw lots over who comes to rescue it from my dreadful flat next November.

*Footsteps climb the stairwell outside and come to a stop on the second-floor landing beyond the flat door.*

**Dan:** *Drops his heel flat with a sharp thud and crouches beside the skirting board, putting two fingers lightly against the split mitre at the top corner.* All right, fine, it's board—because every time she asked me to bring my mitre clamp round and glue this corner, I told her next weekend until there wasn't one. Stop pretending "a year in each house" is anything other than a polite lie, Ben—once that painting goes on somebody's wall tonight, nobody's ever handing it over in November, and we all know it. Either we say out loud it's for keeps, or nobody touches those paper strips while I glue what she actually asked me to fix and take it home in my van.

*Two brisk knocks sound on the flat door.*

**Ada:** *Sets the tagged key ring on the entryphone shelf beside Ben, then kneels opposite Dan and spreads the dust sheet flat across the floorboards in front of the painting.* Put the paper away, Ben, and go take the meter numbers with him on the landing before he walks in on us. Dan's right about one thing—whoever walks out with this tonight keeps it, because none of you will ever organize handing it on. When you didn't show up with your clamp, Dan, I was the one up on a chair at that chimney breast pinching the split corner shut with my thumb until she said it hung straight, and I'm taking it home with me tonight.

**Ben:** *Leaves the keys untouched on the shelf and calls once toward the closed door.* Give us one minute! *He steps straight onto the edge of Ada's spread dust sheet, nicks the bottom corner off one paper strip, and holds all four level tops out in his closed fist right over the painting.* Fine—*his breath catches slightly*—Dan, it's for keeps then, and I'm not going out to the meters so someone can wrap it behind my back. Pinching the frame on a chair doesn't make it yours, Ada, any more than sitting at that kitchen window with her when I couldn't sleep makes it mine—so all four of us draw right now, and whoever pulls the notched strip walks out with it.

*Through the flat door, the agent calls in from the landing: "Sorry to press you—I've got another appointment at half past, I really need the keys and the flat clear now."*

**Cora:** *Reaches a hand out toward the paper strips in Ben's fist, her fingers going suddenly still an inch short of them before she pulls back with a quick laugh and scoops the tagged keys off the shelf.* Oh, lovely, we're trading tragic credentials while poor Mr. Half-Past camps on the stairs—well, you three had the chair and the late-night tea, whereas when I first moved away she posted me a photograph of that painting just so I'd have the window on my wall. Tell you what, put the raffle away: wrap the real thing up for me tonight, and I'll post you lot her photograph to fight over while I go charm the agent off the landing.

**Dan:** *Presses his thumb firm across the split top mitre to hold the corner shut, lifts the painting clear of the skirting board against his chest, and rises with his right heel held just off the floorboards.* Look at your fist, Ben—Ada won't touch it and Cora pulled her hand back an inch short, because none of us is letting a torn envelope leave us walking out of this empty flat with nothing of Mum's left. Open the door and give the man his keys, Cora; this place isn't hers anymore, and I'm carrying this down to my van right in front of all three of you so nobody gets shut out of the room while it's decided.

## Scene 2 — *The entrance*

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.*

*(Carried forward: Upstairs, the keys have been handed to the agent and the flat is closed behind them. Down at the propped-open entrance, Dan holds the unwrapped painting against his chest, his thumb pressed over the split top corner; Ada carries her folded dust sheet; Ben still holds the four torn envelope strips. At the kerb, the cab that just dropped off the half-past viewer idles with its engine running near Ada's car and Dan's van. Nothing is settled about who takes the painting home.)*

**Cora:** *Steps onto the threshold between Dan and the pavement, lifting two casual fingers toward the idling cab at the kerb before turning back with a bright grin.* Well, providence sent me a warm taxi right on cue, so nobody gets to use the train excuse twice. Tell you what, Dan—tuck the painting in Ada's dust sheet and pop it on my back seat before he pulls away, and you can bring your famous mitre clamp up to mine on Saturday to glue that corner properly while I feed all three of you and hand over the photo she sent me.

**Ada:** *Folds her right cuff into the same hard square as her left, then steps onto the threshold beside Dan and shakes the dust sheet open between the painting and the street.* Get in the cab while you've got one, Cora—you haven't cooked for the four of us once since you left home, and that frame isn't rattling across town on a back seat. Dan, your coat zip's an inch off bare oil, so lay it into this sheet and slide it flat in my boot right now. You can follow me back to mine in the van and clamp that corner on my kitchen table tonight, because my wall is the only place this painting stays in one piece and the only house all three of you actually know the way to.

**Ben:** *Pockets the four untouched paper strips, steps onto the threshold on Dan's other side, and slips his forearm between Dan's coat zip and the bare oil board while setting his other hand firm under the bottom rail of the frame.* Calling your house the only one that counts isn't a rule—*his breath catches slightly*—Ada, it's just pulling rank, and Cora's cab is already running at the kerb. None of you would touch a fair draw upstairs, and in the last ten minutes all three of you have tried to walk off with it into a boot, a taxi, or a van. Under any fair rule, nobody who tried to grab it first gets custody while we're deadlocked—it comes home with me tonight until all four of us agree, and Dan can bring his clamp round to mine.

*At the kerb, the cab driver cracks the front passenger window and glances impatiently toward the doorway.*

**Dan:** *Tightens his grip on the side rail against Ben's hand without letting go of the split top corner, angling the painted face clear of his coat zip as his right heel lifts and holds off the threshold step.* Listen to the three of you—Saturday supper at Cora's, tonight on Ada's table, "deadlock custody" at Ben's, and in every single version I'm just the joiner trotting round with a clamp to fix it for somebody else's wall like my home doesn't even count. Take your hand off the bottom rail, Ben; inventing a rule on the spot where you're the one pure referee who gets to take it home is the exact same grab as Ada's boot and Cora's cab. Mum asked *me* to mend this corner, I put her off until she died, and it's going in my van tonight so I can finally fix it and hang it on my own wall.

*Through the cracked passenger window at the kerb, the cab driver calls out: "Are you getting in or not? I'm holding up the lane here."*

**Cora:** *Her fingers go suddenly still on her bag strap for a second before she flashes a bright grin toward the kerb, waves the cabbie on, and catches the loose corner of Ada's open dust sheet.* Go on without me, love, clear the lane—turns out I'm taking the scenic route! Honestly, Ada, none of you ever tried learning the way to mine once I left, which is why Mum was the only one who sent a photo of that window through my letterbox. Tell you what, boys, before you yank an unglued frame into kindling on the step, tuck it in this sheet and let me carry it home—you three can split my saved cab fare for a drink, and I'll leave that corner split just how she kept it so Dan doesn't have to be anybody's joiner.

*The cab pulls away from the kerb and disappears down the street, leaving the kerb empty in front of Ada's car and Dan's van.*

**Ada:** *Presses her thumb once against her folded cuff, fishes her car keys from her pocket, and lifts her end of the dust sheet right up beneath the frame.* Bring your corner up over the paint instead of dragging it on the step, Cora—you've waved your cab off now anyway, so you're getting a lift in my passenger seat—and Ben, let go of that bottom rail before you and Dan wrench the mitre clean apart on the pavement. Keep your clamp in the van, Dan; that corner stayed open on the wire I hung for her every day she lived upstairs, and it doesn't need mending tonight to clear your conscience any more than it needs Ben inventing referee rules. I carried the lease, the boxes, and the keys from start to finish—fold the sheet over it and let me put it in my boot, because I am not driving away from this kerb carrying nothing.

**Ben:** *Presses steadily upward under the bottom rail to keep the split top mitre closed instead of pulling back, using his free hand to bring the slack of the dust sheet smoothly over the lower edge of the oil board without letting go of the frame.* I'm pressing up into the joint, Ada, not wrenching it—and you aren't the only one standing at this kerb with nothing left of that kitchen once we drive off. Fine—*his breath catches slightly*—Dan, forget custody at mine, because you only got to carry this downstairs on your word all four of us would settle it at the door. Since none of you would touch a blind draw, here's one fair rule nobody can call a grab: nobody gets to ask for it for themselves—each of us names one of the other three out loud right here, and whoever gets the count wraps it up and takes it home for keeps.

**Dan:** *Tucks the top edge of Ada's lifted dust sheet under his thumb over the split corner without yielding an inch of the side rail, his right heel lifting and holding off the concrete step.* My van's ten feet away, Ben, and I stopped right here on the step like I promised—so don't dress up a parlour vote where we all pretend to be unselfish while the three of you trade favours and leave me out of it. Look at all four of us clamped round one dust sheet: Ada finally said the only honest thing all afternoon, that none of us can stand leaving this kerb carrying nothing of Mum's. She asked me to mend this frame every time I came round, I kept telling her next week until she was gone, and I'm not playing fake charity—I want it on my own wall, and it's going in my van unless you look me in the eye and tell me why my home matters less than yours.

**Cora:** *Folds her corner of the dust sheet neatly up over the painted window, her fingers going suddenly still against the cloth for a beat before she looks Dan in the eye with a crooked smile.* Oh, lovely, Ben's invented doorstep Eurovision—and Dan, don't ask me whose home counts least when none of you could pick my street out of a lineup. Tell you what, Ada, I'll gladly take that passenger seat as long as the painting rides on my knees all the way up to my flat instead of disappearing into your boot. You boys can convoy right behind us, peel the photograph Mum sent me straight off my fridge so nobody drives away with nothing tonight, and boo my wallpaper in person while I hang the real thing.

*Behind them, the letting agent comes briskly down the stairs, kicks the wedge out from the building's front door so it latches shut behind the four of them on the front step, nods a polite goodbye, and walks away down the pavement.*

**Ada:** *Draws the last loose flap of the dust sheet taut across the picture wire at the back, brushes her wrist once against her folded cuff, and looks Dan straight in the eye over the wrapped frame.* Your home doesn't matter less, Dan, and I'm not playing Ben's voting game—that door just latched shut behind us, and every other piece of her life went out today without my name on a single list. My knot is still on the wire at the back of this board from the last time anyone hung it for her, and now the flat's gone I am not letting go of the last thing left to keep. Get in my passenger seat and give me your postcode, Cora—I'll drive you right to your door tonight, once you two take your hands off the rails and let me lay this flat in my boot for my own wall.

## Director's Note

### 1. Direction & Conditions Varied (Staging Choices vs. Fixed Play)
- **Promptbook:** `promptbook.md` was empty (`0 bytes`), so no Promptbook instructions had occasion to apply.
- **Performer Framing:** Each sibling ran in an isolated read-only sub-context given only the shared premise, Piece, and Openings, their own single Persona row, and their own single private memory sentence. Performers were directed to play strictly observable speech and physical action one beat at a time, without internal monologue or inventing offstage voices/outcomes, so turns could be shared verbatim as room reality.
- **Conditions Left Open by the Play (Staging Choices):**
  - *The entry buzzer (Scene 1):* `company.md` leaves open who rings downstairs. Once Ada inferred it was the letting agent and Ben buzzed him up to wait on the landing, I paced that external clock across the scene (a second sharp buzz, footsteps stopping on the landing, two knocks, and the agent calling through the door about a half-past appointment) so the flat handover organically forced the siblings out of the room rather than cutting arbitrarily mid-argument.
  - *The idling car & propped door (Scene 2):* `company.md` specifies "One car idles at the kerb" without naming whose car it is. Since Ada and Dan had established a parked car and van in Scene 1 while Cora had arrived by train and talked of springing for a cab, I staged the idling car as the cab that had just dropped off the half-past viewer near Ada's car and Dan's van. Pressing Cora with the cabbie's impatience tested whether she would escape or stay without attributing an unplayed booking to her. Before the final beat, I had the departing agent kick out the door wedge so the building latched shut behind all four siblings.

### 2. What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*, 12 turns to rest):** Ada tried to clear the room by sending Ben to the buzzer and Dan to the bathroom while wrapping the painting for her boot. Ben blocked her with the "list" rule, then tore an envelope into four strips—first proposing annual rotation, then (once Dan exposed rotation as a polite lie) a notched-strip draw for keeps. Cora stepped physically between Ada and the skirting board with sideways trades (saucepans, dinner, and finally the photograph Mother posted her). All four private memories surfaced organically through friction—notably when Dan assumed glass would slide out of the split corner, allowing Ada to counter that it was bare oil-on-board she had hung herself, which pried loose Dan's confession about putting off clamping the mitre. Nobody drew from Ben's fist; Cora took the keys to meet the agent; and Scene 1 rested as Dan lifted the bare painting against his chest (thumb holding the split corner shut) to carry it downstairs in front of the other three.
- **Carried Forward into Scene 2 (*The entrance*, capped at 10 turns):** Mother's flat was locked and handed over; Dan held the unwrapped painting at the propped-open entrance; Ada carried her dust sheet; Ben still pocketed the untouched strips; and ownership remained completely unsettled. Downstairs, Cora tried to commandeer the idling cab, then waved it away when challenged and caught a corner of Ada's dust sheet. Ben pivoted from "deadlock custody" at his flat to a "no one asks for themselves" spoken vote—a classic expression of his Shadow—which Dan and Ada both rejected. While real concessions emerged around family fractures (Ada offering to drive Cora to her flat and acknowledging Dan's home), their competing moves to protect the frame resulted in all eight hands wrapping the painting tight inside Ada's dust sheet while deadlocked on the pavement outside the latched building.

### 3. How the Supplied Material Helped or Made Staging Hard
- **What Helped:** The Piece's physical specificity (`oil of the kitchen window from inside, with a frame open at one corner`, `no resale value`) and asymmetrical private memories generated immediate playable conflict without exposition. Each Persona's Action/Shadow pair produced sharply distinct tactics—Ada's martyred logistics, Ben's self-serving procedural rules, Cora's deniable offers, and Dan's refusal to be sidelined as the family joiner.
- **Play Constraint vs. Staging Choice (Where Staging Was Hard):**
  - *Symmetric unbreakable deadlock (Play design):* Because the Piece is strictly singular and load-bearing (`Without the painting, there is nothing left in the flat to carry any of the four siblings' private memories of Mother`), while all four share an identical undivided goal (`Each wants to take her painting home`) and Shadows that equate yielding with erasure, truthful play creates no internal permission structure for any sibling to let another leave with the painting. Even when Scene 1 ran 12 turns to reach a physical exit and Scene 2 reached the 10-turn cap, ownership remained deadlocked—an emergent property of the Play's architecture rather than performer stubbornness. (Notably, Cora's authored memory—possessing a photograph Mother sent her—slightly complicates `nothing left ... to carry`, since Cora tried twice to trade that photograph as a secondary token, though neither she nor the others accepted it as a substitute for the board itself.)
  - *Offstage agents at the edges of isolation (Play gap vs. staging choice):* Leaving the buzzer ringer and the idling car's driver open in `company.md` gives welcome flexibility, but in isolated turn-by-turn staging where performers cannot invent world outcomes themselves, the Director must supply minimal offstage responses once a sibling presses the intercom or gestures to the kerb so those open conditions don't go inert.
