# Performance: *Mother's Painting*

## Scene 1 — *The flat*

**The flat.** The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.

**Ada:** *Crosses to the entry phone, presses the talk button briefly, then releases it and picks up the roll of bubble wrap left on the floor.* That'll be the landlord's agent for the meter readings—Ben, go down and let him into the cupboard by the stairs, and Cora, check the bathroom cabinets one last time. * kneels beside the skirting board and starts measuring a sheet of wrap against the painting.* Dan, hold the loose corner of this frame steady while I tape the wrap around it for my boot.

**Dan:** *Steps up to the skirting board, puts his boot lightly between the roll of bubble wrap and the bottom edge of the frame, and looks over at Ben and Cora.* Nobody go down the stairs yet—she's sending you both on errands so this ends up in her boot before anyone notices. *Looks down at Ada.* Leave the tape off it, Ada. We haven't agreed it's going home with you, and I'm taking it.

**Ben:** *Steps to the entry phone, presses the door-release button to buzz the agent into the building, then turns back toward the skirting board.* He can find the hall meters himself without any of us going downstairs. Neither of you gets the last thing in the flat by getting tape on it first or putting a boot in the way—if more than one of us wants the painting, the fair rule is a year's turn each, and Cora hasn't even had a word yet. Since that open corner needs gluing and clamping before the mitre splits, the practical way to start the rotation is letting me take it home tonight.

**Cora:** *Glances toward the empty hallway without moving her feet, twirling her keys lightly around one finger before strolling closer to the skirting board.* Hate to spoil the errand, Ada, but I checked the bathroom twenty minutes ago—nothing left in there unless Dan wants to put his boot on a limescale tablet too. Look, before you three draw up a four-year custody rota in front of the meter man, there's a spare hook doing nothing in my hall anyway, so let me tuck the crooked old thing under my arm tonight and save everyone the blood pressure.

**Ada:** *Tears off the sheet of bubble wrap cleanly, sets the roll aside, and slides the wrap behind the top of the frame without touching Dan's boot.* Nobody's sneaking anything, Dan—I've got the inventory clipboard in my bag and every last box accounted for, which is why the flat is empty on time. Shipping a loose sixty-year-old frame around four houses every twelve months will shake the glass-less mitre to pieces, Ben, and carrying it bare under your arm in the rain will finish it off before Cora reaches the bus stop. Move your foot so I can pad the open corner properly, and Ben, get the spare keys off the counter so one of you can sign the agent's meter sheet when he comes up.

**Dan:** *Crouches down, eases the top of the frame forward so Ada's sheet of bubble wrap slides back to the skirting board, and steadies the intact side rail with one hand.* Can we drop the polite act about mitre clamps, spare hooks, and clipboards? We just watched a whole flat go down the stairs without a single argument, and now all four of us are circling a worthless bit of board because once it goes out that door there's nothing of Mum's left in here. *Glances up at Ben, then back at Ada.* Fixing that open corner is my job, not a four-year committee project—so leave the wrap on the floor, Ada, because I'm taking it home tonight.

**Cora:** *Slips her keys into her pocket, reaches behind the tilted frame to pluck up Ada's fallen sheet of bubble wrap, and gives it a brisk little shake.* Ten points to Dan for cutting through the waffle, and thank you, Ada, for pre-cutting a waterproof coat for my bus ride. Tell you what, Dan—let me bundle it home tonight while I've got the wrap in hand, and I'll owe you a curry on Sunday when you come round with your glue pot to rescue that corner properly. Saves Ben playing referee and gets us all out of here before the meter bloke starts charging us rent.

**Ben:** *Scoops the spare keys off the counter, steps up to the skirting board to set them down by Ada's knee, and takes hold of the free edge of the bubble wrap in Cora's hand.* You've got the clipboard, Ada, so you can sign the agent's sheet yourself when he comes up. Two of you don't get to strike a side deal over curry, *catches his breath briefly* Cora, and wave me off as the referee—and Mum moved that painting into every flat she ever lived in without it shaking to pieces. When there's a deadlock over the last piece in a flat, the fair rule is that nobody claiming it for themselves walks out with it tonight; wrap it properly and I'll keep it safe at mine until all four of us agree where it stays.

*Footsteps come up the stairwell toward the landing outside the flat.*

**Ada:** *Folds her cuff into a hard square, picks up the spare keys by her knee to hold them out toward Ben, and sets her free hand steadily under the open corner of the frame.* Five minutes ago you wanted the first year's turn, Ben, so calling your house neutral ground doesn't wash—and Mum didn't carry this to every flat she lived in for it to sit in storage while we hold a committee. I was the last one to hang it for her right on that wall, nursing the wire so that open corner wouldn't give, and it's coming home to go straight up on mine. Take the keys and hold the agent by the hall meters for two minutes, Ben, and Cora, pass that sheet down here so Dan and I can get the frame wrapped before he knocks.

**Dan:** *Shifts his weight onto the ball of his foot, lifting his heel off the floorboards and holding it there as he slides his free hand under the bottom rail beside Ada's.* The only reason you had to nurse that wire, Ada, is because Mum asked me twice since Christmas to glue this corner and I kept telling her next weekend until there wasn't one. I'm not your packing boy wrapping it for your wall, and I'm not coming round to Cora's on Sunday with a glue pot or parking it in Ben's hall while you three decide what counts. *Looks straight at Ada's hand under the open mitre.* Take your hand off the corner before the agent knocks—I'm carrying it home tonight and finishing what she asked me to do.

*Two knuckles rap lightly against the open flat door from the landing.*

**Ada:** *Sets the spare keys crisply on the floorboards at Ben's feet without shifting her palm from under the open corner, glancing once toward the open doorway.* Ben, take those keys and my clipboard out to the landing so he can check the numbers off in the hall, and Cora, lay that sheet over the canvas before we're stood here arguing in front of a stranger. *Looks evenly back at Dan, keeping her fingers braced around the split mitre.* Gluing it tonight won't give you back the weekends since Christmas, Dan, and I'm the one who kept it up on her wall while that corner was open—so hold your side level while I reach the roll and wrap it for my car.

**Ben:** *Glances toward the open flat door and calls out evenly.* Give us two minutes—we'll bring the keys straight out to the landing. *Leaves the keys on the floorboards at his feet, crouches to draw his end of the bubble wrap over the top rail of the frame, and braces his palm along the top edge.* I'm not stepping outside while two of you have your hands under it, Ada, and nursing a picture wire or owing her a repair since Christmas doesn't settle who keeps it. She didn't paint a mitre joint—that's the kitchen window where she sat up with me when I couldn't sleep. If moving it wrecks the frame, the fair rule is putting it on one kitchen wall where all four of us hold a door key and Dan can still glue the corner flat, so ease off the bottom rail and let me take it home to mine.

**Cora:** *Her fingers go suddenly still on her end of the bubble wrap; after a beat, she crouches down, draws the sheet smoothly across the painted window, and presses her folded edge against the side rail above Ada's hand.* Funny thing is, while you three were here nursing wires and sitting up late, Mum sent me a photo of this window when I first moved away—so I've already done my stint with the postcard version. Tell you what, you lot can split custody of my snapshot to go with Ben's spare door keys, and I'll take the full-sized upgrade off your hands tonight before the poor bloke on the landing steps in to cast the deciding vote.

## Scene 2 — *The entrance*

**The entrance.** The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.

*Carried forward from Scene 1: With the agent waiting on the landing, the spare keys were handed over to clear the flat, and the four siblings carried the painting downstairs together without anyone yielding their claim. The painting—draped loosely in the single untaped sheet of bubble wrap—now leans against the inside wall just inside the propped-open front door of the building. Nobody holds agreed possession of it, and what happens to it next remains unsettled.*

**Dan:** *Steps between the propped-open front door and the leaning painting, folds back a corner of the loose bubble wrap to check the open mitre, and nods out toward the kerb.* Whose is the car idling outside—did one of you call a cab on the stairs hoping for a quick getaway? Save the speech about spare door keys, Ben, because nobody's letting themselves into your kitchen on a Tuesday night; the keys upstairs are gone, and whoever walks this out to the pavement leaves the other three seeing it once a year over forced drinks at Christmas. *Rests his hand firmly on the intact side rail of the frame.* I know gluing that corner tonight won't buy back my weekends, Ada, and that's why I'm taking it home instead of watching you hang it out of reach on your wall.

**Cora:** *Steps up to the inner side of the leaning painting, folds the loose corner of bubble wrap back down over the open mitre, and peers past Dan's shoulder toward the kerb with her hand resting lightly on the wrapped edge.* Relax, Dan, it's only the six-o'clock cab I booked after Ada's weather report put me off the bus stop—and the meter's already costing more than the frame's worth. If Ada hangs it you'll glare at that split corner every visit, and if you take it she'll audit your glue job, so slide it onto my back seat before he drives off and you lot will actually have an excuse to visit me before Christmas.

**Ben:** *Steps in between Cora and Dan, steadies the top edge of the wrapped frame flat against the entrance wall, and glances once past the propped-open door toward the idling cab.* A ticking taxi meter isn't deciding this, Cora—especially when you've already got the photo Mum sent you waiting at home. Dan's right that nobody's using a spare key on a Tuesday night, which means whoever carries this past the kerb is keeping it for good. Since all four of us are going to pick ourselves first, the only fair rule left is second choice: nobody's allowed to name themselves, each of us names who else takes it home without ruining Christmas—Ada included—and that settles it right here on the mat.

**Ada:** *Steps in front of the leaning painting, crouches to fold the loose bottom of the bubble wrap neatly under the base rail, and braces both hands beneath its weight.* We're not running a tactical ballot on the doormat, Ben—your second-choice rule just invites another side deal while Cora's cab idles on the meter. Nothing on my wall has ever been out of reach unless you chose not to ring the bell, Dan, and after carrying this clearance from the first box to the agent's keys, I'm not leaving Mum's window to a parlour game. Cora, go tell your driver whether you're getting in or sending him on, and Ben, hold that front door clear so we aren't wedged on the mat while I take this down to my car.

*At the kerb, the idling cab gives a single short tap of its horn.*

**Cora:** *Keeps one palm resting on the wrapped side rail above Ada's hands, leans her shoulder into view of the open doorway, and holds up two fingers toward the cab at the kerb.* Charming of you to count a snapshot in the post as my share of the flat, Ben—remind me to send you a photo of a kettle for Christmas. Still, I'll happily kick off your parlour game and give my second-choice vote to Dan since he actually owns wood glue, and if you three work out you'd rather wave it into a cab than watch it go up on each other's walls, my back seat's warm right outside.

**Dan:** *Presses downward on the intact side rail so Ada cannot lift the base off the mat, looking down at her before glancing up at Ben.* Keep it on the floor, Ada—running the clipboard doesn't buy you sole rights, and the only reason you won't touch Ben's ballot is you know none of us wants to ring your bell just to be supervised in your hall. And don't pretend that rule isn't built for yourself, Ben—after pitching a rotation and neutral storage that both started at yours, you only invented second choice because you reckon everyone defaults to sensible Ben as the safe compromise. Cora's already named me to fix that corner without any side deal, so take your hands off the bottom rail and let me carry it home tonight.

**Ben:** *Drops to one knee beside the entrance wall and braces both palms squarely around the wrapped open corner of the frame.* Stop fighting the weight— *catches his breath sharply* Ada, you're lifting the base while Dan presses the side rail down, and this split corner is spreading right under my hands. Fine, Dan, I want her window in my flat just as much as any of you, and Cora tossing you one vote while her cab honks at the kerb still doesn't settle a four-way claim. When two people are pulling a loose frame apart on the doormat and a third has a meter running outside, the only fair rule left is whoever's forcing the rails lets go—take your hands off the base and the side, and let me carry it home flat before the joint gives.

**Ada:** *Settles the base rail flat onto the entrance mat at once, draws one hand back briefly to fold her cuff into a hard square, then rests both hands steadily under the wrapped bottom edge again.* The base is resting flat on the mat now, Ben, so the only one forcing weight onto those rails is Dan. Nobody minded my supervision while the movers needed booking and every room upstairs needed clearing, Dan, and I didn't hang that window for Mum the last time just to leave you three pulling the corner apart in the lobby. Cora, step out and settle your cab before he leans on the horn again; Dan, ease your weight off that side rail, and Ben, keep both palms steady round that split corner while we carry it flat down the steps to my boot.

*At the kerb, the idling cab clicks its indicator on and pulls away down the street, leaving the kerb empty.*

**Cora:** *Looks out through the propped-open doorway as the cab pulls away, her fingers going suddenly still against the wrapped side rail; after a beat, she crouches beside the mat and rests her free hand lightly along the top edge of the frame.* Well, there goes my getaway car—saved me the fare, and at least Ben can stop blaming my taxi meter for the rush. Tell you what, since your stand-off just left me stranded at the kerb, one of you owes me a lift home anyway: stick me on the back seat nursing this frame flat all the way to mine, let Dan bring his glue pot to finish the corner tonight, and none of you three has to stomach watching one of the other two win.

**Dan:** *Eases all downward pressure off the rail at once, dropping to a crouch beside the mat to cradle the intact side cleanly while his right heel lifts off the floor and stays held there.* Look at the four of us kneeling on a lobby doormat with eight hands clamped on a bit of bubble wrap because none of us can stomach walking out to an empty kerb with nothing of Mum's left. I've taken the strain off the joint, Ben, and booking the movers without asking the rest of us doesn't mean you and Ben march this down the steps to your boot, Ada. I'll call another cab right now and pay Cora's fare home after we drop this flat in my hall to glue and clamp tonight—so take your hands off the bottom rail, Ada, and let me keep one promise to her.

# Director's Note

### What I Directed and Varied
- **Play kept fixed; strict information isolation:** Each performer sub-context received only the shared premise, the `# Human` Promptbook note, the Piece, the active Scene Opening, their single Persona row, and their single private memory sentence. Performers were directed to play strictly observable dialogue and physical stage business (no internal monologue, no self-diagnosing their Shadow aloud, sparing use of Tells and memories earned under pressure, and accurate tracking of the painting's physical handling).
- **Responsive cueing rather than fixed rotation:** Turns were cued according to immediate dramatic pressure in the room (e.g., cueing Dan immediately on Scene 1 Turn 2 when Ada asked him to hold the frame while she wrapped it for her boot; cueing Ada on Scene 1 Turn 11 when Dan challenged her hand directly under the open mitre; cueing Cora on Scene 2 Turns 5 and 9 in direct response to the cab at the kerb).
- **Environmental follow-through on open conditions:** Keeping the authored Openings intact, I followed through on physical timers the Play and cast set in motion:
  1. In *The flat*, after Ben buzzed the downstairs caller into the building (Turn 3), I introduced footsteps coming up the stairwell (Turn 9) and two light raps on the open flat door (Turn 11).
  2. In *The entrance*, after Cora claimed the open "car idling at the kerb" as her pre-booked six-o'clock cab (Turn 2), I introduced a single tap of its horn (Turn 5) and—when no one let go of the painting—had the cab pull away down the street (Turn 9), removing Cora's easy exit.

### What Happened and What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*, 13 turns):** Ada immediately tried to assign final flat-clearing errands while measuring bubble wrap around the painting for her boot. Dan stepped in with his boot to stop the wrap and eventually named the Load-Bearing stakes of the empty flat. Ben buzzed the agent in and proposed two successive "fair rules" (annual rotation, then neutral safekeeping), both starting with the painting going home with him tonight. Cora plucked up Ada's fallen wrap and pitched sideways deals (a spare hook, a Sunday curry for Dan's glue pot). As the agent reached the landing, all four private memories and Tells emerged organically, ending in a four-way physical cluster with all four siblings' hands braced on the bubble-wrapped frame.
- **Carried forward into Scene 2:** Handing the spare keys to the agent cleared the flat without resolving ownership; the four siblings carried the painting downstairs together inside the single untaped sheet of bubble wrap and leaned it just inside the propped-open building door, with possession completely unsettled.
- **Scene 2 (*The entrance*, capped at 10 turns):** Dan dismantled Ben's "spare door key" offer and later exposed Ben's new "second-choice ballot" as a rule engineered to make "sensible Ben" win as the safe compromise—forcing Ben into a rare direct admission (*"Fine, Dan, I want her window in my flat just as much as any of you"*) before Ben immediately retreated behind another rule. Cora cast a genuine second-choice vote for Dan, and when opposing force between Ada lifting the base and Dan pressing the side rail began spreading the open mitre corner, both Ada and Dan backed off the physical strain to protect the frame—without letting go. Even after Cora's cab drove off, the exchange remained in a four-way deadlock, closing at the 10-turn cap with all four siblings crouched around the lobby doormat with eight hands on the wrapped painting in front of an empty kerb.

### How the Supplied Material and Promptbook Worked in Staging
- **Where the Play helped:** The Piece's concrete physicality (`oil of the kitchen window from inside`, `frame open at one corner`, `no resale value`, `every flat she lived in`) and its `Load Bearing` note gave the cast immediate, playable stage business. The open mitre corner became a genuine physical hazard onstage when Ada lifted against Dan's downward pressure, forcing real care for the object even at the height of conflict. Likewise, the Persona `Action`/`Shadow`/`Tell` design was crisp enough that Dan accurately diagnosed Ben's Shadow purely from Ben's observable rule-making across the two scenes.
- **Where the Promptbook had no occasion to matter:** `promptbook.md` (`# Human`) establishes that the Human sets opening conditions before play starts and watches from outside *"unless they explicitly step in to redirect or end play."* The upfront framing worked cleanly, while the mid-play intervention clause had no occasion to fire because the Human watched silently throughout this run.
- **Staging choices vs. structural behavior of the Play:**
  - *Staging choice:* Leaving the identity of the downstairs buzzer caller (Scene 1) and the idling car (Scene 2) open as authored worked smoothly because one-at-a-time sequential cueing let Ada ("the landlord's agent") and Cora ("the six-o'clock cab I booked") establish those details naturally without collision. Conversely, because Scene 1 ended with all eight hands locked on the painting inside the flat, moving the company downstairs to *The entrance* required an explicit Director's carry-forward bridge rather than an in-dialogue exit upstage.
  - *Play structure (not an error, worth noting for the author):* Because the Piece is indivisible (`Each wants to take her painting home`) and every sibling's Shadow treats relinquishing it as an existential defeat (Ada loses belonging if she stops carrying the burden; Dan reads yielding as not counting; Ben generates a new rule whenever an old rule fails; Cora pivots each setback into a new sideways offer), **nothing inside the four Personas authorizes any sibling to let go first**. Left to endogenous character logic without an external Human redirect, faithful play naturally converges on a four-way physical stalemate—producing a resonant closing tableau here, though if the author ever wants a run capable of settling possession from inside the cast alone, one Persona or Opening would need an asymmetric release condition.
