# *Mother's Painting* — Complete Two-Scene Performance

## Scene 1: *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Turn 1 — Ada**
*Ada tears a strip of brown tape from the roll, sticks it ready on her knee, and crouches beside the skirting board with a folded sheet of bubble wrap, reaching to steady the painting by its sound side.*
"Ben, get the buzzer—that'll be the agent for the keys. Cora, check the airing cupboard's clear, and Dan, get a photo of the electric meter before we throw the main switch. I'll wrap this up now and lay it flat in my boot so that loose corner doesn't go altogether."

**Turn 2 — Ben**
*Ben steps to the entry panel by the door, rests his thumb on the plastic casing beside the release button without pressing it, and looks back down at the bubble wrap in Ada's hand.*
"Leave the tape off a minute, Ada—whoever happens to be holding the bubble wrap doesn't just put the last thing in her boot. If all four of us want it, the only fair rule is a straight rota, a year in each house. I've got a corner clamp at home, so I can glue that loose mitre tonight, take the first year, and hand it on next November."

**Turn 3 — Cora**
*Cora pulls the airing cupboard door open with two fingers, glances inside, clicks it shut, and strolls back over, slipping her canvas tote off her shoulder so the mouth hangs open near Ada's elbow.*
"Nothing in the airing cupboard unless someone wants joint custody of a wire hanger on alternating Christmases. Honestly, a four-year rota for a crooked bit of board—just drop it in here and I'll chuck it on the back seat before the agent comes up and charges us rent. You lot already split the decent plates; let me take the wonky window as my booby prize and save Ben gluing his thumbs together."

**Turn 4 — Dan**
*Dan drops his phone back into his pocket without looking toward the meter cupboard, crosses to the skirting board, and rests his fingertips on the top edge of the frame beside Ada's hand.*
"I'm not going out in the hall to photograph the meter while you parcel this into your boot, Ada. Drop the act, all of you—Ada's calling it packing, Ben's inventing a rota that starts at his house and dies there, and Cora's pretending it's rubbish so she can walk off with it in a shopping bag. Every stick of hers is in that van downstairs, and nobody's handing this over next November. Leave the bubble wrap—I'm taking it home tonight and sorting that corner myself."

*(Director environmental cue: From the wall panel beside Ben, the entry buzzer sounds a second, longer ring.)*

**Turn 5 — Ada**
*Keeping one hand steady on the sound stile of the frame, Ada slides the bubble wrap underneath the bottom edge, folds the lower flap up to cradle the open corner, and peels the strip of brown tape from her knee.*
"Let them in, Ben—leaving the agent on the pavement just means I'm the one driving back across town tomorrow with the keys. It isn't going bare into a tote bag, Cora, and carting a split frame round four houses every November will have it in pieces before anyone gets near it with a clamp. Shift your hand an inch, Dan, so I can tape the wrap properly; I'm taking it home tonight where it can go straight up on a wall and stay put."

**Turn 6 — Ben**
*Ben holds down the entry-release button for a two-count, crosses to the skirting board pulling a crumpled receipt from his jacket pocket, and crouches to lay his palm on the loose sheet of bubble wrap, his breath catching briefly on Dan's name.*
"Fine— Dan, if nobody's handing it over next November, getting bubble wrap under it or a hand on the frame still doesn't decide who keeps it. I've just buzzed the street door, so press that tape on to hold the split corner and let me stand it safely by the front door while we deal with the meter and the keys. Soon as they're gone, we tear this into four slips—one mark on the back, blind draw, and whoever pulls it takes it home for good."

**Turn 7 — Cora**
*Cora winds the tote strap round her fingers, glances down at the three pairs of hands pinning the frame by the skirting board, and her fingers go suddenly still on the webbing; after a beat she drops to a crouch beside them and folds the loose side flap of bubble wrap neatly against the wood, keeping her palm there.*
"It's heavy canvas, Danny, not a carrier bag—give me some credit. The agent's coming up the stairs right now and you three look like you're holding a séance on the carpet, so spare us the tombola with Ben's pocket receipt. Stick the end-of-tenancy clean on my card, call it my madness tax, and let me tuck this under my arm on the way down before we embarrass ourselves."

*(Director environmental cue: Footsteps start up the communal stairs below the landing.)*

**Turn 8 — Dan**
*Dan drops into a crouch at the skirting board, closes one hand firmly over the top rail, and sets his other palm over the bubble wrap cradling the split corner before Ada's tape can reach it, his right heel lifting clear of the floorboards and staying held there.*
"I'm not holding the wrap for your boot, Ada—stop talking over me like this was already decided. Put your card away, Cora, and keep the receipt in your pocket, Ben; we're not buying each other off with a cleaning bill or drawing raffle tickets just because you're scared of looking ugly in front of the letting agent. Let them come in and see all four of us on the floor because nobody has keys to anywhere of hers once we walk downstairs. Take your hands off it—that corner's waited long enough to be fixed, and I'm taking it home."

*(Director environmental cue: The footsteps reach the landing outside the flat door.)*

**Turn 9 — Ada**
*Blocked from the split corner by Dan's palm, Ada presses the strip of brown tape flat across the middle of the bubble wrap between Cora's and Dan's hands. Drawing her free hand back, she folds her cuff into a hard square at her wrist, sets the tagged flat keys from her pocket onto the floorboards beside Ben's knee, and slides her fingers back under the bottom edge of the frame.*
"The clean's already booked, Cora, and we're not tearing up receipts on the stairs, Ben. That corner was already open when I hung this up for her in here, Dan, and it held fine the whole time because it went straight onto one hook and stayed there. Ben, take those keys and let them in, and Cora, walk them round to the meter—I'm carrying this down to my boot now so we can finish up properly."

*(Director environmental cue: Two brisk knuckles rap on the open flat door from the landing.)*

**Turn 10 — Ben**
*Ben scoops the tagged keys from the floorboards into his jacket pocket, glances toward the open doorway, and closes that hand round the upright stile of the frame while keeping his other palm flat on the wrap.*
"Come on in—the meter cupboard's right there in the hall. Putting the hook in this wall doesn't make it yours, Ada; I sat with her at that window plenty of nights when I couldn't sleep, and you still don't hear me claiming first rights. Those keys stay in my pocket until everyone takes their hands off this frame. If we can't settle it in thirty seconds without pulling the corner apart, the fair rule is nobody gets it for good tonight—I take it home flat, glue the mitre, and it goes on nobody's wall until all four of us agree."

## Scene 2: *The entrance*

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.*
*(Carried forward: The tagged keys have been handed over to the letting agent upstairs and Mother's flat is closed behind them. Nobody yielded possession upstairs; carried down the stairs under truce, the painting—wrapped loosely round its middle with bubble wrap and Ada's single strip of brown tape, its split corner still open—leans against the wall just inside the propped-open front entrance, in no one's hands yet.)*

**Turn 1 — Cora**
*Cora glances past the propped door toward the car idling at the kerb, pulls a folded scarf from her tote, and stoops by the entrance wall to drape it gently over the bare split corner, slipping her palm behind the taped bubble wrap.*
"Locking it in Ben's cupboard like a hostage until the four of us agree on something is bleak even by our standards, and that engine at the kerb is gassing the whole street. You three had the hooks and the late-night shifts upstairs while I was away—let me drive the wonky window back to mine and call us even. I'll throw in lunch whenever anyone wants to come and inspect the corner, seeing as none of us can buzz upstairs anymore."

**Turn 2 — Dan**
*Dan steps in between the propped front door and the entrance wall, lifts Cora's scarf off the bare split corner to hold it out toward her, and slides his other hand under the sound bottom edge of the frame.*
"Nobody's coming round yours for lunch, Cora—once we walk off this pavement, there's no flat upstairs making the four of us turn up in the same place anymore. And don't lump me in with Ada's hooks and Ben's late nights; that corner only stayed open on the wall because Mum asked me to fix it and I kept telling her next weekend. It isn't sitting in Ben's cupboard while you three sort out who gets it when I'm not in the room. Take your scarf back and let go of the wrap—I'm taking it home."

**Turn 3 — Ada**
*Ada stops a beat with her eyes on the bare split corner Dan has just uncovered. With the leftover roll of brown tape still looped round her wrist, she steps in to the entrance wall, cups one palm carefully over the open mitre to hold the wood flush, and grips the top rail with her other hand, taking its weight.*
"She never said she'd asked you to mend it, Dan—when I hung it up she just stood behind me till it was level, and Ben was the one up here with her after dark. I've still got the final meter numbers and the council tax to close out at mine tonight, and her picture isn't getting shut in a cupboard or chucked loose on a back seat. Cora, take your scarf before it hits the step, and Ben, pull the door wedge once we're out—Dan, if you owe her that corner, bring your glue round to my table on Sunday, because I'm putting this in my boot now."

**Turn 4 — Ben**
*Leaving the wedge under the propped door, Ben steps into the threshold between the entrance wall and the street, closes his hand firmly round the sound upright stile of the frame, and catches his breath on Ada's name.*
"It wasn't a late shift— Ada, and I never meant a cupboard; I used to sit with her at that window when I couldn't sleep. I'm not pulling the wedge while you carry this out to your boot and the four of us scatter off the pavement for good. The only fair rule left is nobody claims it on the doorstep by holding the bills or owing a repair on that corner. Let me take it home flat tonight, and all three of you come round to my table on Sunday—Dan with his glue, Cora with lunch—and we settle whose wall it goes on together."

*(Director environmental cue: Outside at the kerb, the driver of the idling car gives a short tap on the horn.)*

**Turn 5 — Cora**
*Cora takes the scarf back from Dan with her free hand, her fingers going suddenly still in the wool for a beat before she tosses it over her shoulder, keeping her other hand wedged firmly behind the taped middle of the frame.*
"Charming, Danny—cross yourself off the roast potatoes, then, and we both know nobody's turning up at Ben's on Sunday either. Mum sent me a photograph of this when I first moved away, so while you three had the hooks and the late nights, I've been making do with a four-by-six of that kitchen window for years. Let me take the real thing before that driver leans on the horn again, and I'll stick the photo in the post for you lot—corners intact, no clamp required."

*(Director environmental cue: Under four pairs of hands, the open mitre shifts with a dry creak of wood. Outside at the kerb, brake lights flare red as the driver puts the idling car into gear.)*

**Turn 6 — Dan**
*At the creak of the mitre, Dan takes his hand cleanly off the bottom rail at once and steps half a pace back from the entrance wall with his palms open, glancing once toward the red brake lights at the kerb as his right heel lifts off the tiled floor and stays held there.*
"Ease off it—that was the corner going. She didn't run everything she asked me past you, Ada, and right now the three of you are pulling her frame apart on the doormat because none of us trusts the others out of sight with it. If Sunday isn't just doorstep talk to get us all into our cars, take your hands off the wood and let me carry it home flat tonight so I can glue and clamp that joint like she asked me to. Say whose table on Sunday and I'll walk through the door with it whole, and all four of us can settle where it lives to each other's faces."

**Turn 7 — Ada**
*At the creak of the wood under her palm, Ada lifts both hands cleanly off the frame and steps half a pace back from the entrance wall. She folds her cuff into a hard square at her wrist, then slides the leftover roll of brown tape off her arm and holds it out toward Dan.*
"Hands off it, Ben, Cora, before that joint goes altogether—and catch your driver, Cora, he's already in gear. I never knew she'd sent you a picture of it, or kept asking you for the mend, Dan. Take the tape and carry it home flat tonight so you can glue that corner like she asked you to. Ben's table on Sunday at one, not mine—bring the photo with lunch, Cora, and bring the frame whole, Dan, because I'm still arguing for my wall once the glue's dry."

**Turn 8 — Ben**
*Ben eases the sound stile back against the entrance wall so the open mitre takes no strain, lifts his hand cleanly off the wood, and steps back to brace the front door open with his shoulder, stooping to pick up the wooden wedge.*
"Go on and catch your car before he pulls off, Cora, and bring that photograph with you on Sunday so the rest of us can see it. Take the tape and carry it home flat tonight, Dan—glue that corner properly like she asked you. One o'clock at my kitchen table on Sunday, and the rule is simple: once you walk it through my door whole, it doesn't leave that table again until all four of us agree whose wall it goes on."

**Turn 9 — Cora**
*Cora glances from her own hand on the wrap to the red brake lights at the kerb, then slides her palm carefully out from behind the frame without jarring the wood. Pulling the scarf off her shoulder, she sets it folded against the sound base of the bubble wrap, steps past Ben onto the front step with a hand lifted toward the windscreen at the kerb, and looks back into the doorway.*
"Lucky for you three I can't sprint in these shoes—you all just gave me a clear run at the door. Use the scarf to pad your seat for it, Danny; call it my deposit, because once that glue's dry I'm coming back for both at one o'clock. Have the kettle on, Ben—you lot can look at the photo over lunch, not keep it."

**Turn 10 — Dan**
*Dan lowers his right heel flat onto the tiles, takes the roll of brown tape onto his wrist, and stoops by the entrance wall. Tucking Cora's folded scarf under the sound bottom rail, he lifts the painting level against his chest with one palm holding the split corner flush, and steps past Ben's shoulder onto the top step.*
"You'll get your scarf back clean at one o'clock, Cora, and the corner'll be square. Don't any of you put a hook in the plaster before Sunday, though—walking it into Ben's kitchen doesn't mean I'm letting you walk back out with it."

## Director's Note

### 1. What Was Directed and Varied (Staging Choices vs. Authored Play)
- **Fixed boundaries:** Each of the four sibling sub-contexts received only the shared premise, the Piece, the current Opening, the Promptbook's `# Human` rule, their own Persona row, and their own single private memory sentence.
- **Acting direction:** Cast members were directed toward taut, unsentimental naturalism—pursuing possession through their assigned Action rather than explaining their Shadow aloud, holding private memories back unless friction genuinely forced them out, deploying Tells sparingly under real pressure, and never writing reactions for siblings or inventing offstage/intercom voices.
- **Open conditions & environmental pressure:** I left the identities behind `"Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer"` (Scene 1) and `"One car idles at the kerb"` (Scene 2) open at each curtain-up rather than pre-assigning them. Ada organically cast the buzzer as the letting agent arriving for the keys; later, in Scene 2, Ada read Cora's urgency over the horn as Cora's waiting car/driver, which Ben and Cora seamlessly inhabited. To keep real-time momentum, I introduced sparse physical cues drawn directly from the authored Openings and Piece: a second buzzer ring, footsteps mounting the stairs after Ben pressed the entry release, and two raps on the open flat door in Scene 1; then a horn tap, brake lights flaring as the car went into gear, and—when all four gripped the frame at once—a dry creak from the open mitre (`a frame open at one corner`) in Scene 2, paired with a brief stage note that gripping harder while repeating the same refusal would split the wood.

### 2. What Happened and What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*):** Every sibling pursued the painting strictly through their Action—Ada wrapping it for her boot while assigning chores, Ben blocking her with "fair" rules that favoured or insulated him (a four-year rota starting with him, then a four-slip blind draw), Cora joking sideways while offering her tote and then her card for the cleaning bill, and Dan stripping the pretexts bare—particularly that once the flat keys went, nothing would force the four of them into the same room again. Ada's and Ben's private memories surfaced under pressure, while Cora's and Dan's stayed buried. By Turn 10, all four had hands physically pinning the half-taped frame at the skirting board as Ben pocketed the keys and invited the agent into the hall, capping the first exchange in an unresolved four-way grip.
- **Carried into Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** With the flat locked behind them and keys surrendered upstairs, the painting—loosely banded round the middle with bubble wrap and Ada's single strip of brown tape, its split corner still bare—leaned against the entrance wall in **no one's** custody, carrying forward all four competing claims and the two unspoken memories.
- **Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** On the doorstep, Dan confessed putting off Mother's request to mend the corner, and Cora revealed the four-by-six photograph Mother posted her when she moved away. All four briefly locked hands on the frame a second time until the open corner creaked and the car shifted into gear. Dan took his hands off first to save the joint and asked to mend it tonight on the promise of bringing it whole to Sunday's table. That broke the physical deadlock without breaking the Play's fixed desire: Ada yielded even hosting to Ben's table and gave Dan her brown tape (while warning she would still argue for her wall once the glue dried), Ben braced the door and set a unanimous-decision rule for Sunday at one, Cora left her scarf to pad the frame as her "deposit" before catching her car, and Dan lowered his lifted heel flat to the tiles and carried the painting out to mend—with final ownership explicitly still contested for Sunday.

### 3. Supplied Material & Promptbook Assessment
- **Where `company.md` helped:** The interplay between the Piece's physical defect (`a frame open at one corner`, zero resale value, `Load Bearing` as the last vessel of Mother's memory) and the four asymmetrical private memories created immediate dramatic friction—especially Ada assuming the corner had simply held on her hook while Dan knew he had failed to mend it, and Cora countering the others' physical proximity with Mother's posted photograph. Scene 2's explicit clause (`who has it if anyone, and what remains unsettled`) directly accommodated a Scene 1 stalemate in which nobody walked downstairs owning the painting.
- **Where `promptbook.md` mattered vs. had no occasion to matter:** The Promptbook established clean non-interference from outside the fiction once initial conditions were set; its provision for mid-scene human redirection (`unless they explicitly step in to redirect or end play`) had no occasion to trigger since the Human brief asked only to watch the complete two-scene staging.
- **What made staging hard (Play constraint vs. staging choice):**
  - *Structural deadlock pressure in the Play (addressed via staging, no Play change required):* Because `"Each wants to take her painting home"` is symmetrical across four siblings whose Shadows actively punish backing down (Ada equates yielding the burden with not belonging; Ben hides behind rules; Cora deflects vulnerability; Dan treats decisions outside his presence as proof he doesn't count), isolated performers naturally converge on a literal four-hands-on-the-frame clinch—which hit the 10-turn ceiling in Scene 1 and nearly recurred in Scene 2. Rather than a flaw in the Play, the Piece's `Apparent` detail (`open at one corner`) gave the Director a diegetic pressure point: letting the mitre creak under eight hands forced the siblings to separate *who carries it home tonight to mend* from *whose wall keeps it on Sunday*, allowing the Play to rest cleanly while keeping everyone's want intact.
  - *Ambiguous offstage agents across isolated turns (staging choice):* Open nouns like `"Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer"` and `"One car idles at the kerb"` give great atmospheric tension, but in turn-by-turn isolated staging they require an explicit directorial rule preventing performers from inventing offstage voices or conflicting car ownership mid-turn.
