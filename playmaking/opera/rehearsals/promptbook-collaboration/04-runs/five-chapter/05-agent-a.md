# *Mother's Painting* — Staged Performance

## Scene 1: *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Ada:** *Crosses to the skirting board with a clean tea towel and kneels beside the painting.* Ben, get the buzzer—that'll be the letting agent coming up for the meter readings. Cora, check the airing cupboard isn't still dripping, and Dan, put the spare keys on the counter; I'll wrap the frame for my boot before that corner opens any wider.

**Dan:** *Drops the spare keys onto the bare counter with a clatter and steps across the floorboards to stand over Ada.* Nobody's checking the airing cupboard, Ada—the four of us are only still standing in an empty room because none of us wants to leave without that picture. Take the tea towel off it; I've got the wood glue and corner clamp at mine, so it's coming home in my car.

**Ben:** *Lifts the entry phone by the door, says "Third floor, come on up," presses the release button, and hangs the handset back on the wall.* Nobody's putting it in anyone's boot yet—leave it against the skirting board, both of you. We made the rule at nine o'clock that anyone taking furniture went to the back of the queue for whatever was left at the end, and I'm the only one here who didn't put a single thing on that van.

**Cora:** *Strolls past the counter, swinging her canvas tote bag loosely from one thumb, and peers down at the split frame with a bright grin.* Three flights of stairs before the punches start—the letting agent's going to love us. Look, if it saves a bloodbath over Ben's nine-o'clock spreadsheet, slide it into my tote and I'll haul the wonky thing back on the train; my hallway wall's already crooked enough to match the corner.

**Ada:** *Tucks the tea towel firmly around the bottom edge of the frame without standing up, keeping one hand flat on the good side of the wood.* You're not shoving loose mitres into a canvas bag on the Northern Line, Cora, and Dan, clamping fifty-year-old pine dry will split it straight up the grain. The only things on that van with my name on them were the commode for the hospice shop and the drop-leaf table nobody had room for, Ben—now somebody open the hall cupboard so we have the gas reading ready before he reaches the landing.

**Ben:** *Steps across from the door to crouch opposite Ada, resting two fingers steadily on the top rail of the frame.* I photographed both meters twenty minutes ago, Ada, so nobody needs their head in the hall cupboard while you finish wrapping it. Taking the drop-leaf table because nobody else had room still counts on the tally, and we're not shoving an unglued frame onto the Northern Line just to skip a proper decision—under the rule we all started with at nine o'clock, the one person who took zero out of this flat gets last call, and that's me.

**Cora:** *Drops into a crouch beside the skirting board with an easy laugh, parking her tote bag squarely in front of the tea towel.* Relax, Ada, I wasn't going to bat pigeons with it at Euston—I'll happily blow twenty quid on a black cab just to watch the pair of you let go of the wood. Honestly, Ben, disqualifying me over half a box of chipped mugs is tragic; keep your spotless zero for bragging rights and let me haul the wonky kitchen window home, since mine is the only flat shabby enough not to embarrass the frame.

*No footsteps have reached the landing; instead, the entry buzzer by the door gives two sharp, impatient rings from downstairs.*

**Dan:** *Steps in close behind them, lifts one heel off the bare floorboards, and holds it there.* I was still downstairs wedging the street door for the movers at nine o'clock, Ben, so whatever neat little rule you cooked up before I got up the stairs doesn't count. Look at the three of you squatting round the skirting board pretending this is about van tallies and pine grain, when the truth is the room's bare and you're all just grabbing for the last bit of Mum left in the flat. She asked me to fix that open corner—not wrap it in Ada's tea towel or bounce it around a cab—so take your hands off it while somebody holds that buzzer down properly, because the bottom latch sticks.

**Ada:** *Draws her hand off the wood, folds her left cuff into a hard square, then rises and crosses to the entry phone, pressing her thumb firmly onto the release button.* You have to hold it down five full seconds till the bottom latch clicks twice, Ben. That corner wasn't waiting on your clamp, Dan—it was already open the day Mum moved in here, when I stood on a chair on my own and hung it off two pins under the bottom rail so the joint wouldn't pull. Counting a hospice-shop run against me on some nine-o'clock tally doesn't put me behind you or Cora's black cab, so stand up off the floor before the agent walks in and leave the frame on the skirting board till the handover's signed.

**Ben:** *Draws his fingers off the top rail, stands up straight, and holds an open hand out above Cora's tote bag.* Vacant possession means nothing left on the floorboards when the agent steps inside, Ada, or they'll hold back the deposit. And if the nine-o'clock tally's out, *catches his breath briefly* Dan, then unwitnessed claims about wood glue or picture pins don't count either. My car's the only one downstairs with a completely empty boot, so the only fair rule left is I carry it down now to clear the flat and lock it level until all four of us agree.

## Scene 2: *The entrance*

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. The handover upstairs is finished and the flat is locked behind them. To clear the floorboards without letting anyone walk off with it, the painting—still with Ada's tea towel tucked around the bottom rail—was brought down the stairs and now leans against the wall just inside the propped-open entranceway, with no one holding agreed possession. Every claim from upstairs remains unsettled.*

**Cora:** *Leans a shoulder against the propped-open front door between the painting and the pavement, twirling her tote strap lightly round her fingers as she nods toward the idling car.* Leaving an engine running at the kerb is a bit obvious for a heist, isn't it—we all know "locked in a boot till we agree" is just polite for "see you never." Tell you what, Ada gets her good tea towel back right now, none of you three drivers has to trust the others' car keys, and I'll tuck the window under my arm for the station—I'll even post you each a glossy four-by-six of it for your fridges, my treat.

**Dan:** *Steps off the bottom stair to the entrance wall and sets his knuckles against the side rail above Ada's tea towel.* Cut the fridge-photo routine, Cora—making a joke out of it while you block the doorway is no cleaner than leaving an engine idling at the kerb to lock it in a boot and never call. Ada's right that the corner was already open when Mum moved in, because Mum asked me three different Sundays this year to take it off those two pins and clamp it, and I kept telling her next weekend until she died. Nobody's posting me a glossy four-by-six while that mitre's still gaping, so step out of the threshold and let me put it on my back seat and finish the job.

**Ada:** *Clicks Flat 3's empty postbox shut on the hall wall, slips the signed handover sheet into her pocket, and steps beside Dan to set her palm firmly over the tea towel at the bottom rail.* Ben, turn that engine off at the kerb before a warden comes round the corner, and Cora, stop blocking the threshold in case the ground-floor flat comes home. My tea towel stays right where it is—nobody's marching bare canvas to the station for fridge snapshots, and locking it in an empty boot isn't neutral custody, Ben, when you're the one holding the key. Guilt over three missed Sundays doesn't put it on your back seat either, Dan; I showed up the afternoon she needed it hung, and I'm not ending today with nothing of hers at mine except the probate file and a drop-leaf table nobody wanted.

**Ben:** *Pulls a biro from his inside coat pocket and taps it flat against the shut postbox.* It's a single yellow line till half six, Ada—leaving the engine on with the hazards flashing gives us five minutes' loading grace, not a getaway. If you don't trust me holding the car key, pull that handover sheet back out of your pocket and I'll sign a receipt for the frame right here on the postbox. Basic rule of any estate is the person holding the probate file doesn't award herself disputed property as back-pay, nobody clamps wood to clear a guilty conscience, and nobody hauls bare canvas through Tube barriers—so witness the sheet and let me get it in the boot before a warden rounds the corner.

**Cora:** *Her fingers go suddenly still on the tote strap; a beat later she gives a quick laugh and steps in from the threshold right up against the postbox, blocking Ben's biro.* Funny you call it a routine, Dan—a four-by-six of that exact window was what Mum posted me when I first moved away, gaping corner and all, so I've already done my time with the fridge version. Put the biro away, Ben: you can lock Mum's original snapshot in your boot if you're desperate to guard something, I'll pay the warden's fine myself if you run over five minutes, and I'll get a cab straight to this kerb so the actual oil rides home on my knees without touching a Tube barrier.

**Dan:** *Tightens his fingers round the side rail above Ada's hand, lifts one heel off the entrance tiles, and holds it there.* Listen to yourself, Ben—turning straight to Ada to witness your biro on the handover sheet like the pair of you can sign the rest of us out of it while we're standing two feet away. Drop the probate talk and the cab fares: the second that front door clicks shut there's nowhere left the four of us have to meet, and we all know whoever gets that window past the kerb is keeping Mum for good. You're not leaving the corner split like your postcard, Cora, and you're not locking it in that boot on a phony receipt, Ben—let the warden write the ticket, because my hand stays on this rail until it goes onto my back seat to be glued.

**Ben:** *Slips the biro back into his coat, steps past Cora to the entrance wall, and sets two fingers steadily beside the frame's open corner.* Keep your snapshot, *catches his breath sharply* Cora—I didn't need a postcard to know that kitchen window after sitting at it with her whenever I couldn't sleep, and I'm still not trading sob stories on the doorstep to ask for it. If crossing the kerb means keeping it for good, then one common rule settles all four of us: one piece of hers per household, kept intact the exact way she carried it into every flat. That rules out Ada doubling up on the table, Cora doubling up on the photo, and Dan gluing shut a corner she left open through every move—so let the warden write the ticket, because under a fair rule it comes home untouched with me.

**Ada:** *Lifts her free hand to fold her right cuff into a hard square, then slides that palm underneath the tea towel beside her other hand to cradle the bottom rail.* Take your fingers off that split corner before you pinch the mitre out of true, Ben, and Cora, keep an eye out the door for the warden while those hazards are still flashing. I put my name on that drop-leaf table so the agent wouldn't dock Mum's deposit—not as "back-pay"—and you don't get to count a clearance job and a ten-year-old postcard as heirlooms just to invent a rule where only you win. When I hung this upstairs for her, I had to strip the perished cord off the back and carry the whole weight on two bottom pins because the pine's dry right through; crank a clamp onto it like Dan wants or hang it blind in Ben's flat or Cora's hall and the frame'll give way, so my hands stay under this rail until it goes flat in my car.

**Cora:** *Steps in to the one bare side of the frame and hooks her fingers lightly round the pine, grinning across the wood at the three of them.* Oh, brilliant, all four of us clinging to it—if the warden rounds the corner now it'll look like a séance on the doormat, and relax, Ben, nobody was trading sob stories, I was generously offering you vintage Kodak. Tell you what, since I'm not playing lookout while you pull it like a Christmas cracker: Ben gets my snapshot in the post so his own rule puts me back on zero, Ada can come boss my hallway wall with two bottom pins for the price of a biscuit, and I'm happy to hold this side rail all evening till the single yellow bankrupts you and the window rides home in my cab.

**Dan:** *Sets his boot flat on the tiles and brings his other hand onto the side rail without pulling on the joint.* She didn't drag a split corner from flat to flat on principle, Ben—she lived with it broken because none of you mended it and I kept putting her off when she asked me to spline and glue it properly. Nobody's having Ada round for biscuits, nobody's posting snapshots, and balancing dry pine on two nails isn't saving it, Ada—it needs a proper spline on my workbench. Leave the hazards flashing till the tank runs dry if you want, because nobody's settling this without me and my hands aren't coming off this rail until it goes home in my car.

# Director's Note

### What Was Directed & Varied
- **Strict isolation & acting discipline:** Each sibling ran in a separate read-only performer sub-context receiving only the shared situation/Piece/opening, their own Persona row, their own single private memory sentence, and the Promptbook directive. Performers were directed to restrict output to observable speech and stage movement and to save physical Tells for moments of genuine pressure rather than repeating them mechanically every turn.
- **Dynamic cueing rather than fixed round-robin:** Turn order followed whoever was directly pressured or undercut by the prior move (`Ada → Dan → Ben → Cora → Ada → Ben → Cora → Dan → Ada → Ben` in Scene 1; `Cora → Dan → Ada → Ben → Cora → Dan → Ben → Ada → Cora → Dan` in Scene 2), giving all four siblings equal stage weight (five turns each across twenty turns).
- **Environmental pressure on open conditions:** Left the Play's two open external details—*"Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer"* and *"One car idles at the kerb"*—unassigned so the cast could anchor them inside the scene (Ada cast the buzzer as the letting agent arriving for handover; Cora, Ada, and Ben converged on the idling car at the kerb being Ben's car left running on a single yellow line). After Ben buzzed the intercom blindly at Turn 3 assuming footsteps would come up, I introduced one environmental beat before Turn 8 (*two sharp, impatient rings from downstairs, with no footsteps on the landing*), increasing handover pressure and prompting Dan and Ada's exchange over the sticking latch.
- **Bridge between scenes (staging choice):** Because Scene 1 hit the 10-turn cap with Ben invoking "vacant possession" while the others refused his empty-boot custody, I bridged into *The entrance* by having the painting brought downstairs to clear the flat for lock-up—still wrapped at the bottom rail in Ada's tea towel—and leaned inside the propped-open entranceway with no sibling holding agreed possession.

### What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*):** Ada tried to fold taking the painting into final flat-clearing assignments; Dan immediately exposed the shared pretext; Ben countered with a "nine-o'clock furniture queue" rule; Cora slid in sideways with her canvas tote, then a black-cab offer. Under pressure, Dan's Shadow (being downstairs wedging the door when the 9 a.m. rule was made) and private memory (Mother asking him to fix the open corner) collided directly with Ada's Tell and private memory (hanging the painting off two bottom pins with the corner already open when Mother moved in), forcing Ben to pivot from his broken 9 a.m. tally to a "vacant possession / neutral empty boot" rule.
- **Carried forward into Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** Physically, Ada's tea towel stayed tucked around the bottom rail, the painting shifted from the flat's skirting board to the entrance wall with nobody holding possession, and Ben's "empty boot" claim materialized as his car idling with hazards on at the kerb. Dramatically, every disputed ledger item from upstairs (Ada's drop-leaf table and hospice commode, Cora's mugs, Dan's dry-clamping vs. Ada's bottom pins) carried into the hallway.
- **Scene 2 escalation:** Cora's private memory surfaced first in disguise as a breezy joke (*"post you each a glossy four-by-six of it for your fridges"*), which drew fire from Dan and Ada and triggered both Dan's rawest admission (putting Mother off three Sundays until she died) and Cora's Tell and disclosure that Mother actually sent her that four-by-six when she moved away. Dan named the Piece's load-bearing truth (*"the second that front door clicks shut there's nowhere left the four of us have to meet"*), stripping away Ben's biro-receipt maneuver and forcing Ben's private memory (sitting with Mother at that window when he couldn't sleep) into the open—which Ben, true to his Shadow, immediately converted into yet another "fair rule" (*one intact piece per household*). One by one, Ada, Dan, Ben, and finally Cora placed their hands onto all four sides of the frame, ending in a four-way physical deadlock inside the propped-open door as Ben's car idled on the yellow line.

### How the Authored Play & Promptbook Performed
- **Where `company.md` excelled:** The Piece's physical details (*kitchen window oil*, *frame open at one corner*, *taken to every flat*) interlocked tightly with the four isolated private memories even though no performer could see the others' briefs—most notably Ada's memory (the corner already open when she hung it on two pins) and Dan's memory (putting off fixing that corner) locking together across Scene 1 Turn 9 and Scene 2 Turn 2. Likewise, the four distinct Action/Shadow pairs prevented twenty turns of refusal from sounding monotonous: Ada claimed through caretaking burden, Ben through procedural rules, Cora through sideways comedy, and Dan through blunt grievance.
- **Where `promptbook.md` helped and where it created staging friction:** The Promptbook's hard anti-compromise rule (*meeting any attempt to settle who takes the painting with a fresh claim, deflection, or block*) prevented polite sibling capitulation and forced every private memory out into the open, **but** binding all four performers to block every settlement meant neither scene could come to a natural rest on its own—both scenes necessarily ran to the 10-turn cap. Distinguishing Play from Promptbook: `company.md` leaves open *how* the painting gets from the third-floor skirting board to *The entrance* ("Carry forward where the painting is, who has it if anyone"), which works smoothly when one sibling picks it up or proposes temporary carriage, whereas under this Promptbook nobody will concede even temporary hands-on carriage without a counter-grab—requiring the Director to supply a neutral transitional move down the stairs between scenes.
- **Detail with no occasion to matter:** `Apparent: It has no resale value` never needed to be debated aloud; none of the four Personas attempted an appraisal, sale, or cash buyout, treating the canvas strictly as emotional load-bearing cargo from the first turn.
