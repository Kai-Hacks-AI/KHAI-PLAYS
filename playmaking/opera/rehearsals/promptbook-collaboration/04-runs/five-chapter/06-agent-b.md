# Performance: *Mother's Painting*

## Scene 1 — *The flat*

**The flat.** The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.

**Ada:** *Crosses to the wall phone as the buzzer stops, tearing a strip of brown tape from the roll on her wrist.* That'll be the agent for the meter readings—Ben, go down with the front-door set and walk him to the cupboard under the stairs, and Cora, check the bathroom cabinet one last time. * crouches by the skirting board and pulls a clean dust sheet toward the painting.* I'll wrap the canvas now and lay it flat in my boot before the rain starts.

**Ben:** *Presses the entry-phone button to buzz the street door open without moving toward the hall, stepping across to place a foot lightly on the edge of Ada's dust sheet.* He knows where the meter cupboard is, Ada—leave the canvas against the wall a minute. Everything the movers didn't tag is supposed to go in the middle of the floor so the four of us divide what's left by the same rule we used on the books, one pick each in reverse order or out of a hat, before anything goes in anybody's boot.

**Cora:** *Tosses a stray plastic curtain hook from shoulder height into her palm, strolling over to peer down between Ben's shoe and Ada's tape roll.* Oh, brilliant, a lottery—so one lucky winner gets the crooked kitchen window and the other three split half a bottle of limescale remover and this curtain ring? *Nudges the open corner of the frame lightly with the toe of her trainer.* Honestly, save the brown tape, Ada; my back seat's completely empty and I've got the exact nail hole waiting in my hall where the plaster's already ruined, so I can just sling it in mine and spare everybody a committee meeting.

**Dan:** *Steps in beside Cora and hooks two fingers under the sound side of the frame, tilting the open corner clear of her trainer.* Stop pretending this is about rain or drawing names out of a mug—all four of us want the one thing left in here that actually looks like her flat, and Ada didn't even bother inventing an errand for me before reaching for the dust sheet. Nobody's slinging it in a boot or a back seat with that mitre coming apart anyway; it's coming home with me so the corner actually gets glued and clamped before the canvas drops out of it.

**Ada:** *Tugs the dust sheet free of Ben's shoe and sets a firm hand along the top rail of the frame until Dan lowers its weight back onto the cloth.* Set it down, Dan—pulling on one side is what opens that mitre further, and I didn't give you a job because someone still needs to shut the bedroom transom before the agent comes through that door. We aren't drawing straws when there's nothing left to trade against it, Ben, and it certainly isn't rattling loose on Cora's back seat; I've already cleared the dry wall in my dining room for it, and I've got the corner clamps in the garage to set the joint properly tonight.

*The entry buzzer sounds again from downstairs—two short, impatient rings.*

**Ben:** *Holds down the intercom talk button with his thumb—*"Door's open, come straight up to the second floor"*—then clicks the flat door off the latch and steps right back to crouch at the other end of the frame opposite Ada.* Nobody needs to go downstairs, and nobody's taking permanent possession just because they own corner clamps, Ada. When four people have an equal claim on one indivisible item, the only fair rule is a fixed rotation—a year in each house on the calendar, in writing—and until all four of us sign off on the order, that dust sheet stays flat on the floorboards.

*The intercom speaker crackles from the street: "Cab here—not coming up two flights. I've wedged the front door, engine's running on the kerb, two minutes or I'm off."*

**Cora:** *Tosses the curtain hook from hand to hand with a quick grin toward the intercom, stepping casually onto the loose corner of Ada's dust sheet.* Ooh, somebody booked a getaway car—and here's Ben trying to draft a four-year custody treaty on the floorboards while the driver's leaning on the horn. You can't pass a loose-cornered canvas round four houses once a year like a library book, Ben; tell you what, let me hang it in mine so you lot don't spend every Christmas arguing over handover dates, and whoever misses staring at her old kitchen sink gets free tea at my place whenever they like.

**Dan:** *Leaves his palm pressed against the upright of the frame where it rests on the cloth, lifting one heel off the floorboards and holding it there as he shoots a look at the intercom.* That minicab's mine—I wasn't taking a loose mitre onto the bus—and he can drive right off, because I'm not going down two flights for thirty seconds while you three stitch this up behind my back. Ada's already cleared her dining-room wall without telling anyone, Ben wants a four-year contract, and Cora's offering visiting hours over a mug of tea like the rest of us don't count. Forget the rota and forget the tea; I'm carrying it home wrapped in my own coat if I have to, and that corner's getting fixed on my table tonight.

**Ada:** *Lets go of the frame for a second to fold her left shirt cuff twice into a hard, flat square, then presses her palm back over the top rail beside Dan's hand.* You booked a cab behind our backs before the boxes were even down the stairs, Dan, so don't lecture me about clearing a wall—and Cora, shipping it around four postcodes on Ben's rota is a guaranteed way to shake the stretcher apart before next spring. I'm the one who measured and drove the two brass hooks into that chimney breast for her when she moved in here because that crooked frame only sits square if you hang it from double wire, and I'm not handing the last piece of this flat over to a minicab boot or a tea-time rota now that the heavy lifting's done. Go tell your driver to cancel the fare or take the cab yourself, Dan, because I'm locking up for the agent and this canvas is coming home with the paperwork in my car.

**Ben:** *Slides his fingers firmly along the bottom rail opposite Ada and Dan, looking down for a second at the painted windowpane before his breath catches audibly right on her name.* Putting up two hooks here doesn't make it part of the paperwork, Ada—she carried this window into every flat she ever lived in long before this chimney breast. If moving it every year risks the stretcher, then standard fair practice when four people dispute a fragile piece is neutral custody: nobody who jumped the queue with a cleared wall, an empty nail hole, or a secret minicab gets to walk out with it. I'm the only one here who didn't pre-arrange a spot behind everyone's back, so it goes flat in my car tonight, straight to a proper framer on Monday to get that corner pinned on a shared bill, and nobody hangs it anywhere until all four of us sign the same piece of paper.

## Scene 2 — *The entrance*

**The entrance.** The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.

*Carried forward from the flat:* With no one willing to leave anyone else upstairs alone with the canvas, the flat above has been locked and all four of you have come down the two flights together. The painting—draped loosely in the untaped dust sheet, its corner still unfixed—rests upright just inside the threshold of the propped-open front door; no single sibling has possession of it, and who takes it home remains unsettled.

**Cora:** *Leans against the doorframe beside the propped-open door, spinning her car keys round one finger as she glances out at the idling cab, then casually tucks the loose edge of the dust sheet around the upright frame with her free hand.* Well, Dan's driver has earned a tip just for watching four grown adults march an old bedsheet down two flights in formation like we've nicked a Rembrandt. Tell you what, Ben—since you're suddenly Switzerland, there's a workshop two doors from my dry cleaner's; let me pop it on my back seat now, I'll get the corner pinned first thing Monday on my own tab so nobody's chasing quarters of an invoice, and it can just sit behind my sofa till we're all speaking again.

**Dan:** *Steps onto the threshold between Cora and the street, peeling her tucked corner of the dust sheet back off the split mitre and gripping the bare wood.* I booked a cab because I'm the only one of us without a car, Ada, not a heist—and drop the "neutral custody" and "behind the sofa" act, both of you, because we all know whoever drives away from this kerb with it is keeping it for good now the flat's empty. No stranger next to a dry cleaner's is putting pins in that corner on Monday anyway; fixing that frame is my job, and my cab's the only car actually sitting right there at the kerb, so take your hand off the sheet, Cora.

**Ada:** *Slips the tagged key envelope into her coat pocket, steps into the doorway beside Dan to pull the dust sheet back over the exposed canvas, and takes a steady grip on the opposite stile.* Keep the cloth over it, Dan—the damp's blowing straight off the pavement onto bare oil, and you are not marching an unglued frame into the back of a minicab while the meter's running. Nobody's leaving it behind Cora's sofa or handing it to some High Street framer on Ben's committee tab when I've carried every key, inventory list, and utility closing for six weeks without losing a single receipt. Cora, kick that wooden wedge out of the hinge before we jam the communal latch, and Dan, wave your driver off—my estate's parked two doors down, the boot's flat and dry, and I'm taking charge of this frame just like I took charge of the rest.

**Ben:** *Steps squarely onto the front step outside the threshold so neither Dan nor Ada can carry the frame past him toward the pavement, setting one palm against the top rail while pulling a biro from his jacket pocket.* Leave the wedge where it is, Cora, because doing six weeks of meter readings doesn't entitle the person holding all the keys and paperwork to award herself the one unallocated thing left in the building. Basic rule in any fair division: you separate the paperwork from the disputed item, you don't let Cora buy a claim by paying a workshop tab solo, and you don't put bare oil across the back seat of a metered minicab. If Dan's afraid whoever drives off from this kerb keeps it for good, fine—pull a blank slip out of that key envelope, write down a strict seven-day holding limit, and all three of you can film me signing it on this step before I lay the frame flat in my car across the street.

*At the kerb, the idling minicab gives a short tap of its horn, exhaust curling across the wet pavement.*

**Cora:** *Her fingers go suddenly still around the key ring; a second later she drops the keys into her pocket with a quick laugh and hooks two fingers lightly under the bottom edge of the frame between Ada and Dan.* Nobody's filming a hostage video on the front step, Ben, and you can kick your own wedge, Ada. Look, I've made do for years with the blurry snapshot of that window she posted me when I first moved away, so let's save Dan's cab fare: wave the driver off, Dan can ride back to mine right now and glue his precious mitre on my kitchen table, I'll run him home after, and the real thing can hang on my wall till you three actually agree on something—which, at current speed, gives me till about twenty-forty.

**Dan:** *Sets his second hand under the bottom rail beside Cora's fingers instead of signalling the cab, his right heel lifting off the doormat and staying held in the air.* I'm not riding back to yours like an odd-job man to glue it on your table and get dropped home empty-handed, Cora—she asked me to fix this frame, not tidy it up for your wall. Put the biro away too, Ben, because seven-day slips and Ada's six weeks of receipts are just polite ways of dodging the truth: the flat's locked behind us, and whoever walks away from this step without that window goes home with nothing of hers left. It isn't going to be me just because you three have cars parked outside and expect me to wave my ride off, so clear the step while my cab's still running.

*At the kerb, the minicab's brake lights flare red as the driver shifts into gear, passenger window rolling halfway down into the drizzle.*

**Ada:** *Presses her hip against the upright stile to hold it fast on the mat, refolding her cuff with her free hand into a tight, hard square before clamping both palms back onto the shrouded frame.* So Cora got a photograph in the post, Dan got asked to glue a corner he never came back to mend, and you think I'm walking home with nothing of hers in my pocket except final gas bills and the agent's keys, Ben? I stood on a kitchen chair on my own the day she moved in here to hang that crooked window where she could see it from the kettle, and I'm not signing your seven-day slip on the doorstep like a courier now the flat's cleared. Your cab's pulling off, Dan—let Ben run you to the tube once we're done, and take your hand off the rail so I can carry this two doors down to my boot before the drizzle soaks through the sheet.

**Ben:** *Clicks the biro shut, drops it into his pocket, and plants both hands firmly on the shrouded top rail between Ada's and Dan's grips, his breath catching audibly right before her name.* Trading a posted snapshot, an unglued joint, and six weeks of gas bills doesn't turn a four-way split into a three-way auction, Ada—and refusing to stand in the drizzle bidding memories of that window after dark doesn't forfeit my quarter or make me Dan's lift to the tube. If nobody's signing a doorstep slip, then the rule is simple: this canvas doesn't go into a boot unless all four of us are sitting in the same car watching where it goes. Let the minicab pull off and leave your estate locked, because either you three get into mine right now and ride with it to a paid lock-up under four signatures, or all four of us stay standing on this mat until the streetlamps come on.

**Cora:** *Slips her other hand from her pocket to cup the loose mitred corner lightly through the dust sheet, flashing a grin across the top of the frame toward the kerb.* A paid lock-up, Ben—honestly, she lugged that kitchen window into every flat she ever lived in, and your big rescue plan is locking it in a tin garage where nobody sees it except the spiders? Fine, if all four of us have to ride in the same car watching it, pile into mine—we'll hang it on my hall nail just to air the damp off the canvas, I'll buy the takeaway, and you three can spend all night arguing over the paperwork in my sitting room while Dan saves his cab fare.

*At the kerb, the minicab's passenger window slides shut; its indicator clicks once and the car pulls away into the drizzle, leaving the kerb empty.*

**Dan:** *Watches the minicab's tail-lights disappear into the drizzle without loosening either hand on the bottom rail, wedging his shoulder hard against the inside of the doorframe.* Yeah, I kept putting it off while she was still upstairs, Ada—which is why nobody's packing me off to the tube empty-handed while the three of you settle things once I'm gone. And forget piling into Ben's car for a tin garage or Cora's for a takeaway and a "temporary" hall nail, because whoever's holding the ignition keys still decides where that window stays tonight. If all four of us have to watch where it goes, leave your three cars locked on the street and ride the bus back to mine while I glue that corner properly—or let the streetlamps come on, because I'm not taking my hands off this wood.

# Director's Note

### 1. Staging Choices & Variations Within the Fixed Play
- **Strict performer isolation & observable-only acting:** Each sibling ran in a separate read-only sub-context given only the shared premise, Piece, Scene Opening, their own Persona row and single private memory sentence from `company.md`, and the instruction in `promptbook.md`. Performers were directed to produce strictly observable speech and physical action inside the space so turns could be relayed verbatim without leaking private thoughts.
- **Bridging the open condition between the two Openings:** `company.md` leaves open *who* rings the entry buzzer in Scene 1 (*The flat*) and *whose* single car idles at the kerb beside the propped-open front door in Scene 2 (*The entrance*). Ada initially assumed the buzzer was the letting agent, and Ben buzzed the street door open from upstairs without going down. When the exchange kept circling the canvas, I introduced a double buzz and, once Ben pressed the intercom talk button, answered from the street as a minicab driver who had wedged the front door and was idling on a two-minute fuse at the kerb—leaving open *which* sibling had ordered the cab. Dan immediately claimed it on his next turn (*"That minicab's mine—I wasn't taking a loose mitre onto the bus"*).
- **Escalating kerb pressure in Scene 2:** Starting from Scene 2's fixed opening (`One car idles at the kerb`), I introduced three incremental beats of street pressure as the siblings remained deadlocked in the doorway—a short horn tap (before Turn 5), brake lights flaring as the cab shifted into gear (before Turn 7), and the cab finally pulling away into the drizzle before Turn 10—forcing Dan to choose between keeping his ride or keeping his hands on the frame.

### 2. What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*):** Ada tried to dispatch Ben and Cora on errands while wrapping the painting for her own boot; Ben stepped onto the dust sheet and proposed a lottery, then a written annual rotation, and finally "neutral custody" in his own car to a Monday framer; Cora undercut both with sideways offers (her ruined hall plaster, open-house tea); Dan called out the evasion and gripped the frame to fix the open mitre himself. Because `promptbook.md` barred any easy compromise, Scene 1 did not rest on an agreement and reached the 10-turn cap in a physical four-way stand-off around the dust sheet.
- **Carry-over to Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** Since none of the four would leave the others alone with the canvas (explicitly voiced by Dan in Turn 8) while the flat had to be locked, all four carried the untaped, dust-sheeted painting down the two flights together and set it upright inside the propped-open front door. Nobody held sole possession, the corner remained unfixed, Dan's minicab idled at the kerb, and every competing claim carried forward intact.
- **Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** At the threshold, the physical geometry tightened—Ben blocked the front step with a biro and a "seven-day slip" rule, then escalated to demanding all four ride together to a paid lock-up; Cora slipped into offering a Monday workshop tab, a ride for Dan, and takeaway at hers; Ada pulled rank on six weeks of clearing the flat and hanging the double wire for Mother; Dan let his minicab drive away into the rain rather than take his hands off the bottom rail. All four private memories surfaced organically under pressure—Ada's double hooks on the chimney breast, Cora's posted snapshot, Ben's guarded slip about *"memories of that window after dark"* while refusing to bid them aloud, and Dan's admission that he kept putting off fixing the corner while Mother was alive.

### 3. Where the Authored Material & Promptbook Helped, Had No Occasion to Matter, or Made Staging Hard
- **What helped:** The pairing of `Load Bearing` (*nothing left in the flat to carry their private memories of Mother*) with the physical detail of the `open corner` in `company.md` gave all four Personas concrete handholds—tape, dust sheet, mitre, clamps, damp—so an emotional fight could be staged through physical blocking rather than exposition. `promptbook.md` kept the energy taut and revealed Ben's and Cora's Shadows brilliantly: every time a "fair rule" or "light joke" was blocked, both had to invent narrower rules and sideways bargains that exposed how badly they wanted the painting in *their* car tonight.
- **Staging friction (Promptbook vs. natural scene rest & transition):** Because `promptbook.md` requires every Persona to meet *any* attempt to settle with a fresh claim, deflection, or block, neither scene could reach a consensual resting point; both ran to the 10-turn cap, and moving physically from *The flat* to *The entrance* required a directorial cut between scenes (marching the disputed canvas downstairs together after locking up) rather than a character-agreed exit upstairs. That is a direct consequence of the Promptbook's stress-test constraint rather than a flaw in `company.md`.
- **Minor friction in `company.md` (Ben's Tell):** Ben's Tell is *"Catches his breath on ordinary names,"* while `company.md` names the four siblings (`Ada`, `Ben`, `Cora`, `Dan`) and refers to their mother only as `Mother` without giving her an ordinary given name. As a result, Ben's performer twice described his breath catching *"right on her name"* / *"right before her name"* in italics just before addressing *"Ada"* in dialogue while referring to Mother as *"she"*. If the Tell is meant to catch on *Mother's* given name (or a place/street name), supplying it in `company.md`—or clarifying that it catches on his siblings' ordinary names—would sharpen that beat; separately, `"Resale value: none"` in `Piece` never needed to be debated aloud once all four treated the canvas purely as an irreplaceable keepsake, though it quietly prevented anyone from proposing a sale-and-split rule.
