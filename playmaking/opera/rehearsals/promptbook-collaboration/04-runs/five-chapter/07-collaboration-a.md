# Performance: *Mother's Painting*

## Scene 1: *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Ada:** *Ada crosses to the wall by the door, rests a finger over the intercom button without pressing it yet, and glances down at the painting propped against the skirting board.*

That'll be the letting agent for the meter readings and the spare set. Ben, go down and walk him through the cellar cupboard—you've got the torch on your phone. Cora, check the bathroom cabinet one last time, and Dan, bring that roll of bubble wrap in from the landing so I can wrap the frame before the loose corner catches on my boot liner.

**Dan:** *Dan steps over to the skirting board instead of the door and stops beside the painting, his right heel lifted and held just off the bare floorboards.*
"Drop the rota, Ada—the bathroom cabinet's been empty for an hour. All four of us are still standing in here because nobody wants to leave without the picture. So when did it get decided it's going in your boot—just now, or while I was down at the van?"

**Ada:** *Ada presses the intercom button firmly, speaks into the grille—"Come up to the second floor, the latch is off"—then lets her hand drop and looks straight at Dan.*

Nobody decided anything while you were at the van, Dan. The top mitre's split clean open—if you hang it as it is, the glass and canvas come straight out the bottom, so it needs gluing and clamping before it goes on anybody's wall. I've got the paperwork, the council forms, and the spare keys in my bag already; taking the frame home to mend it was just the last job left in the room, unless Ben or Cora came up the stairs planning to say otherwise.

**Ben:** *Ben stays where he is instead of heading for the door, slipping his phone back into his trouser pocket and glancing once toward the stairwell before looking at Ada.*
"He knows where the cellar cupboard is, Ada—it's his agency's padlock on it. And anybody can buy a tube of wood glue and a clamp on the way home; once that frame goes in someone's boot 'for mending,' it isn't coming back out again. If all four of us are still standing here for it, we put it under the same rule for everybody before he reaches the landing: twelve months on each wall, and we draw right now for who takes the first year. Cora, are you in?"

**Cora:** *Cora peels a stray tab of brown tape from her sleeve, rolling it lightly between finger and thumb as she steps out of the bathroom doorway.*
"Twelve months each—what, with handovers at motorway services every November? Listen to the three of you over five quid's worth of crooked kitchen window. Tell you what, save tearing up raffle tickets before the agent hits the landing: let me tuck it under my arm for the train tonight—it'll just about cover the damp patch in my hall—and the first one of you who actually makes the trip up to mine for lunch can nick it straight back off the hook."

**Ben:** *Ben pulls a folded receipt from his trouser pocket, tears it briskly into four strips—nipping one short—and lines the tops up level inside his closed fist, extending his arm toward the middle of the room; only on his sister's name does his breath snag.*
"Nobody's taking an unglued frame onto a train tonight, Cora, and nobody's hanging it over a damp patch. Fine—scrap the twelve months if motorway handovers are a joke, and scrap holding it up at yours until one of us comes for lunch. Four strips, one short, one draw for keeps before he reaches the landing. Dan, you're stood right by it—pull first."

**Dan:** *Dan doesn't reach for the strips; he crouches beside the skirting board and presses the split top corner of the frame together between his thumb and forefinger.*
"Put the receipt away, Ben—we're not raffling off the last thing left of Mum's on a torn-up till slip. She asked me twice before summer to bring my clamp round and glue this corner, and I kept telling her next week, so it isn't just a job for your bag, Ada, and it isn't five quid's worth of damp-cover. I'm taking it home—unless one of you wants to drop the act before he hits the landing and say out loud why you actually need it."

**Ada:** *Ada unbuttons her wrist and folds her cuff twice into a hard square, her eyes fixed on Dan's thumb pressing the split mitre.*

Because I'm the one who hung it for her when she moved in here, Dan. That corner was already parting then—I had to drive a second pin into the plaster to take the strain off the wire while she sat on a packing crate telling me, "Up a fraction on the left, Ada." Once that agent hits the landing, my list is finished, and everything I'm taking out of this flat is council forms and final bills in my handbag. Don't tell me doing the work was an act—and put your hand down, Ben, I'm not drawing a till receipt to see if I'm allowed one thing of Mum's that isn't admin.

**Cora:** *Cora's fingers go suddenly still on the rolled tab of brown tape. Then she drops it into her coat pocket and gives Dan a quick, light laugh.*
"All right, keep your hair on, Dan—there isn't even a damp patch, I was just trying to save us a pile-up before the agent walks in. Mum sent me a photo of that window when I first moved away, just so I'd know she had it up; that's the only version I've had while Ada was driving pins in the plaster and you were promising clamps. So call it a fair swap—let me take the actual paint home tonight, and I'll post you lot the photo for Ben's raffle; corners on mine aren't even split."

*Footsteps reach the second-floor landing outside the flat door.*

**Ben:** *Ben lowers his arm, pockets the four receipt strips, and glances once at the flat door before looking down at his brother, his breath catching short on Dan's name.*
"Dan—don't talk to me like I tore up a till slip because I've got nothing in that picture. Before she ever carried it from flat to flat, I used to sit with her at that actual window when I couldn't sleep. So that's four of us with a claim on it, and he's right outside on the landing. If a draw isn't good enough, take your hand off the corner and we put it under the one rule nobody can rig for themselves: one vote each, out loud before that door opens, and nobody says their own name. Ada, you're eldest—call yours first."

*A knuckle raps twice on the unlatched flat door.*

**Ada:** *Ada steps to the unlatched door, presses her palm flat against the panel to keep it shut, and calls evenly toward the landing—"Just a moment, please, I'm getting the spare keys out"—before pulling the key ring from her handbag and looking back at her brothers and sister.*

All right, Ben. You want the eldest to go first so this is settled before he comes through the door, I'll do my part. If nobody says their own name, mine goes to Ben—you sat with her at that window before any of the flats, and you won't carry an open mitre loose onto a train tonight. Dan, take your hand off the corner and call yours, and Cora, get yours ready—because in twenty seconds I'm taking my hand off this door and walking him down to the meters.

**Dan:** *Dan straightens up beside the skirting board, lets go of the split corner, and stands with his right heel lifted and held off the floorboards.*
"Ben gets the window, Cora gets ruled out for the train, and you don't even bother ruling me out, Ada—just 'take your hand off.' One word about me putting off that clamp while Mum was alive and I'm not even in the running. Nice setup too, Ben, calling on the eldest first when you knew she'd martyr herself with the council bills and hand hers straight to you. Mine goes to Cora, because a photo in the post isn't a share—so that's Ben and Cora on one each, and two of you left before that door opens to show whether anybody here was ever going to say my name."

*Outside on the landing, the letting agent clears his throat and eases the unlatched door inward against Ada's hand, clipboard in view.*

**Cora:** *Cora steps across to the skirting board beside Dan, angling her shoulder between the frame and the widening crack of the door, and flashes him a quick grin before looking at Ben.*
"Careful, Dan, back me up in front of witnesses and you'll ruin our whole reputation. Mine goes to Dan—there, somebody said your name, and now you're actually on the hook for that clamp. Over to you before the clipboard makes it all the way in, Ben: say my name and I'll spring for a cab to the station, plus a spare chair by that window at mine whenever you can't sleep—though my kettle wakes the dead, so I won't blame you if you pick his glue instead."

*The letting agent steps into the doorway with his clipboard and glances down at the skirting board: "Sorry to hurry you—I just need the walls clear for the checkout photos and one set of keys."*

**Ben:** *Ben steps to the skirting board, slides one palm under the bottom rail of the frame and the other along its sound side, and lifts the painting clear of the plaster so the wall stands bare.*
"You left one out, Cora—mine goes to Ada, because she isn't walking out with a bag full of final bills as the only one nobody named. That puts all four of us on one vote each, dead level, so nobody's written off and nobody loses it for good. It does twelve months on each wall in the order our names were just called—mine first, then Cora's, then Dan's, then Ada's—and I'll glue and clamp the corner tonight before it hangs anywhere. The wall's clear for the photo; I'm wrapping this on the landing unless somebody wants to stand here in front of him and argue that one vote each isn't fair."

## Scene 2: *The entrance*

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb—a minicab waiting to take Cora to the station. The empty flat has been handed over; Ben holds the painting wrapped in the bubble wrap from the landing.*

**Dan:** *Dan steps out onto the threshold in front of the propped door, turns squarely into Ben's path, and sets his palm against the bubble-wrapped side of the frame.*
"The bloke with the clipboard's upstairs now, Ben, so hold on. You gave Ada your vote so she wouldn't walk out with just the bills, then invented 'order our names were called' on the spot so you take it tonight, glue the corner Mum asked me to mend, put Cora in that cab empty-handed, and still leave Ada holding nothing but council forms for three years. You said it yourself ten minutes ago—once that goes home with someone tonight, it isn't coming back off their wall. None of us agreed to your rota upstairs, and I'm taking it home to put my clamp on it—so let go of the wrap."

**Ben:** *Ben keeps both hands firm on the bubble-wrapped frame, angling the split top corner back out of reach of Dan's palm without yielding his place on the step.*
"Mind that top mitre, Dan—push against the wrap and you'll open the other corner before it gets anywhere. Don't lecture me about Cora's cab and council forms when you've just told all three of us that 'putting your clamp on it' means keeping it for good." *He glances past Dan's shoulder toward the idling minicab and looks at his eldest sister, his breath catching short on her name.* "Ada—if nobody trusts a promise made upstairs, we put four handover dates in writing on our phones right now before Cora's driver pulls off, and nobody walks away with the first year unless two of the others clear them off this pavement. You've heard Dan say his is for keeps; say who takes Year One."

**Ada:** *Ada shifts her handbag higher onto her shoulder, steps onto the threshold between Ben and Dan, and slides one steady hand under the sound bottom rail of the bubble-wrapped frame.*

You told me to call first upstairs because I'm eldest, Ben, and then used that exact order to put yourself first and leave me waiting three years with the council forms. The keys are handed in and my list is finished—I'm not standing on the step refereeing which of my brothers walks off with it; I'm taking Year One in my car tonight. Dan, bring your glue and clamp round to mine on Sunday so you mend the corner like you promised her, and Ben, put the handover dates on our phones right now with Cora down for next November once the frame's solid for a train. If it takes two of you to clear me off this pavement before that cab pulls away—Cora, tell them whether that's straight, and both of you take your hands off the wrap.

**Cora:** *Cora draws her phone from her coat pocket, then her fingers go suddenly still around the dark screen. She drops it straight back into her pocket untouched, takes half a step toward the idling minicab, and gestures lightly at the three of them.*
"Oh, dead straight, Ada—you and Dan get Sunday afternoon round yours with the wood glue while I ride off with a calendar alert for next November. Look at the three of you clamped onto one roll of bubble wrap; pull any harder and we'll get one side of the frame each. Tell you what, since none of you trusts the other two across town with it: slide it flat on my back seat while it's wrapped, I'll buy Dan's return ticket up to mine on Sunday with his clamp, and Ben can text us all four November dates before I hit the station. Go on, two of you clear me before my driver gives up—or keep playing tug-of-war on the step and I'll just wave from the window like the Queen."

**Dan:** *Dan keeps his palm braced against the side of the wrapped frame, his right heel lifting and holding an inch clear of the step as he looks past Ada and Ben at Cora.*
"Stay out of the cab, Cora—you dropped your phone straight back in your pocket dark because none of us believes in four years of November alerts, and riding off waving only sticks us with settling it behind your back. And you and Ada can both drop 'come round on Sunday with your clamp' like I'm the odd-job man you toss an errand to for a second vote. 'Next Sunday' is what I told Mum till her flat was empty. Neither of you gets my clearance to do house calls—so before that driver pulls off, either two of you clear me to take it home tonight, or tell me to my face why my flat's only good enough to keep the toolbox in."

**Ben:** *Ben glances down at Ada's hand under the sound bottom rail, slowly lifts both palms clear of the bubble wrap, and steps half a pace back onto the pavement between the threshold and the idling minicab.*
"You're right, Dan—the rota's dead. Cora's screen never even lit up because none of us believes in November alerts, and offering you a Sunday errand with wood glue just to buy a vote wasn't straight. Whoever puts that frame in a car or a cab tonight keeps it for good, the way she kept it up in every flat. Taking my hands off doesn't take my claim out—it stops three of us snapping the mitre on the step, and stops Ada saying I'm putting myself first because I'm holding it. One rule for all four of us now, for keeps, no errands and no handover dates: set it against the doorpost, Cora stays out of that cab, and nobody lifts it off the stone until the other three step back for them."

**Ada:** *Ada brings her free hand quickly to the sound upright side to take the weight as Ben lets go, lowers the bubble-wrapped frame carefully against the dry wooden doorpost, and takes her hands off the wrap; she unbuttons her right wrist and folds the cuff twice into a hard square.*

Nothing's wrong with your flat, Dan, and Sunday wasn't an errand to buy your vote—it was the one way your clamp went on that corner like she asked and the four of us still ended up round the same table. Fine, Ben: no rotas, no favours, for keeps, and it's set against the post. Cora, motion your driver to hold on two minutes, because I'm not settling this behind a cab window either. All three of you stepped back quick enough when Mum moved in and I stood on a crate driving two pins into the plaster for that frame, and you stepped back all day while I signed off the van, read the meters, and put the final bills in my bag—so step back once more now the work's finished, and let me take her picture home.

**Cora:** *Cora turns toward the kerb, raises two fingers toward the minicab's windscreen, then faces the doorpost again and holds the mouth of her shoulder bag wide open toward Ada with a quick tilt of her head.*
"Two minutes on the meter, then—nobody's sneaking off. And don't lump me in, Ada—I didn't step back when Mum moved in; I'd moved away, and while you three had the actual kitchen, all I ever got of your two pins in the plaster was the photo she sent me. Tell you what, since it's for keeps: tip every last council form and final bill out of your handbag into mine right now—I've got a working biro and a long train home—and let me take the real window off that post for once. Go on, swap you the whole stack of utilities sight unseen—or say no quick before this cab costs me twenty quid just to lose three-nil."

**Ada:** *Ada closes her hand firmly over the zip of her own handbag against her hip, looking down once at Cora's open bag before meeting her eyes.*

No—pull your bag shut, Cora. My name's already on the checkout sheet for these bills; I'll finish dealing with them on Monday, and the minute they go in the post my handbag's as empty as yours. I know you'd already moved away when Mum came here, and you aren't losing three-nil on this step—Ben and Dan haven't budged an inch for me either. Nobody's swapping her window for a stack of utilities, so before your two minutes are up on that meter, either tell these two you're stepping back for me, or wave the driver on and stand here with the rest of us until three people step back.

*At the kerb, the minicab driver lowers the passenger window an inch and looks toward Cora.*

**Cora:** *Cora's fingers go suddenly still on the open mouth of her bag. Then she pulls the zip shut, steps to the kerb, and stoops to the cracked passenger window.*
"Go on without me, mate—keep the fare on the card." *She waves a brisk hand forward down the street and turns back onto the pavement facing Ada, Ben, and Dan.*
"There—bag's shut and my lift's waved off, Ada, so nobody's folding just to catch a train. Tell you what: all three of you step back off that post for me right now and Ada can run me and the frame to the station in her boot—much gentler on Ben and Dan than putting me up on a sofa all night while we outstare a roll of bubble wrap, and I warn you both now, I nick the good cushions."

*The minicab pulls away from the kerb down the street. Inside the hall, the letting agent's footsteps come down the last flight of stairs toward the propped-open front door.*

**Ben:** *As the footsteps come down the last stairs inside the hall, Ben steps to the threshold, eases the bubble-wrapped frame six inches off the wooden doorpost onto the outer brickwork clear of the door, and lifts both hands straight back off the wrap; turning back to the pavement, his breath hitches short on Cora's name.*
"Cora—you've asked for yourself, Ada's asked for herself, and Dan's asked for himself, like I only stood it there to referee who nicks my sofa cushions. I'm still awake at three in the morning too—that window isn't worth less to me because I didn't wave off a cab or hold onto a bag of council bills. None of us has budged, he's coming to unprop this door right now, and you've got no train lift left. So the same rule holds, moved indoors off the street: all four of us go back to mine, Cora gets the sofa, the wrap stays sealed against my hall skirting board tonight, and nobody touches it until three of us step back."

*The letting agent reaches the vestibule, kicks the wooden wedge free, gives the four of them a brief nod, and pulls the building's front door shut behind them until the latch clicks.*

**Dan:** *Dan steps between Ben and the outer brickwork without touching the wrap, glancing once at the locked front door before facing the other three.*
"That wrap isn't going onto your skirting board, Ben—once Cora catches a morning train and Ada and I leave for work, it's yours by default behind a shut door, and Mum's frame sits split inside plastic another night like I left it all summer. Look at us since that latch clicked: Ada only wanted Sunday to get us round one table, Cora threw away twenty quid not to ride off alone, and you're staging an all-night vigil so you aren't up at three by yourself. Nobody's budging because we all think whoever walks away empty-handed tonight loses the other three along with the flat. Cora can have my sofa tonight, and all three of you can sit round my table while I take that plastic off and finally clamp the corner I kept putting off for Mum. Step back for me right here on the pavement first, though—no morning stitch-up, and when you leave mine it stays on my wall."

## Director's Note

### 1. What Was Directed & Varied (Staging Choices vs. Fixed Play)
- **Strict isolation of briefs:** Each sibling performer received only the shared premise, Piece, scene opening, `promptbook.md` instruction, their own Persona row, and their own single private memory sentence.
- **Non-rotational cueing (`promptbook.md`):** Following `promptbook.md`, I passed play after every turn to whichever sibling was most directly struck or cornered by what was just said or done rather than using fixed rotation (Scene 1: `Ada -> Dan -> Ada -> Ben -> Cora -> Ben -> Dan -> Ada -> Cora -> Ben -> Ada -> Dan -> Cora -> Ben`; Scene 2: `Dan -> Ben -> Ada -> Cora -> Dan -> Ben -> Ada -> Cora -> Ada -> Cora -> Ben -> Dan`).
- **Pacing open environmental pressures:**
  - *In Scene 1:* The Play leaves open who rings the entry buzzer. Ada's opening move named him as the letting agent and her Turn 3 buzzed him up to the unlatched second-floor door; I paced his ascent along the stairwell (landing footsteps at Turn 10, knock at Turn 11, easing the door open at Turn 13, asking for clear walls and keys at Turn 14) so the room had to vacate naturally.
  - *In Scene 2:* The Play specifies `The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.` To prevent isolated performers from making conflicting claims about whose car was idling unattended, I specified the idling car as Cora's pre-booked minicab for the station (extending Cora's Scene 1 train detail), and brought the letting agent downstairs at Turn 11–12 to kick free the door wedge and latch the building shut.

### 2. What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*):** Ada tried to fold taking the painting into her final administrative sweep; Dan refused her errand and named that all four were lingering for the picture. As Ben cycled through three "common rules" (a twelve-month rotation draw, torn receipt strips, and a "nobody says their own name" vote), pressure forced all four private memories into the open. When Ada voted for Ben, Dan for Cora, and Cora for Dan, Ben's own rule trapped him: unable to vote for himself without breaking his rule or vote for Cora/Dan without losing the painting, Ben voted for Ada to engineer a 1–1–1–1 tie, lifted the frame off the skirting board, and used the letting agent's arrival as a shield to award himself Year One ("in the order our names were just called").
- **Carry-forward into Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** Ben carried the painting downstairs wrapped in the landing's bubble wrap; Ada had handed off the keys and meters (finishing her list); the 1–1–1–1 vote and Ben's unilateral claim on Year One remained completely unaccepted by the other three, alongside Ben's own Scene 1 admission that *"once that frame goes in someone's boot 'for mending,' it isn't coming back out again."*
- **Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** Out of the agent's earshot, Dan immediately blocked Ben on the step, and soon Ben, Dan, and Ada all had hands on the wrapped frame at once. When every rota collapsed, Ben stepped back and set a unanimous-consent rule ("set it against the doorpost... nobody lifts it off the stone until the other three step back"). Ada set it down and asked for keeps; Cora offered to take all of Ada's remaining utility bills in exchange for the painting, was refused when Ada clutched her handbag zip shut, and waved her minicab away down the street rather than fold to catch a train. Forced to shift the wrapped frame six inches onto the outer brickwork as the letting agent locked the building behind them, Ben finally admitted he is still awake at three in the morning too—yet still tried to move the sealed frame onto his own hall skirting board. Dan stepped between Ben and the brickwork, naming that none of them will let go because walking away empty-handed feels like losing the other three along with Mother's flat, offering his sofa and table to fix the frame tonight while still demanding they step back on the pavement so it stays on his wall.

### 3. Where the Play & Promptbook Helped, Had No Occasion to Matter, or Made Staging Hard
- **Helped:**
  - *`promptbook.md` (`# Collaboration`):* Breaking fixed rotation let two-person clashes play to the bone (e.g., `Ada -> Dan -> Ada` at the start of Scene 1, and `Ada -> Cora -> Ada -> Cora` over the handbag/cab in Scene 2) instead of diffusing tension around a polite circle.
  - *Interlocking Shadows, Tells, and physical Piece detail:* The single open mitre corner in `company.md` connected Ada's two pins in the plaster, Dan's postponed clamp, Cora's train risk, and Ben's sleepless window without requiring directorial invention.
- **Made staging hard (Play gap vs. staging constraint):**
  - *Promptbook vs. isolated performer sub-contexts (staging architecture tension):* `promptbook.md` asks that *"whichever sibling is most affected by what was just said or done responds next... passing play directly from sibling to sibling."* Because performers were isolated from one another's private Shadows and memories, the performers could not self-select who spoke next; the Director had to adjudicate "most affected" after each move.
  - *Unassigned shared props across isolated actors (open Play condition):* Open conditions like `Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer` and `One car idles at the kerb` work effortlessly in a shared writers' room, whereas four isolated subagents risk simultaneously claiming the same car or inventing incompatible callers unless the Director pins down or paces those shared facts.
  - *Convergence toward deadlock (Play structure):* Because all four want the painting home, it cannot be divided or sold, and it carries the last trace of Mother (`Load Bearing`), stripping away false rotas naturally drives the cast into a four-way refusal to yield; resting Scene 2 once the emotional truth of that deadlock was fully articulated prevented repetitive "step back for me" turns.
- **Little/no occasion to matter:**
  - `Owner: This rehearsal` is meta-bookkeeping outside the fiction, and `It has no resale value` surfaced only once as a passing joke ("five quid's worth of crooked kitchen window") since monetary value was never at stake for any sibling.
