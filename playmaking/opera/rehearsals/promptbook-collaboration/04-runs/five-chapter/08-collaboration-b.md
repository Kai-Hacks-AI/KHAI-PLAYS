# *Mother's Painting* — Two-Scene Performance

## Scene 1: *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**ADA:** *(Crosses to the entry phone by the door and holds down the talk button without pressing the release.)* "All signed—you're fine to pull out, thank you." *(Lets the button go, picks up a leftover sheet of bubble wrap from the windowsill, and kneels beside the painting at the skirting board.)* "Right, the van's off. Ben, get the numbers out of the meter cupboard before the light goes, and Cora, check the bathroom shelf's wiped—I'll get this wrapped and flat in my boot before that split corner catches on anything."

**DAN:** *(Steps across the bare floorboards and sets the toe of his shoe on the loose edge of the bubble wrap.)* "Hold on. Giving Ben and Cora busywork doesn't make that yours, Ada—unless the three of you already sorted out who gets it before I walked up the stairs."

**ADA:** *(Leaves the bubble wrap pinned under his shoe and steadies the painting upright, pressing the split corner of the frame together between thumb and forefinger.)* "Nobody sorted anything behind your back, Dan, and getting the keys handed over by five isn't busywork. This corner's come right open—it needs gluing and clamping before it's hung anywhere, and neither Ben nor Cora said a word about taking it when we were tagging the boxes."

**BEN:** *(Stops short of the meter cupboard, pulls the leftover strip of box labels from his coat pocket, and creases four blank squares to tear them clean.)* "Nobody said a word earlier because the labels were for the van, Ada—anything loose waited till the rooms were empty. You don't get first claim for holding the bubble wrap, and Dan doesn't get it for standing on it. Either we fold names on the sill right now or we rotate it twelve months apiece, all four of us under the exact same rule."

**CORA:** *(Strolls back from the bathroom doorway, twirling the damp microfibre cloth around two fingers, and eyes Ben's strip of labels.)* "Oh, brilliant, a twelve-month custody rota—who pays the postage when the corner falls off in the post, Ben? Tell you what, save your little raffle tickets: my hall wall's got a patch of damp plaster that's practically crying out for a wonky kitchen window, so I'll happily spare the three of you the eyesore and stick it on the train with me tonight."

**BEN:** *(Sets the four torn labels in a neat line along the windowsill and pulls a thick box marker from his coat pocket.)* "Fine—scratch the rota, nobody's putting an open mitre in the post, or hanging oil over damp plaster either. One draw settles it for good. Pretending you're doing us a favour taking an eyesore doesn't let you walk out with it, Cora, any more than owning wood glue gives it to Ada. One cross, four folded slips—unless one of you wants to say straight out you're passing and make it three."

**DAN:** *(Steps off the bubble wrap to the windowsill and pushes Ben's neat line of four blank labels into a single pile.)* "Put the marker away, Ben—I'm not passing, and we're not drawing raffle tickets off the sill just so nobody in this family has to say an honest sentence. Mum carried that painting into every flat she ever lived in, and now the rooms are bare I actually want it on my wall—so drop the act about wood glue and damp plaster and say straight out why you're still standing here for it."

**CORA:** *(Her fingers go suddenly still around the damp cloth; a beat later she tosses it onto the radiator with a quick laugh.)* "God, Dan, are we holding auditions now—best speech on the bare floorboards takes the prize? Relax, Ben, I'm not passing, it's just my kitchen faces a brick airshaft—let me borrow a painted window over the kettle for a bit, and the day I move somewhere with actual daylight, Dan, you can come take it off my hook yourself."

**ADA:** *(Slides the freed sheet of bubble wrap neatly under the base of the frame, rises, and folds her sleeve cuff into a hard square.)* "Nobody's hanging oil paint over a boiling kettle, Cora, and the two of you aren't striking a side deal over Ben's head and mine. It isn't an act about wood glue, Dan—I was the last one to hang it for her, right up on that wall, nursing that split corner onto the hook so it sat straight. Pick that wet cloth off the radiator before it marks the paintwork, Cora, because we're not settling this with kettle steam or raffle tickets."

**BEN:** *(Caps the marker, gathers the four blank labels off the sill, and kneels to fold the bubble wrap snugly over the painted window, taping it shut around the split corner with two unmarked squares before pocketing the other two.)* "Cora's right about one thing—we're not standing in bare rooms holding auditions over who hung the hook last or who sat up by that window longest," *(his breath catches short in his throat)* "—Dan. All four of us are still in the flat, and that makes four equal claims whether you call a rule an act or not." *(Tucks the wrapped painting upright under one arm, snaps a quick phone photo inside the meter cupboard, and pulls the front door open onto the landing.)* "It's five o'clock. This stays sealed under my arm down the stairs—not in Ada's boot, and not on Cora's train—until the keys are handed back and we settle it under one rule outside."

## Scene 2: *The entrance*

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.*

**CORA:** *(Steps out past the propped front door, hitches her overnight bag higher on her shoulder, and flashes two fingers toward the windscreen of the idling minicab before turning back to Ben.)* "Right, my cab's burning diesel at the kerb and the five-forty-two isn't going to wait for a constitutional convention. Tell you what—slide the hostage onto the back seat with me now so I make my train, and once you three finish drafting Ben's rulebook on the pavement, the winner gets a free roast at mine when they come up to claim it—Ada, you can even pack your wood clamps."

**DAN:** *(Steps out onto the pavement between Ben and the minicab's rear door, hands shoved in his coat pockets.)* "Save the Sunday roast invite, Cora—we just handed back the keys to the only place that ever got the four of us in the same room, and we all know nobody's taking a train up to yours to pry it off your wall. Whoever puts that frame in a car tonight keeps it for good, so it's not going on that back seat on an IOU—either tell him to kill the meter while we settle it here, or catch the five-forty-two without it."

**CORA:** *(Her fingers go suddenly still on the strap of her overnight bag; a beat later she steps back from the kerb toward the entrance, leaving the minicab idling behind her.)* "Charming—good to know my spare room's such a tough sell, Dan. Fine, let the meter tick; there's a six-forty-two. Look, Mum sent me a photo of that painting when I first moved away—I've done my stint with the glossy print, so let me upgrade to the real split frame tonight and I'll post you three the snapshot to fight over, no wood glue required."

**ADA:** *(Unprops the building's front door so the latch clicks shut behind them, then steps back onto the pavement and presses two fingers lightly against the taped split corner under Ben's arm.)* "Signal your driver to switch the engine off then, Cora, if there's a six-forty-two. You've already got a print Mum put in the post for you—now those keys are handed back, all I've got in my pocket is the van receipt, and two paper labels aren't holding an open mitre together. Let me lay it flat in my boot and clamp the frame properly at mine; if Dan reckons losing the flat means the four of us never stand in the same room again, you all know where my kitchen is once it's up."

**BEN:** *(Steps back onto the dry doorstep so the taped corner clears Ada's fingers, sets the wrapped frame upright between his shoes, and pulls the last two labels and the marker from his coat pocket—tearing the two squares into four strips and numbering them one to four against the back of his phone.)* "Send a photo of the van receipt round tonight and we'll split it four ways, Ada—paying the movers doesn't buy the frame, and hanging it in your kitchen for the rest of us to visit isn't any fairer than Cora's Sunday roast." *(Pockets the phone and marker, folds the four numbered strips shut, and holds them out on his flat palm.)* "No post, and no IOUs. We draw one to four right here: One clamps the mitre and keeps it twelve months, and a year from tonight Two knocks on their door to carry it home in person. That keeps the corner whole, gets us in the same room once a year, and still makes the five-forty-two if Cora pulls One—same terms for all four of us."

**DAN:** *(Looks down at the four folded strips on Ben's palm and doesn't touch them; his right heel lifts clear of the pavement and holds there.)* "Two knocking on One's door once a year isn't the four of us in the same room, Ben—it's five minutes on a doormat with the other two left out of it, and whoever pulls Four gets shut out for three years. Put the slips away, because nobody else is gluing that corner: Mum asked me to fix that frame, and I kept telling her I'd do it next time I came round until there wasn't a next time—so I'm taking it home tonight and fixing it myself."

**ADA:** *(Steps onto the edge of the doorstep between Dan and the wrapped frame at Ben's shoes, folding her sleeve cuff into a hard square.)* "That corner was already split in my hands when I hung it for her, Dan—because I didn't say 'next time', I got on with putting it up so she had her window on the wall. Owing her a repair you left too late doesn't mean you walk off with the painting for good tonight. Keep your money for the van, Ben, and pocket those slips: the frame goes flat in my boot tonight, Dan can bring his glue round on Sunday to finish the job she actually asked him for, and Cora isn't dragging an open mitre onto the six-forty-two."

**BEN:** *(Leaves the four folded strips sitting on his open palm, drops his free hand to grip the top edge of the wrapped frame between his shoes, and holds his ground on the step.)* "I'm not pocketing anything—I didn't carry this down the stairs just to split a van receipt. When I couldn't sleep, I used to sit with her at that window till it got light, and I want it home on my wall tonight just as badly as either of you," *(his breath catches abruptly)* "Ada." *(Shifts his open palm half an inch closer to Cora and Dan while keeping his grip on the frame.)* "Nobody's carving Cora and me out over a pot of glue on Sunday. Dan fixes the mitre wherever One hangs it—even if that means getting on a train instead of saying next time—and every year all four of us stand in the same room on the day it changes walls. Take a strip while that cab's still here, because nobody walks off with this frame on a side deal."

**CORA:** *(Steps up to the doorstep, plucks one folded strip lightly off Ben's open palm without unfolding it, and holds it pinched between two fingers like a raffle stub.)* "A glossy snapshot from years ago isn't a buyout, Ada, and the back seat of that cab is just as flat as your boot. Go on, I'm in—I won't peek till the pair of you clear Ben's hand, and if I pull One tonight, I'll buy Dan's return train ticket up to mine myself so he can glue that corner on my table before anybody gets to say 'next time'."

**DAN:** *(Sets his right heel flat back on the pavement, reaches out to lift one folded strip from Ben's palm, and keeps it pressed shut under his thumb.)* "I don't want a Sunday pass to your kitchen behind their backs, Ada—and if Cora pulls One, she doesn't need to buy my ticket, I'll be on the train with a clamp this week myself. Nobody's taking that frame off a wall twelve months from tonight unless all four of us are actually standing in the room, not stuck in a group chat making excuses—so stop acting like the van receipt makes you Mum, Ada, and pick up a strip before Cora misses the six-forty-two as well."

## Director's Note

### What Was Varied or Directed (Staging Choices vs. Authored Play)
- **Subagent setup:** `define_subagent` failed in this environment (`creating agent directory: generic::failed_precondition: sandbox is stopped`), so the four isolated performers were launched using the built-in read-only `research` subagent (no write tools, no subagent tools, no MCP tools, and instructed never to inspect disk files). Each sibling received only the shared situation, the Piece, the active scene opening, their own Persona row and single private memory sentence from `company.md`, and `promptbook.md`.
- **Acting & subtext direction:** Directed performers not to state their Shadows aloud, to keep private memories unspoken until scene pressure forced them out, to let physical Tells appear only when emotionally pinched, and to keep external callers/drivers offstage so the dramatic pressure remained strictly among the four siblings.
- **Threshold & open-condition choices:** Keeping the authored Play fixed, I varied/specified two open conditions to sharpen pressure:
  1. At Turn 10 of Scene 1, I reminded Ben of the five-o'clock flat handover Ada had established so the exchange in the flat would find a physical resting threshold heading downstairs (leaving what Ben did with the painting and his claim entirely up to him).
  2. For Scene 2's open condition (`One car idles at the kerb`), I set the idling car as Cora's pre-booked minicab to the train station—accommodating Cora's Scene 1 reference to catching an evening train while distinguishing it from Ada's parked car boot. In late Scene 2, I also directed Cora and Dan not to invent drawn numbers unilaterally before all four siblings had drawn.

### What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **Non-rotational flow (`promptbook.md`):** Cueing whichever sibling was most affected by the preceding move produced sharp, asymmetric exchanges rather than round-robin turns:
  - *Scene 1:* `Ada -> Dan -> Ada -> Ben -> Cora -> Ben -> Dan -> Cora -> Ada -> Ben`
  - *Scene 2:* `Cora -> Dan -> Cora -> Ada -> Ben -> Dan -> Ada -> Ben -> Cora -> Dan`
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*):** Ada tried to fold the painting into chore management (wrapping it for her boot while assigning Ben the meter and Cora the bathroom). Leaving Dan out of the assignments and naming the "split corner" immediately pulled Dan in to pin the bubble wrap under his shoe. Ben stepped in with four blank box labels to put the choice under a common draw/rota, and Cora deflected sideways by offering to hang "the eyesore" over damp plaster on her train home. Dan swept Ben's labels aside and demanded an honest sentence, cracking Cora's Tell and admission of her brick-airshaft kitchen, then Ada's cuff-folding Tell and memory of hanging the split frame last for Mother, and finally catching Ben's breath mid-sentence as his sleepless nights by the window nearly slipped out before he wrapped the painting in Ada's bubble wrap with two blank labels and carried it downstairs under his arm.
- **Carry-forward into Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** Both physical props and unresolved stakes carried directly across scenes: the painting sealed in Ada's bubble wrap with two of Ben's blank labels tucked under Ben's arm; the remaining two blank labels in Ben's pocket (which he tore into four numbered strips on the doorstep); the handed-back flat keys leaving Ada with only the van receipt in her pocket; the still-unglued mitre; Cora's train deadline; and Dan's and Ben's still-unconfessed private memories.
- **Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** With Cora's cab idling at the kerb and Ada clicking the building's front door shut for good, Dan blocked Cora's back-seat IOU ("Whoever puts that frame in a car tonight keeps it for good"), drawing out Cora's memory of the glossy photograph Mother sent when she moved away. When Ben revived the draw on the doorstep, Dan's heel lifted off the pavement as he confessed Mother had asked *him* to fix the frame and he kept saying "next time" until it was too late. Ada countered with her own steadiness and tried to claim the frame for her boot with Dan gluing it at hers on Sunday—which finally forced Ben past his rule-shield to confess sitting with Mother at that window when he couldn't sleep (`his breath catches abruptly: "Ada"`). Once Ben risked asking openly for himself, his rule evolved into an honest pact: Dan repairs the mitre wherever `1` hangs it, and all four stand in the same room every year on the day it changes walls. Cora and Dan each took an unopened strip, resting the Play on the doorstep with two strips left on Ben's palm and Ada confronted with letting go of the burden to belong.

### Where the Material & Promptbook Helped or Made Staging Hard
- **Where the Play + Promptbook helped:** Linking the Piece's two physical details (`an oil of the kitchen window from inside` and `a frame open at one corner`) to four asymmetrical private memories gave `promptbook.md` concrete emotional tripwires. Casual mentions of "wood glue," "open mitre," "kettle steam," or "wonky kitchen window" organically struck specific siblings' hidden memories and drove live back-and-forth volleys (`Ada -> Dan -> Ada`, `Cora -> Dan -> Cora`) without artificial prompting.
- **Staging friction (architecture vs. Play):**
  1. *Promptbook vs. isolated contexts (staging constraint, not a gap in the Play):* `promptbook.md` specifies that *"whichever sibling is most affected by what was just said or done responds next... passing play directly from sibling to sibling."* Because isolated performers do not know their siblings' hidden Shadows or private memories, a speaker cannot always know whose secret nerve they just struck, and waking all three non-speaking subagents on every turn to self-assess would create out-of-turn noise. Staging required the Director to evaluate "most affected" from outside (weighing direct address, physical action, Persona Shadows, and private memories) and pass play directly to that sibling.
  2. *Unattributed external prompts in `company.md` (open condition in the Play):* Both `Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer` (Scene 1) and `One car idles at the kerb` (Scene 2) leave open who is buzzing or whose car is idling. That openness is productive on stage, though with isolated performers it helps either to let Turn 1 establish the fact immediately (as Ada did with the movers on the intercom) or to supply one crisp condition at the scene break (assigning the idling car as Cora's cab) so separate contexts do not invent conflicting cars.
  3. *Shared chance mechanics:* Once Ben introduced numbered slips (`1`–`4`), isolated performers could have broken physical continuity by privately deciding what number they drew; directing them to keep drawn slips folded until all four had drawn preserved both continuity and the Play's suspended threshold ending.
