# *Mother's Painting* — Two-Scene Staged Performance

## Scene 1: *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Ada:** *Ada crosses to the wall intercom, presses the talk button without answering, and clicks the door release. She turns back into the empty room, picks up the roll of brown tape and a bundle of bubble wrap left near the doorway, and steps over to the painting against the skirting board.*

"That'll be the landlord's agent for the meter readings. Ben, meet him on the landing with the clipboard. Cora, check the kitchen cupboards one last time, and Dan, get the keys off the counter—I'll wrap the frame now and put it in my boot before we lock up."

**Ben:** *Ben picks up the clipboard, stays back from the landing door, and steps toward the skirting board, tearing a blank strip from the bottom edge of the backing sheet.*

"Leave the wrap off it a minute—" *Ben catches his breath briefly.* "—Ada. He's got the stairs to climb yet, and that frame's already open at the corner without rushing it into a boot. It wasn't marked for anyone's load, so we settle it under one rule before the keys come off the counter: four slips, all four names in, straight draw. Nobody takes the last piece out of here just for picking up the tape first."

**Cora:** *Cora leans a shoulder against the kitchen doorframe, glancing briefly toward the bare cupboards before looking back into the room, twirling her car key ring lightly around one finger.*

"Cupboards are clean as a whistle, Ada—not even a stray biscuit tin left for second prize. Though before Ben turns the landlord's clipboard into a village raffle, I've got an empty back seat and half a tube of wood glue at mine." *Cora's fingers go suddenly still on the key ring; a beat later she gives a light laugh and waves a hand loosely toward the skirting board.* "I can just cart the old thing off with me and save Ada's boot the splinters—unless someone's suddenly desperate for a crooked kitchen window coming apart at the corner."

**Dan:** *Dan glances once at the keys on the counter without reaching for them, takes two steps toward the skirting board, and stops with his right heel lifted and held an inch off the floorboards.*

"The keys can stay right where they are. Ada's handing out little errands so our backs are turned while she tapes it up, Ben's tearing up paper like it's the church raffle, and Cora's acting like she's doing everyone a favour carting off scrap. Just say what we're actually doing here: it's worth nothing to a dealer, Mum dragged it into every flat she ever had, and all four of us are hovering in an empty room because every single one of us wants to take it home—me included. So put the tape down and put the paper away; nobody's settling this while I'm sent off to fetch the keys."

**Ada:** *Ada sets the roll of brown tape down on the floorboards beside the painting, keeps the folded sheet of bubble wrap tucked under one arm, and turns back her left sleeve cuff, creasing the fabric between thumb and forefinger into a hard square.*

"Nobody's sneaking anything behind your back, Dan—someone still has to sign the inventory and hand over the meter numbers while the three of you stand in the middle of the floor arguing." *She presses the folded corner of her cuff flat, then squats to lay the bubble wrap smoothly on the boards right in front of the skirting board, between their feet and the frame.* "I booked the van, I cleared the loft, and I've got the solicitor's box in my boot already. We're not drawing scraps of paper for it like strangers, Ben, and it isn't going loose onto a back seat to rattle apart, Cora. I'm taking care of the frame the same way I've taken care of the rest of this flat—and whichever of you isn't opening that landing door when he knocks can at least stay clear of the corner joint."

**Ben:** *Ben tucks the torn paper strip beneath the clip on his board, crouches once, and sets the clipboard flat down in the centre of the bubble wrap on the floorboards, weighting the plastic sheet where it lies before straightening up.*

"Booking the van and taking the solicitor's box doesn't buy first rights to what's left against the wall. That's admin, not a claim. And saying out loud that all four of us want it doesn't settle who walks out the door with it—" *Ben catches his breath briefly.* "—Dan. It just leaves four of us standing in an empty room waiting for someone to blink. If drawing slips is too much like strangers, fine: we put it under a family rule instead. Twelve months on each wall, passed on the same date every year—start with the youngest or the eldest, I'm not even asking to go first—and whoever takes the first turn glues that open corner flat. That's the same deal for everyone, nobody gets shut out, and someone can actually answer the landing door when he knocks."

**Dan:** *Dan lowers his heel flat to the floorboards, steps right up to the edge of the bubble wrap, and looks down for a moment at the open corner of the frame before looking up at Ben and Ada.*

"Drop the yearly rota, Ben—none of us believes that for a second. Once those keys go back to the agent, there's no flat left to bring us back here, and the four of us aren't meeting in a motorway car park every autumn to hand Mum's kitchen window round in a circle. Whoever carries it down those stairs today is keeping it on their wall for good, and everyone in this room knows it—that's why Ada's got bubble wrap on the floor and why your clipboard's parked on top of it." *Dan nudges the corner of the plastic sheet with the toe of his shoe.* "And booking the van doesn't hand it to you by default, Ada. You sorted the loft and the solicitor's box before anyone even asked the rest of us, same as always. I'm not buying a polite custody schedule so three of us walk out quiet, and I'm not stepping aside while it goes into your boot—I want it in my flat, for keeps, same as you."

**Cora:** *Cora pockets her keys, pushes off from the kitchen doorframe, and strolls over beside the skirting board, stooping to pick up the roll of brown tape from the floorboards and turning it lightly between her hands.*

"Lovely how 'youngest or eldest first' puts me neatly at the back of the queue till year three, Ben—and look at the floor, Ada's got a beach towel down at dawn and you've parked a clipboard on top to hold the spot." *Cora's fingers go suddenly still on the roll of tape as her eyes drop briefly to the open corner of the frame; a beat later she slips the roll onto her wrist like a bangle and gives a quick grin.* "All right, Dan, nobody's calling it scrap. Tell you what: wrap it up properly in Ada's plastic right now so nobody panics about my back seat, and let me play Switzerland—it comes home to mine out of the crossfire, and the three of you get a standing invite for Sunday roast whenever you feel like checking on the kitchen window. I'll even do the Yorkshires, and nobody has to loiter in a motorway car park."

*From the wall by the door, the entry buzzer sounds again—two short, impatient bursts from downstairs.*

**Ada:** *Ada looks toward the buzzing intercom, then back at the three of them; she folds her right cuff up to match the left, creasing the edge into a tight square. Stooping, she lifts Ben's clipboard off the bubble wrap, takes the painting carefully by both sides—her thumb bracing the open corner joint flush—and lowers it face-up onto the centre of the plastic sheet. Carrying the clipboard, she crosses to the wall by the door, holds the entry-release button down for a long three-count, sets the clipboard beside the keys on the counter, and steps straight back to the edge of the bubble wrap.*

"Funny how it's only 'admin' once the boxes are downstairs, Ben—and funny how that buzzer went twice just now without one of you moving an inch toward the wall." *She extends an open palm toward Cora's wrist.* "Nobody stopped any of you from clearing the loft or dealing with the solicitor, Dan; you left it to me because you knew it'd get done, and when those keys go back in two minutes there's nothing left to do for her except keep what's on this sheet together. We're not playing pass-the-parcel once a year, and we're not leaving it at Cora's in exchange for Sunday lunch. Hand me the tape, Cora, and one of you take the counter keys to the door before he walks off and costs the estate another month's rent."

**Cora:** *Cora steps half a pace back with a light laugh, drawing her taped wrist in out of reach.*

"Easy, Ada—you don't have to look quite that alarmed at the threat of my roast potatoes, I wasn't going to make them compulsory." *She glances down at the painting lying face-up on the bubble wrap, and her fingers go suddenly still against her forearm. After a beat, she folds her arms loosely, tucking the roll of tape under her elbow, and gives a quick, crooked smile.* "Funny seeing it flat on the floor like an oversized postcard, though. When I first moved away, Mum actually sent me a photograph of this painting—kitchen window, split corner and all. So tell you what: since you've already got the solicitor's box in your boot, you can have the photo for the archive free of charge and I'll upgrade to the clumsy wooden version. Call it a fair swap, leave the tape parked on my arm till we're settled, and if the bloke on the stairs tries charging for an extra five minutes, take it out of my share—my shout."

**Ben:** *Ben leaves the clipboard where it lies on the counter, steps up to the edge of the bubble wrap, and drops to one knee on the floorboards, pressing one palm flat against the open margin of the plastic sheet beside the frame. He looks down at the painted kitchen window.*

"Parking my clipboard by the keys doesn't put me on door duty while this gets wrapped behind my back. And trading a photograph between two of you isn't a fair swap when there are four of us in the room—especially when you've already got the photo she sent you at home—" *Ben catches his breath briefly.* "—Cora." *His eyes stay on the canvas lying face-up on the plastic.* "It isn't a postcard. I used to sit with her at that window when I couldn't sleep—half the night sometimes, with the rest of the flat dark, just sitting there beside her till it got light." *He looks up at the three of them, keeping his palm flat on the sheet.* "All right—no paper slips, and no yearly rota. Whoever carries it down those stairs keeps it for good, same as—" *he catches his breath slightly* "—Dan said. He's coming up the stairs right now, so we settle it under one rule for all four of us: one vote each, out loud, and nobody gets to name themselves. First name with two votes carries it out, and nobody touches the tape till all four of us have spoken."

**Dan:** *Dan steps onto the open margin of the bubble wrap opposite Ben, crouches briefly to touch the split corner of the frame with the side of his thumb without pressing it shut, then straightens up, shifting his weight forward so his right heel lifts and holds off the floorboards.*

"Convenient timing for a selfless vote, Ben—banning everyone from saying their own name thirty seconds after telling us you sat up with her till dawn. We all know an honest vote comes back one apiece, and we all know what 'nobody names themselves' means in this room: the three of you trade favours over my head and nobody says Dan, same way the loft and the solicitor got sorted before anyone rang me." *He points straight down at the split corner of the frame, his right heel still held clear of the floor.* "And don't talk about keeping it together like it's another job on your list, Ada. Mum asked me to fix this frame. Not you, not Ben, not Cora—me. And I kept putting it off. Every time she mentioned that open corner I left it for next visit, right up until there wasn't one left. So I'm naming myself out loud: I'm taking it home, and I'm mending the corner like she asked me to. Keep your show of hands—if you three are going to shut me out of the one thing she left me to do, don't hide behind a voting rule while the agent climbs the stairs. Say it to my face."

*Outside on the landing, no footsteps come up the stairs; through the bare front window, a car horn gives a single short tap from the street below.*

**Ada:** *At the tap of the horn outside, Ada glances once toward the bare front window, refolds the already-creased edge of her left cuff tighter into a hard square, and kneels on the floorboards at the top margin of the bubble wrap between Ben and Dan. She sets one palm steady along the top rail of the frame and braces the open corner gently with her other hand, without pulling the plastic sheet beneath Ben's palm or Dan's shoe.*

"He hasn't come up the stairs—that horn's from the street. Cora, open the front window and call down that we're bringing the keys out in two minutes." *She looks straight up at Dan.* "You asked for it to your face, Dan, so I'll say it plainly: while you kept leaving that split corner for next visit, I was the last one to hang this painting for Mum. I stood here holding that open corner together with my own hands to get it up on the wall for her." *She looks briefly from Cora to Ben, keeping both hands steady on the wood.* "The solicitor's box in my car isn't an archive, Cora—it's final bills and probate forms nobody else offered to carry. I'm not playing a rigged vote after Dan's already named himself, Ben, and I'm not walking out of her last flat with nothing left of hers except the paperwork just because I showed up to do the work. Take your hand and your shoe off the sheet, and pass me the tape—I'm taking it home."

**Ben:** *Ben brings his other knee down onto the floorboards and moves both hands off the plastic margin onto the bottom rail of the frame, holding his end of the wood flat against the bubble wrap without pulling on the joint.*

"You think saying it to your face settles anything—" *Ben catches his breath briefly.* "—Dan? You named yourself out loud, and five seconds later—" *he catches his breath again* "—Ada put both hands on the wood and said the exact same words, while the tape stays out of reach and the agent sits on his horn in the street. One of us hung it last, one of us was asked to mend the corner, one of us got the photograph when she moved away, and I sat with her at that window when I couldn't sleep. None of us is walking out of here agreeing our piece of her counts for less than the next person's, and shouting 'I'm taking it home' just turns the last two minutes in her flat into a tug-of-war over a split frame." *He glances once toward the clipboard by the keys, keeping both hands steady on the bottom rail.* "I'm not letting go of this rail until we put it under the one rule left that doesn't rank one claim on her above another: that strip of paper is still clipped to the board on the counter—four pieces, three blank and one marked, drawn blind right here before the keys go downstairs. Same one-in-four shot for all four of us, or we all kneel here holding the wood while he drives off."

## Scene 2: *The entrance*

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. Unable to leave anyone alone with the painting upstairs while locking up and handing off the flat, the four siblings have brought it down together from the empty flat: it leans against the hallway wall just inside the propped-open front door, folded loosely inside Ada's sheet of bubble wrap—untaped, its frame still open at one corner. Cora still has the roll of brown tape. No one holds sole possession yet, and all four claims—along with Ben's call for a blind draw—remain unsettled.*

**Cora:** *Cora stands just inside the propped-open front door, glancing out briefly at the car idling at the kerb before turning back toward the loosely wrapped painting against the hallway wall, picking lightly at the stuck end of the brown tape with her thumbnail.*

"Tell me the whole street didn't just watch four grown adults escort one sheet of bubble wrap down the stairs like the Crown Jewels." *She looks down at the untaped fold of bubble wrap leaning against the wall; her fingers go suddenly still on the roll of tape. After a beat, she scrapes the loose tab of tape up half an inch with her nail and gives a quick grin.* "And nice try counting that photograph as my share upstairs, Ben—a bit of photo paper isn't quite the same view once the flat's locked up for good. So before anyone starts passing round paper scraps for a lucky dip on the doormat, let me slide it onto my back seat: I'll take half those probate forms off Ada's hands right now so she isn't left carrying the grim admin on her own, Dan can bring his glue round to mine this weekend and mend that corner like she asked him, and Ada's welcome to boss us both till it's hung straight. No skin off anyone's nose, nobody gets shut out, and we don't end up drawing straws on the pavement."

**Dan:** *Dan steps between the propped-open front door and the hallway wall, glances once out toward the car idling at the kerb, then stoops to fold back the loose top flap of bubble wrap so the split corner of the frame shows bare against the plastic before straightening up just inside the threshold.*

"Press that tape back down, Cora. Nobody's buying the weekend DIY club at yours—me trotting round on Saturday to glue a frame onto your wall before getting sent home, and Ada handing over half the probate folder on the pavement. I spent long enough telling Mum 'this weekend' to know what 'come round this weekend' is worth the second we drive away from that kerb. Once one of us pulls off with this painting, the other three aren't getting past the front step." *He rests two fingers lightly against the hallway wall just above the exposed corner of the frame.* "And holding that joint together with your hands to get it onto her hook doesn't settle it either, Ada—you hung it broken so it'd stay up, she still asked me to mend it, and you've already got the solicitor's box in your boot. And I'm not drawing scraps off Ben's clipboard so three of us can blame a bit of paper when we drive home to nothing. The flat's locked—there's no upstairs left. I'm taking it home in my car, fixing that corner properly, and putting it on my wall like I should've done while she was still here to see it."

**Ada:** *Ada glances out past the propped-open front door toward the car idling at the kerb, then pinches the loosened crease of her right cuff back into a hard square. Stepping in beside the hallway wall on the inner side of the painting, she folds the top flap of bubble wrap smoothly back down over the exposed split corner—staying clear of Dan's fingers on the wall above it—tucks the loose side margins neatly behind the upright rails of the frame, and keeps one palm flat against the plastic to hold the fold shut.*

"Leave the probate forms where they are, Cora—splitting one file between two houses just means missed deadlines I'll end up sorting out anyway, and I didn't mention the solicitor's box so you could buy me off with half my own paperwork." *She looks straight at Dan across the top of the wrapped frame.* "And stop peeling the plastic back in an open doorway. I hung it broken because she wanted her kitchen window on the wall while she was still alive to look at it, Dan—not propped on the floor waiting for a visit you never got round to making. You don't get to carry it off now just to square things after the flat's locked." *She glances toward Ben, her palm steady on the folded wrap.* "And I'm not leaving it to four torn scraps of paper in the entrance hall, Ben, so it ends up rattling loose on a back seat or sitting unfixed for another year. Cora, press that tab of tape across this flap; Ben, unprop the front door once we're over the step—we've handed the keys back, we've got no business leaving the building open to the street—and Dan, clear the threshold so I can get this flat into my boot in one piece."

**Ben:** *Ben stays clear of the propped-open front door and steps squarely in front of the wrapped painting. Pulling a folded receipt and a pen from his jacket pocket, he marks a small cross in one corner against his palm, drops the pen back into his pocket, tears the paper cleanly in half and in half again into four equal squares, and folds each square tight. He shakes the four tabs once inside his cupped hands, then rests his closed fist on the wrapped top edge of the frame, clear of Ada's palm and Dan's fingers.*

"I'm not unpropping that door behind your back while you carry the frame out to your boot. And funny how 'nobody gets shut out' in your weekend plan found a part for everyone in this doorway except me—" *Ben catches his breath briefly.* "—Cora—just because I haven't been elbowing past the rest of you toward the kerb. We've handed the keys back, we've got no business blocking an entrance hall we've already signed off, and blaming a bit of paper isn't worse than shoving your own family off the step—" *he catches his breath slightly* "—Dan. None of us is standing aside because someone else gave an order. Either three of us step back right now and tell the fourth to load it in their car—which isn't happening—or all four of us pull one folded square out of my hand before that door gets unpropped. Same conditions on whoever draws the mark: that brown tape goes flat across the wrap right here, and that open corner gets glued shut properly within the week so it isn't rattling loose or sitting broken. Four equal squares in my hand, one mark—nobody crosses the step with the frame till all four of us draw."

*Outside the propped-open front door, the car idling at the kerb switches on its hazard lights, their amber click-flash steady against the doorstep.*

**Cora:** *Cora glances out past the propped-open door at the amber click-flash of the hazard lights on the doorstep; her fingers go suddenly still on the lifted tab of brown tape. After a beat, she smooths the half-inch tab back down onto the roll itself, hooks the tape higher up her left wrist out of reach, and gives a quick, crooked grin.*

"Fair cop, Ben—you'd have had a three-a.m. kettle pass at mine too, I just didn't want to brag about the kitchen. And message received from the pair of you: apparently my Sunday roasts and weekend hospitality are a tough sell once we pull away from this kerb." *Reaching over the top of the wrapped frame with thumb and forefinger, she slips one folded square from the thumb-side of Ben's fist without unfolding it, holding the closed paper tab up between two fingers.* "So tell you what, I'll break the ice on Ben's receipt raffle. Tape stays parked on my wrist till Ada and Dan pull their squares, I'll seal the bubble wrap free of charge for whoever gets the cross, and if the cross lands with me I'll even post Mum's photograph on—my treat on the stamps—so somebody else still gets that kitchen window. Three left in his hand—one apiece for Ada, Dan, and the dealer—and those hazard lights out there aren't getting any quieter."

**Dan:** *Dan stays planted just inside the propped-open front door between the wrapped frame and the doorstep, shifting his weight forward so his right heel lifts and holds an inch off the floor.*

"I'm not clearing the threshold for your boot, Ada. You're right about one thing—mending that joint tomorrow doesn't undo me leaving her waiting while she was upstairs. Neither does holding a broken corner on a hook give you the right to wave me out of the doorway like the removal lad." *Taking his fingers off the wall, he reaches to Ben's fist on the top edge of the frame, pulls one folded square free without opening it, and holds the closed paper tab pinched tight between thumb and forefinger, his right heel still held clear of the floor.* "And don't dress up a torn receipt like it makes things square, Ben, or pretend posting a photograph round takes the sting out, Cora. It's just the only way left to stop Ada marching out to her car like the rest of us weren't standing here. Three of us are about to walk past those hazard lights with nothing of Mum's left to put on a wall, and once this door shuts behind us, there's no flat left to bring the four of us back to the same step. Two squares left in his hand, Ada—pull yours so we can open them right here, because if that cross is between my fingers, this frame's going straight into my car and onto my bench."

**Ada:** *Ada glances once at the steady amber flash of the hazard lights on the doorstep, then looks back at the three of them. Keeping the top flap of the bubble wrap pressed flat beneath her left palm, she runs her right thumb firmly along the hard square of her folded left cuff, reaches up to Ben's fist on the top edge of the frame, and slips one of the two remaining folded squares free without opening it. She holds the closed paper tab pinched against the folded square of her left cuff.*

"Nobody was marching past you like you weren't standing here, Dan. When there's a van to book or a loft to clear or probate forms to file, the three of you know my number well enough—it's only when the last job's done and the door's about to shut behind us that nobody wants an eldest sister any more." *She nods once toward Ben's closed hand and the roll of tape on Cora's wrist.* "All right, Ben—you're holding the fourth square, and that car's got its hazards going at the kerb. Cora, lift the end of that brown tape now so the wrap gets sealed flat before anyone crosses the step; Dan, stand ready to clear the doorway; and the last one over the threshold pulls this front door shut properly behind us. If that cross is under my thumb right now, this frame's going straight into my boot and up on my wall tonight—and if it lands with one of you and that corner's still sitting open by Sunday, I'll be on your doorstep myself to see it's done right. Open all four together, Ben."

*As the four folded receipt squares are pinched open together in the doorway, Cora's square is blank, Ada's square is blank, the fourth square in Ben's palm is blank, and the small ink cross shows in the corner of the square between Dan's fingers.*

**Ben:** *Ben holds the unfolded blank square flat on his palm for a beat, tilted open toward the three of them, then slips the scrap into his jacket pocket and lifts his other hand cleanly off the top edge of the wrapped frame.*

"Blank here. All four of us pulled from the same hand, and the rule stands. Let the plastic go—" *Ben catches his breath briefly.* "—Ada. The mark's yours—" *he catches his breath again* "—Dan. Fair's fair. Get that brown tape pressed flat across the flap right here before it goes over the step, and glue that open corner shut within the week like we agreed—don't leave the window sitting unfixed on a bench." *He steps back beside the propped-open front door, setting one hand steady on the door's edge in the amber flash from the kerb.* "Seal it up and take it out. Once you're all clear of the threshold, I'll unprop this door and pull it shut behind us."

**Cora:** *Cora looks down at the unfolded blank square between her fingers; her fingers go suddenly still on the paper. After a beat, she flips the blank scrap over once, lets out a quick laugh, and drops it into her coat pocket.*

"Hang on—not even a consolation voucher on the back of mine? Daylight robbery, Ben." *Sliding the roll of brown tape off her wrist, she pulls out a long strip, presses it firmly across the folded flap of bubble wrap clear of Ada's hand, tears the end clean with her thumbnail, and smooths the tape flat.* "There—sealed up, free wrapping service as advertised. Tell you what: I'll get copies run off that old photograph Mum sent me and stick them in the post for the runners-up club—my shout on the stamps, no thank-you notes required—and Dan, send us a picture once you've glued that corner shut so I've got the before-and-after and Ada doesn't have to kick your door in on Sunday." *Pocketing the rest of the tape, she gives a small two-fingered salute and steps over the threshold onto the doorstep, pausing half-turned in the amber flash from the kerb.* "Right—that's one of us clear of the step. Don't all rush to miss me at once."

**Ada:** *Ada looks down at the blank square of paper between her fingers, folds it neatly in half, and slips it into her pocket. Pinching the crease of her left cuff tighter between thumb and forefinger into a hard square, she lifts her palm cleanly off the taped bubble wrap, rests the side of her thumb for one beat against the wrapped split corner, and drops both hands to her sides.*

"Fair's fair. Carry it from underneath the bottom rail, Dan—don't lift it by the top edge or that open corner will pull wider before you get it to your car. Leave the joint clamped overnight once the glue's in, and hang it level when it's dry." *She steps clear of the wrapped frame, crosses the threshold onto the doorstep into the steady amber flash from the kerb, and pauses half-turned toward the open doorway.* "Stick that copy in the post when you've had them printed, Cora. I'll have the probate forms divided up and posted out by Friday—sign where I've marked the pages and send them straight back to me so nothing stalls, and send word on Sunday once that window's up on your wall, Dan. Make sure the front door latches shut behind you, Ben—I'm heading to my car."

**Dan:** *Dan looks down at the inked cross in the corner of his paper square, lowers his right heel flat to the floor, and slips the scrap into his jacket pocket. Stooping beside the hallway wall, he slides both hands underneath the bottom rail of the taped frame, lifts it steady against his chest with the wrapped split corner upright, and steps across the threshold onto the doorstep into the amber flash from the kerb.*

"Both of you just announced you were off and neither of you has moved past the step." *He glances from Cora to Ada in the hazard light, then back to Ben at the door edge, settling both palms firmer beneath the bottom rail.* "Pulling an ink cross off a receipt doesn't mean I earned this more than you three—we all know it doesn't. It's going straight onto my bench tonight, not next visit: glued, clamped overnight like you said, Ada, and hung level in the morning before I touch anything else. And nobody said we didn't want an eldest sister once the boxes were gone. Post those reprints, Cora, and I'll send the picture once the corner's shut—but if any of you wants to look at the actual window instead of a copy, don't invent an annual rota and don't wait on a probate form for an excuse. Just ring my phone and come round. Unprop the door, Ben—I've got the bottom rail, we're clear of the threshold."

## Director's Note

### What I Varied and Directed
- **Strict informational isolation:** Each sibling was staged in a separate read-only context receiving only the shared premise, Piece, Openings, their own Persona row and private memory sentence, the Promptbook `# Knowledge` constraint, and strictly observable behavior and spoken dialogue as turns occurred.
- **Cueing & non-speaking environmental pressure (staging choices within open conditions):** Rather than running a fixed round-robin, I cued whichever sibling had just been directly challenged or bypassed. To connect Scene 1's *entry buzzer* with Scene 2's *idling car at the kerb* without introducing an unauthored fifth speaking character into the room, I added three brief, purely external sound/light beats: a second impatient buzz from downstairs when nobody went to the door (Scene 1, before Turn 9), an empty landing and single horn tap from the street (before Turn 13, clarifying that whoever buzzed had stayed at the kerb rather than climbing the stairs as Ada assumed), and hazard lights clicking on from the idling car outside the propped-open front door (Scene 2, before Turn 5).
- **External adjudication of the blind draw:** When Ben tore a pocket receipt into four folded squares (one marked with a cross) in Scene 2, I instructed performers not to declare the contents of a drawn slip on their own turn so no isolated context could unilaterally award itself the painting. Once all four agreed to open together (Turn 7), I supplied the simultaneous reveal from outside the fiction—placing the ink cross in Dan's square so Ben lost under his own rule, Ada had to take her hands off the frame, Cora had to seal the wrap for someone else, and Dan could no longer read the outcome as exclusion.

### What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*):** For nine full turns, all four siblings fought through proxies—Ada assigning errands while laying bubble wrap down, Ben proposing first a four-name draw and then an annual rota, Cora commandeering the roll of brown tape onto her wrist while offering Sunday roasts, and Dan exposing the room's shared motive—without anyone revealing their private memory. Once Ada lifted the painting onto the bubble wrap and demanded the tape (Turn 9), the Promptbook trigger fired in a four-turn cascade: Cora revealed the photograph Mother sent when she moved away (Turn 10), Ben revealed sitting sleepless with Mother at that window and proposed a no-self-vote (Turn 11), Dan revealed putting off Mother's request to fix the split corner (Turn 12), and Ada revealed holding that broken corner together as the last to hang it for Mother (Turn 13). Scene 1 rested on a physical four-way stalemate—Ada and Ben holding opposite rails on the floor, Dan standing on the wrap's margin, Cora keeping the tape out of reach—as Ben circled back to demanding a blind four-slip draw (Turn 14).
- **Carried into Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** Because neither Ada nor Ben would leave the other alone upstairs while locking up and handing over the flat keys and clipboard, the painting carried down into the entrance hall loosely folded inside Ada's untaped bubble wrap (corner still open), with Cora still wearing the brown tape on her wrist and no sibling holding sole possession. Every memory voiced in Scene 1 reshaped Scene 2's bargains: Cora tried trading half of Ada's probate load and hosting Dan's glue repair (inadvertently leaving Ben out, which Ben immediately clocked); Ada used Dan's missed visits against him; and once Cora broke the standoff by drawing the first receipt square, Dan and Ada followed. Losing the draw forced Ada to divide the probate forms rather than hoard the whole burden, Ben to stand by a rule that left him empty-handed, Cora to seal the wrap and offer reprints of her photograph, and Dan to drop his raised heel, lift the frame from the bottom rail as Ada instructed, and invite his siblings round without an excuse.

### How the Authored Material and Promptbook Performed
- **Where they helped:** The Promptbook `# Knowledge` rule (*withhold private memory until another move threatens to cost them the painting*) gave Scene 1 remarkable structural compression—preventing early confessional monologues and forcing every sibling's Action, Shadow, and physical Tell to carry the first two-thirds of the scene before all four memories broke under real material threat. Every Piece attribute (*no resale value*, *moved to every flat*, *kitchen window from inside*, *frame open at one corner*) actively anchored the dialogue and blocking; none went unused.
- **Play gap vs. staging choice:** Two seam points required deliberate staging care rather than changes to the Play:
  1. *The entry buzzer & scene transition:* Scene 1 opens with *"Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer"* and Scene 2 opens at *"The entrance"* (*"Carry forward where the painting is, who has it if anyone"*). When Ada immediately buzzed the door release on Turn 1 assuming an agent was coming up the stairs, the Play left open whether anyone actually arrives at the flat door. Keeping the ringer waiting at the kerb (staging choice) preserved the four-sibling chamber pressure without inventing an offstage character's dialogue, while Scene 1's four-way physical deadlock required framing the between-scene descent neutrally (bringing the untaped frame down together so nobody was left upstairs with it) to avoid deciding possession on the cast's behalf.
  2. *Chance mechanics inside isolated roles:* Ben's Persona (*puts a difficult choice under a common rule*) naturally generated physical chance devices (slips, blind draws) that isolated performers cannot resolve fairly on their own without breaking isolation; adjudicating the draw outcome as a stage fact from the Director's seat handled that cleanly within the fixed Play.
