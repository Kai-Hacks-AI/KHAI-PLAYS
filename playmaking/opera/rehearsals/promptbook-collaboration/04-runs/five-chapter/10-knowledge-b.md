# Performance: *Mother's Painting*

## Scene 1: *The flat*

**Opening:** The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.

**Turn 1**
**Ada:** *Crosses to the wall phone by the door, picks up a roll of bubble wrap left on the floorboards under one arm, and looks over at the others.* Ben, get the buzzer—that'll be the agent for the meter readings. Cora, check the kitchen cupboards one last time, and Dan, bring the dustpan from the hall. *She steps toward the skirting board, reaching down for the painting.* I'll wrap this up and put it on my back seat before we lock up.

**Turn 2**
**Dan:** *Doesn't move toward the hall; instead steps over to the skirting board beside the painting and lifts his right heel, holding it an inch off the floorboards.* Nobody's fetching a dustpan, Ada. You always hand out jobs when you want the room cleared. When did anyone decide that's going on your back seat? Every one of us is still standing in this empty flat for that painting.

**Turn 3**
**Ben:** *Stops short of the wall phone and holds a level hand out toward the skirting board.* Hold on—leave the bubble wrap off it a minute. *His breath catches slightly on the name—* Ada, nobody's putting it on a back seat just because they reached for it first, and nobody's settling this by standing over it either, Dan. She took that picture to every flat she lived in, and one corner of the frame's already open. Let's put it under one fair rule right now: four of us in the room, so it's either a year in each house on rotation, or a straight four-way draw before anybody touches that buzzer.

**Turn 4**
**Cora:** *Leans a shoulder against the doorframe toward the kitchen, drumming her fingers lightly against her coat pocket with an easy grin.* Oh, terrific—a custody schedule. What are we going to do, post a loose frame around the country every January until it arrives at Ben's house as four sticks and a roll of canvas? *Her fingers go suddenly still against the fabric.* Look, save yourselves the committee meeting—my place already looks like a charity shop annex, and I've actually got wood glue under the sink. Let me tuck the poor thing under my arm for now so you three don't pull it in four directions in front of the meter man.

*(Stage beat: On the wall by the door, the entry buzzer sounds a second, longer ring.)*

**Turn 5**
**Ada:** *Sets the bubble wrap down on the floorboards, crosses to the wall phone, presses the entry button, and speaks evenly into the receiver.* Come on up—we're still inside. *She hangs the handset back on its hook, then folds her right cuff into a neat, hard square before turning back to the room.* Nobody's tucking an open frame bare under an arm down the stairs, Cora, and we aren't drawing lots over the last thing left in this flat, Ben. One of us has to see it's properly bound and looked after in one place so it doesn't come apart. Dan, pass me the end of that wrap so I can secure the corner before whoever's on the stairs knocks—it's coming home with me where I know it'll be kept whole.

**Turn 6**
**Dan:** *Steps between Ada and the roll of bubble wrap without picking it up, his right heel lifting off the floorboards and holding there.* You only buzzed that door to put a clock on us, Ada—and you answered Cora, answered Ben, and went straight back to giving me orders like I'm not even in the count. Quit dressing this up as rescue work, or "for now," or a rota, when every one of us just wants the last thing of hers in our own house. I'm not passing the wrap—I'm taking the painting home, and I'll fix the corner myself.

*(Stage beat: Footsteps reach the landing outside; there is a brisk double-knock at the flat door.)*

**Turn 7**
**Ben:** *Steps to the flat door, rests his palm over the latch to keep it shut, and calls evenly toward the panels.* Just one minute, please. *He turns back into the room, his breath catching briefly on the name—* Dan, saying you're taking it isn't an answer; any of the four of us could stand over the skirting board and say the same thing. Fine—if an open frame shouldn't travel every year, scrap the rota. Then we put one straight rule on it before I lift this latch: nobody gets to award it to themselves, and nobody lifts that painting off the wall until three of us agree whose house it goes to.

**Turn 8**
**Cora:** *Pushes off the kitchen doorframe with a short laugh, twirling her car key around one forefinger as she glances toward the knocking door.* Brilliant—a three-way majority vote with a stranger parked on the landing. What do we do, blow white smoke through the letterbox when we pick a Pope? *Her fingers go suddenly still around the key for a beat before she gestures lightly toward the roll on the floor.* Look, Ada's already ruled out Dan, Dan's ruled out Ada, and Ben's busy being Speaker of the House—by pure arithmetic I'm the only harmless option left. Wrap it to Ada's standards right now, stick it in my boot so Ben can let the poor man read the meters, and you three can come round on Sunday, inspect the corner, and vote me off the island over biscuits if I've ruined it.

**Turn 9**
**Ada:** *Folds her left cuff into a tight, hard square to match the other, then takes one measured step sideways around Dan and crouches to pull a flat sheet of bubble wrap across the floorboards.* You want it wrapped to my standards just so you can shut it in your boot, Cora? You don't get to leave me the job and vote me out of the picture, Ben. *She looks straight up at Dan, her voice level.* And I'm not dressing anything up as rescue work, Dan—when Mother moved into this flat, I was the last one to hang that painting for her. Ben, hold that latch another thirty seconds, and Cora, put your key away; I'm laying this wrap out to take it home to mine, not to pack it off for the three of you.

*(Stage beat: Beneath Ben's palm, the door handle shifts slightly from the landing outside, followed by another polite tap on the panel—the flat has to be handed over.)*

**Turn 10**
**Ben:** *Takes his palm off the shifting door handle without opening the door, crosses the floorboards, and crouches to set one hand flat on the sheet of bubble wrap between Ada and the skirting board.* I'm not holding the door thirty seconds while you pack it, and I wasn't playing Speaker of the House so I could be counted out— *he catches his breath short on the name* —Cora; I only tried to put a fair rule on this so none of us had to stand here and ask for ourselves. *He looks at the painted kitchen window resting against the wall.* You hung it in this flat, Ada, I know—but I used to sit with her at that window when I couldn't sleep. If the rule now is we say plainly why we want it before anyone opens that door, there's mine: I'm asking to take it home.

## Scene 2: *The entrance*

**Opening:** The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.
**Carried forward from *The flat*:** Having handed the empty flat over upstairs, the four siblings stand at the threshold of the building's entrance. The painting—loosely wrapped in the sheet of bubble wrap, its open frame corner still unfixed underneath—leans upright just inside the propped-open front door, unclaimed; Ada and Ben have both laid out their plain claims to take it home, and who leaves with it remains unsettled.

**Turn 1**
**Cora:** *Stands just inside the propped-open entrance, glancing out toward the car idling at the kerb before flicking a loose edge of the bubble wrap with restless fingers and letting out a light laugh.* Well, at least we brought the tribunal downstairs—another minute on the step and the whole street'll think we're dividing up a stolen Rembrandt instead of a wonky kitchen window. *Her fingers go suddenly still against the plastic.* Look, since Ben wants proper credentials—when I first moved away, Mother sent me a photograph of that painting. I've made do with the glossy copy ever since, so technically I'm only asking for an upgrade; let me take the real thing home, and you three are very welcome to pass the photo round instead.

**Turn 2**
**Dan:** *Steps beside the propped door, folds back a loose edge of the bubble wrap to bare the open corner of the frame, and stands with his right heel lifted off the threshold.* Listen to us—turning the doorstep into an audition for the last piece of her left, like having the nicest story means the other three walk away fine with nothing. You want proper credentials, Ben? Mine aren't pretty: that corner's still open because Mother asked me to fix the frame, and I kept putting it off. Keep the photograph, Cora—let me take it home and finally fix what she asked me to.

**Turn 3**
**Ada:** *Presses a thumb along her loosened right cuff, refolding it into a hard square, then bends to draw the bubble wrap neatly back over the bare corner Dan exposed.* I felt that corner give when I hung it on her wall—I didn't know she'd asked you to mend it, Dan. Bring your glue round to mine on Saturday and fix the frame properly on my kitchen table; Cora, you've already got the print she sent you, and Ben, my kettle's on any night you can't sleep. *She straightens, setting one steady hand under the sound side of the wrapped frame.* I know I've spent all day handing out jobs to get us through clearing her rooms—don't send me home with the flat handed over, the work finished, and nothing of hers left to look after. Ben, take the open side carefully with me as far as my car, and Dan, pull the door off the prop behind us.

**Turn 4**
**Ben:** *Steps up to the wrapped painting, sets both hands carefully under the open-cornered side, and plants his feet square inside the threshold without moving toward the street.* I'm not ringing your bell at three in the morning for a kettle when I can't sleep— *his breath catches in his throat on the name* —Ada, and we all know once this goes into one car for keeps, the other three of us have lost it. Upstairs, the only reason you and Cora gave against a rota was that an open frame wouldn't survive moving. Well, Mother took that painting into every flat she lived in, and Dan just told us she asked him to glue that corner—so once it's mended, it's strong enough to travel again. Nobody leaves this kerb empty-handed for good: we agree right here on a year in each house, and we draw on this step for who takes first turn tonight.

*(Stage beat: At the kerb, the idling car's driver glances out toward the propped-open doorway and taps the horn once, briefly.)*

**Turn 5**
**Cora:** *Glances back toward the kerb at the tap of the horn, letting out a quick, dry laugh as she drums her fingers against her bag strap and looks at Ada and Ben holding opposite ends of the frame.* Careful, we're getting honked at now—we look like the world's worst removal firm staging a hostage swap on the doorstep. *Her fingers go suddenly still against the leather.* And nice try, Ada, really, but a glossy four-by-six isn't the painting—I'm not getting bought off for keeps with the postcard version. I'm with Ben on a year in each house, and skip the draw tonight: let Dan take first turn so he actually glues that corner before it travels—I'll happily go last in the queue, since I've already got the preview copy to keep my wall warm.

**Turn 6**
**Dan:** *Leaves the door prop untouched, steps between Ada and Ben to set one hand on the top edge of the wrapped frame, and lifts his right heel off the threshold, holding it there.* Even after hearing why that corner's open, Ada, you still put me on doorstop duty and tried to book me into your kitchen on Saturday just so your house becomes the new flat. And quit pretending a rota means nobody leaves empty-handed, Ben—three of us are walking away tonight with nothing, and drawing lots on the step over an open corner will only split it worse. Take Cora's order straight: let me take it home tonight so I can glue and clamp that frame the way I owed her, then I bring it to yours a year from tonight, Ada, then Ben's, then Cora's—let go of the ends and let me fix it first.

**Turn 7**
**Ben:** *Glances once toward the kerb, keeping both hands firm under the open corner of the frame without letting go.* Don't press down on the top edge—that joint's still loose. I know three of us walk away empty-handed tonight— *his breath catches briefly on the name* —Dan; I'm going home to stay awake without it too. Mending the corner before it travels is a fair rule, so take first year and glue it properly—but you don't get to buy Ada off with second turn over my head. With Cora trading to last, the only fair order runs straight up by age from the youngest: yours tonight, mine next year, then Ada's, then Cora's—and I'm not letting go of this corner until Ada gives her word right here that it moves on every year.

**Turn 8**
**Ada:** *Lowers her side of the wrapped frame gently until it rests safe on the threshold, eases her hand free, and presses her right cuff flat into a hard square against her wrist.* Fair enough, Dan—take it home tonight and mend the corner under your own roof the way she asked you to. And you have my word, Ben: Dan's first, yours next year, mine the year after, then Cora's—when my twelve months are up, I'll take it off my wall and pass it on. Just glue that joint properly before it travels again; I didn't hang it last for her to stand here pulling it in half on the step. *She steps back to the propped-open entrance door and sets one steady hand against its edge.* Dan, lift my end from underneath instead of the top rail, and Cora, tuck that loose sheet in out of the wind—go on and get it out to the kerb with Ben before they honk again. I'll pull the prop and shut up behind us.

**Turn 9**
**Dan:** *Sets his right heel flat down on the threshold, takes his hand off the top rail, and crouches to slide both palms underneath the sound side of the wrapped frame, lifting it level with Ben's end.* Still telling me where to put my hands even when you're letting go, Ada. Deal—mine tonight, yours a year from tonight, Ben, then Ada's, then Cora's, and it'll leave my flat with four tight corners. *He angles his shoulders toward the kerb to clear the doorway, keeping the frame steady and level with Ben's hands.* Don't hang back on the step shutting up on your own, Ada—pull the prop and walk out to the kerb with us; there's nothing of hers left behind that door now anyway.

# Director's Note

### What Was Directed & Varied
- **Play fixed; isolated casting:** Each sibling ran in a separate read-only sub-context given only the shared premise/Piece/openings, their own Persona row, their own single private memory sentence from `company.md`, and `promptbook.md`. Performers were directed to write strictly observable speech and physical action (no internal thoughts) so turns could be shared verbatim without leaking unspoken motives.
- **Open-condition pressure (staging choices):** Kept the authored openings intact while letting the external clocks implied by `company.md` advance naturally in real time—in Scene 1, the unanswered entry buzzer rang a second time, then after Ada buzzed the visitor in on Turn 5, footsteps reached the landing with a knock and later a shifting door handle; in Scene 2, the idling car at the kerb gave a single brief tap of its horn. Cue order followed live dramatic pressure rather than a rigid loop.

### What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*, 10 turns):** Ada tried to fold taking the painting into final flat-clearing chores; Dan blocked her at the skirting board; Ben tried to shelter behind procedural rules (first a rotation/draw, then a three-of-four majority rule while holding the flat door shut); and Cora undercut the rules with sideways offers to take it "for now." Only at the climax of Scene 1—when Cora's boot offer and Ben's three-way rule threatened to use Ada's wrapping labor to shut her out—did Ada reveal she hung it last (Turn 9), which in turn forced Ben off the door latch to drop his procedural cover and reveal sitting with Mother at that window when he couldn't sleep (Turn 10).
- **Carried into Scene 2 (*The entrance*, 9 turns):** All ten turns of observable history carried forward, along with the painting loosely wrapped in Ada's bubble wrap (its open corner still unglued), leaning unclaimed just inside the propped-open entrance while Ada and Ben's claims stood in the open and Cora and Dan still held their memories.
- **Resolution in Scene 2:** Ben's new "plain credentials" standard threatened to exclude Cora and Dan, prompting Cora to reveal the photograph Mother sent her (Turn 1, still couched sideways as "only asking for an upgrade") and Dan to bare the loose corner and confess putting off Mother's request to fix it (Turn 2). Ada made one final bid to host everyone around her table so she wouldn't leave with nothing left to look after (Turn 3), but Ben connected Dan's promised repair with the Piece's **Yearbook** (*Mother took it to every flat*) to prove a mended frame could survive an annual rotation (Turn 4). Cora backed the rotation, ceded herself last place, and nominated Dan for first year to glue the joint (Turn 5); Dan accepted (Turn 6); Ben enforced a straight youngest-to-eldest order (Dan, Ben, Ada, Cora) conditioned on Ada's word (Turn 7); Ada gave her word and released her end while still assigning the carry and door prop (Turn 8); and Dan set his lifted heel flat down, lifted the frame with Ben, and called Ada out to the kerb rather than letting her lock up alone (Turn 9).

### Where `company.md` and `promptbook.md` Helped, Had No Occasion to Matter, or Made Staging Hard
- **Where `promptbook.md` helped:** The `# Knowledge` rule (*withholding private memories until another move threatens to cost them the painting*) prevented premature exposition. Eight full turns of Scene 1 ran purely on Persona Action, Shadow, and Tell before a single memory surfaced, staggering the four revelations across the Scene 1/Scene 2 hinge (S1 T9–10, S2 T1–2) so each confession landed as a defensive counter-move.
- **Where `company.md` helped:** The Piece's **Apparent** detail (*frame open at one corner*) and **Yearbook** (*Mother took it to every flat*) interlocked directly with Dan's private memory and Cora's Scene 1 objection, giving the cast concrete material to negotiate an earned settlement without softening their Personas.
- **Had no occasion to matter:** The Piece detail that the oil *"has no resale value"* never needed to be invoked (none of the four ever reached for a cash buyout or valuation rule because all four treated the piece as emotionally load-bearing from Turn 1). Similarly, fixing *whose* car idled at the kerb in *The entrance* proved unnecessary once a brief horn tap supplied shared departure pressure.
- **What made staging hard (Play gap vs. staging choice):**
  - *Play gap vs. staging bridge between scenes:* `company.md` places an urgent knock/buzzer on Scene 1 and jumps to the propped front door in Scene 2, leaving open how the painting physically leaves the flat if the siblings deadlock inside. Because Ben left the shifting door handle to crouch over the bubble wrap on Scene 1 Turn 10, advancing to *The entrance* required a directorial bridge (treating the flat handover as completed between scenes and positioning the loosely wrapped painting unclaimed inside the propped doorway) rather than inventing dialogue for an unauthored agent on the landing.
  - *Staging constraint for `# Knowledge`:* Because `promptbook.md` restricts siblings to acting *only* on shown words and physical behavior, standard prose turns risked leaking internal monologue when relayed to other performers; enforcing strictly observable stage directions and dialogue in the performer brief kept Shadows hidden behind physical Tells.
