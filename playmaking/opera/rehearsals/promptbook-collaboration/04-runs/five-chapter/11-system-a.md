# *Mother's Painting*

## Scene 1 — *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Ada:** *(crosses toward the hall wall at the sound of the buzzer, glancing once at the painting propped against the skirting board as she folds her left cuff into a neat, hard square)* Leave the intercom—I'll get it and sign the driver off. Ben, check the meters in the hall cupboard before we lock up; Cora, see if they left a broom behind the kitchen door. Dan, don't touch that frame yet, the corner's split—I brought a roll of blanket wrap in my boot so I can take it back with me without the mitre coming right apart.

**Dan:** *(stops near the skirting board, lifts his right heel and holds it an inch off the bare floorboards, staring straight at Ada)* Hang on. When did we decide it was going in your boot? Nobody checked meters or brooms for the last three hours, Ada—we've all just been hovering around the skirting board waiting to see who grabs the painting first. And if the three of you already sorted this out before I got here, just say so instead of sending us on chores.

**Ben:** *(takes his keys out of his pocket, steps halfway between the hall cupboard and the skirting board, and holds up an open hand toward Dan)* Nobody sorted anything out before you got here, Dan. And Ada—blanket wrap in the boot doesn't settle where it goes. Everything else in the flat went on the list and we drew order for the furniture; if more than one of us is asking for the last piece, we put all four names in and do it the same way, before anybody signs the driver off.

**Cora:** *(leans a shoulder against the bare kitchen doorframe, tapping a light rhythm on her elbow with her fingertips)* Oh, brilliant—four scraps of receipt paper in an empty mug while the driver stands on the pavement leaning on the bell. *(smiles lightly, looking from Ben to Ada)* Look, save the stationery. None of you actually want a crooked bit of hardboard with the corner yawning open hanging over your good sofas—chuck it on my back seat, I've got a patch of damp in the hallway it'll cover nicely, and I'll buy you all a terribly solemn pint on the corner so we don't have to hold a raffle in an empty room. *(her fingers on her elbow go suddenly still)* Deal?

*(The entry buzzer sounds again from downstairs—two short presses.)*

**Ada:** *(stops with her hand short of the entry phone at the two buzzes, drops her arm, and folds her right cuff down to match the left in a tight, hard square)* Nobody sorted anything behind your back, Dan—somebody still has to read the meters so the final bill doesn't bounce, just like somebody had to book the van, clear the loft, and drive over here the week Mother moved in to put the rawlplug in that plaster and hang the thing straight when the corner was already splitting. *(turns fully back toward Ben and Cora)* It isn't going over a damp patch on a back seat unpadded, Cora, and it isn't a toaster you pull out of a mug, Ben—if I don't clamp and glue that mitre properly tonight, the board drops clean out of the bottom rail. Ben, buzz whoever's at the street door and find out what they want while I get the tape measure on the meter numbers, or are all three of you standing guard over the skirting board until dark?

**Ben:** *(steps to the entry phone, holds down the talk button for a second—)* Give us two minutes, we're just finishing up! *(—releases it without pressing the door latch, then pulls the folded van sheet from his jacket pocket and tears four narrow strips off the bottom margin)* Putting a rawlplug in the wall doesn't make it yours, Ada, and clamping a corner isn't a reason to skip everyone else—whoever takes it home can pay twenty quid at a framer's or let you glue it first. And don't tell the room nobody wants it when you're trying to walk out with it under your arm, *(catches his breath slightly)* —Cora. Look: four strips, one mark on the back of one of them. Anyone who wants the painting takes a strip right now; whoever gets the mark takes it home tonight, no trading, no favours. If you don't want it, keep your hands in your pockets.

**Dan:** *(steps up to Ben, keeps his right heel raised off the floorboards, and snatches one of the paper strips without unfolding it)* Put your list away after this, Ben—calling a raffle over Mother's last thing in the room doesn't make it tidy, it just means none of us has to admit why we're still standing here. *(looks down at the open mitre against the skirting board, jaw tight)* And Ada, you weren't the only one who knew the corner was split—Mother asked me three times since Easter to bring glue and fix that frame, and I kept telling her next weekend. So I'm in the draw, and if I pull the mark I'm taking it home tonight and fixing it myself.

**Cora:** *(pushes lightly off the doorframe with a small, dry laugh, plucking one of the paper strips from Ben's hand between two fingers without looking at the back)* Oh, go on then, stick me down for a ticket—can't leave Ben holding spare confetti after he's torn up his precious van sheet. *(waves the little strip airily toward Dan and Ada)* Look, save yourselves the heart attack if I pull the winning smudge: Ada can have custody of the corner with her clamps for the weekend, Dan can supply the guilt-glue, and I'll just borrow it until my landlord finally replasters—basically storage with a view of a sink, you can all come visit it on bank holidays. *(lowers her hand to her coat pocket; her fingers around the strip of paper go suddenly still)* Come on then, Ada, pick your slip before the poor bloke downstairs puts his thumb right through the bell.

**Ada:** *(presses the edge of her folded cuff flat into a hard square with her thumb, steps across to Ben, and takes one of the two remaining strips, keeping it pinched shut between finger and thumb without looking at the underside)* I'm not gluing the mitre over a weekend just to hand it back on Monday like the removal firm, Cora, and twenty quid at a framer's isn't the point, Ben. When I put that hook up for Mother the afternoon the curtains went in, she stood right by that radiator holding the bottom rail together with both hands while I got the wire over the pin so the kitchen window in it sat level with hers—and once we lock that front door tonight, there isn't another job left in this place for any of you to ring me about. *(holds her closed slip out flat at waist height toward the three of them)* Go on, then—all four at once on the count, before that bell goes again, and whoever doesn't pull the mark goes straight in the hall cupboard to read the electric while I deal with downstairs.

**Ben:** *(holds his strip out at waist height to match Ada's, turns it flat on his open palm to show blank white paper, and holds it steady for a beat)* Mine's blank. One of you three got the corner with the driver's biro initial on the back. *(steps back to the hall cupboard and puts his hand on the latch, looking past the doorframe at the painted window propped against the skirting board)* Fair's fair—I'm out, and I'll read the electric. Only before you turn yours over, since I'm not saying this for myself anymore, we put one rule on whoever holds the mark: it doesn't go in a dark hallway or shut in a spare room. I used to sit with her at that window when I couldn't sleep, long before she started carrying it from flat to flat—so whoever takes it tonight hangs it by a table with a spare chair, and if someone's awake at two and rings the bell, *(catches his breath)* Ada—or Cora, or Dan—the rule is the kettle goes on and nobody asks why. *(pulls the cupboard door open an inch)* Go on, turn yours over.

**Cora:** *(lifts her hand from her coat pocket, flips the strip face-up across her knuckles with a quick flick, and glances down at the blue biro scrawl; her fingers go suddenly still)* ...Well, don't everyone cheer at once, you'll rattle the skirting board. *(holds the strip out flat so the biro initial shows, forcing a bright, crooked grin)* Look at that—I win a crooked window and three insomniacs ringing my bell at two in the morning. Tell you what, before Ada confiscates my prize on structural grounds: I don't even have a patch of damp in the hallway—she posted me a snapshot of this thing on the wall the very first week I moved away, and I've still got the curling little photo stuck to my fridge. *(gives a light, sideways shrug toward Ada and Dan)* Come round to mine right after we lock up—bring your blanket wrap and the wood glue, Ada, bring the biscuits, Ben—we'll prop it by my kitchen table tonight while the corner sets, and if you all hate how it looks over my kettle you can fight me for visiting rights next Sunday. Deal?

**Ada:** *(turns her strip over on her palm to show the blank white paper, stares at it for a quiet second, then slips the scrap into her cardigan pocket and presses her folded right cuff hard between thumb and forefinger)* Blank as well. Fair enough, Cora—it's yours. *(glances once at the bare nail hole above the radiator, then down at the split corner against the skirting board, her voice steady again)* Only if it's going over your kitchen table tonight, we're doing it properly so the rail doesn't give way on your kettle: nobody lift it off that skirting board until I bring the blanket wrap and the corner clamp up from my boot. Dan, you've got wood glue in your toolbox—you're holding the mitre square with me on Cora's table while it sets; Ben, write down both the day and night numbers off that meter and shut the mains switch; Cora, press that latch and find out who's been leaning on the downstairs bell before I go down to the car. Deal.

## Scene 2 — *The entrance*

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.*

**Dan:** *(stands just inside the propped-open front door with his toolbox in one hand, lifts his left heel off the threshold step and holds it there, looking from the idling car at the kerb back to the other three)* Right—before anyone shuts that boot or starts handing out jobs again, let's get one thing straight. The painting's still sitting against the skirting board upstairs, and Cora pulled the mark—not a committee. I brought my toolbox down from the van because I owe Mother that corner fixed, not because Ada volunteered me to hold her clamp. So whose car is actually idling at the kerb right now, and who's walking back up those stairs with me to bring it down—because if the three of you are about to load it straight into Ada's boot "just while the glue sets" and sort out next Sunday without me, I'm leaving the glue right here on the step.

**Ada:** *(stops at the bottom of the entrance step with the rolled blanket tucked under one arm and the metal corner clamp in her hand, glances back at the exhaust from the car at the kerb, then sets the clamp down firmly on the lid of Dan's toolbox and refolds her loosened left cuff into a tight, hard square)* That's the letting agent's clerk idling at the kerb, Dan, waiting to take the keys so he can get home—my car's locked three doors down, and my boot's staying shut. *(holds the roll of blanket wrap out toward Dan and Cora without stepping past him onto the stairs)* Nobody volunteered you to get you out of the way, and nobody's cutting you out of Sunday. I put that frame up on Mother's wall when she moved in; I don't need to be the one carrying it down the stairs just to prove I'm not driving off with it. Take the wrap up with Cora right now and fold it under that bottom rail before you lift it off the skirting board—Ben, get your flat key off your ring with mine so we've got the full set ready—and I'll keep the clerk waiting at the window till you're both back down and the door's on the latch.

**Ben:** *(pulls out his keyring alongside the torn van sheet with the meter readings, works his thumbnail into the split ring until his flat key is halfway off, then stops with the brass key wedged crosswise in the loop)* Hold on—no keys go through that car window while the painting's still upstairs against the skirting board. I've switched the mains off, so the flat's already dark; Cora, take the wrap from Ada and go up with *(catches his breath)* —Dan right now, fold it under the bottom rail before you lift it, and bring it straight down to your back seat while Ada and I stay right here on the step in plain sight. And Dan's right about Sunday—we're not leaving it at a joke about visiting rights and then scattering once the clerk drives off. Same rule for all four of us before we hand this place back: tonight we glue the mitre at Cora's table, and first Sunday of every month the kettle goes on at hers, fixed right now, nobody left out. *(holds up his keyring with the half-drawn key)* My key stays on this ring till the painting's down the stairs and we've got a rule on Sunday.

**Cora:** *(takes the roll of blanket wrap from Ada with an easy scoop of one arm, tucking it against her ribs like a rugby ball as she grins across the step at Ben and Dan)* First Sunday of every month—listen to you, turning my tiny kitchen into a parish hall before we've even paid the milk bill. *(fishes her own keyring from her coat pocket, slips her brass flat key clean off the split ring with a bright little clink, and tosses it lightly into Ada's free palm)* Tell you what: pencil in the first Sunday if it gets Ben's key off that ring before his thumb turns blue, only you're on washing-up rota, Dan brings biscuits that aren't sawdust, and if you all get sick of staring at my kettle by November you can just text an excuse like normal siblings, no hard feelings. *(lays her palm flat against the rough wool of the blanket wrap; her fingers go suddenly still for a beat before she tips her chin toward the dark stairwell)* Come on then, Dan, bring the guilt-glue—let's go rescue our crooked window before that poor clerk runs out of petrol. *(turns and starts lightly up the stairs toward the flat)*

**Dan:** *(keeps his left heel held an inch off the threshold step for a beat, picks up Ada's metal clamp off his toolbox lid and drops it inside with a sharp clink, then pulls his own keyring from his pocket)* Good thing I still have my flat key on me, Cora, or you'd be locking up upstairs with your teeth. *(looks squarely from Ben and Ada toward the stairs)* And drop the November excuse clause—don't build everyone a polite exit before we've even got the painting off the skirting board. The only reason we're all stalling on the step over keys and rota rules is because the minute that clerk pulls away from the kerb, Mother's place isn't ours anymore and none of us has an excuse to just turn up. So yes, Ben—first Sunday stands, all four of us, and tonight I'm finally gluing that mitre like I told her I would. *(lowers his heel flat, sets his toolbox down inside the doorway beside the wedge so his hands are free, and starts up the dark stairs after Cora)* Keep the window rolled down out there, Ada—you'll get my key as soon as the frame's down on the pavement.

*(Cora and Dan's footsteps climb the dark stairwell overhead.)*

**Ada:** *(catches Cora's key, pulls her own keyring from her cardigan pocket, and works her worn brass flat key free of the split loop until it drops against Cora's with a small clink; stares for a beat at the bare ring left on her finger, then pockets it and folds her right cuff tighter into a hard square)* I got that key cut on the High Street the same afternoon I put the hook in the plaster and hung that painted window for her. *(glances up the dark stairwell after the footsteps, then looks steadily at Ben)* First Sunday stands, Ben—all four of us, no November excuses. Only don't expect me to turn up empty-handed and sit with my hands in my lap while Cora boils a kettle: tonight I'm bringing the spirit level and a proper brass picture hook from my boot so that frame sits level over her table, and first Sundays I'm bringing something hot for the middle of it. *(holds out her hand beside the two loose keys toward Ben's torn van sheet)* Tear me off those meter numbers for the agent so you keep a copy, leave your key wedged on your ring till you see that frame come round the banister, and take Dan's toolbox when they reach the bottom so he keeps both hands under the rail—I'll stand right here by the kerb so that clerk doesn't put it in gear before they're down.

*(Upstairs, the flat door pulls shut and double-locks; slow, careful footsteps start back down the dark stairs and round the last turn of the banister, with Cora and Dan carrying the painting wrapped in the grey blanket between them, supporting the bottom rail.)*

**Ben:** *(looks up as the blanket-wrapped frame rounds the banister, pushes his brass flat key the rest of the way off the split ring with his thumb, and glances at it once before laying it onto Ada's open palm)* She took that one off the hook behind the kitchen door one night after we'd sat at that window till it got light—told me to keep it on my ring so I wouldn't stand out here pressing the buzzer at three in the morning. *(tears off the bottom of the van sheet with the duplicate meter numbers, sets the slip beside the three keys in Ada's hand, and pockets his own copy with his keyring)* Give the clerk that slip; I've kept the copy for the final electric bill. Washing-up rota's fine by me, *(catches his breath)* —Cora, and I'll stop at the corner shop on the way over for milk and biscuits so the rule starts tonight—nobody turns up empty-handed, and first Sunday's fixed for all four of us. *(picks up Dan's toolbox from beside the wedge and backs flat against the propped door to clear the threshold)* Three keys ready—Dan's makes the full set as soon as they've got that bottom rail onto Cora's back seat. Mind the step with that corner; I've got the tools.

**Cora:** *(eases her end of the blanket-wrapped frame carefully down the bottom step opposite Dan, keeping one palm tucked firm under the bottom rail as they step out into the street air)* Easy on the threshold—if we drop it in front of Ada now she'll revoke my tenancy on the spot. *(pauses on the pavement with the wrapped painting held steady between her and Dan, glancing from the three brass keys glinting in Ada's palm to Ben holding the toolbox; her fingers against the blanket over the split corner go suddenly still)* ...Right, alright, message received—November excuse clause officially torn up. Tell you what: I've got three spare hooks on the back of my kitchen door doing absolutely nothing right now, so stop at Timpson's on Monday if you fancy it, get three front-door keys cut off mine, and you can all let yourselves in when the kettle's too slow—saves my buzzer getting worn out like hers did. *(smiles lightly, shifting her weight toward her car up the street)* Drop your last key in Ada's collection plate once we've got this tucked on my back seat, Dan, and don't let me beat you three back to mine—somebody's got to hold the spirit level straight while I find four mugs that actually match.

# Director's Note

### What Was Varied & Directed (Staging Choices vs. Fixed Play)
- **Strict isolation & observable-only cues:** Each sibling ran in a separate read-only `research` sub-context holding only the shared premise, `Piece`, active scene opening, their own Persona row and single private memory sentence, and `promptbook.md`. Cues followed dramatic address rather than rigid round-robin (e.g., Dan cutting straight across Ada's opening chore assignments; Cora flipping her slip immediately after Ben in Scene 1 Turn 11 to avoid redundant blank-slip turns; Dan opening Scene 2 after Scene 1 closed before he answered `"Deal"`).
- **Authored-open conditions supplied as pressure:**
  1. *Second press on the entry buzzer (Scene 1):* After all four siblings ignored the opening buzzer across four turns to circle the painting, I added `*(The entry buzzer sounds again from downstairs—two short presses.)*` to keep departure pressure live without introducing an outside speaker into the flat.
  2. *Adjudicating Ben's 4-strip draw:* Once Ben tore four strips off the van sheet and all four entered the draw, I held the physical strip state outside the performers (preventing premature or conflicting reveals on Turns 8–9) and placed the marked strip in Cora's hand (`Ben`, `Ada`, and `Dan` blank). Awarding Cora the mark tested all four Shadows simultaneously: Cora could no longer hide safely behind a sideways joke, Ben lost under the "fair" rule he used to avoid asking for himself, Ada lost custody of the burden, and Dan still owed Mother the unfixed mitre.
  3. *Identity of the idling car & stair retrieval (Scene 2):* When Dan asked whose unfamiliar car was idling at the kerb (since he would recognize Ada's car), I resolved the open condition by connecting both scenes: the idling car was the letting agent's clerk (who had rung the buzzer twice) waiting for the final flat keys, while Ada's car stayed locked three doors down after she fetched her blanket wrap and clamp. Once Cora and Dan climbed the stairs to fetch the painting, I supplied two brief off-step audio/physical transitions so Ada and Ben had a quiet two-hander beat on the threshold before Cora and Dan carried the wrapped painting out into the street.

### What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*, 12 turns):** Allowed past 10 turns because the strip-draw was actively resolving rather than looping, coming cleanly to rest on Ada's `"Deal."` Ada tried to fold taking the painting into practical transport; Dan exposed the three-hour standoff and confessed putting off Mother's request to glue the frame; Ben imposed a four-strip draw and—only *after* drawing blank and no longer speaking "for myself"—revealed his sleepless-night memory disguised as a 2 a.m. kitchen-table rule; Cora pulled the mark, admitted the curled snapshot on her fridge, and made a deniable tonight/Sunday offer; Ada accepted while ordering nobody to lift the canvas until she fetched her wrap and clamp, volunteering Dan's glue without asking him.
- **Carried into Scene 2 (*The entrance*, 8 turns):** Because Ada forbade lifting the frame without the blanket wrap, the painting remained upstairs against the skirting board in the dark flat (`who has it if anyone`: Cora held the winning slip; nobody yet held the painting). Unsettled were Dan's sidelined consent, Ada's impulse to manage the handover, and whether Cora's Sunday offer was merely a polite exit. At the propped-open door, Dan named the Play's `Load Bearing` truth directly—once the clerk drove off with the keys, they would have no excuse to turn up—forcing Cora to drop her `"November excuse clause"`, Ada to leave her boot shut and hand over the wrap and clamp, Ben to hold his flat key back until "first Sunday of every month" became binding, and Cora and Dan to carry the blanket-wrapped painting down together, ending with Cora offering the three spare hooks behind her own kitchen door for cut keys.

### Supplied Material, Promptbook & Staging Friction
- **Where `company.md` helped:** The `Piece` (`Apparent` split corner and kitchen-window oil; `Load Bearing` nothing left in the flat to hold private memories) interlocked tightly with all four Personas, Shadows, Tells, and memories. Crucially, the exact wording of Scene 2's opening—*"Carry forward where the painting is, who has it if anyone, and what remains unsettled"*—cleanly accommodated the painting still sitting upstairs against the skirting board when Scene 1 rested, turning the trip back up the dark stairs and the handover of the four flat keys into the spine of Scene 2.
- **Where `promptbook.md` helped / had no occasion to matter:** The System rule kept every performer strictly in Play Mode without meta-commentary or behavioral explanation; its exception clause (*"unless the Human explicitly asks for analysis"*) had no occasion to matter since the Human watched silently throughout.
- **Staging friction (multi-context mechanics vs. the Play itself):** No structural defect appeared in the authored Play. Two points required active Director mediation solely because performers ran in isolated sub-contexts: (1) hidden-state props invented mid-scene (Ben's four paper strips) require external state-keeping so isolated performers cannot contradict one another on who drew the mark, and (2) Scene 2's open condition (*"One car idles at the kerb"*) needed a single canonical world-fact once Dan asked whose car it was so Ada and Ben wouldn't invent different drivers. *(Minor harness note: Dan's sub-context emitted two stray un-cued turns after his first Scene 2 turn before going idle; those were discarded so strictly one cued turn stood per beat.)*
