# *Mother's Painting* — Staged Performance

## Scene 1: *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Ada:** *Crosses to the wall phone by the door, wiping grit from her palm onto her jeans before lifting the handset.* That'll be the agent for the meter readings and the keys. Ben, check the airing cupboard's actually clear; Cora, if there's an old tea towel left in the kitchen drawer, bring it through so I can wrap the kitchen-window oil for my boot before we lock up.

**Cora:** *Peels a stray strip of parcel tape around her knuckle, glancing toward the skirting board with a light laugh.* Tea towels went out with the kettle, Ada—tell you what, save your upholstery the splinters from that dodgy corner and let me tuck it under my arm for the train, my place could use an extra window anyway.

**Ben:** *Pulls the airing cupboard door open, looks inside, and clicks it shut again before stepping toward the skirting board.* Airing cupboard's empty. Hold on, though—nobody's tucking that in a boot or on a train just yet. Everything else in the flat went on the list and got drawn in order; if more than one of us wants the oil, we put all four names in and do it properly before the agent comes up.

**Dan:** *Steps closer to the skirting board, glancing once at the open corner of the frame before looking back at the other three.* Spare us the list, Ben—all four of us walked past this thing twenty times today pretending it was scrap so nobody had to say out loud why we're still standing in an empty flat. Buzz the agent up or tell him to wait on the stairs, Ada, because I want it too, and you don't get it just by announcing where it's going in your car.

**Ada:** *Presses the speak button, tells the intercom "Two minutes, come on up to the second floor," clicks the release, and hangs the handset back on its hook. She turns to face them, folding her right shirt cuff once, then again, into a tight square.* I didn't put it on the list because it's coming apart at the mitre and won't survive a train rack or being tossed in a move—I'm the one who drove over and hung it for her when she moved in here, and I've got the wood glue and clamps at home to fix it properly. Dan, take the meter numbers off the hall cupboard before he reaches the landing, and let's not turn the last five minutes into a row on the doorstep.

**Cora:** *Her fingers go suddenly still on the scrap of parcel tape for a beat before she tucks both hands into her pockets and flashes a crooked grin.* Easy, you two, let the poor bloke reach the landing before we start drawing straws over split pine. Nobody said anything about a luggage rack, Ada—let me wrap it in my coat and nurse it home on my lap, and if that corner budges a millimetre before my stop I'll courier it straight back to your clamps, deal?

**Dan:** *Stays where he is, his right heel lifting off the bare floorboards and holding an inch in the air.* Read the meters yourself, Ada—I'm not sticking my head in the hall cupboard so you lot can stitch up a deal behind my back. And drop the talk about clamps and train laps; she dragged that canvas to every flat she ever rented, there's nothing else of hers left in these rooms, and that's the only reason any of us cares about a split bit of pine.

**Ben:** *Pulls the folded inventory sheet from his jacket pocket, tears a blank strip off the bottom into four equal squares, and holds them flat on his palm.* Fine—Dan's right about what it is, which is exactly why nobody walks out with it on who brought wood glue or who speaks fastest before the bloke hits the landing. Ada glues the mitre this week, and then it does a year in each house—*a quick, sharp catch of breath*—Ada, me, Cora, Dan, drawn out of a hand right now for who starts. Take a square before he knocks, and I'll read the meters myself.

**Ada:** *Pulls the hall cupboard open herself, jots the meter digits onto the back of the movers' receipt, and sets the tagged flat keys on the shelf under the intercom. She looks at Ben's open palm without touching the paper, then takes a step toward the skirting board.* Put those away, Ben. When I hung that frame for her in here, I had to hold the bottom corner together with both hands just to get the wire onto the hook—passing it round four houses every year will shake it to bits. Let me fix it and hang it in my hall where it stays put; you all have keys to mine whenever you want to see it. Cora, hand him that receipt when he reaches the door, and let me get this down to the car before he's standing in the middle of us.

**Dan:** *Sets his heel down and steps straight between Ada and the skirting board.* Leave it where it is, Ada. Once that goes up in your hall, Ben's little paper rota is dead by Christmas and the rest of us are trooping round yours on Sundays like you've inherited the job of being Mum. Anyone can glue and pin a loose corner—I can fix that frame myself—so nobody's sneaking it down to a car boot just because the agent's on the stairs.

## Scene 2: *The entrance*

*The keys have been handed over upstairs. Downstairs, the front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. Carried down out of the empty flat, the painting leans against the hallway wall just inside the propped-open front door; nobody has possession of it yet, and what happens to it remains unsettled.*

**Cora:** *Stops just inside the propped-open door, drumming her fingers lightly on her coat pocket as she glances from the idling car at the kerb to the painting against the wall.* Careful—four of us hovering by an open door with an engine running outside looks like the world's saddest art heist, and we can't even hide upstairs anymore. Look, since Dan's vetoed Ada's hall and Ada's vetoed Ben's lottery, let me play Switzerland and take it home on my lap—Mum sent me a photo of that window when I first moved away, so I've done my stint with a picture and I'll happily send you three a snap of it on my wall instead.

**Ben:** *Steps clear of the doorway so the exhaust from the kerb doesn't drift past him, taking the four paper squares back out of his pocket and creasing one corner inside his fist.* Keeping the thing on your own wall and texting us a photo isn't Switzerland, Cora, that's just winning without a vote. If Ada says the mitre won't survive travelling every year, fine—scrap the rota. One trip, for keeps. Four slips in my coat pocket, one folded corner, youngest to oldest; whoever pulls it takes the painting home today and pays a framer to pin it properly.

**Ada:** *Nudges the wooden wedge back an inch with her shoe so the front door swings partway shut against the exhaust from the kerb, then folds her left shirt cuff twice into a hard square.* Funny how oldest goes first when the meters need reading and the keys need handing back, Ben, and dead last into your coat pocket the minute the work's finished. Keep your Sundays to yourself, Dan, and spare us the phone snaps, Cora—I was the last one to hang that canvas for Mum when she moved in here, and I'm not leaving it to a blind dip on the doormat so a shop stranger can staple the pine. Mind your coat off that split corner, Dan, and hold the street door clear while I brace the bottom rail out to my car.

**Dan:** *Shifts his weight onto the ball of his foot beside the hallway wall, his heel lifting off the tiles and staying up.* I'm not holding the door for you to load your boot, Ada, and I'm not dipping into Ben's coat pocket just because he threw me "youngest first" like a bone. That corner's still open because Mum asked me to fix it for her and I kept telling her I'd bring my tools next time I came round—until there wasn't a next time. No shop stranger's stapling it and it's not going in your clamps; she asked me to mend it, and I'm taking it home to do it.

**Ben:** *Opens his fist, flattens the folded inventory sheet like a tray between them, and lays the four paper squares face-down across it.* The odds are one in four whether you pick first or fourth, Ada—take first pick if you don't trust the maths. And—*his breath catches*—Dan, nobody's taking it to a shop stranger now; use your own tools and mend the corner like you told her you would. But we can't start scoring who hung the wire or who got a photo in the post, because there's no fair measure for that—we draw right here for whose wall it lives on once the glue's dry, and all four of us walk out knowing it was settled straight.

**Cora:** *Her fingers go suddenly still against her coat pocket as she looks down at the four squares on Ben's sheet; after a beat, she flashes a quick, dry grin and waves a hand over the paper without touching a slip.* Fold the casino up, Ben—if a neighbour walks in now they'll think you're running three-card monte on the doormat. Nobody's arguing with Dan mending the mitre like he promised her, so tell you what: let me nurse it up the line on my lap today before Ada annexes it into her boot, and I'll spring for Dan's train fare up to mine this weekend with his toolbox—curry's on me while the glue dries, Ada's clamps get a rest, and it can hang safe on my wall till you three sign a peace treaty.

**Ada:** *Crouches briefly beside the frame against the hallway wall, checks the gap in the open corner with her thumb without lifting the canvas, and straightens up.* Carrying it down the stairs has opened that mitre wider already, so it's going nowhere near a train lap today, Cora, even with a weekend curry thrown in to keep it on your wall. Dan, if Mum asked you to pin that corner, bring your toolbox straight round to mine tonight and finish the job you promised her—you don't win the painting for keeps because you left it too late while she was here. And put the moving sheet away, Ben; we already split every stick of furniture off that paper, and I didn't hang this window for her upstairs just to raffle it off the back of an inventory.

**Ben:** *Presses the creased corner of the slip flat with his thumb, pulls a pen from his inside pocket, and sets it across the four squares.* Fine—no raffle, and nobody takes a widening mitre onto a train or parks it on their own wall waiting for a peace treaty that'll never happen. Dan pins the corner tonight before the frame gives out, and right now, before anyone lifts it out to that car, each of us takes a square and writes down who keeps it—any name here except your own. If you won't leave it to luck, leave it to who the rest of us trust with her window.

**Dan:** *Drops his heel flat against the tiles and looks down at the pen on Ben's palm without touching it.* Put the pen away, Ben—we all know how "any name except your own" goes: Ada and Cora write down sensible neutral Ben just to block each other, nobody writes me after I admitted I let her down, and you win the canvas without once saying why your breath catches every time you talk about it. I know leaving it too late doesn't win me a medal, Ada, so don't summon me round yours or offer me a curry like a hired joiner before showing me the door. All three of you just agreed I'm pinning that corner, so it comes back to mine tonight to get mended—and nobody's settling where it lives on scraps of paper while that car's idling outside.

**Ben:** *Slides the pen back into his inside pocket, gathers the four squares into his palm, and folds the inventory sheet shut.* All right, you want me to ask for it plain instead of keeping score? I used to sit with her at that window when I couldn't sleep, and I want it on my wall just as much as any of you. There—now all four of us have said our piece, and there's still only one frame. Upstairs you told Ada that once it goes up in someone's house any deal is dead by Christmas, so the same rule holds for your workbench: take it tonight and pin the mitre, no hooks in any wall, and Sunday afternoon all four of us sit down and nobody hangs it for keeps until three of us agree whose house it stays in.

# Director's Note

### What Was Varied or Directed
- **Fixed Play, isolated briefs:** Kept `company.md` completely fixed. Each sibling ran in an isolated read-only sub-context given only the shared premise/Piece/opening, their own Persona row and single private memory sentence, and `promptbook.md`.
- **Observable-only constraint:** Directed performers to output strictly what could be seen and heard in the room (physical action in italics and spoken dialogue, with Tells allowed to surface under pressure rather than mechanically on every beat).
- **Dynamic cueing & open conditions:** Left open authored details unforced—Ada interpreted the downstairs buzzer in *The flat* as the letting agent arriving for meter numbers and keys, creating an organic two-minute countdown, while the idling car at the kerb in *The entrance* remained an unclaimed threshold pressure (exhaust drifting in, engine running). Turn order followed whoever held the sharpest dramatic pressure rather than a fixed rotation (Ada opened Scene 1 on the buzzer; Cora opened Scene 2 at the propped-open door).

### What Happened & What Carried Across Scenes
- **Scene 1 (*The flat*):** Ada folded claiming the painting into final flat-clearing orders; Cora countered sideways with a joke about saving Ada's boot from splinters by taking it on the train; Ben blocked both with the inventory-list precedent and tore four slips for a yearly rotation; Dan named what everyone was avoiding, refused to duck into the hall cupboard lest a deal be struck behind his back, and stepped physically between Ada and the skirting board as the agent reached the landing.
- **Carry-forward into Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** With the keys surrendered upstairs and nobody permitted to claim the frame, the painting carried down to lean unclaimed inside the propped-open building entrance alongside Ben's four torn inventory squares, Ada's grievance over carrying the practical burden without authority, and the unresolved three-way veto between car boot, train lap, and lottery.
- **Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** Threshold pressure forced every private memory into the open. Cora tucked Mother's mailed photograph inside a "Switzerland" offer (including train fare and curry for Dan); Dan admitted the mitre was still open because he kept putting off Mother's request to fix it until she died; Ada used the stair-widened mitre to insist Dan bring his tools to *her* house rather than "winning" the canvas by having been late. Ben kept mutating his procedural rule—from yearly rota to single-winner draw to a "write any name except your own" ballot—until Dan clocked Ben's observable Tell (*breath catching*) and exposed the ballot as a way to win without ever asking plainly. Stripped of his cover, Ben confessed sitting with Mother at that window when he couldn't sleep, pocketed the slips, and framed a provisional ceasefire: Dan takes the canvas home tonight strictly to pin the corner ("no hooks in any wall"), with permanent custody deferred to Sunday under a three-of-four rule.

### Material & Promptbook Assessment
- **Where `company.md` helped:** Linking the Piece's physical flaw (`frame open at one corner`) directly to Ada's and Dan's private memories—and giving each sibling a distinct physical Tell—let character psychology collide through concrete stage business. Because Tells were staged as observable behavior, Dan's performer could genuinely notice Ben's breath catching across turns and force Ben out from behind his rules without any private-sheet leakage.
- **Where `promptbook.md` helped or had no occasion to matter:** The System instruction (*"Remain in Play Mode throughout with no commentary, explanations of behavior, or questions to the Human..."*) kept all twenty turns crisp and theatrical with zero meta-leakage. Its exception clause (*"unless the Human explicitly asks for analysis"*) had no occasion to matter during the run, as the Human watched silently from outside the fiction.
- **Staging friction (staging choice vs. Play gap):** Moving from *The flat* (an emptied flat being locked/vacated) to *The entrance* (*"Carry forward where the painting is, who has it if anyone"*) creates a delicate physical bridge when Scene 1 ends in a deadlock where no sibling lets another take possession: someone still has to get the canvas down the stairs without that transit counting as custody. Rather than treating this as a Play defect, I bridged it via a neutral staging carry-forward (brought down when handing over the keys and leaned unclaimed inside the propped-open front door), which Ada then turned into active stakes (*"Carrying it down the stairs has opened that mitre wider already"*). Likewise, *"One car idles at the kerb"* leaves ambiguous whether the vehicle belongs to a sibling, a cab, or a stranger; leaving it unspecified worked cleanly as shared physical atmosphere (exhaust at the propped door, departure urgency) even alongside Ada's references to her own car boot.
