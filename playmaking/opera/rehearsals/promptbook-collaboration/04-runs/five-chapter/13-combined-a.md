# *Mother's Painting* — Staged Performance

## Scene 1: *The flat*

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Ada**
Ada crosses to the wall phone by the door, presses the entry button without lifting the handset, and turns back to the empty room while folding her left cuff into a hard square. She picks up the roll of bubble wrap beside the skirting board and crouches by the painting.

"That'll be the landlord for the meter readings. Ben, take the keys down to the lobby and walk him through the cellar—Cora, check the kitchen cupboards one last time. I'll wrap this and get it into my boot before he comes up."

**Ben**
Ben pulls the ring of keys from his jacket pocket, stops short of the hall door, and steps across the bare floorboards to stand between the painting and the doorway. His breath catches audibly right on her name.

"Hold on—Ada. Nothing went into anyone's car today without all four of us agreeing to it first. Leave the wrap on the floor; if this missed the list, we tear four slips of paper and draw for it fairly before anybody goes down to the cellar."

**Dan**
Dan steps across the bare floorboards toward the skirting board, stops a pace from Ada, and lifts his right heel, holding it off the floor.

"What list, Ben—the one the three of you worked out before I even got here? Nobody missed that painting; all four of us have been stepping around it since noon waiting for the other three to go downstairs, and we're not tearing up paper slips for another polite little family lottery. Ada didn't even bother inventing an errand to clear me out of the room, so drop the wrap—I'm carrying it home under my arm."

**Cora**
Cora glances once past the kitchen doorframe, drumming her fingers lightly against her elbow, then strolls over and crouches at the other end of the painting across from Ada. She nudges the split corner of the frame with her thumb, and her fingers go suddenly still against the wood.

"Cupboards are bare, Ada, unless somebody called dibs on a shelf liner—and Dan, tuck this under your arm and the whole frame's coming apart on the stairs. Tell you what, Ben, toss me the keys: I'll go down and charm the landlord through the cellar myself if you three throw in the wonky kitchen window as hazard pay."

**Ada**
Ada slides the edge of the bubble wrap firmly underneath the bottom rail of the painting, lifting the split corner clear of Cora's thumb, and tears a strip of brown tape from the roll with her teeth.

"Nobody's drawing scraps of paper as if we're dividing kettle leads, Ben, and Cora, it isn't hazard pay—that mitre needs clamping and gluing properly before it's hung anywhere, and my workbench is the only place set up for it. You three have already loaded the dresser, the armchairs, and every box you asked for; Dan, go out onto the landing and tell the landlord we'll be two minutes while I tape the corners so the glass doesn't shift."

**Dan**
Dan steps in beside the skirting board, plants his palm flat on the top rail of the frame above Ada's bubble wrap, and lifts his right heel, holding it off the floorboards.

"I'm not stepping out onto the landing so the three of you can carve this up behind a closed door, Ada—and drop the speech about your workbench. Mum asked me three times since autumn to bring a clamp round and glue that corner, and every time I told her I'd do it next weekend. Ben, go meet the landlord you're all pretending isn't walking up the stairs right now, because nobody else is touching that frame—I'm taking it home and fixing it myself."

**Ben**
Ben slips the keys back into his jacket pocket, steps up to the skirting board, and sets his hand firmly on the intact upright edge of the frame. His breath catches short as he says his brother's name.

"Leaving a job undone since autumn doesn't give you dibs, Dan—and there isn't even glass in an oil painting to shift, Ada. If the four of us can't agree before the landlord reaches this landing, the only fair rule is nobody claims it today: anything disputed stays with whoever's holding the meter paperwork until the final bills clear, so step back and let me lock it in my flat till we've squared the account."

**Cora**
Cora lets out a quick laugh and hooks her hand onto the loose upright rail opposite Ben's, tapping her fingertips lightly against the wood until her fingers go suddenly still on the frame.

"Oh, 'hold it with the meter paperwork'—nice try, Ben, once this crosses your threshold we'll never see it again, right alongside Ada's imaginary glass and Dan's mythical clamp. Tell you what: Mum sent me a photograph of this painting when I first moved away, and I've still got it at mine—you can lock the snapshot up with the gas bill, free of charge, and I'll take the actual paint off your hands right now."

**Ada**
Ada presses the strip of brown tape onto the bubble wrap, folds her right cuff into a hard square, and grips the bottom rail of the frame with both hands, holding its weight down against the skirting board.

"Canvas, then—not glass. Take your hands off the rails before you split that corner clean in two. You want to talk about autumn promises and posted photographs? When Mum moved into this flat, none of you were even in the postcode—I was the one standing on a kitchen chair driving the hook into that plaster, and I'm the last person who hung this painting for her. Ben, take the meter sheets out to the stairs; Dan, lift from the base if you won't let go, because this is going straight down to my car."

**Ben**
Ben drops to one knee beside the skirting board, pulls his free hand empty from his jacket pocket, and grips the intact upright rail with both hands, pinning his side of the frame to the floorboards. His breath catches hard in his throat on Cora's name, and catches again on Ada's.

"All right, leave the gas bill out of it, Cora—and driving a hook into that plaster doesn't settle anything, Ada. Mum took this painting with her to every flat she lived in, long before this postcode, and I used to sit with her at that window when I couldn't sleep. Cora already has a photograph of it at hers, and clamps and picture hooks aren't sitting at that window—by any fair count this stays out of your boot and comes home with me."

## Scene 2: *The entrance*

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. None of the four siblings yielded their claim upstairs while clearing out for the landlord; all four have come down to the ground-floor entrance together, where the painting—its bottom rail still wrapped in Ada's taped strip of bubble wrap, its split corner still open—leans against the wall just inside the propped-open front door.*

**Cora**
Cora steps to the propped-open front door, glances out at the car idling at the kerb, and drops a hand onto the frame just above Ada's taped bubble wrap, sliding the painting an inch along the wall toward the threshold. She flicks the loose end of brown tape once with her fingernail, and then her fingers go suddenly still against the wood.

"Lovely maths upstairs, Ben—I've got a snapshot at mine so I'm all squared up, is it? Next time I owe you twenty quid I'll post you a picture of a note. Tell you what, before that car at the kerb runs out of petrol watching us: you three can split every box I put my name on today—Ada can even bill me for the bubble wrap—and I'll walk this wonky frame straight out to the pavement and save us an audience."

**Dan**
Dan steps straight into the propped-open doorway between Cora and the front step, sets his hand firmly on the top rail of the frame above the split corner, and lifts his right heel, holding it off the entrance tiles.

"Let whoever's idling at the kerb stare, Cora—nobody wants your cast-off boxes, and sliding that frame along the wall so we look polite for the street is only pulling the open mitre wider. Ben doesn't get to cross me off his 'fair count' as if sitting at an old window years ago wipes out what Mum asked me to do this autumn, and Ada's picture hook belongs to the landlord's plaster now. The flat's gone, and the one thing on this painting that isn't finished yet is the corner she asked me to glue—so keep your boxes, because I'm taking it home tonight and clamping it shut."

**Ada**
Ada steps up beside the wall, wedges the toe of her shoe against the wrapped bottom corner so the frame cannot slide another inch toward the step, and pulls the spare flap of bubble wrap taut up the canvas under Cora's hand before folding her left cuff into a hard square.

"Nobody is dumping their boxes back in the hall for me to re-sort, Cora, and you didn't even bring a clamp with you today, Dan—if we leave an unfinished job to you it'll sit propped behind your sofa till next Christmas. Every flat Mum moved into, somebody had to carry what couldn't get broken and see it safely onto the next wall instead of just sitting by it or promising next weekend. Ben, step out to the kerb and wave that driver on before they block the cars in; Cora, pull the doorstop once I've taped this upright, because I'm taking charge of the frame tonight."

**Ben**
Ben stays inside the front hall without glancing toward the idling car, steps in beside Ada at the wall, and grips the intact side rail, pinning the loose flap of bubble wrap flat against the wood. His breath catches short right on Dan's name.

"That driver can find their own way round—and the rule in any clear-out is simple: nobody unpacks loaded boxes on the pavement to buy a trade, Cora; nobody gets dibs for a repair left undone since autumn, Dan; and the one running the sort doesn't award the last unlisted piece to herself, Ada. With the photograph already at Cora's, there's only one fair way this lands—take your shoe off the corner and let me carry it out to my car."

**Cora**
Cora gives a short, bright laugh, folds her corner of the bubble wrap back down off the painted window, and hooks her hand directly onto the bare wood of the split side rail. She taps her thumb twice beside the open mitre, and then her fingers go suddenly still.

"Pull your own doorstop, Ada—I'm not wrapping service for your boot, and Dan, blocking the doorway with empty pockets still isn't a clamp. As for Ben inventing a brand-new bylaw on every landing: tell you what, forget the boxes—put the whole final meter bill on my tab, and I'll tear that snapshot at mine clean in half if it gets me off your exclusions list, so shift over and let me take the actual window home."

**Ada**
Ada keeps her shoe wedged hard against the bottom corner, pulls a fresh strip of brown tape taut across the lower half of the bubble wrap to bind the split mitre inward, and slides her free hand underneath the base of the frame.

"Leave the wrap over the paint, Cora—paying one last meter bill at the finish line doesn't buy the canvas, and tearing up a photograph Mum posted you would only waste two things instead of saving one. And I'm not a hired clerk running a sort for your benefit, Ben: nobody quoted bylaws at me when the van needed booking or the flat needed scrubbing down, and this stayed off the list because a split frame can't go in a Luton van—it has to lie flat across a back seat. Dan, clear the threshold before someone catches a heel on the step, and Ben, go work out the meter split with Cora by the postboxes while I carry this out."

**Dan**
Dan shifts a half-step back to plant himself square across the propped-open doorway, sets both hands on the top rail to hold the frame down against the floor, and lifts his right heel, holding it clear of the threshold step.

"There it is—it didn't 'miss' the list upstairs, Ada, you left it off yourself before the Luton van even pulled up, deciding where it was going behind my back before I'd set foot in the building. That clamp isn't mythical either, Cora; it's been sitting in my kitchen drawer with a tube of wood glue since November while I kept telling myself there was another weekend, and you don't get to buy it off me with a meter bill any more than Ben gets to legislate me out of the hall. Nobody's carrying this across that step to a back seat—let go of the rails so we don't snap the corner right here on the tiles, because I'm carrying it home flat in my own two arms tonight."

**Ben**
Ben keeps both feet planted on the entrance tiles away from the postboxes and slides his second hand down the intact upright rail just above Ada's shoe, holding his edge of the frame plumb against the wall. His breath catches audibly right on Ada's name.

"Nobody's stepping over to the postboxes: meter bills get split four equal ways, Cora—you don't buy Mum's things off the rest of us, and threatening to rip up the photograph she sent you doesn't wipe out that you've already got it. You just admitted you kept this off the morning sheet before the Luton van pulled up, Ada, and in any honest split, hiding something off the list forfeits your claim to it. An open mitre can't go down the street in bare arms either, Dan—it needs a flat back seat, and mine's the only one left with a clean claim, so take your hands off the frame."

**Cora**
Cora lets out a bright laugh and brings her second hand onto the bare wood of the split side rail just above Ada's fresh strip of brown tape, gripping her edge of the frame with both hands. She drums her fingertips lightly once against the wood, and then her fingers go suddenly still.

"Not stepping near the postboxes, Ada—bold speech from the woman who hid this off the morning sheet—and Dan, keeping wood glue shut in a drawer since November doesn't win custody either. Tell you what, though: Ada's right that tearing up my snapshot would be a waste, and Ben swears having a photograph counts as having the painting—so I'll get three copies printed and posted out to you lot on Monday, my treat. Then everybody's got what Mum sent me, and I'll take the split frame home tonight."

**Ada**
Ada drops the roll of tape into her coat pocket, folds her right cuff into a hard square, and crouches to wedge both palms underneath the wrapped bottom rail, bracing the base of the frame tight against the entrance tiles.

"Keep your Monday reprints, Cora—a chemist's copy of your snapshot isn't the painting I hung on Mum's wall. And don't accuse me of hiding it off the morning sheet, Ben: the morning sheet was the van manifest, and this leaned in plain sight against the skirting board from the minute I lifted it off the hook so the movers wouldn't crush that split corner with the wardrobes before Dan even arrived. Keeping it off a lorry doesn't forfeit my claim—it's the only reason there's still a frame here for the three of you to grab at. Dan, step clear of the threshold; Ben, kick the doorstop out and shut the street door behind me, because I've got the base and it's going onto my back seat."

## Director's Note

- **Direction & staging choices:**
  - Kept `company.md` and `promptbook.md` fixed, isolating each sibling in an independent read-only `research` sub-context supplied only with the shared situation and Piece, the active scene's Opening, `promptbook.md`, their single Persona row, and their single private memory sentence.
  - Directed each performer to act strictly within one live, observable beat per cue (no internal monologue, time skips, or dictating siblings' reactions) so siblings could physically block or redirect one another in real time.
  - Routed turn-passing dynamically per `# Collaboration` (*whichever sibling is most affected responds next without fixed rotation*): `Ada -> Ben -> Dan -> Cora -> Ada -> Dan -> Ben -> Cora -> Ada -> Ben` in Scene 1, and `Cora -> Dan -> Ada -> Ben -> Cora -> Ada -> Dan -> Ben -> Cora -> Ada` in Scene 2. I cued Ada first in Scene 1 (as eldest organizer reacting to the entry buzzer) and Cora first in Scene 2 (stung directly by Ben ending Scene 1 using her photograph to exclude her).

- **What unfolded & what carried across scenes:**
  - In *The flat*, Ada buzzed the landlord in, assigned errands to Ben and Cora while skipping Dan entirely, and crouched to wrap the painting for her own boot. Ben blocked the exit with a paper-slip lottery; Dan called out the phony ritual and his exclusion; Cora offered sideways to handle the cellar if given the frame as "hazard pay." When Ada slid bubble wrap under the split mitre and claimed only her workbench could clamp it, she hit Dan's secret guilt directly—forcing out his private memory first (Turn 6), followed in cascade by Cora's posted photograph (Turn 8), Ada's hanging the painting last on the kitchen plaster (Turn 9), and Ben's sleepless nights sitting with Mother at that window (Turn 10).
  - Because Scene 1 ended in a four-way physical grip on the frame at the 10-turn mark while the landlord came up for handover, I carried that exact physical and emotional state into *The entrance*: none of the four yielded possession upstairs, so all four came down together with the painting—still taped along its bottom rail in Ada's bubble wrap, its top mitre still open—leaning inside the propped-open front door. In Scene 2, every confession from Scene 1 turned into live ammunition: Ben repeatedly used Cora's snapshot to disqualify her (driving her from trading boxes, to offering the whole meter bill and threatening to tear the snapshot, to offering chemist reprints for the others), while Ada let slip in Turn 6 that she had kept the painting off the morning Luton-van list—immediately validating Dan's Scene 1 suspicion ("the list... worked out before I even got here") and uniting Dan, Ben, and Cora in accusing her of hiding it behind their backs.

- **Where `company.md` and `promptbook.md` helped, had no occasion to matter, or made staging hard:**
  - *Helped:* `promptbook.md`'s `# Knowledge` rule (*withhold private memory until another move threatens to cost them the painting*) paired with the concrete physicality of the Piece (oil canvas without glass, split mitre corner) worked remarkably well: all four held back their memories through the early tactical maneuvering and revealed them organically in Turns 6–10 only when physically crowded off the frame, even catching Ada's stressed slip about "glass" on an oil painting. Non-fixed `# Collaboration` also produced sharp dramatic hinges whenever one sibling's excuse struck another's Shadow.
  - *Had no occasion to matter:* `# Human`'s intervention clause and `# System`'s analysis-on-request clause had no occasion to fire since the Human watched silently throughout. Additionally, because Scene 2's Opening leaves *"One car idles at the kerb"* unattributed, the cast treated the idling car as a watching stranger/obstruction on the street while assuming their own cars were parked nearby rather than treating it as a single shared ride.
  - *Made staging hard (Play/Promptbook friction vs. staging choice):* `# Agent` instructs every Persona to meet *"any attempt to settle who takes the painting with a fresh claim, deflection, or block rather than an easy compromise."* While this generated relentless escalation and prevented premature collapse, it also made a genuine dramatic *rest* nearly impossible inside the fiction: no sibling could yield an inch or pause the claim-making, pushing both Scene 1 and Scene 2 into the 10-turn cap in a four-handed deadlock and requiring a directorial bridge to move the still-contested painting downstairs when clearing the flat for the landlord. Finally, placing a single vivid Tell in each Persona row led performers to fire their physical Tell on nearly every turn rather than saving it for moments where their Shadow was pierced.
