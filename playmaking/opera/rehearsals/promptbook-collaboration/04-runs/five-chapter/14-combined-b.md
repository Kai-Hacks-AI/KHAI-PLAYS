# *Mother's Painting*

## Scene 1: The flat

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

**Ada:** *Steps toward the intercom by the door without pressing it yet, glances down at the canvas resting against the skirting board, and begins tearing a strip of brown packing tape off the roll on the counter.* "That'll be the landlord's agent for the meter readings. Ben, go down and walk him through the basement cupboards; Cora, check the bathroom cabinet one last time; Dan, take the broom to the hall. Leave the picture where it is—I've got a blanket in my boot for the loose corner, so I'll load it in mine once the keys are signed over."

**Dan:** *Leaves the broom untouched against the wall, steps over beside the skirting board by the canvas, and lifts his right heel, holding it an inch off the floorboards.* "Nobody needs to sweep the hall, Ada, and the bathroom cabinet's been empty since ten o'clock. The only reason the four of us are still standing in a bare flat is because every one of us intends to walk out with that picture—and you're not clearing me out of the room so you can decide it's yours while my back's turned. It's coming home with me."

**Ben:** *Leaves the intercom untouched, steps to the counter, and tears four equal strips off the brown tape roll, pressing them in a row along the edge before taking a pen from his jacket pocket.* "Nobody's loading it while backs are turned—*his breath catches short*—Ada, and nobody takes it just by standing over the skirting board, Dan. The agent's on the step, the picture has zero resale value, and this flat has to be empty in five minutes. We put it under one common rule: all four names on identical strips—*a brief catch in his throat*—Cora's and mine included, because a rule's only fair if everyone's in it equally. Face down on the counter, one draw before anyone presses that buzzer, and one of us carries it out cleanly without anyone having to stand here asking for it." *He marks the four strips and folds the first one shut.*

**Cora:** *Saunters to the counter, taps a light beat beside the row of open tape strips, and gives a quick, bright laugh—before her fingers go suddenly still against the laminate.* "A parcel-tape tombola while the letting agent freezes on the step—honestly, Ben, you know how to throw a wake. Put the pen away and chuck the picture on my backseat; my flat's the only one tatty enough to suit a busted frame anyway, so I'll do the three of you a favour and haul the eyesore home myself while you lot fight over who gets the broom."

**Ada:** *Presses the intercom button firmly to buzz the downstairs door, then turns back to the counter and folds her left shirt cuff up into a hard, neat square before peeling the three unfolded tape strips off the laminate and sticking them back onto the roll.* "The agent's coming up the stairs now, and we are not raffling Mother's things off on sticky tape like leftovers, Ben—or tossing a split mitre joint loose onto Cora's backseat to rattle apart before the first roundabout. That corner needs wood glue and a proper clamp tonight before the canvas warps, which means it comes home to my workbench where it'll actually get seen to. Ben, get the meter clipboard off the sill; Dan, step clear of the wall so the man can inspect the skirting."

**Dan:** *Crouches instead of stepping aside, lifts the painting off the skirting board by its unbroken edges, and straightens with it held against his hip, lifting his left heel and holding it off the floorboards.* "There—the skirting board's clear, since you went ahead and buzzed him up without the rest of us getting a say. Drop the carpentry routine, Ada, and quit pretending this is a raffle prize or an eyesore, you two—Mum carried this window into every flat she ever lived in, and once that door shuts there's nothing else of hers left. And that split corner isn't going near your workbench: she asked me to fix the frame, and I kept putting it off visit after visit until she wasn't here to ask anymore. I'm taking it home and fixing it myself."

**Cora:** *Steps between Dan and the hall door, drumming a light tattoo against her hip—until her fingers go suddenly still on the fabric of her coat.* "Easy, Dan—if that corner survived you putting it off visit after visit, sprinting past the letting agent with it now won't magically glue it shut. And I'm allowed to call it an eyesore: Mum sent me a photograph of that painting when I first moved away, so I've already served my time with a postcard-sized kitchen window. Tell you what—hand over the real thing for my car before our guest reaches the landing, and I'll let you bring Ada's clamps round to mine any Sunday you fancy clearing your conscience, free of charge." *She flashes a quick grin and holds one open hand out toward the intact edge of the frame.*

**Ben:** *Drops the pen and the one folded tape strip into his jacket pocket, crosses quickly to the hall door, and plants his palm flat against the closed panel above the latch.* "Leave it where it is, Dan, and Sunday visits at Cora's aren't a deal. Since you tore up an equal draw and decided personal history with the picture is the rule instead, then we apply that rule straight across all four of us. A photograph in the post or a repair you kept putting off doesn't outrank sitting at the actual window inside that frame—and a workbench and wood glue doesn't even put—*his breath catches short*—Ada in the running. I used to sit with Mum at that window when I couldn't sleep. Under the rule you lot just set, the canvas comes home with me, and this door stays shut on it until we've dealt with the agent."

**Ada:** *Folds her right cuff up into a hard square to match the left, collects the meter clipboard off the sill since nobody else moved for it, and steps close to Dan, touching the empty pin-hole in the bare plaster wall before holding her free hand steady beneath the bottom edge of the frame.* "Not in the running, Ben? You sat by that window thirty years ago, Cora got a snapshot in the post, and Dan never showed up with the glue—I'm the one who stood right here and hung that painting on this wall for Mum the last time it ever went up, holding that split corner together with my thumb while she guided it level from her chair. Every job the three of you left behind fell to me, right down to lifting it off the hook this morning, and you don't get to hand me all the weight and then tell me I have no part in her. Ben, take your hand off the latch before the agent knocks; Dan, pass me the frame and pick up the broom so we can clear this flat properly."

**Dan:** *Draws the painting higher against his chest, clear of both outstretched hands, and backs two steps to the bare kitchen window, lifting his right heel and holding it off the floorboards.* "Oh, so you already lifted it off the hook this morning before the rest of us even got here—and I still just get handed the broom like I don't count? Think about what you just said, Ada: you had your thumb right on that split corner the day you hung it, with a whole workbench at home, and Mum still asked me to fix it, not you. Quit dressing this up as chore tallies, or Ben's scoreboard for sleepless nights, or Sunday visiting hours at Cora's—we're just fighting over who mattered most to her now she's not in that chair to choose. Let the agent knock; my hands aren't coming off this frame until I'm walking it out to my car."

## Scene 2: The entrance

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb.*

**Cora:** *Steps onto the threshold beside the propped-open door, jerks a thumb over her shoulder toward the car idling at the kerb, and taps a brisk beat against the doorjamb.* "Top marks for the museum-heist grip all the way down the stairs, Dan—though if we're judging who mattered most, Mum clearly only posted me a snapshot when I moved away because a wooden frame wouldn't fit the pillar box. Anyway, that's mine idling on the kerb with the heater already warm, so I'll do you lot a proper bargain: slide the canvas onto my backseat before a warden books me, and I'll mail Mum's photograph to whichever one of you wins the martyrdom trophy—save Dan buying glue, save Ada's thumb, and save Ben keeping night-shift score." *She gives a quick, crooked smile and reaches two inches toward the unsplit side of the frame—then her fingers go suddenly still in mid-air.*

**Ben:** *Steps past the propped-open door onto the pavement, planting himself squarely between the threshold and the idling car at the kerb while keeping both hands clear of the frame.* "Leaving your engine running at the kerb doesn't buy you a deadline—*his breath catches short*—Cora, and mailing out a snapshot isn't a bargain. Look at this morning: Ada unhooked that canvas before we arrived and killed an even draw, Dan snatched it off the skirting and marched it downstairs like grip equals title, and you've pulled up idling when you already have Mum's photograph at home. Nobody's keeping a night-shift score—just a plain rule in any fair clearing: nobody takes a second piece while others have none, and nobody wins by grabbing first. Under the only fair rule left, it goes home with the one of us who kept his hands off it. Switch your ignition off, and pass it over cleanly—*a sharp catch in his throat*—Dan."

**Ada:** *Tucks the signed meter receipt into her coat pocket, steps out past the threshold onto the pavement between Dan and the kerb, and refolds her loosened left cuff into a hard, tight square before taking her car keys in hand.* "Keeping your hands in your pockets while somebody else does the lifting isn't a fair rule, Ben—if I hadn't taken that canvas off the hook before the movers came through at eight, it'd be crushed flat, and you don't get to leave me every job right down to signing the agent out and then disqualify me because my hands touched the work. And I didn't haul it away to my workbench the day I hung it, Dan, because Mum wouldn't sit looking at a bare wall for one night; she only asked you to bring glue round to give you a reason to show up. Cora, cut that engine—you've already got her photograph at home, and an open joint isn't roasting against your car heater; Ben, kick that wedge inside so the front door latches; Dan, walk that frame two cars down to my boot while I open the tailgate, because I hung it for her and I'm seeing it home."

**Dan:** *Turns the canvas inward against his coat, plants the ball of his foot squarely in front of the door-wedge, and lifts his right heel, holding it an inch off the threshold stone.* "Eight o'clock—so you cleared the wall before the rest of us even got here, Ada, and you still expect me to trot two cars down like your errand boy and load it into your boot. Hear what you just admitted: if Mum left that corner broken just to give me a reason to keep coming round, then this frame was between her and me, not your workbench. Quit inventing rules where the bloke with his hands in his pockets wins by default, Ben, and drop the snapshot swap with your engine running so you can bolt first, Cora—the flat's signed over, and the second that door latches, three of us walk to our cars with nothing left of her and no excuse left to phone each other. Well, you're not deciding I don't count on the pavement too—step aside, because I'm taking it to my car."

**Ben:** *Leaves the door-wedge untouched, steps squarely into Dan's path on the pavement beside Ada, and holds one open hand out flat at chest height.* "I'm not kicking that wedge in—*his breath catches short*—Ada, and my hands aren't in my pockets, Dan. Listen to what the three of you intend to do with it tonight: Ada's taking clamps and glue to it like one last job on her list, Dan's out to mend that corner after she's gone to prove he counted, and—*a brief catch in his throat*—Cora's idling at the kerb to chuck it near a heater and post out a snapshot. A canvas isn't leverage to make us phone each other, and arriving at eight doesn't buy the right to alter it. Mum carried that window into every flat she had and kept it up without letting anyone haul it to a workbench. Under the one fair rule left, the last thing of hers goes where it hangs untouched, just as she kept it. That leaves my wall—so put the frame in my hands before anyone moves for a car."

**Cora:** *Steps off the threshold past Ben and Ada to the idling car at the kerb, swings the rear passenger door wide open, leans in to click the heater dial off while leaving the engine running, and straightens beside the open door.* "There—heater's off, Ada, backseat's cool as a larder, engine stays on. That's your fourth 'only fair rule' since the kitchen counter, Ben, every single edition magically ending on your wall—and counting a photo in an envelope as a second keepsake is like handing me a postcard of the seaside and calling it a holiday. Besides, if the winning law is hanging it untouched with the split corner open, I backed the busted-frame look ten minutes ago. Tell you what, since Dan suddenly went from mocking visiting hours upstairs to worrying nobody's got an excuse to phone: sidestep the pavement blockade, slide the canvas onto my backseat right now, and the three of you can take turns ringing me every month to check I still haven't touched the glue—keeps Ben's non-interference rule intact, saves Ada a job, and gives Dan a reason to dial." *She flashes a quick grin over the open door frame and drums two light beats on the metal rim—before her fingers go suddenly still against the steel.*

**Ada:** *Steps across to the kerb squarely in front of the open rear doorway of Cora's car, blocking the gap to the backseat with her hip, and presses her right cuff tighter into its hard square.* "'Saves Ada a job'—there it is. I'm useful enough to hang her picture, clear her flat, and sign her keys away, and the second the heavy lifting's done the three of you want me sent home empty-handed so nobody has a reason to ring me either. Well, you're not calling neglect a principle, Ben, or turning it into a game in Cora's backseat: I felt how loose that split mitre was under my thumb when I hung it, and if either of you hangs an oil canvas 'untouched' after jolting it home in a car, the bottom rail will give way and drop it straight onto the floorboards. Cora, shut this door before a cyclist takes it off its hinges; Ben, step out of the middle of the footway; and Dan, turn that canvas off your coat buttons before you scratch the paint and bring it down to the blanket in my boot—because it's coming home with the one person who'll actually keep it whole."

**Dan:** *Turns the canvas outward off his coat buttons, supporting its unbroken rails so the painted kitchen window faces the pavement, and steps off the threshold along the building wall opposite the way to Ada's boot, lifting his left heel and holding it off the paving stone.* "There—it's off my buttons, Ada, and at least you finally said the quiet bit out loud: you're terrified that once the jobs are finished, nobody's going to ring you either. Still doesn't mean you get to clear the wall behind our backs at eight and then take over the one thing Mum actually left in my hands like I don't even count in this family. And quit dressing neglect up as a rule, Ben, or turning it into monthly check-in calls from a backseat, Cora—Ada's right that hanging a split mitre untouched will drop the canvas straight onto the floorboards, because Mum never wanted it left broken; she asked me to mend it. Nobody's holding her window hostage for phone calls—it's coming home in my car to get fixed tonight."

**Ben:** *Crosses the footway to the brickwork directly in Dan's path, stopping short before the painted kitchen window now facing outward, both hands raised near the side rails without touching them.* "My first rule on the counter gave me one shot in four—*his breath catches short*—Cora; Ada tore that up. And a split mitre only drops the canvas if you hang it off wire instead of resting the bottom rail flat along a shelf—far safer than Dan rushing a glue job tonight without clamps after putting it off visit after visit. Hear what just came out on this pavement: Cora wants it so we ring her monthly, Ada thinks leaving empty-handed means nobody will phone her again, and Dan's marching off with it to prove he counts. By any fair rule, Mum's last piece doesn't go to someone using it as a receipt for chores, a test of rank, or bait for phone calls—it goes where it carries no ultimatum. Stop walking—*a brief catch in his throat*—Dan, and hand it here to sit flat on a shelf at mine."

**Cora:** *Leaves the rear door open where Ada stands, takes two brisk paces along the kerb to swing the front passenger door wide open instead, and turns toward Dan and Ben with a quick, bright laugh.* "Whoa—don't flatter yourself, Ben; the monthly phone call was a wind-up for Dan, not a cry for help. After twenty minutes on this pavement I'm tempted to change my number anyway. And 'resting on a shelf with no ultimatum' is Rule Number Five from a bloke whose breath catches every time he pretends he isn't asking for the picture just as hard as the rest of us. Fine—new bargain, zero strings: lay the canvas flat across my front seat right now, Dan, I'll rest the bottom rail safely along my hall shelf at home, and none of you ever has to phone me, visit, bring clamps, or feel guilty again. Clean getaway all round." *She pats the window ledge of the open front door with an easy grin—and then her fingers go suddenly still on the rubber trim.*

## Director's Note

- **What I directed and varied (staging choices vs. fixed Play):**
  - Kept `company.md` and `promptbook.md` strictly fixed across four isolated performer sub-contexts, giving each performer only the shared premise, Piece, current scene opening, their own Persona row and single private memory sentence, and the Promptbook rules. Performers were instructed to write strictly observable speech and physical action for their own sibling (no internal thoughts, no scripting others' reactions) and take one turn when cued.
  - **Open conditions left to the cast:** I left open *who* rang the entry buzzer in Scene 1 (Ada cued first on logistics and framed it as the landlord's/letting agent for meter readings, buzzing him up on Turn 5) and *whose* car idled at the kerb in Scene 2 (Cora opened Scene 2 after waiting out the final three turns of Scene 1 and claimed the idling car as hers).
  - **Non-rotational cueing (`# Collaboration`):** Rather than cycling in a fixed loop, I cued whichever sibling was sharpest hit by the preceding move, producing non-rotational sequences (`Ada -> Dan -> Ben -> Cora -> Ada -> Dan -> Cora -> Ben -> Ada -> Dan` in Scene 1; `Cora -> Ben -> Ada -> Dan -> Ben -> Cora -> Ada -> Dan -> Ben -> Cora` in Scene 2).

- **What happened and what carried across scenes:**
  - **Scene 1 (*The flat*):** All four initially pursued the painting under cover of their Actions—Ada assigning empty tasks to clear the room, Dan calling out the unspoken grab, Ben tearing four strips of brown tape for a "common rule" draw, and Cora offering sideways to haul the "eyesore" home—holding back their private memories until Ada buzzed the agent up and claimed the split mitre for her workbench (Turn 5). That physical deadline forced all four private memories out in rapid succession (Turns 6–9): Dan picked up the painting off the skirting board and admitted putting off Mother's frame repair; Cora stepped across the hall door and revealed Mother's mailed photograph; Ben held the door latch and revealed sitting with Mother at that window when he couldn't sleep; and Ada held the bottom rail and revealed hanging it last with her thumb on the split corner, letting slip she had lifted it off the hook at 8 a.m. Scene 1 rested on Dan's Turn 10 realization—that Ada had her thumb on the split corner with a workbench at home and Mother *still* asked Dan to mend it—leaving Dan holding the frame as the agent arrived.
  - **Carried across to Scene 2 (*The entrance*):** Dan carried the painting down to the propped-open entrance in his hands (turning it against his coat, then outward so the painted kitchen window faced his siblings on the pavement); Ada carried the signed meter receipt and the 8 a.m. unhooking revelation; Cora's photograph and Ben's broken draw carried forward as ammunition against them ("nobody takes a second piece"; "Rule Number Five"); and what remained unsettled deepened from *who mattered most to Mother* to Dan's threshold realization that once the front door latches, three of them leave with nothing of hers and no excuse left to phone each other—provoking Ada's rawest admission (*"so nobody has a reason to ring me either"*) and Cora spotting Ben's observable breath-catch Tell on Turn 10.

- **Where the supplied material and Promptbook helped, had no occasion to matter, or made staging hard:**
  - **Helped:** The Piece's physical specifics (`oil of the kitchen window`, `frame open at one corner`, `no resale value`, `Load Bearing`) paired with `# Knowledge` (*withhold private memory until another move threatens to cost them the painting*) paced Scene 1 cleanly: memories arrived under genuine physical jeopardy rather than as early exposition, and immediately interlocked (Ada's thumb on the loose corner vs. Dan's unkept promise to glue it). Observable Tells also carried across blind sub-contexts cleanly enough for Cora to call out Ben's breath-catching in dialogue.
  - **Had no occasion to matter:** The conditional clauses in `# Human` (*unless they explicitly step in to redirect or end play*) and `# System` (*unless the Human explicitly asks for analysis*) never triggered because the Human watched silently throughout.
  - **Made staging hard (distinguishing Play/Promptbook tensions from staging choices):**
    1. *Promptbook tension (`# Agent` vs. resting):* `# Agent` requires every Persona to meet *any* attempt to settle who takes the painting with *"a fresh claim, deflection, or block rather than an easy compromise."* In Scene 1, the approaching agent and disclosure of all four memories produced a natural dramatic rest at Turn 10; in Scene 2, once every memory and Shadow was already exposed on the pavement and no external agent forced closure, that rule structurally prevented settlement and drove an escalating four-way block (Ben inventing five successive "common rules," Cora opening the front car door when Ada blocked the rear), requiring the 10-turn cap to close on Cora's frozen kerbside tableau.
    2. *Staging constraint vs. `# Collaboration`:* `# Collaboration` specifies passing play *"directly from sibling to sibling"* based on who is most affected. Because isolated sub-contexts cannot self-arbitrate turn order without breaking Play Mode, adjudicating "most affected" from the Director's chair was a staging choice required by isolation rather than a defect in `company.md`.
    3. *Minor transition gap in `company.md`:* Moving from all four siblings standing inside *The flat* to *"One car idles at the kerb"* in *The entrance* leaves implicit when an engine was started (especially once three siblings had already mentioned their own cars in Scene 1). Leaving that gap open worked cleanly in practice—Cora claimed she had pulled her car up to the kerb during the handover, and Ada reconciled her own car as parked *"two cars down."*
