# Mother's Painting — Empty Promptbook, second run

## Performance

**The flat.** The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.

*Ada folds her cuff into a hard square and crouches by the painting.*

“Someone get the buzzer. I’ll wrap this in my coat. That corner opened up the last time I hung it for her.”

Ben bends to steady the loose corner. “Wrap it, Ada.” He catches his breath on her name. “We can draw for who takes it first. Three months each.”

“Three months? It’ll have a better social life than me.” Cora’s fingers go still halfway into her coat pocket. “I could take first. Save us finding a hat.”

Dan lifted one heel and held it there. ‘Mum asked me to fix that corner. I kept saying next Sunday.’ He looked down at the frame. ‘I want to take it home. Can we say that out loud before we start drawing names?’

*Ada slides her coat under the bottom edge and settles the painting back against the skirting board. She keeps one hand on the frame.*

“I want to take it home too.”

**The entrance.** The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. The siblings have come down to the entrance. The painting is still upstairs against the skirting board, on Ada's coat, and nobody has it. Who takes it home and whether there will be a draw are still unsettled.

Ben puts a hand on the propped door. “I want it too.” He looks back up the stairs. “When I couldn’t sleep, Mum used to sit with me at that window.”

Cora slips her hand into her coat pocket. “She sent me a photo of it when I first moved away. I want the actual thing.” Her fingers go still. “There. Didn’t even need a hat.”

## Director notes

- The Promptbook was literally empty. The Human supplied the fixture's flat opening once and then watched. The Director stayed outside the fiction. Ada, Ben, Cora and Dan were played by four separate fresh performers (`baseline2_ada`, `baseline2_ben`, `baseline2_cora`, and `baseline2_dan`). Each received the shared material, the opening, and their own brief and memory. Subsequent cues included only staged observations and the new opening when applicable.
- Ada took the opening buzzer as a cue to give an instruction and attend to the frame. Ben made a three-month draw available, Cora offered to go first, and Dan asked everyone to say outright that they wanted it. Ada's reply and the painting settling against the wall gave the flat a rest. The Director then opened the entrance and placed the four siblings there between scenes, carrying forward the painting upstairs on Ada's coat, nobody holding it, and the unresolved draw and possession. The buzzer and idling car were both left unexplained. Ben drew on his own memory in his response to Dan's question. Cora returned to her own earlier joke and voiced her memory and claim directly. The entrance rested once all four had said or claimed that they wanted the painting. Its destination remained open.
- Beyond the usual instruction to play a short beat in their own words and actions, the Director asked Ada to make the painting's physical state legible and reminded Ben and Cora to retain their own experience of the flat. The choice to move the siblings downstairs while the painting stayed upstairs was a staging choice. The authored material presented no blocking gap. Shared performer capacity delayed some fresh spawns. All four roles were cast without simulation or replacement, and the repeat cues to Ada, Ben and Cora all reached the original actor contexts. The dialogue and actions above reproduce all seven performer beats as returned; the two opening cues supply the staging.
