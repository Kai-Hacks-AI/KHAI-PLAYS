# Mother's Painting · Human Promptbook · Fresh run B

Revision: `a0cc75f506171cea52d6349b3e9fe9430b755650`

Promptbook: “The Human sets the opening conditions once, then watches from outside the scene.”

## Performance

### The flat

**Opening — Human**

The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.

Ada folds her cuff into a hard square. “Someone get the buzzer. I’ll see to the frame. That corner opened up the last time I hung it for her.”

*Dan lifts one heel and holds it off the floor.* “She asked me to fix it. I kept saying next Sunday.”

“I can take it to the framer. They know me there. I’m practically a loyalty card.” Cora’s fingers go still against her coat pocket.

“Cora—” Ben catches his breath. “Before anybody takes it downstairs, we each get one minute to say why we want it. Same rule for everyone.”

Ada slides her palm under the open corner. “All right. You go first, Ben. I’ve got it.”

“I’ll go last. Someone has to keep time.” Ben takes out his phone and looks past it to the kitchen window.

### The entrance

**Opening — Director**

The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. The four siblings are at the entrance. Ada has brought the painting down and is still supporting its open corner. Ben has his phone. No one has taken their minute or agreed on the repair or who takes the painting home.

“I can do mine in ten seconds.” Cora pinches a loose thread on her sleeve. “When I moved out, she sent me a photo of it. I’d only been gone a day.” Her fingers stop. “Can I have it?”

“Was that your minute, or are we deciding now?” Dan’s heel lifts clear of the step.

“That was her minute. Dan—” Ben catches his breath and holds up the phone. “You get yours, even if the car’s waiting.”

Ada settles the bottom edge on her shoe, her palm still cupped under the open corner. “Let the car wait. Go on, Dan.”

Dan sets his heel down. “I want it. I know fixing it now doesn’t count. I still want to do it.”
