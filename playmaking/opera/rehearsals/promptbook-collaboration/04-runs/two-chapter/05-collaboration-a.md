# Mother's Painting — Collaboration A

**Promptbook · Collaboration**

> When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move.

## The flat

*Human opening.* The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.

Ada folds her cuff into a hard square. “Can someone get the buzzer? I’ll take the painting. I hung it here for Mum.” She crouches and puts one hand under the loose corner of the frame.

Dan lifts one heel and holds it off the floor. “Leave it there, Ada. I want it too. Mum kept asking me to fix that corner, and I kept telling her next week.”

Ada keeps her palm under the loose corner. “Well, I can hold it while you fix it. We can decide where it goes after.”

Dan crouches beside her. “I want to fix it at mine. Can we talk about who gets it while we’re all still here?”

## The entrance

*Director opening.* The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. The four siblings are together at the door. The painting remains upstairs against the skirting board, and no one is carrying it. Dan has proposed repairing it at his place and asked everyone to talk before leaving. Where it goes remains unsettled.

Ada turns back toward the stairs. “Dan, if you take it home to fix, can you bring it to mine next Sunday? I’ll make lunch. We can all decide then.”

Dan lifts one heel and keeps it up. “I want to keep it, Ada. If we agree on that today, can we still come for lunch?”

Ada picks at the fold in her cuff. “Of course you can come for lunch. I still want it, Dan. I’d already picked out a place to hang it.”

Dan sets his heel down and turns to the other two. “Ada and I both want to keep it. What do you two want?”

“I want it at mine. Mum sent me a photo of it when I first moved away.” Cora’s fingers go still on the doorframe. “You can all come round and look at it. I’ll even get the good biscuits.”

“I want it at mine too.” Ben catches his breath on “Mum.” “She used to sit with me at that window when I couldn’t sleep.” He looks at Dan. “Can we agree that whoever fixes it still has the same say as everyone else about where it goes?”

Dan sets his heel down. “Yeah. I’ll fix it whoever ends up with it. If you’re all okay with me taking it home today, I’ll bring it to lunch and we can decide together.”
