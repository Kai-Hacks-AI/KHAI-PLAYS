## Performance

### The flat

**Human opening**

The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.

**Ada**

*Ada folds her cuff into a hard square, then lifts the painting with one hand cupped under the loose corner.*

“Someone get the buzzer. I’ll take this home.”

**Ben**

Ben lifts a hand toward the frame, then drops it. “Ada—” He catches his breath. “If more than one of us wants it, it stays here till we’ve all had a say. That’s fair, isn’t it?”

**Ada**

*Ada sets the painting back against the skirting board and keeps two fingers on the top edge.*

“All right, Ben. You first.”

**Ben**

Ben looks down at Ada’s fingers on the frame. “I want it. Mum—” He takes a breath. “She used to sit with me at that window when I couldn’t sleep.”

**Cora**

“I’ve already got the photo Mum sent me when I moved away. Might as well make it a set.”

*She grins. Her fingers go still against her coat pocket.*

**Dan**

*Dan lifts his heel and holds it off the floor.*

“I want it. Mum asked me to fix that corner. I kept telling her next Sunday.”

### The entrance

**Fixture opening at the Director’s cut**

The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. Carry forward where the painting is, who has it if anyone, and what remains unsettled.

**Staged continuity**

The four siblings are at the entrance. The painting is still against the flat’s skirting board upstairs. Nobody has it here and no decision has been made.

**Ada**

*Ada turns back toward the stairs.*

“Dan, come up with me. Show me how you’d carry it without opening that corner any further.”

**Dan**

*Dan puts one foot on the bottom stair and looks back at Ben and Clara.*

“I’ll show you. All four of us go up.”

**Cora**

*Cora takes her hand off the front door and turns toward the stairs.*

“Let the car go. I can miss a lift for an oil painting with a wonky corner.”

## Director notes

- The only Promptbook instruction was “When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move.” I read the Director skill and the 02-company fixture. The Human supplied the fixture’s opening once from outside the scene. I staged the second opening as a cut and supplied the continuity stated above.
- Ada, Ben, Cora and Dan were four fresh independent performer contexts, with one active performer at a time. Each received the common premise, the Piece, that Collaboration instruction, their own Persona and memory, the opening, and observable beats through their cue. Repeat cues used the original performer contexts and supplied skipped public beats. The staging directions were one short beat at a time, own dialogue and visible action only, no tool use or sources, and wait for the Director’s next cue. No context was replaced.
- In the flat I picked Ada to start after the buzzer. Her unilateral pickup affected all three siblings, and I used Ben as the tiebreak. Ben’s rule most directly challenged Ada’s claim and addressed her. Her invitation put Ben next. After Ben made his claim I used Cora as the tiebreak between siblings still unheard, then Dan as the sibling still waiting to state a claim. The Promptbook leaves equally affected claims to the Director’s judgment.
- I cut after all four claims were audible. The painting stayed upstairs where Ada set it. Her contact with the frame ended in the cut, and no transfer, repair, response to the buzzer or ownership decision was staged. Ben’s nights at the window, Cora’s photograph and Dan’s deferred repair were public because they said so. Ada’s memory of hanging it remained private.
- At the entrance I cued Ada because Dan’s claim and repair memory put pressure on her initial claim and her handling of the frame. Ada asked Dan directly, and he responded. His invitation reopened access equally for Ben and Cora, who had been left out of Ada’s invitation. I cued Cora as the tiebreak and rested once she committed to turning back toward the stairs. The group had a proposed next action and the painting still had no settled destination. Ben rested through the entrance scene.
- Dan’s second beat called Cora “Clara” in a stage direction. The performance preserves the wording. For Cora’s next cue I relayed the visible look as directed toward her and Ben, consistent with the four people already onstage.
