# Mother’s Painting

## Promptbook

**Human** “The Human sets the opening conditions once, then watches from outside the scene.”

**Collaboration** “When a move affects a claim to the painting, the sibling most affected makes the next move.”

## Performance

### The flat

*The movers have taken the last box. The four siblings are still in Mother's empty flat. The painting is against the skirting board. Someone downstairs rings the entry buzzer.*

Ada folds her cuff into a hard square and crouches by the painting. “Someone get the buzzer. I’ll hold this corner. It came loose the last time I hung it for her.”

Dan lifts one heel and holds it there. “Let me hold it, Ada. Mum asked me to fix that corner. I kept telling her I’d do it next time.”

Ada slides her free hand under the frame. “You take the corner, Dan. I’ve got the weight.”

Dan cups the loose corner. “I want to take it home, Ada. I mean keep it.”

Ada keeps her hand under the frame. “I want to keep it too, Dan.”

Dan holds the loose corner steady. “If I’d gone down for the buzzer, would it still be here when I got back?”

### The entrance

*The front door of the building is propped open. One car idles at the kerb. All four siblings are here. Ada bears the weight of the painting, and Dan steadies its loose corner. Who will take it remains unsettled.*

Ada plants both feet just inside the doorway. “I’d have waited for you here.”

“Mum sent me a photo of it when I moved away. I could take the original and send you both a copy. Very modern.” Cora’s fingers go still against her coat.

Dan lifts his heel off the threshold. “I could send you a photo of it from my place, Cora.”

“I’ve got the photo, Dan.” Cora holds out both hands. “I can take the weight for a bit.”

Ada lifts her end just enough to make room for Cora’s fingers. “Underneath. I’ll wait till you’ve got it.”

Cora slides both palms under the frame. Her fingers go still as it settles against them. “I’ve got the weight.”

Ada lets go of the frame and folds her cuff into a hard square.

“Cora.” Ben catches his breath. “Can we agree it doesn’t go in the car until all four of us have had a chance to ask for it?”

“Agreed. I can be a wall for another minute.” Cora keeps both palms under the frame. “Go on, Ben.”

“Cora.” Ben catches his breath. “I want to take it home. When I couldn’t sleep, Mum used to sit with me at that window.”

Cora looks down at the painted window. “I was asking before, Ben. I made it sound like a joke. I want to take it home too.”

Each performer started in a separate fresh context, with one performer active at a time. The common brief included the premise, full Piece and exact two Promptbook lines. Each received their own Persona and memory only. I asked for one short in-scene move or a rest and left other siblings’ moves to their performers. I gave no direction about what to say, what to decide, or whose eventual claim should prevail. The performers used no tools or outside sources. Each return to a performer used their original context, with the intervening observable moves or new setting supplied in the cue. No context was restored or replaced.

### Handoffs and rests

- I opened with Ada because her Persona acts on clearing work. Her placing a hand at the broken corner implicated Dan’s way of claiming a stake through the repair. Dan directly asked Ada to yield the hold. Ada’s invitation returned the next move to Dan, who stated his claim to the person holding the painting with him. Ada’s competing claim returned the cue to Dan. Cora and Ben stayed quiet in the flat.
- I cut to the entrance on Dan’s question and cued Ada because it challenged the person who had proposed sending someone to the buzzer while taking hold of the painting. Her answer addressed whether she would have waited. It left custody and claims where they were, so I cued Cora from the siblings still resting.
- Cora’s offer of copies challenged both people at the frame. I chose Dan because he had just made the risk of being left out explicit and his stated claim was to keep the object itself. Ada had an equally explicit claim of her own and had been bearing the weight. The Promptbook gives no tiebreaker here. Dan’s counteroffer named Cora and she answered.
- Cora offered to take the weight, so Ada, who was bearing it, responded. Ada invited Cora’s hands under the frame. Cora took the load, and Ada released her hold. I read the release as the completion of the agreed temporary support, with no new claim attached, and used that opening to cue Ben. His long rest let the successive directly affected siblings answer. I gave no requirement for anyone to take a round.
- Ben’s suggested condition on loading the painting went to Cora because he addressed her and she was carrying the load. Her assent and invitation made Ben next. Ben attached his claim to a direct address to Cora, who then made her earlier claim explicit. I rested the scene at that point. Ben would have the next move if the exchange continued. No performer chose a rest on their own cue.

### Carryover and staging choices

Ada and Dan had taken the weight and loose corner respectively, and I carried this physical arrangement to the entrance by an offstage move with all four siblings present. Their memories of hanging the painting and putting off the repair became public through speech in the flat and remained available in the entrance scene. Cora’s photograph and Ben’s sleepless visits to the window became public only when they spoke at the entrance. I introduced no further pressure, assigned no identity to the person at the buzzer or the car, and gave no Human cue during either scene. The offstage walk, points of ambiguity in the cue order, and cut were staging choices. The buzzer was never answered onstage, and Ada’s reply answered the unresolved question it prompted.

By the cut Cora was taking the weight, with Dan’s hand last established at the loose corner. Ada had let go. All four had voiced claims, and Cora had agreed to hold off loading the car until everyone could ask. The painting’s destination, the repair and any broader agreement were still open.
