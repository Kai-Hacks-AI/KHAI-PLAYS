# Review of Draft Opera Guidance for `TO TUNE` and Standard Tenors

## 1. Assessment: *“The nine KHAI Tenors were derived from Navarasa.”*

**Decision:** **Revise (do not assert historical derivation).** The supplied sources verify that Opera’s nine standard Tenors were adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults and support a **comparison** with the nine *rasas* (*Navarasa*), but they do not establish that the KHAI list was historically derived from *Navarasa*.

### Evidence Chain

- **Purpose:**
  - The nine standard Tenors give Playwrights and Directors a shared starting register (dominant key) for authoring or tuning a `Pitch` (`TO TUNE`) at Play, Plot, or individual object/element scope (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors`, lines 1–15).
  - A Tenor shapes pacing, attention, and delivery without altering fixed events or authored facts and while leaving performance choices open; the author completes the Pitch by supplying `Undertow`, `Nerve`, and `Echo` (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors`, lines 3, 15; `ChBrain/khai` `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md#pitch-defaults-the-standard-registers-to-tune-from`, lines 3–16, 29).
- **Sources & Evidence:**
  - `ChBrain/khai` `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md#pitch-defaults-the-standard-registers-to-tune-from` (lines 17–27) defines the nine default tenors carried into Opera (`grieving`, `comic`, `tender`, `furious`, `heroic`, `dread`, `abject`, `wondrous`, `still`) and the four-facet `TO TUNE` structure (`Tenor`, `Undertow`, `Nerve`, `Echo`), with **no mention or citation of *Navarasa***.
  - `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors` (lines 5–13, 28) adapts those nine labels into starter Tenors and cites only the legacy KHAI file (`Adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults.`, line 28).
  - The supplied source notes cite Phillip B. Zarrilli’s article on Indian performance (`https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455`, table on p. 258), which compares nine *rasas* with their associated performer states—supporting a structural correspondence with the nine KHAI Tenors.
- **Limits:**
  - Neither `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md` nor `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md` records how the legacy KHAI list originated or attributes it to *Navarasa*. Thus, correspondence supports a **comparison**, whereas *“were derived from Navarasa”* overstates the evidence.
  - **Uninspected source note:** `https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455` (Zarrilli, p. 258) was offline/inaccessible in this review environment and could not be inspected directly; references to its table rely on the supplied packet summary.
- **Target File/Section & Proposed Edit:**
  - **Preserve** the attested attribution in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md` (line 28, closing attribution line below `#several-pitches-in-one-scene` applying to `#standard-tenors`): keep `Adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults.` without claiming derivation from *Navarasa*.
  - **Change** the proposed sentence wherever *Navarasa* is discussed (e.g., draft Opera commentary, PR notes, or if expanding the provenance note at line 28 of `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md`) to:
    > “The nine standard Tenors are adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults (`packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md`); while the nine registers correspond closely to the nine *rasas* (*Navarasa*; cf. Zarrilli, p. 258), the legacy KHAI source does not record whether the list was derived from them.”

## 2. Assessment: *“The rehearsal demonstrated that the Play, Place, Persona, and Piece can carry distinct Pitches in the same scene.”*

**Decision:** **Revise (do not claim rehearsal demonstration for simultaneous multi-object Pitches in one scene).** Rehearsals demonstrated whole-Play tuning and sequential Plot-level Pitch override-and-return across three Plots (`Dry Comedy` → `Quiet Wonder` → `Dry Comedy`), whereas combining distinct Pitches on the Play, Place, Persona, and Piece within the same scene remains **unrehearsed proposed staging guidance**.

### Evidence Chain

- **Purpose (and the Director’s Intent for the Shoe):**
  - The mixed-Pitch scene example in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (lines 17–26) gives the Playwright and Director a model for directing attention to different elements at distinct registers inside a single scene of *The Giant’s Shoe*: `Play · Comic` (deadpan parade logistics), `Place · Dread` (mounting pressure in the square from the brass warm-up and narrowing route), `Persona · Tender` (Tomas’s care handling the fragile heel), and `Piece · Wondrous` for the shoe.
  - For the **shoe (`Piece · Wondrous`)**, the Director’s intent is specifically to **“give the oversized shoe a moment to register at the child’s eye level”** (line 24) while keeping the shoe’s authored physical properties intact (lone eight-foot boot, buckling flour-paste heel seam) and leaving Tomas free to choose how to respond (line 26).
- **Sources & What the Rehearsals Demonstrated:**
  - **Original single-Pitch rehearsal** (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/pitch/03-observations/README.md#observations-to-tune-in-the-giants-shoe`, lines 3–5; `#1-line-gesture-and-silence-across-all-four-runs-2--2--4`; `#2-how-the-question-lands-across-tenors-dry-comedy-vs-quiet-wonder`): Across four runs (`2 × 2`), each run tuned the **entire Play with one shared Pitch** (`Dry comedy` in runs `01`–`02` vs. `Quiet wonder` in runs `03`–`04`), explicitly noting at line 5 that separate Pitches on individual Personas or multi-Pitch scopes *“remain untested here.”*
  - **Mixed-Pitch three-Plot rehearsal (PR #25)** (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md#observations--play-wide-pitch-default--middle-plot-override-2--2--4`, `#1-four-run-pitch--consequence-matrix`, `#2-observable-register-shifts-across-the-three-plots`; `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return`): Across four runs (`2 × 2` at `Staging limit: 0%`) of a three-Plot adaptation, Condition B runs (`02`, `03`) demonstrated **sequential Plot-boundary switching**—starting in the Play-wide `Dry Comedy` default (Plot 1), overriding Plot 2 with `Quiet Wonder` on `“Is the giant coming?”` (stilling Maren’s finger-tap and shifting to eye-level gravity), and returning cleanly to `Dry Comedy` in Plot 3 with zero lingering wondrous phrasing while carrying physical consequences forward.
- **Limits (What Remains Open):**
  - **Simultaneous in-scene object Pitches are untested:** `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal` (line 30) explicitly marks concurrent multi-object Pitches within the same Plot (`Place · Dread` + `Persona · Tender` + `Piece · Wondrous` under `Play · Comic`) as **“Not yet rehearsed here,”** leaving open how a Director arbitrates simultaneous object-scoped Pitches within a single turn.
  - **Promptbook handoff dependency:** Even for sequential Plot overrides, `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return` (line 9) and `#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence` (lines 20–23) note that clean Plot-boundary transitions relied on explicit Pitch-replacement instructions in the rehearsal’s shared `promptbook.md`, rather than establishing that `khai-director/SKILL.md` alone produces those transitions unprompted.
- **Where the Mixed-Pitch Decision Appears & Proposed Status Wording / Cue:**
  - **Locations:**
    - Sequential Plot-override rehearsal record and scope boundary: `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md#1-four-run-pitch--consequence-matrix` and `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return`, `#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence`, and `#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal`.
    - Illustrative four-scope scene guidance: `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (lines 17–26, where line 19’s hypothetical framing—*“imagine a comic Play-wide Pitch alongside three Pitches linked to individual objects”*—should be preserved rather than restated as rehearsal-proven).
  - **Proposed wording to describe its status** (for draft Opera guidance / PR summary):
    > “Rehearsals demonstrated a Play-wide default Pitch (`Dry Comedy`) overridden on a middle Plot (`Quiet Wonder`) and returning cleanly to the Play default on the following Plot with explicit Promptbook handoffs. Assigning distinct Pitches simultaneously to the Play, Place, Persona, and Piece within the same scene (`pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene`) remains proposed staging guidance that has not yet been rehearsed.”
  - **Associated Director staging cue** (from `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence`, lines 20–23, for `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-director/SKILL.md` line 9 if codifying the rehearsed handoff):
