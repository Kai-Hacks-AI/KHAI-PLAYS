# Review of Draft Opera Guidance: `TO TUNE` and Standard Tenors

> **Source inspection note:** All linked repository sources were inspected directly except Phillip B. Zarrilli’s article (`https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455`, cited in the Sourcebook for its nine-rasa table on p. 258), which was offline/inaccessible and could not be inspected directly.

---

## 1. Assessment: *“The nine KHAI Tenors were derived from Navarasa.”*

**Verdict:** **Revise (unsupported causal claim).** The available sources confirm adaptation from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults and support a structural comparison with *Navarasa*, but do not confirm historical derivation.

### Evidence Chain

- **Purpose:** The nine standard Tenors (`grieving`, `comic`, `tender`, `furious`, `heroic`, `dread`, `abject`, `wondrous`, `still`) give Playwrights and Directors a starting dominant register (`Tenor`) for authoring or tuning a `Pitch` (`TO TUNE`) at Play, Plot, or element scope—shaping pacing, attention, and delivery while keeping events/world facts fixed, preserving performer agency, and asking the author to supply `Undertow`, `Nerve`, and `Echo` (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors`).
- **Source:**
  - `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md#pitch-defaults-the-standard-registers-to-tune-from` (`ChBrain/khai`, lines 8–28) defines the four-facet `TO TUNE` structure and the exact nine default tenors (`grieving` through `still`), with **no citation or reference to *Navarasa***.
  - `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors` (lines 1–15, 28) carries those nine labels into Opera and cites only `"Adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults"`.
  - Zarrilli (`https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455`, p. 258; **not inspected directly**, summarized in the Sourcebook) compares nine rasas with their associated performer states.
- **Limits:** Because `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md` gives no provenance for its nine labels and Zarrilli (`doi:10.1017/S0266464X11000455`, uninspected directly) establishes only an external nine-rasa parallel, the correspondence supports a **comparison**, while the actual origin of the KHAI list remains **unconfirmed**. Writing *"were derived from Navarasa"* overstates a parallel as documented lineage.
- **Target File/Section & Proposed Edit:**
  - **Preserve** `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors` (and its footer attribution on line 28, *"Adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults"*) without adding an unverified derivation claim.
  - **Replace** the proposed sentence in draft Opera guidance/commentary (or in any provenance note attached near `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors`, line 28) with:
    > *"The nine KHAI Tenors are adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults (`packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md`); while the nine registers correspond closely enough to compare with the nine rasas (*Navarasa*), the legacy source gives no citation confirming that the list was derived from *Navarasa*."*

## 2. Assessment: *“The rehearsal demonstrated that the Play, Place, Persona, and Piece can carry distinct Pitches in the same scene.”*

**Verdict:** **Reject/Revise (conflates sequential Plot-override rehearsals with unrehearsed concurrent element-scope guidance).** Neither rehearsal tested Play, Place, Persona, and Piece carrying distinct Pitches simultaneously inside one scene.

### Director’s Intent, Rehearsal Record, and Open Questions

- **Purpose & Director’s intent for the shoe:** The multi-Pitch scene example (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene`, lines 17–26) gives a Director distinct registers to steer attention across elements within one scene— deadpan parade logistics for the crossing (`Play · Comic`), mounting route/brass pressure in the square (`Place · Dread`), Tomas’s careful handling of the fragile heel (`Persona · Tender`), and **`Piece · Wondrous` for the shoe**. Specifically, the Director’s intent for the shoe is to **give the oversized eight-foot boot a moment to register in awe/astonishment at the child’s eye level** without altering its authored physical properties (lone-boot construction and softening flour-paste heel seam) or dictating Tomas’s response.
- **Source (what the rehearsals actually demonstrated) & where the mixed-Pitch decision appears:**
  - **Single-Pitch baseline rehearsal:** `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/pitch/03-observations/README.md` (`#observations-to-tune-in-the-giants-shoe`, lines 3–5; `#1-line-gesture-and-silence-across-all-four-runs-2--2--4`; `#2-how-the-question-lands-across-tenors-dry-comedy-vs-quiet-wonder`) ran four isolated single-scene runs (`2` `Dry comedy`, `2` `Quiet wonder`) using **one shared Play-wide Pitch per run**, explicitly noting (line 5) that separate Persona or multi-Pitch scopes remained untested.
  - **Sequential Plot-override rehearsal (PR #25):** `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md` (`#observations--play-wide-pitch-default--middle-plot-override-2--2--4`, `#1-four-run-pitch--consequence-matrix`, `#2-observable-register-shifts-across-the-three-plots`, `#3-world-fact-trigger-continuity--agency-verification`) staged four runs of a **three-Plot** adaptation (`Staging limit: 0%`): two all-comic runs (`Dry Comedy → Dry Comedy → Dry Comedy`) and two mixed runs (`Dry Comedy → Quiet Wonder → Dry Comedy`, overriding line 5 of `plot-2-question-and-response.md`). In the mixed runs, carriers stilled Maren’s finger-tap and lifted/glided the boot to answer `“Is the giant coming?”` at eye-level gravity in Plot 2, then snapped cleanly back to `Dry Comedy` in Plot 3 (`0` lingering `Quiet Wonder` flags) while carrying Plot 2’s physical load and spoken promises forward.
  - **Where the mixed-Pitch decision appears:** The **four-scope simultaneous scene example** (`Play · Comic`, `Place · Dread`, `Persona · Tender`, `Piece · Wondrous`) appears as an `"imagine..."` illustration in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (lines 17–26). The **scoped Plot-override evaluation and explicit boundary marking concurrent multi-object Pitches as unrehearsed** appear in `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md` (`#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return`, `#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence`, and `#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal`, lines 27–30).
- **Limits (what remains open):**
  1. **Concurrent element-scoped Pitches in one scene are untested:** Combining `Play`, `Place`, `Persona`, and `Piece` Pitches within the same Plot/scene—and how a Director arbitrates competing registers inside a single turn—has not been rehearsed (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal`, line 30).
  2. **Promptbook handoff dependency even for sequential Plot overrides:** Clean switching into `Quiet Wonder` in Plot 2 and clean return to `Dry Comedy` in Plot 3 relied on explicit Pitch-replacement instructions in `02-company/promptbook.md`, so `khai-director/SKILL.md` alone was not independently tested (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return`, line 9; `#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence`, lines 20–23).
- **Proposed Status Wording & Staging Cue:**
  - **Status wording (replacing the proposed sentence in Opera guidance/notes, while preserving the hypothetical `"imagine..."` framing in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene`):**
