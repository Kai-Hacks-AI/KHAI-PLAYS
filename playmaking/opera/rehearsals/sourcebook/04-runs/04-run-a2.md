# Review: Draft Opera Guidance for `TO TUNE` and Standard Tenors

## 1. Assessment: *“The nine KHAI Tenors were derived from Navarasa.”*

**Verdict:** **Revise.** The available sources establish that Opera’s nine standard Tenors were adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults and support a structural **comparison** with the nine *rasas* (*Navarasa*), not a confirmed historical **derivation**.

### Evidence Chain

- **Purpose:** The nine standard Tenors give Playwrights and Directors a shared starting register (dominant tone/key) for authoring or tuning a Pitch at Play, Plot, or individual object scope—shaping pacing, attention, and delivery while preserving authored facts/events and performer choices—before completing the Pitch with `Undertow`, `Nerve`, and `Echo` (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors`, lines 1–16; `ChBrain/khai` `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md#pitch-defaults-the-standard-registers-to-tune-from`, lines 3–16, 29).
- **Sources:**
  - `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors` (lines 5–13) carries over the exact nine Tenor labels and glosses (`grieving`, `comic`, `tender`, `furious`, `heroic`, `dread`, `abject`, `wondrous`, `still`) from `ChBrain/khai` `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md` (lines 17–27) and credits that file directly at line 28 (*“Adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults”*).
  - Per the supplied source notes, Phillip B. Zarrilli’s article on Indian performance (`https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455`, table on p. 258) compares nine *rasas* with their associated performer states, paralleling the nine registers in the KHAI list.
- **Limits:**
  - `ChBrain/khai` `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md` provides zero citations and makes no mention of *Navarasa*, leaving the authorship origin of the legacy KHAI list unconfirmed.
  - Zarrilli’s article (`https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455`) was offline/inaccessible and could not be inspected directly in this review; even as summarized in the packet, it supports comparing the nine KHAI Tenors against *Navarasa*, not asserting causal derivation.
- **Target Location & Proposed Edit:**
  - **Preserve as-is:** Keep the list under `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors` (lines 1–16) and its existing provenance credit at the bottom of `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md` (line 28, under `#several-pitches-in-one-scene`), which `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence` (line 18) confirms works cleanly without change.
  - **Change in draft Opera guidance (or if expanding line 28 of `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md`):** Replace *“The nine KHAI Tenors were derived from Navarasa.”* with:
    > *“The nine standard Tenors are adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults (`packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md`); their nine registers correspond closely to the nine rasas of Navarasa, though the legacy KHAI source records no citation confirming direct derivation.”*

## 2. Assessment: *“The rehearsal demonstrated that the Play, Place, Persona, and Piece can carry distinct Pitches in the same scene.”*

**Verdict:** **Revise.** Rehearsals demonstrated a Play-wide default Pitch yielding to a single middle-**Plot** override and returning cleanly to the Play default with explicit Promptbook handoff instructions. Combining distinct **Play, Place, Persona, and Piece** Pitches simultaneously within the same scene has not been rehearsed and remains **proposed staging guidance**.

### Evidence Chain

- **Purpose & Director’s Intent for the Shoe:** The multi-scope example in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (lines 17–26) gives the Director a way to direct attention across different scopes at distinct registers inside one scene of *The Giant’s Shoe*: `Play · Comic` (deadpan parade logistics), `Place · Dread` (mounting pressure from the brass warm-up and narrowing route), `Persona · Tender` (Tomas’s care with the fragile heel), and `Piece · Wondrous` (*“Give the oversized shoe a moment to register at the child’s eye level,”* line 24). Specifically, the Director’s intent for the shoe (`Piece · Wondrous`) is to carve out an eye-level beat of awe around the eight-foot boot for the child without altering the shoe’s authored physical properties (lone left boot, buckling flour-paste rear seam) or scripting how Tomas responds (lines 24, 26).
- **Sources (What Rehearsals Demonstrated & Where the Mixed-Pitch Decision Appears):**
  - **Single-Pitch baseline (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/pitch/03-observations/README.md`):** Four isolated 5-turn runs of *The Giant’s Shoe* (`#observations-to-tune-in-the-giants-shoe`, lines 3–5; `#1-line-gesture-and-silence-across-all-four-runs-2--2--4`; `#2-how-the-question-lands-across-tenors-dry-comedy-vs-quiet-wonder`) demonstrated that changing a single `Tenor` sentence (`Dry comedy` vs. `Quiet wonder`) across one shared Play-wide Pitch shifts delivery, chalk-line framing, and silence while holding world facts and `Undertow`/`Nerve`/`Echo` fixed. Line 5 explicitly notes that Persona-linked or multi-Pitch scopes *“remain untested here.”*
  - **Sequential Plot-override rehearsal (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md`):** Four runs (`2 × 2`, `Staging limit: 0%`) of a three-Plot adaptation (`#observations--play-wide-pitch-default--middle-plot-override-2--2--4`, lines 1–3; `#1-four-run-pitch--consequence-matrix`; `#2-observable-register-shifts-across-the-three-plots`) compared all-comic runs (`Dry Comedy` → `Dry Comedy` → `Dry Comedy`) against mixed runs (`Dry Comedy` → `Quiet Wonder` → `Dry Comedy`). Both mixed runs shifted cleanly into eye-level gravity on `“Is the giant coming?”` in Plot 2 and snapped back to brisk `Dry Comedy` logistics in Plot 3 with zero lingering override flags while carrying Plot 2’s physical grip and spoken consequences forward (`#3-world-fact-trigger-continuity--agency-verification`, line 36).
  - **Where the mixed-Pitch decisions appear:**
    - The **rehearsed Plot-override result** and its scoping appear in `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md` and `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md` (`#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return`, `#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence`, `#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal`).
    - The **four-scope single-scene combination (`Play`, `Place`, `Persona`, `Piece`)** appears as an illustrative example (*“imagine a comic Play-wide Pitch alongside three Pitches linked to individual objects”*) in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (lines 17–26) and is explicitly categorized under untested scope limits in `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal` (line 30).
- **Limits (What Remains Open):**
  1. **Simultaneous object-scoped Pitches in one scene:** `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal` (lines 29–30) confirms that concurrent `Play · Comic` + `Place · Dread` + `Persona · Tender` + `Piece · Wondrous` tuning within one Plot has **not been rehearsed**, leaving open how a Director arbitrates simultaneous object-scoped Pitches inside a single turn.
  2. **Director-only Plot transitions without Promptbook scaffolding:** `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return` (line 9) and `#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence` (lines 20–21) note that clean Plot 2 → Plot 3 reversion relied on explicit Pitch-handoff instructions in the rehearsal `promptbook.md`, so it does not independently establish that `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-director/SKILL.md` alone produces clean Plot-boundary transitions.
- **Target Location & Proposed Wording / Staging Cue:**
  - **Preserve illustrative framing in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (lines 17–26):** Keep the *“imagine…”* framing so the four-scope scene remains clearly marked as an authoring/staging example rather than an empirical rehearsal claim.
  - **Proposed status wording for draft Opera guidance:**
    > *“Rehearsals demonstrated a Play-wide default Pitch (`Dry Comedy`) switching cleanly to a middle-Plot override (`Quiet Wonder`) and returning to the Play default in the final Plot when supported by explicit Promptbook handoff instructions. Assigning distinct Pitches simultaneously to the Play, Place, Persona, and Piece within the same scene—such as tuning the shoe to `Wondrous` so it registers at the child’s eye level—remains proposed staging guidance and has not yet been rehearsed.”*
  - **Optional companion Director staging cue** (from `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence`, line 23, targeting `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-director/SKILL.md` line 9):
    > *“Use the Play’s default Pitch and any Plot- or object-linked Pitch in scope to tune pacing, attention, and staging, updating the active Pitch when a Plot overrides it or returns to the default.”*
