# Review of Draft Opera Guidance for `TO TUNE` and Standard Tenors

## 1. Assessment: “The nine KHAI Tenors were derived from Navarasa.”

**Verdict:** **Do not adopt as written.** The claim overstates the record: available sources confirm that Opera’s nine Tenors come from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults and support a **comparison** with *Navarasa*, but they leave actual historical derivation unconfirmed.

### Evidence Chain

- **Purpose:** The nine standard Tenors (`grieving`, `comic`, `tender`, `furious`, `heroic`, `dread`, `abject`, `wondrous`, `still`) give Playwrights and Directors a starting dominant register for authoring or tuning a Pitch at Play, Plot, or element/object scope (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors`; `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md#pitch-defaults-the-standard-registers-to-tune-from`). A Tenor shapes pacing, attention, and delivery without altering authored facts, events, or performer agency, while the author supplies `Undertow`, `Nerve`, and `Echo` to complete or adapt the Pitch (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors`).
- **Source & Evidence:**
  - Opera’s nine labels and glosses in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors` (L5–L13) are adapted directly from the nine default tenors in `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md#pitch-defaults-the-standard-registers-to-tune-from` (L17–L27), as credited at the bottom of `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (L28).
  - Per the supplied source notes, Phillip B. Zarrilli’s article on Indian performance (`https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455`, p. 258 table; external DOI source not directly accessible for inspection) compares nine *rasas* with their associated performer states. The structural correspondence between those nine registers and the nine KHAI Tenors supports comparing the two lists.
- **Limits:**
  - `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md` gives **no citation or mention of *Navarasa*** (or Zarrilli) for its nine default tenors, and `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md` cites only `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md`.
  - Because Zarrilli’s article (`https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455`) could not be inspected directly and documents Indian performance theory rather than KHAI’s authoring history, the origin of the legacy KHAI list remains **unconfirmed**. Asserting that the KHAI Tenors “were derived from Navarasa” converts an unverified parallel into a genealogical fact.
- **Target Location & Proposed Edit:**
  - **Preserve in file:** Keep the existing provenance line at the end of `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (L28: `Adapted from the [legacy KHAI Pitch defaults](...).`), which accurately states the confirmed source without claiming *Navarasa* derivation.
  - **Revise if referencing *Navarasa*** (in review/guidance prose or as an expanded note in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors` / L28):
    > “Opera’s nine standard Tenors are adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults (`packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md`). Their nine registers correspond closely to—and can be compared with—the nine *rasas* of *Navarasa*, though the legacy source does not record whether the KHAI list was derived from *Navarasa*.”

## 2. Assessment: “The rehearsal demonstrated that the Play, Place, Persona, and Piece can carry distinct Pitches in the same scene.”

**Verdict:** **Reject and revise.** Neither rehearsal staged simultaneous Play, Place, Persona, and Piece Pitches within one scene. That four-scope setup is an **unrehearsed illustrative example (proposed staging guidance)**, whereas the rehearsals demonstrated (a) one shared Play-wide Pitch per run and (b) a Play-wide default Pitch with a sequential middle-Plot override and return under explicit Promptbook transition instructions.

### Evidence Chain

- **Purpose (and Director’s intent for the shoe):**
  - Multi-scope Pitch links allow a Director to direct attention across different registers within one scene without changing authored facts or performer choices (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene`, L19–L26).
  - Specifically for the shoe (`**Piece · Wondrous.** Give the oversized shoe a moment to register at the child’s eye level.`, L24), the Director’s intent is to give the eight-foot boot a brief wondrous beat of attention at the child’s eye level inside an otherwise deadpan-logistic crossing (`Play · Comic`, L21), alongside mounting square pressure from the brass warm-up and narrowing route (`Place · Dread`, L22) and Tomas’s careful handling of the fragile heel (`Persona · Tender`, L23)—while the shoe retains its authored physical properties and Tomas still chooses how to respond (L26).
- **Source & Evidence (What the rehearsals actually demonstrated):**
  - **Original single-Pitch rehearsal (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/pitch/03-observations/README.md#observations-to-tune-in-the-giants-shoe`, L3–L5; `#1-line-gesture-and-silence-across-all-four-runs-2--2--4`; `#2-how-the-question-lands-across-tenors-dry-comedy-vs-quiet-wonder`):** Staged four single-scene runs of *The Giant’s Shoe* (`2 × 2`), each tuning the **whole Play through one shared Pitch per run** (`Dry comedy` in `01`–`02` vs. `Quiet wonder` in `03`–`04`, changing only the `Tenor` sentence). It showed that a shared Play-wide Tenor shifts delivery, chalk-line safety vs. stillness, and silence while holding world facts and `Undertow`/`Nerve`/`Echo` fixed, and explicitly noted (L5) that Persona-linked or multi-Pitch scopes remained untested.
  - **Mixed-Pitch rehearsal (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md#observations--play-wide-pitch-default--middle-plot-override-2--2--4`; `#1-four-run-pitch--consequence-matrix`; `#2-observable-register-shifts-across-the-three-plots`):** Staged four runs (`2 × 2`) of a **three-Plot** adaptation at `Staging limit: 0%`, comparing all-comic Condition A (`Dry Comedy → Dry Comedy → Dry Comedy`) against mixed Condition B (`Dry Comedy → Quiet Wonder → Dry Comedy`). Condition B demonstrated **sequential Plot-level override and return**: switching cleanly from the Play-wide default (`Dry Comedy`) into Plot 2’s override (`Quiet Wonder`) on `“Is the giant coming?”` and snapping cleanly back to `Dry Comedy` in Plot 3 with zero lingering wondrous phrasing while carrying Plot 2’s physical grip consequences forward (`#3-world-fact-trigger-continuity--agency-verification`, L36).
- **Limits (What remains open) & Where the Decision Appears:**
  - **Where the mixed-Pitch decision appears:**
    - The four-scope same-scene example (`Play · Comic`, `Place · Dread`, `Persona · Tender`, `Piece · Wondrous`) appears as a hypothetical illustration (*“imagine a comic Play-wide Pitch alongside three Pitches linked to individual objects”*) in **`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene`** (L17–L26).
    - The rehearsed sequential scope (`Play` default + middle-`Plot` override + return) and the explicit exclusion of simultaneous multi-object Pitches appear in **`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return`** (L9–L12) and **`#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal`** (L28–L30), backed by **`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md`**.
  - **What remains open:**
    1. **Concurrent same-scene element Pitches:** Simultaneous `Play` + `Place` + `Persona` + `Piece` Pitches within a single Plot—and how a Director arbitrates competing object-scoped registers inside one turn—have **not been rehearsed** (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal`, L30).
    2. **Unassisted Director transitions:** Even the rehearsed Plot-boundary switch and return relied on explicit Pitch-transition handoff rules in the rehearsal’s shared `promptbook.md`; `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return` (L9) and `#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence` (L20–L23) note that it does not independently establish that `khai-director/SKILL.md` alone produces those transitions without that Promptbook handoff (or the optional `khai-director/SKILL.md#L9` wording update).
- **Proposed Status Wording & Staging Cue:**
  - **Preserve in `pitch-defaults.md`:** `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (L19) already frames the four-scope block with *“imagine…”* rather than claiming rehearsal proof (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence`, L18).
  - **Replacement status wording (for guidance/review summaries):**
    > “Rehearsals demonstrated tuning a Play with one shared Pitch per run (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/pitch/03-observations/README.md`) and, with explicit Promptbook Pitch-transition handoffs, overriding a Play-wide default Pitch (`Dry Comedy`) on a middle Plot (`Quiet Wonder`) before returning cleanly to the default (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md`; `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal`). Combining distinct Pitches across the Play, Place, Persona, and Piece within the same scene (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene`) remains proposed staging guidance that has not yet been rehearsed.”
  - **Staging cue if advancing the claim:** Before describing same-scene multi-object Pitches as rehearsed, stage a dedicated rehearsal in which `Play · Comic`, `Place · Dread`, `Persona · Tender`, and `Piece · Wondrous` are simultaneously active within one Plot to test how the Director arbitrates concurrent registers (including giving the shoe an eye-level wondrous beat inside a comic crossing) without overriding performer agency.
