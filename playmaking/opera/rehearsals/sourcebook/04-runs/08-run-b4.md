# Review: Draft Opera Guidance for `TO TUNE` and Standard Tenors

---

## 1. Assessment: *“The nine KHAI Tenors were derived from Navarasa.”*

**Verdict: Reject / Revise.** The sentence overstates the primary record by turning a structural parallel into a confirmed historical derivation.

### Evidence Chain

- **Purpose:** The nine standard Tenors give Playwrights and Directors a shared starting register (`Tenor`, the dominant key) when authoring or tuning a `Pitch` (`TO TUNE`) at Play, Plot, or element scope. A Pitch tunes pacing, attention, and delivery while holding authored facts and events fixed and keeping performer choices open; authors complete a Pitch by supplying `Undertow`, `Nerve`, and `Echo` (`playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors`, lines 1–15; `ChBrain/khai`: `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md#pitch-defaults-the-standard-registers-to-tune-from`, lines 3–16).
- **Source & Evidence:**
  - Opera’s nine Tenors (`grieving`, `comic`, `tender`, `furious`, `heroic`, `dread`, `abject`, `wondrous`, `still`) in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors` (lines 5–13, 28) are adapted directly in label and gloss from the legacy KHAI defaults in `ChBrain/khai` (`packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md#pitch-defaults-the-standard-registers-to-tune-from`, lines 17–27).
  - As summarized in the packet’s Sourcebook (`#origin`), Phillip B. Zarrilli’s article on Indian performance (`https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455`, p. 258 table) compares nine *rasas* with their associated performer states, supporting a conceptual comparison across the nine registers.
- **Limits (and Uninspected Sources):**
  - `ChBrain/khai`’s `packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md` (lines 1–30) contains **no citation or reference to Navarasa** (or Zarrilli). The primary sources leave the historical origin of the legacy KHAI list unconfirmed; only a comparative parallel is supported.
  - *Source inspection note:* The external DOI source (`https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266464X11000455`) was offline/inaccessible and could not be inspected directly; its p. 258 comparison is cited solely from the supplied Sourcebook summary.
- **Target Location & Proposed Edit:**
  - **Preserve** the verified provenance line at the end of `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (line 28): `Adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults.`
  - **Change** the proposed sentence wherever Navarasa is discussed in `TO TUNE` guidance (or if added to `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#standard-tenors`) so it states provenance and comparison without claiming derivation:
    > *“The nine standard Tenors are adapted from the legacy KHAI Pitch defaults (`packages/khai-arch/defaults/pitch.md`). Their nine registers parallel the nine rasas of Navarasa (cf. Zarrilli, p. 258), though the legacy source records no citation confirming direct derivation.”*

## 2. Assessment: *“The rehearsal demonstrated that the Play, Place, Persona, and Piece can carry distinct Pitches in the same scene.”*

**Verdict: Reject / Revise.** Rehearsals demonstrated single-Pitch Play runs and **sequential Plot-level** Pitch overrides across scenes, not **simultaneous Play, Place, Persona, and Piece Pitches** inside the same scene. The four-scope scene combination is proposed staging guidance, not a rehearsed finding.

### Evidence Chain

- **Purpose & Director’s Intent for the Shoe:**
  - The mixed-Pitch scene illustration in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (lines 17–26) models how a Director can aim attention at distinct registers within one crossing of *The Giant’s Shoe*: `Play · Comic` (deadpan parade logistics), `Place · Dread` (mounting pressure in the square via brass warm-up and narrowing route), `Persona · Tender` (Tomas’s care on the fragile heel), and `Piece · Wondrous` for the shoe.
  - **Intent for the shoe (`Piece · Wondrous`, line 24):** *“Give the oversized shoe a moment to register at the child’s eye level.”* This cue asks the Director to give the eight-foot boot a brief beat of awe at the child’s sightline while keeping the shoe’s authored properties intact (lone left boot, softening flour-paste rear seam) and leaving Tomas free to choose how to respond (lines 24–26).
- **Sources & What the Rehearsals Actually Demonstrated:**
  - **Single-Pitch Rehearsal** (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/pitch/03-observations/README.md#observations-to-tune-in-the-giants-shoe`, lines 3–5; `#1-line-gesture-and-silence-across-all-four-runs-2--2--4`; `#2-how-the-question-lands-across-tenors-dry-comedy-vs-quiet-wonder`, lines 22–36): Four runs (`2 × 2`) tuned the entire single-scene Play through **one shared Pitch per run** (`Dry comedy` vs. `Quiet wonder`, varying only the `Tenor` sentence). Line 5 explicitly notes that separate Pitches linked to individual Personas or multi-Pitch scopes *“remain untested here.”*
  - **Mixed-Pitch Rehearsal (PR #25)** (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md#1-four-run-pitch--consequence-matrix`, lines 9–14; `#2-observable-register-shifts-across-the-three-plots`, lines 18–28; `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return`, lines 7–12): Four runs (`2 × 2`, `Staging limit: 0%`) of a **three-Plot** adaptation demonstrated **sequential Plot-level override and return**—starting in Play-wide `Dry Comedy` (Plot 1), switching cleanly to Plot 2’s linked `Quiet Wonder` override on `“Is the giant coming?”` in both Condition B runs, and snapping cleanly back to Play-wide `Dry Comedy` in Plot 3 while carrying physical and spoken consequences forward.
- **Limits / What Remains Open:**
  - **Concurrent multi-object Pitches in one scene are unrehearsed:** `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal` (lines 29–30) explicitly records as *“Not yet rehearsed here”* simultaneous multi-object Pitches inside the same Plot (`Place · Dread` + `Persona · Tender` + `Piece · Wondrous` under `Play · Comic`), leaving open how a Director arbitrates competing object-scoped Pitches within a single turn.
  - **Promptbook scaffolding dependency:** `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return` (line 9) and `#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence` (lines 20–23) note that clean Plot-boundary switching and zero lingering `Quiet Wonder` in Plot 3 relied on explicit Pitch-transition handoff rules in `02-company/promptbook.md`, and do not independently prove that `khai-director/SKILL.md` alone produces the switch and return without a follow-up skill update.
  - *Source inspection note:* Cross-referenced company/run files (`04-runs/*.md`, `02-company/promptbook.md`, `references/pitch.md`, `khai-director/SKILL.md`) were not included in the supplied snapshot packet and are cited via the supplied observation and evolution records.
- **Where the Mixed-Pitch Decision Appears & Proposed Status Wording:**
  - **Decision & scope locations:** The four-scope concurrent scene example appears in `playmaking/opera/skills/khai-playwright/references/pitch-defaults.md#several-pitches-in-one-scene` (lines 17–26, introduced with *“imagine a comic Play-wide Pitch alongside three Pitches linked to individual objects”* and left unchanged per `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#2-smallest-optional-revision-warranted-by-the-evidence`, line 18). Its unrehearsed boundary is recorded in `playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/05-evolution/README.md#3-scope-limits-of-this-rehearsal` (lines 29–30).
  - **Proposed status wording (replacing the draft sentence):**
    > *“Rehearsals demonstrated sequential Plot-level Pitch switching—a Play-wide `Dry Comedy` default overridden by `Quiet Wonder` in a middle Plot and returning cleanly to `Dry Comedy` in the next Plot with explicit Promptbook handoffs (`playmaking/opera/rehearsals/mixed-pitch/03-observations/README.md#1-four-run-pitch--consequence-matrix`; `05-evolution/README.md#1-did-the-updated-guides-support-the-switch-and-return`). Give the oversized shoe a moment to register at the child’s eye level.` explicitly framed under `"imagine..."` as an illustrative Tenor example rather than a rehearsal-validated multi-object run.
