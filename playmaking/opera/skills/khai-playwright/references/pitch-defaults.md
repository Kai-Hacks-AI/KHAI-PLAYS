# Standard Tenors

Choose a starting register for a [Pitch](pitch.md) at any scope. The Playwright can start from these Tenors when authoring a Pitch, and the Director can consult the same list when tuning a run. Pitch can shape pacing, attention, and delivery while preserving authored facts and events and leaving choices open to the performers.

- **grieving**: loss and compassion. The ache of what cannot be helped.
- **comic**: the ludicrous and the social. Wit, deflation, the knot tied and untied.
- **tender**: longing and attraction. Intimacy and the pull between figures.
- **furious**: heat and confrontation. The clash and the violence of will.
- **heroic**: courage and resolve. The cost met head on.
- **dread**: foreboding and the closing dark. The thing approaching.
- **abject**: the repellent and the unclean. Recoil and what cannot be stomached.
- **wondrous**: awe and astonishment. The strange made present.
- **still**: quiet and release. Acceptance, the calm after.

Complete the Pitch by choosing an Undertow, the feeling it should strike in the audience (Nerve), and what should linger (Echo). The Tenors above can be adapted to fit the work.

## Several Pitches in one scene

In [*The Giant’s Shoe*](../../../rehearsals/pitch/02-company/README.md), imagine a comic Play-wide Pitch alongside three Pitches linked to individual objects. The directions below are examples of Tenor.

- **Play · Comic.** Play the crossing with the deadpan practicality of parade logistics.
- **Place · Dread.** Give the square a sense of mounting pressure through the brass warm-up and the narrowing route.
- **Persona · Tender.** Let Tomas’s care show in the way he handles the fragile heel.
- **Piece · Wondrous.** Give the oversized shoe a moment to register at the child’s eye level.

Each Pitch shapes its own scope. The square stays the same place, the shoe keeps its authored properties, and Tomas still decides how to respond.
